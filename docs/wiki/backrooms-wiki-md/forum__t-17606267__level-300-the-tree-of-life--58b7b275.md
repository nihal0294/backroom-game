I really want to like this page. You clearly put effort into it, and the strange mix of wonderland-esque fairytales and religious stuff isn't half bad, at least on a moment-to-moment basis.

But I feel this page suffers from trying to do too much. You have 5 different areas, 6 plants, 5 entities (one of which is sapient) and 4 groups. That's a hell of a lot for one page, and it makes everything feel underdeveloped as a result. You open by saying that almost all plant life in the Backrooms originates from this tree. That's an extremely radical concept, and one that opens many questions. How do the plants get from 300 to their respective levels? How are they fed by the tree's roots if the roots aren't always physically connected to the plants in question? If the wheat from Level 10 came from the tree, why is it only found in Level 10 and not on other, similar levels? I'm not saying these are unanswerable, quite the opposite, I'm instead trying to say that this is quite a deep idea that's treated as some minor point.

Same thing goes with the tree people. This is a sentient entity as old as the entire Backrooms, you should be able to say more than just their basic biology and a rough outline of their culture. Again with the M.E.G. base, and the flora and the fauna, and the mystery of the top of the tree. You're brimming with ideas, but they just don't go anywhere.

This really hurts the big twist in the offset. If I'd spent a lot more time with the tree people, maybe the realization of their inhumanity would have hit harder, but as it stands all it's contradicting is a few paragraphs that barely give me an indication of what they're supposed to be like. Same thing with the blocking of all the exits. That *should* show what a dire situation this is, that such an important level had to be blocked off, but the importance of the level really didn't feel as prominent to me as you probably hoped for, because you spent more time discussing weird animals which don't really contribute to the page other than via the general vibe they project.

Also, maybe I'm going mad, but I cannot figure out what this sentence is trying to say, I don't think it's even grammatically correct.

> The fact that The Tree of Life's photosynthesis and the connection of its roots to the soil of each level are reason enough for no one to try, in any case.

-1

17 May 2026 23:13

Many elements in this page seem to aim to create a sense of grandiosity, yet they ultimately rely on big, extravagant claims about lore rather than fleshing out the level itself. The tree is the origin point for backrooms plants, and is connected to all levels with plants, yet it doesn't bring any further conceptual intrigue to those levels beyond inserting itself in them. The tree has every species of animal living within it, presumably from the largest of megafauna to the most extreme of hadal invertebrates to the most specific oddballs endemic to any lake anywhere, yet they are not given much importance in relation to the M.E.G. and the level-specific entities. *Sequoiadendron sapiens* have existed since the beginning of time, implying they're older than Earth, and have lived through who knows how many millions or billions of years, yet their society isn't shown to be much deeper culturally than a simple, exuberantly ordinary village. We're given the impression this is possibly one of the most important levels in the backrooms, yet everything building this level isn't given much development, leading everything to feel shallow.

I do like how the focus of the offset is gradually laid out as the page progresses. The mystery as to what's at the top of this unfathomably large redwood, the small glimpses of rumors of what lies up there, mentions of various animals and entities appearing strangely and doing unnerving things, it's all building a feeling that something is off here. The offset itself is a nice refresher too, strengthened by being a story where things happen. Unfortunately, a lot of the build-up is drowned out by the rest of the page. Between every small glimpse that actually ties into where the story is going, there's a large quantity of text that does not do much for the page other than clinically describe the properties of what exactly we are looking at. By the time I reached the offset, I was surprised that the story became wholly centered around an oddly specific fruit that only gets a few brief mentions. The buildup is present, but not to a point where everything in the preceding page works together to lead to an ultimate conclusion. Many elements end up becoming non-sequiturs as a result, ultimately not having mattered for what the story actually is.

I feel bad saying this, but the page here feels especially like it's a level in a video game. The real-world plants and animals and ecosystems are all far off in the background to set the tone without interacting much with the main focus of the story, the dozen or so level-specific organisms are given the depth of NPCs that the player either collects, befriends, or avoids, and the angels are what gate later/high-level areas like the blue raspberries and whatever boss is presumed to lie at the top of the tree. The naming conventions are very self-evident; The daybats are bats who like the day, the moss bears are patches of moss with the strength of bears, the tree people are people who are also trees. The same dozen or so organisms are all that matter here, like the same dozen or so enemies and items a player passes by in a dungeon. There isn't much to suggest that these elements all exist in their own world, having deeper life and nuance to be written with, each element being a character in its own right. The page isn't very open-ended; it gives out the lines of, here's what this level is, here's what the things in it are, here's what the intended story is. In terms of potential creative elaboration or character development, the page ends up feeling flat.

On a more personal note, I personally don't like the idea of every plant in the backrooms coming from one central tree. Plants are incredibly diverse, wonderfully complex creatures that all give an enormous amount of personality to an enormous amount of unique environments. To centralize them all to a single organism feels limiting. It feels like something born of plant blindness, of the idea that these are all basically the same thing anyways, so there's no point making tons of writing about them. For the record I'm not saying I think that was the intention here, I'm sure a lot of care went into writing this page, but I just can't shake the feeling that this premise is a bit reductive. It makes me wonder if the backrooms even has things like flowers, or fruits, or seeds. This isn't really a factor in my -1, and I understand that anyone can choose not to follow what's laid out here in their own writing, and I acknowledge that I may be biased in this regard because I like plants quite a bit and want to see them elaborated upon more in fictional worlds in general. I just felt like this was worth mentioning.

I hope this doesn't seem like a malicious hitpiece or anything, this is my first time critiquing a page in a discussion forum. I think there are a lot of ideas here that are interesting on paper; I like the idea of an individual tree so big it becomes a level itself, the idea that it has something strange hidden at the top, I like the animal entities described here. I especially like the moss bears, they're really weird in a fittingly eerie way. I think the ideas here could work to create a great page, though I don't think the page here develops them well enough.
