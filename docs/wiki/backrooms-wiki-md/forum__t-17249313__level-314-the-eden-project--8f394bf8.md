-1 Way beyond the scope of the backrooms

05 Jun 2026 21:06

Any narrative where humans are in any way suggested to have some control over the very nature of the backrooms has to be done tactfully and extremely carefully, and I feel like this misses the mark by a looooong shot.

We have a device engineered out of ??? that does something ambiguous (I still don't get it even after reading the explanation on discord) and there's some experimentation happening that I don't really get the point of even after reading twice? Like I just don't get the narrative. What's going on here exactly?

The first two offsets are fluff and they're not particularly exciting to read. The fourth offset is inscrutable to me, I don't get what happened in the offset before it which means I don't get the significance of the ending, and the third offset is just the VERY vague experiment log that I'm sure you've planned out behind the scenes, but the front end aspect of it is just very very confusing. I think if you're going to talk about physics in the backrooms, you owe it to the readers here to make things explainable or understandable. The use of complicated and abstract jargon just makes this real hard to comprehend.

There's also the issue of the excessive technology in general with this article. You're telling me we have complex AI systems, reality-warping devices, devices that understand the Backrooms' inner workings (detection of levels?? There's a concrete way to do this now?), but people are simultaneously struggling to survive? Where are people getting the materials to do this? How are they doing it in the first place? What changed in the backrooms that allowed us to distort reality and understand it to such a fundamental degree that we can't replicate outside of the backrooms?

I feel like this is way too ambitious of an idea for this site and I also think that writing something of this scale requires not just the ability to parse physics texts yourself, but also to explain it to a generalised audience. Don't get me wrong, I love scientific articles myself and I tend to get into the nitty-gritty jargon as well, but if I can't google a term and understand it, or if there's so much fictional technobabble that I can't even understand what the article is trying to say, then you as an author have failed to convey the idea to me. Especially because I saw you're trying to expand on this, I think you ought to get your facts straight for the audience before you continue writing further.

Other than all that, there's an issue of sloppy writing and awkward dialogue that I can't ignore. The first offset is too barebones for what it implies (how many 'voids' are the in the backrooms?), the second offset just sounds too pastiche for what it's trying to be (an advert for something that's too good to be true as it is described), and the third and fourth offsets just seem way too plastic for dialogue between two people.

I think if you intend to expand on this, you owe it to yourself and readers to perfect the most important 'hook' article before you start looking to further expand. You're allowed to leave some of the exact mechanisms for other articles if you so please, because you're not obliged to make every article you ever write completely thorough right? But even ignoring that, the article itself definitely needs to be polished more.
