Re: Level idea "A regular neighborhood" (Level 307)

16 Jan 2025 22:09

Alright! Since this is still in the early stages, I’m just going to do a concept crit. Language and format can come later.

Things I Like

- the rings- this layout stands out as distinct for me, and makes for a distinct experience traversing the level

- the under-the-car pocket dimension, and the guy blowing off steam by running over entities. That was a great touch- finally getting some measure of power of entities and totally absusing it. What’s worse- getting hit by a car and dying, or just being imprisoned in an infinite void? Interesting thought

- the benevolent vending machine (have you heard of a podcast called “Everything is Alive”? It’s a guy who interviews inanimate objects- it’s fantastic. There’s an episode where he interviews a vending machine- could be worth a listen, for ideas on developing its personality.) There aren’t a whole lot of things in the Backrooms that try to help humanity, so the little moment of “you can have anything you want” comes as a huge relief.

- realizing that the residents of the vending machine were actually created by the machine (that was a great reveal)

Questions  
These are questions I had while reading. I like the raw materials of this level, but a lot of ideas feel like they aren’t fully explained. In some cases, details are introduced in the middle of the writing, when stating them in the introduction would help give readers a more cohesive sense of what’s going on. Some things I’m unsure about:

- Does this level have a day/night cycle, or does it only switch when the sun shallower entity switches it?

- What does this level look like, overall, in normal circumstances? How does it appear to someone who no-clips into it? Can people see all the other rings, from within any given ring? Or only when they stand at ground level? How does it look while standing on the rooftops? Do rings disappear into nothingness, in some conditions? Do sun swallowers affect different rings separately? (does each ring have its own sun?) Are there rings of light and nothing?

All of these things need explicit description, at the beginning. The logs can come later, and describe peoples' personal experiences witnessing and discovering these things, but the information needs to come first.

- *“There is also, 5 rings away from the car rings, a door to level 276, where entities will walk out.”*

Specify where this door is. If it’s the front door of a house, this should be clearly stated. Is every house in a Hive ring an entrance to the Hive, or just one random house?  
Also, I would clarify the numbering of the rings. Every ten rings, cars appear, and five rings from the cars, the doors appear- so the hive rings are always halfway in between the rings with cars.

- The situation of the MEG explorer in the level needs clarification. If he’s there with a team, that should probably be stated in the first log. How many people is he with? Does the team have a name? Did they enter by accident, or are they on a deliberate mission? How many times have they been there? How much of what they know is the result of their own exploration, vs stories from other wanderers?

- *“I looked over the fence just in time to see one of my colleagues disappear. My best friend. You fuckers didn't even try to find him. I'd know if you did. You just told me he was dead! I'm this close to leaving M.E.G. You'd better help me.”*

Also, what’s happening here isn’t clear- there’s too much backstory left unexplained. Is this what’s going on: the protag watched a Sun Shallower touch his friend; his friend vanished; the rest of his team (reasonably) assumes he’s dead at this point; the protag wants to search for him, but everyone else has already given up; protag gets mad; he considers quitting the MEG; he threatens the rest of his team to help him find his friend; this all happened earlier in the day and he’s venting later, in his log. This makes the above sentence make sense. But regardless if this is what’s going on, or not, the situation needs to be described in more detail.

- *“I ran over a Sun Swallower. My theory was confirmed. There are no neighborhoods over any fences. It is always day. There are no entities anymore.”*

Same with this. This needs way more explanation- I can’t tell what’s going on. What’s the guy’s theory? How is it always day, if the sun can be switched off? Have the other rings vanished, or are they never real until someone enters them? Did the entities vanish/get sun-swallowed, or did he run them all over? How are all these things connected?

- *“I'm going to go to the M.E.G base, turn in my logs and quit. I'm done with you liars.”*

Same with this. Why are they liars? What happened between this guy and the rest of the MEG?”

- How does the MEG know what Under the Car looks like? And how can they know about the insanity effect of looking at the ceiling? The only way to know this, and have it recorded on the page, is either for someone to have survived it and recorded their experiences, or for two operative to have entered the sub-level together, and one watched the other look up and go insane- and maybe that person babbled a bit about what they saw. Also, is there any way out of this sub-level? Or are MEG operatives trapped here, and uploading what they witness to the archive for posterity?

Thoughts & Ideas  
These were ideas I had for ways to expand on the ideas of the page, and improve the cohesion.

- Right off, I like the title, but I don’t think it really reflects what’s going on in the level. This neighbourhood is anything but regular. The reason I like the title is because it implies that everything is normal and fine, which is a great set-up for subversion or the uncanny valley- a place where something’s “off” but otherwise perfect. That’s not what’s going on here, though. This level is visibly very weird, right from the get go. I would consider re-working the title to something that characterizes it more accurately.

- I personally think that when writing a level with an overall “theme” that’s been done before (eg. a suburban neighbourhood), it’s important to emphasize the unique features of the level and centre the story out of them. The rings of houses in this level is what stands out for me. It’s an uncanny layout. But if this is a defining feature of the level, what if the concept went even further? Emphasize the infinite nature of the rings. What if, as rings get closer to the centre, everything starts to shrink, in order to fit? (Conversely, things in the outer rings get bigger?) Are you familiar with the mathematical concept of a limit? What if wanderers cannot reach the centre of the rings, but as they get closer and closer, they gradually lose more and more of themselves, as everything in the level gets compressed into a singularity? What if the MEG personnel exploring the level log their attempts to reach the centre, and reality breaking around them?

What if every ring is slightly different? Ex. Houses of a different colour, gravel yards instead of lawns, sidewalk chalk drawings, duplexes vs detached houses. Having each ring a bit different would prompt wanderers to keep jumping fences, to see if there’s a pattern or something useful or an exit- only to lead them deeper with false hope.

Another way to work with it would be to have some kind of anomaly/horror/revelation at the centre of the rings. Though this feels more predictable.

- What if wandering the rings, going in circles, has an effect on wanderers’ minds? What if they start to feel hopeless, and eventually just trudge the neighbourhood round and round, hoping an exit will appear, until the Wretched Cycle takes them?

- What if Under the Car and Sanctuary weren’t the only pocket dimensions here? What if other things could suck you into a void space, like the gas tanks? Garbage bins? Kiddie pools? Mailboxes? (What else is in a suburb?)

- *“If you manage to move the 10-tonne vending machine, there is a possible exit to The Frontrooms.”*

I would get rid of this. The whole premise of the Backrooms is being trapped, so as soon as there’s an exit, the story’s over and the stakes are dead (unless it’s part of the Frontrooms canon). This could be a possible exit to another level, though. It could be changed to something like this: “There appears to be the edge of a doorway visible behind the vending machine. If you are able to move it, there may be another exit beyond. (Unconfirmed.)” Or, maybe the MEG guy who reaches the vending machine could just pitifully ask the machine if the exit to the Frontrooms is behind it.

- *“I can't let you out of the backrooms though. After all, there is no exit.”*

Likewise, I’d get rid of this line. Things are more mysterious and scary when there is no definitive answer. Part of the stakes of the Backrooms story is the question of whether an exit even exists. Answering this question also kills a major part of the story.

- I get the impression that fence-hopping is an unstable activity. Especially since the rings aren’t quite connected. What if it’s also possible to exit this level by hopping a fence? Every once and a while, someone hops a fence and lands in a different level?

- *“If anyone else comes here, stay in the houses. It's safer, believe me.”* Except for the houses that open directly to the Hive. I would have the guy mention this.

- Question to you- have you ever watched "The Good Place"? If you haven't, there's an episode about people who

[+ Spoiler](javascript:;)

[- Hide](javascript:;)

are trapped in a place where they can have absolutely anything they want, at a mere ask. And they are *miserable*.

  
I would highly recommend watching this episode, for some philosophical inspiration about what living in the Sancturary vending machine would be like. It might start off amazing for people, and end up horrible, as they get bored but are too addicted to safety to leave. It would be fantastic to work a story about this into the logs, or have the vending machine comment on what happens to wanderers who chose to stay there.

\* I like the sun swallowers, but they feel a bit random, unrelated to the rest of the level. What if the entity was something like a nothing-figure pushing a nothing-lawnmower that trims things out of existence? Again, this is just an idea. The Backrooms are an infinite place with infinite potential, so there’s definitely allowed to be a degree of randomness. And randomness can make a place more frightening. YMMV for how much the contents of a level should reflect a cohesive “theme”, vs being completely unrelated and incomprehensible.

Regardless, I think the Sun Swallowers need more development, including how they’re talked about on the page. They kind of come in like a random element, halfway through- whereas I feel like an entity this dangerous and incomprehensible would normally get its own section, discussion, and dedicated logs documenting it. The page would also explicitly warn readers that a strange, poorly understood entity exists there, and that the MEG understand very little about it.

\* *“If you open the gas tank and manage to crawl inside, you will end up in what seems to be The Void, however, it is theorised that there is a gas pool at the bottom of this area of The Void as the car must run on something… right?”*  
Couple of points about this:

Anytime someone mentions the Void, I like to double-check: is this supposed to be FANDOM Void, Wikidot Void, or the Blue Channel? The FANDOM and Wiki Void are very different. Our equivalent of FANDOM’s Void- the emptiness surrounding the Backrooms- is the Blue Channel. Is that where people are supposed to end up? Or are they going to Wiki Void- the purgatorial, broken existence screw-up?

If the void has a gas pool, what if this is just a pocket void that exists inside the level? It doesn’t have to be one of the places above.

Also, I would work out how exactly wanderers get inside the gas tank. In the Exits section it mentions cutting the tank open, but even so I don’t think the gas tank of a car is big enough to crawl inside. I’m having trouble finding specs on how wide the average tank is, but looking at pictures of them, they’d probably have to have the entire bottom cut out for a person to get their shoulders through. To make this more believable, what if opening a gas tank, or making a hole in one, causes the void beyond to suck a person through, like zero gravity?

- I would say that there is only one *known* instance of the vending machine. It’s an infinite level, so no one can definitively say if there’s only one of something

- For the Sanctuary sub-level, I would include a part in the logs about the MEG explorer running into the group of three that lives there. How else would the MEG know about them?

- For the entities, I don’t think Facelings spawn in the Hive, so I wonder if it makes sense for them to be there. The Hive page explicitly mentions Hounds, Crawlers, and Death Moths. I imagine other entities could spawn in the Hive as well, but given the theory that Facelings are the result of the Backrooms trying to replicate humans, I would think they have a different source.

I would put this question to #backrooms and see what other people think

- *“This level consists of a black floor and ceiling that stretch for (theoretically) infinity, ending in white walls that go upward for infinity.”*

This seems to be a contradiction (unless I’m not understanding right). If the walls went upwards for infinity, it wouldn’t be possible to see a ceiling. I like that looking up into infinity makes people go insane, but the contradiction at the heart of this needs to be fixed. What if the space extends infinitely side to side (like the narrow space under a vehicle), and the ceiling is a weird, infinite recursion that looks like the underside of a car? Staring into the infinite car parts folding in on themselves makes people go insane?

- Because there are two different MEG operatives recording the logs, I would include their names, a long with their ID numbers. It will make it clearer, at first glance, that logs are coming from different people

- The sun swallowers should be listed along with the other entities in the level, rather than appearing in ‘Exits’ for the first time. (It can be separate from those that emerge from the Hive)

- What if the vending machine was related/connected to the vending machines in the Snackrooms somehow? Or in other levels? (There seem to be a few with vending machines that never run out.) This could be an opportunity to address a little hole in the lore- why do machines like this appear throughout the Backrooms? (And does this sentient vending machine have anything it wants to say about it?)

—-

Alright, thanks for sticking with me! I picked a lot of things apart, but don't be discouraged. Mostly I feel like the story is rushing through itself, a little bit. Slow down, take the time to describe things. Describe more about how the rings and the sun swallowers work together. Tell us more about the protagonist, what happened to his friend, and why he's come to hate the MEG. Work out the details- enjoy creating the nuances the of level. This place is very weird (that's a compliment!), and I want to see just how weird, upsetting, and disorienting it can get.
