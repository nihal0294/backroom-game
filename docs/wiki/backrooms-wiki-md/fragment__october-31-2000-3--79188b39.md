# Halloween...

# Halloween…

"We're home!"

**"Okay, champ, go out to the garage and start setting up the spare tablecloth for pumpkin carving."**

**"And make sure not to grab the wrong bucket of tools. It should be at eye level for you."**

**"Don't use them until Mom or I are there with you—we don't want another last-second doctor trip!"**

After I had managed to get myself out of the perplexing corn maze, I started to make my way back to my house, the rusty wheels of the barrow squeaking in protest of the newly added weight of the giant fruit. The sun began to touch the horizon, and the sky transitioned from warm reds and oranges to the darker, cooler tones of blue and purple. It was about that time of day, when all the lights that had decorated the outside of every house sprang to life, where the inflatables were reanimated with air and light, and all the magic of the holiday came back for one night.

I had just managed to get back home when the purple and orange lights flickered to life at my house. Dragging the wagon back to the garage, forcing it open to make room for the late pumpkin carving to begin.

Rummaging through the indoor car storage, I managed to dig up an old tablecloth and an old carving kit. After setting everything up, I started scooping out the gourd and its guts, some of it sticking to my fingers, arms, and clothes. Once it was all done, I needed to think of what I wanted to sculpt on the canvas. Maybe I could do an intricate design like my mom always does? Or maybe I can do a basic face as my dad does? Yeah, that sounds more plausible and doable. Once I drew out a toothy, smiling face on the outside with a marker, I got to work.

*"Eww! Love! Stop flicking the guts at me, it's gross!"*

*"Plus, we need to save the seeds. Dad wants to try to grow some pumpkins for next year."*

"But won't they be small like the last time dad tried that?"

**"Hey, it was a fine try! This time they will be bigger! Better! Stronger!"**

"And not plop on the floor like your lasagna?"

**"HEY!"**

Even as I finished the pumpkin, something still felt off. While everything was nice and fun before now, except for the haunted house, it all felt kind of empty. Like something was missing.

Where were my parents? Where was everyone I used to know? The farmer wasn't anywhere near the farm, and no one was outside either. Even with their porch and indoor lights on, they were just never there.

Let's not think about that now… maybe my parents were out doing their own Halloween stuff this year?

Yeah! I know Mom always liked to go to a Halloween party with her old friends, and Dad just liked to watch the game at the neighbor's house when I got much older. Maybe that's where they went. Yeah, let's go with that.

"You did amazing, sweetie! I love how your pumpkin has such a creepy grin!"

"Thanks, Mom! But I wish I could have done something like yours. You made a kitty!"

"Well, Mom did go to art school for quite some time. And she learned from the best on how to use the tools, me!"

"Uh-huh, and what about your pumpkin, Davinci? How did it turn out?"

"U-Uh, well, no artist shares their secrets to the worl- HEY, CHAMP NO-"

"Oh! It looks like mine, Mom!"

"Aww, how cute! Like father, like child!"

"Y-Yes! That was intentional! Taking inspiration from yet another great artist in the family!"

"Heh, you sure have love bug, you sure have!"

  
After getting the jack-o-lantern ready, I put one of those fake electrical candles inside to light it up, then put it out on the front porch for any trick-or-treaters to see.

Oh! Speaking of which, I need to go get my costume now! It's almost time to go!

However, I needed to choose what I wanted to dress up as first. I remember I used to love dressing up as video game characters, but sometimes I just dressed as a character from a TV show or movie. I also have always been interested in astronauts; the unknown is both scary and, in a strange way, comforting. These thoughts filled my head as I made my way to my bedroom, absent-mindedly opening my closet door, only to bebroken out of my internal dissociation as the plastic helmet of my old astronaut costume fell on my head. Ow! Well, that's enough for now. Time to put on this cheap, mass-produced suit and plastic helmet and get to candy collecting!

"Look, Mom! I'm a T. rex! Raaaaaa!"

"Fantastic, Honey! But didn't you say you wanted to be an astronaut like Dad was?"

"I did, but dad said yesterday he wanted to try and be a caveman instead, so I wanted to be a dinosaur to scare him!"

"Ooooh, that is wounderful Honey! When you go and get Dad, make sure you go up behind him and roar as loud as you can, that will surely show him who's the big dino around here!"

"Will do mom!"

  
After struggling with my old rubber boots, I stumbled my way half blind into the storage closet, fumbling around to see if I could find the old pumpkin pails that I always brought with me on Halloween. It always seemed so bottomless whenever I took it with me as a kid, never filling up to the top as I stopped at every house around the block. Such a small basket could hold a biblical flood's worth of candy at the end of the night.

Once everything was in order, I was about to leap out of the front door and begin my journey, until I remembered that no one was home tonight, so no candy would be out for the other kids. I didn't want any of the other trick-or-treaters to be empty-handed at my house! Luckily, my parents had a bowl already made for such kids. Quickly grabbing a piece of paper from the study and a marker, I wrote a letter that said “Please only take one!’ and left it behind the bowl on top of the side chair on the front porch.

Now I am ready!

The Neighborhood was alight with Halloween lights, kids running house to house, getting their own candy and sweets from neighbors and others alike. Every house I went to, I knocked on their door, and they were always so kind, complimenting my poorly made costume, giving me lots of candy, and just being the sweetest people. Some of them even dressed up as well! I remember one house that was themed after a graveyard, dressed as zombies and skeletons, even acting like them as well, making zombie noises and everything, very fun! It was like I was a kid again, having fun and running about, carefree and enjoying the night. That magic was back once more.

"Mom! Dad! Let's go to the big houses! Tommy at school said that their parents will be giving out really big-sized candies!"

*"Oh, sure thing, hun! Let's just get a few houses near home first."*

**"I did also hear that Tommy's parents were also giving out treats to the adults as well, would love some!"**

'Oh! Can I have some?"

**"Oh, not until you're a lot older, champ… Sorry."**

"Aww.."

**"But I'll see if I can get you a second bar of chocolate for you."**

"Yay!"

But I knew if I wanted the *good* stuff, I had to go to the rich people part of the neighborhood, the gated community that I had passed by earlier. Thankfully, the gates to that community were open for the night, so I didn't have to climb over the pointy metal fence. I never really understood just how big these houses were or how spaced out they were from each other. But all that was worth the sharing-size chocolate bars, family-size candy bags, the real good stuff!

[![RichNBH_edit](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--resized-images/fragment:october-31-2000-3/RichNBH_edit/medium.jpg)](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--files/fragment:october-31-2000-3/RichNBH_edit)

Once the crowds of kids started to die down, when there were only a few stragglers left, and the front porch lights of the neighborhood houses started to turn off, that's when I decided I had finally gotten my fill of the candy gathering. Making my way back home, I realized how peaceful it all was when everything was quiet and dark. When no one was out, no voices, just the sound of the crickets, wind, and nothingness. Making me realize just how exhausted I became after all that walking and exploring, not to mention the weight of all the candy I had gathered in the pale.

Approaching my house’s front door, I left the now-empty bowl of candy outside, deciding to deal with it in the morning. Opening the door to the inside of my house, taking off my helmet, and abandoning it in the front room as I closed the door. All I cared about now was sitting in the living room, popping in one of the many Halloween movies we have, and eating my hoard of candy until I was sleepy on the sofa.

Placing the heavy goodie back on the coffee table, I bent down to the cupboard below the living room TV, which was filled to the brim with movies of all sorts, from old VHS Horror classics to new DVD releases. Sorting through all of them, I finally found one I wanted, The Thing, a long-time favorite movie of mine. Fishing out the old VHS from the stacks of other movies, I gently pulled it from its packaging, put it into the VCR, and pressed play so the movie would finally play.

Dragging my exhausted body back to the couch, I pulled the pale back to me as I plopped down on the cloth cushions, carelessly opening a chocolate bar to shove in my mouth. I didn't fully register the cocoa's sweetness; I just chewed, swallowed, and went for another. All while the movie played on, not even processing what the moving pictures in front of my face were, even as the scenes started to get graphic and gory.

All I knew was that I had finally felt at peace, I had finally felt as if I was back home, even without everyone I knew being here. My house was the same, the neighborhood the same, the orchard, the old house, even the vague remembrance of my neighbors being the same as I last remembered them.

Even the old wistful longing that I so wanted after all these years was back, even for a second, even without the clear memory of what it was like.

It all felt the same.

It all looked the same.

And that's all I wanted.

Even as I drifted back to sleep, the colors of the outside world faded with my own consciousness. Part of me knew I would not be back here for a VERY long time.

*"Did you have fun tonight, hun?"*

"Yeah! So much fun! I really liked it when we got all that candy! When Uncle Bob came by with all that soda! And when Macy came by and showed me her monster card collection! And when we got to jump on the tampoline in her backyard! It was so fun!"

"I wish this could last all year."

*"We wish so too, hun. But all things must move on. Time must move on so we can have more fun tomorrow."*

*"You will always have the memories of today with you. Even if all the candy is gone, if all the lights are turned off. You still remember that feeling. The warm and cool feeling of light and hope."*

*"Keep it with you, my love. You will always be home with those memories."*

"I love you, Mom."

*"I love you too, baby. Now, please get some sleep."*

*"Tomorrow will be another day."*

My vision came back to me.  
Hazey, foggy, and heavy.  
Just like the concrete ground beneath me.  
I slowly pushed myself back up.  
It was the same place I was before Halloween came around.  
The same cold grey prison I was in before.

I should feel hollow.  
I should feel a pit in my stomach.  
Being back here after I was in what was heaven.  
for the first time in forever.  
Thrown back into the nothing oblivion of halls, walls, and corridors.

And yet.  
I don't feel that harrowing pain.  
Only the warmth of my memories.  
The joy of what I had experienced.  
The desire to keep going.  
To see another Halloween.

I look back to where the door once was.  
Only being met by the same grey slab of stone.  
Like it was never there to begin with.  
For a moment  
I was scared.  
The pit is slowly forming.  
What if all that I had experienced was a lie?  
What if all of that was just a mirage?  
What if what I saw was my final moments?

However.  
The pit quickly disappeared once I glanced down at my old bucket.  
It was still filled with the same survival tools.  
But now it was filled with just as much candy.  
All the candy that I had gotten from trick-or-treating was there.  
Even the oversized canybars.  
Dumping everything out onto the cold ground.  
I could only see gold.  
In the form of sweet, delicious candy.

This was all real.  
What I had seen was indeed the truth.  
My memories had materialized into a moment of clarity.  
Out of oblivion had come peace.  
Had come my home.  
Even for a moment.  
It was all very real.

And all I could do now.

Was wish for this to happen again next year.
