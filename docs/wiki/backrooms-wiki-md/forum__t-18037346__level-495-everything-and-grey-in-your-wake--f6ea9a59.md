Reading, Voting, and Commenting on Every Rewritecon Entry #2

02 Jul 2026 16:10

The second article in my epic quest brings me here: to another entry in the "9 circles of Hell" series, we might say.

The parallel's to Dante's Limbo are obvious. Besides the strange mixing of classical and Christian afterlife ideas (which Dante did quite frequently), the place across the river might be thought of as Limbo itself: not really good (in this case it's really more of an unknown than middling), but just… better than the alternatives. You can probably dig a lot of symbolism out of that.

It's emotional, it's atmospheric, and whilst I can't really comment on the nonsense poetry at the beginning (due to how anything written in that style is inherently "vibes"-based), I do like the idea of the Backrooms having its very own Jabberwocky, so to speak.

**Easy +1**. Very good work here.

+1 very sad

02 Jul 2026 16:17

I think the only negative note I have is that my brain hurt trying to read the prologue, but like that's intentional so hey. It's about the Ferryman himself, best I can tell, so it'd make sense that what's written about him is half understandable, half not. I appreciate that. Also the dialogue was a little hard to tell what's coming from who, but like eh nitpick.

The actual story, too, is quite sad. I don't think we're meant to know entirely what's going on here. There's a level of "how much is this just in the woman's head", and I like that. I wasn't sure if she was also dead, if this is an afterlife in itself where you need a coin to reach an even better one, and the woman has given her dead child away to reach this better one, knowing she will never get to go to it but her child will. I know greek or whatever mythology had something like that. You pay for a better afterlife. That one coin is the only way to the good afterlife, lest you have to walk back down the bank towards… blackness. Perhaps being poor means you get no afterlife?

It's really fascinating, and I think deserves deconstruction on what it's doing. I also really enjoyed it.
