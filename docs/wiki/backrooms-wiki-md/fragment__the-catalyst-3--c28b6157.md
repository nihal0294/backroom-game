# The Complexities of Retribution

# **Chapter 3**

### The Complexities of Retribution

  
  
  

The day had not gone particularly interestingly for Corporal Philip Goode. He was at a rank too high for training, but too low for any kind of interesting assignments, and so he had drawn the short straw to guard the servers. Here, wrapped in the layers of barbed-wire walls and guard patrols of [Thebes](level-11.md), it was safe enough to be boring. All he did was sit here, and watch the command lines as they whirred past on the monitors. There wasn't really much reason to guard this place anyway, it wasn't like there weren't locks and bars, and men with swords to stop an attacker from reaching this point.  
  
But that day, someone slipped between the locks of steel, evaded the guards of strength, and climbed the walls of spikes.  
  
The corporal was just wondering whether he could learn to sleep standing up at his post in the middle of the room of lights, when the mystery shade made itself known. A blue arm wrapped around his mouth, and the other round his arms, with surprising strength for what felt like a pool noodle.  
  
'Terribly sorry for the interruption, young man,' beamed a voice in his head, 'Keycard and passcode, if you wouldn't mind.'  
  
It was an uncharacteristically polite request from someone who was technically an enemy. Philip vaguely nodded his restrained head towards a sheaf of papers on the only table in the big room. He was marched over to it, and the unknown assailant seemed to study the documents from a distance, not releasing their grip.  
  
'You literally write down your passwords and leave them next to the server‽' the unknown asked rhetorically, in a tone that suggested that leaving passwords out was a travesty most foul, '*And* you leave the keycard right there‽ No wonder you get hacked all the time.'  
  
The corporal tried to struggle, taking advantage of the sudden lapse in attention, but was quickly squeezed tighter.  
  
'I wouldn't,' warned the assailant, 'I think I ought to dispose of you now. Don't worry, you'll be just fine. *Afterwards*.'  
  
The corporal was suddenly slammed into the floor with the same strength that held him. The movement was controlled such that it would only knock him unconscious, and not cause more damage than a little concussion.  
  
Later, when the full outage of all U.E.C. online systems was traced back to the locked server room, the fully-conscious guard would give this version of events as to why all the data cards were magnet-wiped. The most unfortunate thing was, that they had next to no idea who it was that had made their way in and out without being seen, in broad daylight.

---

Reggie slid through a mucky lake in [Level 466](level-466.md) near-silently, with only the merest sucking sound in the dusk marking their presence.  
He had gotten good at moving silently in the last few weeks — it was a necessary skill in this kind of career.  
  
Their stylish mask was the only part of their body that was visible — it had been somewhat adjusted, now bearing a red splash on the bottom left corner of the wooden circle, perfectly mimicking the horrific burn underneath. Reggie's face scar was also depicted on the blue surface, as a lurid red line going from cheek to forehead, not that such areas were topologically visible.  
  
They slowly stalked out of the water onto the beach of Lantern Town.  
  
He wasn't noticed, the [H.D.F](u-e-c-team-homelanders.md) were short staffed at present, and so surveillance of the lake had been dropped until they were assigned more guards.  
  
Reggie skulked along the sand until they reached a worn down, but well built shack, marked "Storage."  
The padlock wasn't hard to open, only requiring one sharp tap with a claw hammer to split the cheap monkey metal apart.  
  
'Security is their highest priority indeed,' thought Reggie, and laughed soundlessly under the mask.  
  
Inside were racks of tinned food, all yellow and black off-brands; bottles of [Almond Water](object-1.md), mostly in grey steel bottles; even small amounts of [Lucky O' Milk](object-28.md) amongst them; but Reggie ignored the food supplies, his object of interest lay elsewhere.  
  
At a far corner of the room, there were a few crates that proclaimed themselves to be agricultural implements, but Reggie knew this not to be the case. The foam-inlaid boxes contained modified [Pixie Guns](object-71.md). Terrible things, they could kill dozens before needing recharged, and the modifications made them deadly only to those they were aimed at.  
  
'Time to destroy these weapons of mass destruction,' thought Reggie, then set to work.

---

More water, and more darkness.  
  
The flickering shadows of [Level 502](level-502.md) got darker as Reggie slid through them. Another storage room loomed ahead of him, characterized by a segment of data centre blocked off by planks. Another lock presented itself, but he ignored it, pressing the hole on one side of their head against the thin door. Someone inside was trying hard to make no noise.  
  
When Reggie kicked the door it did not come off its hinges. Most doors, even quite secure ones, were not any stronger than it's accessories, so it's locks and hinges.  
Most doors would run out of atoms before the average hinge cracked, and so when the full force of vengeance hit the lock, it smashed out of the door along with the section of leaky plywood it had been attached to.  
The rest of the door began to fracture, and a few seconds later fell into the ruined frame.  
  
Reggie stepped over the pile of tinder and faced the guard beyond, who was currently keeling over from the twisted lock hitting him in the groin.  
All it took to take him down was a simple engagement maneuver of going in close and striking the face, after removing the helmet of course.  
After the guard was verifiably recumbent, and Reggie had checked his pulse, he moved to the locked crate in the centre of the room.  
  
'I thought you would come,' said a voice behind them.  
  
Reggie turned to the armoured figure that had just come through the shattered door. They were some dim captain of the U.E.C, unidentifiable except for their rank. They were holding a modern pistol, probably a rather expensive one given the economy of the backrooms.  
Several similarly clad soldiers filed in, all bearing machetes and expressions of contempt.  
  
'Good morning Captain,' said Reggie cheerily, 'What business do you have here this fine day?'  
  
'Stopping you, whoever you are,' the captain fumbled their fluid speech at this point, 'actually, um, who are you?'  
  
'I am a reflection of your mistakes,' said Reggie, 'a twisted retributioner, haunted by your depravity.'  
  
'I need a name.'  
  
'You may know me as *The Catalyst*,' replied Reggie, 'The Cerulean Catalyst of Regret.'  
  
'And what do you have against us?' asked the obviously oblivious Captain.  
  
Reggie carefully removed his mask of blue, exposing the full extent of their scars.  
  
'I'm sure you will work it out eventually. For now, I must go.'  
  
Reggie surreptitiously slid a blue hand into a pocket, looking for the tiny [berries](object-74.md) they knew would be there.  
  
'Don't move,' said the captain, tracking Reggie's slow movement with her pistol.  
  
'I don't think this counts as moving,' said Reggie politely as they vanished.
