Novote (but overall I still liked it)

16 Mar 2025 12:46

Similar to Monster's entry, the actual writing style is excellent. It's catchy and engaging, and it does grasp my attention.

In addition, most of the parts are a wonder of efficient writing, where each sentence serves a purpose and conveys the worldbuilding efficiently, again, in an interesting manner.

> Of all the Monarchs, Rashad is probably the hardest to communicate with. How do you talk with a being of living fire? Regardless, he is friendly to anyone who wants to contribute to the library.
>
> It's… amazing how he does it. He calls up stone from deep within the level and shears it into thin tablets. Then with a fiery finger, he carves every word you say into the stone. He links everything together with metal, despite the pain it causes him, and sets it into its place within the grand library. Despite being made of stone, the tablets are as light as any book.
>
> Of course, not everything there is true, and not everything there was told by humans. Be careful what you read. Some knowledge is not meant for us. And whatever you do, don't destroy any book. Angering Rashad is like angering a wildfire, and he will burn you alive and bind your soul to your burning ashes.

Let's take this part as an example. In the first sentence alone, we know what he looks like and what he does, given in a way that feels natural thanks to the testimony. It doesn't feel like you give this information to the reader, but rather it flows naturally with the medium and the context it presents.

The testimony continues by presenting how he keeps the information, again, with the emphasis on the wonder of the character allowing for the information to feel fluid, efficient and interesting. It even answers some more specific questions by explaining that, because stories are told by humans, with their own perspective and access to information, not everything can be considered as true. It also shows (even tho we expect it) that Rashad holds the knowledge dear, and that, like all characters in the Grove, they are prone to anger if you disrespect them. It's a relative paradise, and it resonates with the ending that explains how humans could very well ruin it.

Hell, the whole autumn part is GREAT, because it's a season where we harvest our resources and keep them in handy for harsh times to come. Rashad and the whole area incarnates this feeling, and even shows in their character design its warmth, but also his temper. Without actually telling it, we can see how fitting their activities are for this part of the Grove. That's good! That's efficient!

However, some other parts cruelly lack in this natural flow of information, and instead of making me understand the details, confuse me by their lack of explanation. Every sentence serves a purpose, but some of these sentences are lacking.

> I cannot guarantee there will be no stray Hounds sniffing about or other, more dangerous creatures.

﻿  
With this example, I'll essentially say the same thing as I did for Monster's entry, mostly because the two are related. This bit suffers the issue of hinting at a lot, yet delivering nothing. It mentions creatures (multiple it seems) that are so ancient, so powerful and primal that even the Primordials fear them. They've been around forever, and in the aforementionned tale, we have one that showcases incredible power that is said to have been a calamity to repel or live with.  
﻿  
Yet, for something that is implied to be so much, to have shaped the history of the Grove so much, I don't see anything about them. What are they? Why are they so dangerous? How did the Primorials adapt to them? The efficient worldbuilding of the Autumn section is absent here, as we lack the meaningful sentences to explain this information.  
﻿  
There have been other plot points like this that confused me, such as how human creation allows the Grove to mirror it (something that was mentioned in previous pages but not explained), how this shaped their links with humans and explains their respect towards the species. Same with how the hunt works, the reasons why they are so welcoming in their level yet attacking on sight everywhere else is important for the cohesion, yet wasn't explained.  
﻿  
So in conclusion, while the article gives a lot in an efficient way (and that shows how good you are at being an efficient storyteller), we still lack some of this information, which makes some parts of the worldbuilding feel paradoxical or clunky even tho it becomes much clearer after some discussion with you. Hence the novote. If some of these points were explained in the page like how the other (very great) worldbuilding bits were told within the context of the narrative, then it would absolutely be an upvote because again, the writing style and portrayal of the information is a marvel of efficiency and storytelling that I think many authors could inspire themselves from when it comes to give lore to readers.
