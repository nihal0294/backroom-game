-1, almost there but not quite

05 Jul 2024 20:29

Granted, that's your first page, and the concept is focused and the format interesting, but-

The characters just feels very artificial. kinda drives down the page. Like with the person who lost his friend looking quite devoid of sadness or grief. Message development also comes very artificially, with people giving testimonies as soon as they need some.

Having nothing beyond the apologies is also a blunder. The story is that the harmless yet dangerous phenomenon shouldn't get published. So having consequences to this would be a nice ending to the page. It stops abruptly and it's a shame. There are people trying to post, not succeeding, and talking about it. But that's it.

Like with Object 100, the page isn't as much about the page as it is about the story. However, this story is unfinished, and feels very bitter, as if there was a lack of consequence even tho it screams like there should be some, with the MEG refusing the article twice, or some characters being unsure about posting.

If the message is "don't stop using something because it has a 0.001% chance of killing you" (which is very valid when you know things like toxicology), it is unfinished. The M.E.G. fears consequences, and showing them would only be a logical ending to the page. Hell, you can even develop the story by having the people finding a way to keep it around, and have the M.E.G. realize they are wrong. Whatever the morale as long as the story is complete. I shouldn't just be people trying to post a random page and failing. Wha's the point of making an page that revolves around Phenomenon 23 when it is just an excuse to talk about M.E.G. database moderation? Developping the story would allow the narration to be more intertwined with the context, which will make both context and use worthwhile, instead of having the phenomenon in itself look like an excuse to have all the forum posts and M.E.G. moderation.

Yet, outside of this and the writing in itself that flows weirdly, the format is good, super cool CSS, and the story feels engaging nonetheless. It's just cut short too soon.
