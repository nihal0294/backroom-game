Plot’s great, I’ll give it that. The presence of a possibly world-ending entity in the Backrooms that turns out to just be a false flag operation by the federal government to destroy the Backrooms? Cool, sign me up.

The subtle details are mixed in well — the DARPA theming, the mentioning of US code law, and the exaggerated claims that clue you into the fact that this entity doesn't exist. I like all that. Gives the page a bit of charm, so to speak.

I think the pacing is off, though. Nothing is given time to breathe. The broadcasts and reports feel one-dimensional, existing solely to further the plot. In this case, the subtlety is gone — each report gives you exactly what the title says it will give you and nothing more. The world-ending threat is gone within the span of a minute or two after you introduce it — there is no sense of urgency or tension besides what the M.E.G. says.

Progression of the page:  
1. We found a concerning entity.  
2. We need to take action against it.  
3. [Opposing opinion]  
4. [Silencing said opinion]  
5. [Failed attempt to spread opposing opinion]  
6. Climax  
7. Plot twist  
8. Resolution

It feels kind of formulaic? The progression from 1 to 6 is insanely quick, and 7 is introduced right after the climax. 8 just becomes a lore dump, then. There are definitely interesting ideas here, but they don’t seem to be the best executed.

Thanks for replying.

I think you're right, but this was intentional. While it may not be for the best from a storytelling perspective, this is a database of only the most relevant and important communications related to the event stored on the DARPA database and wouldn't include things that might space out the progression of the story as well. I also intended it to feel as if things \*were\* happening too fast: if you look at the timeline, the actual events of the story take place over the course of a single month, with some secret shadow background happening a little before. This is an incredibly fast progression from "all is good" to "collapse of the entire backrooms". The narrative is supposed to imply that the sensationalism of propaganda pushed the situation to happen with a speed that is frightening to the normal citizens of the backrooms, and the way I chose to portray it in the work was with condensed passages that gave exactly the info they needed and nothing else, while cutting out large time gaps to make the reader feel powerless, unable to get the full story until the very end. 7 is intended to build intrigue until the reveal in 8, which is both the apotheosis of the work and the falling action.

You are right, it is formulaic. However, most stories follow a very similar structure, so I don't feel that bad about it. I understand the meaning of what you're trying to tell me and you have good criticism, but I don't think it would help my work to act on it.
