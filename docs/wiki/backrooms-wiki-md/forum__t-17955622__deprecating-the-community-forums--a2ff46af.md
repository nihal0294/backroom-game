Deprecating the Community Forums

25 May 2026 17:41

Hello everyone, we would like to announce with a heavy heart that we are **considering** deprecating the community forums on **Wikidot**.

What does this mean? Well currently, absolutely nothing. Staff are still discussing and voting on what to do (if anything), however a majority of staff have agreed it is in our best interest to depreciate use of the forums. We also agreed on involving the community in this discussion due to the nature of this issue.

Authors and Ex-Staff are free to read our thread in the staff server [here](https://discord.com/channels/1183804982022127636/1508450022730240040/1508450083836792842), but for those of you who don’t meet the requirements to join the majority opinion was:

- A Discord account is a hard requirement to join the wiki  
- The forums are seldom used  
- New authors unaware of this fact go to the crit forums only to get no responses  
- They make the community look inactive and a waste of time to join

Additionally the dissenting opinion was that:

- They do not take up staff resources to maintain  
- They allow legacy members who joined without Discord to still participate in the community  
- They allow people to participate who do not actively use Discord due to country bans or other issues with the platform itself

Staff have not come to a final consensus yet, nor do we have a planned sunset date. If you wish to participate in the community discussion please do so in this thread for forum users, or [here](https://discord.com/channels/695664941247627274/1508523386870169610/1508523386870169610http://) for Discord users

We would also like to say, that deprecating the community forums **doesn't include article comments nor Oversight**. Those forums will remain supported, with no plans on deprecating use any time soon.

Additionally we have a quick 5 minute survey about the forums we would like you to fill out. It is optional but will help us in making our final decision. You can find it here:  
[Click Here](https://forms.gle/3ZmZp7Eg13DKK2wL9)

Thank you for your time, if you have any questions, please feel free to make a ticket or message a staff member directly. Thank you.

Re: Deprecating the Community Forums

25 May 2026 19:44

I personally hate the shift toward Discord servers sequestering away information in a way that locks it out of web searchability (like how to fix xyz issue, opinions on abc product, etc) to the point that somehow Reddit is now the most reliable place to find real human people giving actual advice and opinions. Also I'm old and just like forums, but anyway.

While the main argument for archiving most of the non-page forums is that having a Discord account is a requirement for site membership (for verification purposes), there are also people who joined before this policy started. These people have no obligation to use the Discord server and some may have no access to it. As long as these users exist, we shouldn't be removing large chunks of the forums.

Another thing is that I think the forums are generally much better for some of the communication that's done in the Discord server. For example:

- Early rounds of crit are easier done through the site forums because Wikidot is more stable with posting large chunks of text. It's also much easier to read chunks of text (Discord can fling you around for no reason when scrolling through a long message), portions of text can be copied (Discord only lets you copy entire messages on mobile), and early crit is naturally a slower conversation based on the sheer amount of fixing that happens. Later on, it might make sense for a critic and writer to move to Discord DMs to polish a page, but at the start I think forum use is better for crit.
- Community discussions (like this one!) are better conducted in slower environments like a forum. Too many discussions in the community feedback Discord channel have been derailed into unrelated topics, distracted by users asking to be brought up to speed because the backlog is too long to reasonably catch up on, or devolved into outright fights. Forcing people to collect their thoughts and post a polished conclusion on their stance leads to much more productive discussions. It also cuts down on the amount and overall disruptiveness of trolling comments.

That being said, some discussions are best done in chatroom settings. To cover that portion, I think setting up an IRC-Discord [Matrix-Discord conversion bot](https://matrix.org/ecosystem/bridges/discord/) can help bridge the gap so site-only users can still use the Discord server's channels where an equivalent is unused or doesn't exist in the site forums.

My proposal for a revamped site community forum setup:  
**ANNOUNCEMENTS**  
-> Staff Announcements  
-> Contact Staff  
-> Authors' Announcements (low activity, but kept due to being a utility thread)  
-> Community Feedback (moving the current forum in Discord to site forums)  
->Community Projects  
**WRITING HELP**  
-> Draft Critique  
-> Research, Resources, and Questions (intended as a place to look for experts on topics, sensitivity writers, or fact-checking assistance)  
**GENERAL**  
-> General Discussion

Forum categories I propose removing could be covered via the above mentioned IRC Matrix-Discord bot, so that people can still get idea crit or participate in lore discussions.

Edit 2: Proposing we offer Matrix rather than IRC as an alternative to Discord

A few more things to consider

25 May 2026 23:18

If a suggested solution for a problem just boils down to "people should just do such-and-such", then that is not solution. People won't just do such-and-such — they have never done such-and-such, *especially* in this community, and they won't suddenly start now. If it is not staff-mandated, it simply won't happen, and however horrid being *forced* to do something is, sometimes it is the only viable option for actually doing things.

I thought it important to mention this before the main body of my post due to comments of similar nature being made in the equivalent commfeed post in the Discord server, and suggestions of that sort are simply of no help to anyone.

---

I had a brief conversation about this with just a few days ago, and while in the past I have been a major proponent of shifting over to the forums, that is not what I'm focused on here. The others above have also been more than sufficient enough in their argumentation in favour of the forums, so you can assume I would argue similar. I have to question, however, how viable a mixed approach would be, especially taking into account the upcoming release of the Backrooms movie and how that will be affecting the overall numbers, whether that be of drafts/pages or of people.

Our newer members weren't there to experience it, but during the original "Kanewave", both the staff team and the GLMP were completely and utterly overwhelmed by the sheer influx of people coming in. It was simply impossible to keep up in any sense of the phrase. Now, I cannot speak on the exact scale of what's coming (perhaps I am wrong, and this post is entirely unfounded), but seeing the projections, I can only really summarise it as "a fuck ton". Not to imply the conditions by which we function under at the moment are the same as they were in ye olden days of 2022.

The question then naturally arises: when in our current "lull", with a very rudimentary (and admittedly, unoptimised system) "mixed" system, where both paths are available, the forums go and *historically* have gone fully ignored by both staff and the GLMP, not to mention general users. What can be expected to happen, then, when there will be even more people to consider and account for? When attention's already split and failing to manage, I can only imagine it will be nothing that can even remotely be described as "good."

Personally, like I've already stated, I'm in complete favour of making the forums the primary method of site-participation — 's suggestions are more than sufficient, for a start at the very least — but I think it's even more crucial to just choose one and make it the staff-mandated method of contributing to the site. People should be free to do whatever they choose to, of course (in particular, under a forum-only system, there should be no punishment for going to Discord DMs nonetheless), but when someone new comes to the site, there shouldn't be any confusion as to what approach will actually let them do what they want to do, whatever that may be!

There's certainly an order of priorities here, don't get me wrong, If anything, moving commfeed over to the forums, even if there wasn't an incoming flood of people, is an excellent idea in and of itself, and I am in complete support of it. I would say that the worries of such potential flood go beyond just a chaotic commfeed channel. Allow me to port over some (edited) excepts I wrote from that aforementioned conversation I had with a few days ago. You can give me your thoughts here, of course:

> It very much a "hindsight is 2020" type of situation in that you have Kanewave 1.0 to reference and build off of. You're guaranteed to be getting 3 things:
>
> - More unskilled writers
> - More unskilled critics
> - *Way* more "illiterate" readers
>
> All these in tandem lead to a chain reaction:
>
> 1. Newbies will come in with what most likely will be very flaccid drafts;
> 2. With way more critics, they'll have a comically easy time getting the minimum amount of critiques, and in the excitement of reaching the bar, they'll post their page in quite the sad state;
> 3. The higher amount of readers means there will be way more voters, well, voting, uncritically at that, thus even the worst of pages will have an easy time reaching the +10 minimum;
> 4. You now have (a varying amount depending on the scale) bad pages staying on the site in spite of pre-existing contributors' wishes.
>
> ---
>
> There are few points of difference with the original Kanewave, some in favour, some against:
>
> - Quarantine is no longer playing a role in the process, and as such, people have way less time to dedicate to project like this one. Ideally this entails calmer waters for all involved.
> - There now is an actually, at the very least, half-competent staff team lead by actually adults, themselves leading the actual project as a whole. Comparatively speaking, there current situation is *way* better than the past.
> - The movie turns the Backrooms from a collection of, admittedly, niche collection of *internet-based* horror pieces to an *IP*. Seeing the numerical estimates for this movie, even if the movie sucks complete and utter **ass**, you're most likely gonna be dealing with more people than the amount we had to deal with way back in 2022. In this, I hope I'm wrong.
> - The posting system in comparison, as merely a filtration system and nothing more, is a **million** times worse than the pure-greenlight system of back then. While it definitely was a pain to get a page on the site, there was a hard barrier there holding up the dam. Now? Free for all for everybody. That is not to say the current system should be changed, but it very much is a natural consequence of prioritising ideas of learning and growth for all.

I say all this, of course, but in practice, a mere move to the forums wouldn't actually solve anything — you obviously cannot just manifest people out of thin air to help out. I do, however, think a move to the forums as the primary method of contributing to the site would make it all *way* more manageable for all parties involved, or, at the very least, all parties involved in formal posting methods (the staff team and the GLMP).

General moderation, especially outside of commfeed, was nigh-impossible during Kanewave 1.0 — the amount of staff, which was *higher* than it currently is for a frame of reference, simply didn't suffice, to the point where any channel that was not one of the "main" channels for communication was basically neglected. 'Tis in the very nature of chatrooms, this, I'm sure you're aware.

A mixed approach then, to me, simply wouldn't be realistic under such similar conditions. That is not say it couldn't theoretically work — it is a sort of a Platonic Ideal in isolation —but I simply don't think it's viable as we currently are. Certainly a bit of a pessimistic outlook, but I think it's apt. If staff and the GLMP cannot handle the forums when there *isn't* any pressure on them, I sincerely doubt it would be any easier when there is.

Instinctually, one might default to just getting rid of the forums as a solution (and it would definitely be one less thing to think about), but that not only implies people, contributors that is, are already investing themselves in them to the point where they're genuinely taking up resources, but that the Discord server is not getting enough attention when it should be. In practice, that isn't what's happening, however. The place is fucking dead!

This is directed towards the staff team more than anything (which I'm hoping here, they're gonna view this post), but when knowing what is to come and the benefits that can be reaped by transitioning over to the forums (again, only in regards to ***contribution***, **not** communal matters), I simply cannot fully get behind the idea of leaving it all on a Discord server of all things. To be completely and utterly blunt, it mostly boils down to "we've grown used to it, and it's pretty comfortable", which is true! But is that really enough a good enough reason? If the goal of the site is to grow and showcase better and better writing over time, to me, moving seems like the obvious choice. The others have made some quite excellent suggestions as to how that could be reasonably achieved while also incorporating the Discord side of things into it. Take note!

---

All this said though, I mentioned in my original post, even if forums become the "main thing", people should be allowed to handle matters of critique and greenlights in private Discord DMs should they wish to. Even more importantly than that, I think making a choice between the two should happen sooner rather later. The point is that there is *lie* taking place here, a false image being given to all coming in, when there shouldn't be one. If Discord ends up being chosen, I welcome the decision.
