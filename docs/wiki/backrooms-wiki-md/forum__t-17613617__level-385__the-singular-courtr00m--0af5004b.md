Level 385: The Singular Courtr00m

10 Mar 2026 13:43

**Concept Title:** The Singular Courtroom.

**Sensitive Content:** Suicide

**Page Type:** Level

**Genre:** Horror, specifically Lovecraftian horror

**Central Narrative:** A level that is not from around here, told from the perspective of the only person who found and documented it.

**Brief Description:** Level 385 appears to be a vast expanse of smooth, dark, rock-like material, the surface waterlogged like a salt lake. The skies are a vibrant yellow, dotted with great black clouds. In the approximate "center" of the whole space stands a marble courthouse, the only building on this level. It has no windows, a single entrance in the form of a pair of wooden double doors, and doesn't show signs of wear. Rather, it is absolutely perfect, immaculate. The interior consists of a single courtroom, furnished similarly to those in the Frontrooms, save for the abundance of heavy black curtains that line the walls and carpet most of the floor.

The whole of Level 385 is empty. There are no signs of life, only the everlasting presence of the White Juror.

It isn't an entity in the common sense, more like a living thought.

Entrances to the level appear randomly across all levels, usually taking the place of existing exits temporarily. One can exit Level 385 by approaching the border of the space; they will then be taken to the level they were last in.

This story mostly revolves around the encounter between the Juror and the only Wanderer to enter this place, with the focus more on the Juror than the Wanderer. The Wanderer is just an example of the extent of the Juror's power. The first bit tells us how the Wanderer came to enter this place and encounter the Juror's presence.

The second part tells us that, even after leaving, he can't seem to shake the memory of this place, can't stop being fascinated by Level 385, but he tries to stop himself because he instinctively knows that going back is bad. In reality, the rational thoughts aren't instinct: he's unconsciously feeling the Juror's thoughts as they bleed in as its influence is. As the noose tightens, so do the thoughts, and the man kinda gets to a foggy conclusion as his "instincts" slowly become clearer, shaping into blurry thoughts. He realizes the Juror's trying to feast on his mind, his life force, but it can only do that if he's close enough. Unwilling to die to an eldritch being in this way, he commits suicide to "die honorably" instead of becoming feed. The Juror isn't revealed throughout the whole story, adding mystique.

The reason the level has a courtroom is the Juror's namesake. A juror is part of a jury, which is in turn part of a court trial, which happens in a courthouse. I want to describe the Juror as something that likes to judge humans as he watches them and "execute" those it deems guilty. It's its twisted view of justice, and he mostly does it because he's bored and hungry, toying with his food before he eats it.

Since it's fascinated with the justice system, it puts its own interpretation of a courthouse in its domain. In short, he's an eldritch god that likes to roleplay and eat his other roleplay partners: the humans unwillingly playing the role of the accused. The emptiness is intended to evoke a sense of dread and fear of the unknown, as well as amplifying the liminality and outlining the fact that this isn't an average level that is part of the Backrooms. It is an addition, a forced addition, created by this elder being.

It's also because this place is like the King in Yellow's Carcosa and Cthulhu's R'lyeh: the eldritch being's domain, as part of the Lovecraftian cliche.

As for why I described the courthouse as perfect, it can invoke a sense of the uncanny.

**Purpose:** I intend for this to evoke dread, curiosity, and uncanniness. It can also build into the Backrooms by imposing that there is something that is out of even this dimension, and it's here with us.

**Hook:** I think the idea of a constantly looming threat that has no face is enough to grab attention. That, and suspense. The suicide ending also makes a good Hamlet, though I will try to portray this as his only 'escape', that there is no other option.

**Additional Notes/Remarks:** Based on Robert Chambers' The King in Yellow and Lovecraft's works.
