First, let me start with the positives. I enjoyed how Kirai itself was never directly shown in the first offset. The reader, along with the main character, gets glimpses of it, and that creates a far more effective atmosphere than the pre-rewrite iteration where it just rambles to the reader about the Pantheon and MetaTM. Secondly, I like how Kirai mostly restricts itself to nonverbal communication within offset two. It makes it more far more challenging to guess its intentions, and I like how the eldritch creature demands an apology like a wet cat. I do think it makes sense for Overseer-A to be nonplussed about screwed-up situations, though I believe putting himself in the same room as an unpredictable entity goes a bit too far. This also has the double-edged sword effect of decreasing the reader's fear for Kirai, because well, if the Overseer could stroll right up to the entity and be just fine, then that kinda takes some of the tension out of the whole scene.

In summary, you've gotten really well at handling the rule that horror is often best when it's kept at a distance rather than shoved into the reader's face, and I commend that. Unfortunately, there were a few things, primarily in the zeroth offset, that bugged me.

[+ Read my glorious 1k word crit](javascript:;)

[- Hide crit](javascript:;)

The tone of the page is cosmic/body horror. A whole host[1](javascript:;) of elements on the main page compliment this, from the ever-present ticking of the timer to the frustrated flipping through columns of tabs to find what you're looking for. I usually dislike the use of tabs in articles for this very reason, but this page happens to use it to its advantage and deepen the sense of immersion, so hey? Regardless, I'll start listing the elements that I found to be incongruent with the article's tone, distracting me upon a first read:

**1. The Treatment of Suicide**

Self-explanatory here, but I felt like the depiction of suicidal impulses here was a bit shallow, and quickly forgotten by the plot. Especially in this case, the protagonist considers ending their own life to escape an incurable illness, a theme that likely rings resonant to many people in real life. I wish the decision was treated with more weight, as the researcher seems almost content to not have the chance to say their last words to their friends, a circumstance which I'm sure most people would respond to with a lot more distress.

Furthermore, our main character doesn't just briefly consider the (implied) protocol of self-termination; they almost go through with it. In the article, the switch in their demeanor is executed like this:

> Just as you begin to tilt the bottle upward, you feel yourself freeze. You refuse to go out like this. To die with the absolute minimum of dignity. To die without even having *tried* to fight for your life.
>
> *Doesn't the M.E.G. keep records for this sort of thing? We have our database, sure, but not everything is included in there. Surely I can find something of use, right?*

It seems like the attempt was brushed over rather quickly? I would've liked to see the protagonist display a bit more emotion at just having stared down death in the eye. Hands trembling, throat tight, etc. It doesn't have to be much nor drag down the plot, but just enough so that they feel more human and not purely a researcher that acts with complete rationality to prevent further casualties.

**2. The Description of Kirai**

Alright, so remember what I said about distance and fear of the unknown? The third tab, doubling as a proxy entity page, completely overloads the reader with information about Kirai. Not Abyssal Ooze, the dreaded substance that our main character can't bring themselves to prefer over death, just Kirai, who is a… guy with tentacles? The silhouette of the guy over the white backdrop forfeits the sense of mysteriousness allocated by placing it in a dark setting in offset one. It's like showing us a monster under broad daylight. Okay, disregarding the image for a moment, the clinical description itself brings up many details that I can't see being necessary in nor out of universe. The greatest offender of this phenomena was the snippet about the bangs, which thankfully no longer exist, but several similar sins remain. Why would the M.E.G. mention its height, or go to the lengths to estimate it, when they could just say it's around the height of a standard human (or its host, if it doesn't alter that portion of their anatomy)? Why would they mention details about its hair? Why its clothing, which suffers from the same irrelevance as its hair because it's not like Kirai wears anything strange or noteworthy. Most of all, this passage humanizes the entity, as something anthropomorphic, capable of style and perhaps capable of reason.

Please ditch the trench coat; I understand that's there for artists to muse-i-fy your dear entity, but it reeks of 'god oc' and harkens back to a bygone era of this website.

Okay, where were we? So, I personally dislike the aspect of it granting favors to wanderers. It doesn't connect to any other aspects of the entity, and it makes Kirai seem more amiable than I would've liked. I know that the M.E.G. claims that his deals demand a price, but since we don't really see the consequences of making a deal nor have details contributing to the horror, I don't think it fits in the article. Also, you mentioned that Kirai wanders. How does the environment react to its presence? Perhaps levels become quieter, like the departure of birds shortly before the arrival of an apex predator. Give us something to really sell that this thing is beyond human comprehension.

Lastly, if you feel the need to mention that the entity is well-spoken and charismatic, perhaps it would be of value mentioning that nothing it says is to be trusted and all efforts exerted by the entity towards socializing is in service of tricking wanderers into spreading its infection. I have no idea if this is the case in-universe, but I believe it's what the M.E.G. would write about a hostile organism.

**3. Nitpicks**

The protagonist under-reacts in a myriad of scenarios. I don't know about you, but an average person's first reaction to finding their fingers rotten and calcified would probably be to scream or at least try to wipe the ooze away on impulse (before remembering it's a bio-hazard). You could say that this protagonist in particular is much more professional and composed than most people, but this isn't established in their inner monologue, with the events of them forgetting to tie their shoelaces at the beginning suggesting the opposite.

I felt like the timer was too long for how brief offset zero was. For comparison, I finished the main page with nine minutes left on the clock. Puts a bit of a damper on the 'racing for your life thing'.

The use of my wikidot username in the second offset was more goofy than unsettling for me. Especially paired with the seriousness of the situation, 'we lost one of our team members, centurys-lute' just doesn't cut it.

Overall, I feel like this is really close to an upvote from me, and so much better in execution than its previous iteration.

**-1**
