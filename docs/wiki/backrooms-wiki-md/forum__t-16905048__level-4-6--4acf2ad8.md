Re: I have recently made office level with a twist too it

Hello! Saw that no once had replied to this yet, so I gave it a read.

Right off the bat, what I like most about this level concept is the glitched movement. There are other “data glitch” levels, but they're either made of immaterial information, or completely twisted beyond human comprehension. A place where physical movement has unintended effects strikes me as a distinct take on the concept- like a broken physics engine. I like that something as simple as trying to walk in a straight line in this sub-level could get someone horribly lost. Imagine being able to see the exit, standing right in front of it, but having no idea where or how to move in order to reach it. Nightmarish.

Having said that, this page needs a lot of work. There are a lot of SpaG errors and issues with tone and flow. The backstory of the page also feels incomplete. Because of this, I’m going to ignore the language issues and just do a concept crit. Once the story and idea are straightened out, the language can follow.

Backstory

- So, I think it needs to be clearer who’s perspective this page is from. Because of the M.E.G message near the end, I’m assuming this is an M.E.G database page about level 4.6. Including the introduction with Cooper/Steve’s conversation is fine. But, if this is ultimately an MEG page, then Cooper’s file needs to be acknowledged as a piece of 3rd party evidence that the M.E.G has collected. Ie. Something as simple as “The following document was recovered with the help of Cooper and Steve.”

- Also, there needs to be some more context about *why and how* the M.E.G is using Cooper’s file. What’s the story here? It says that Steeve/Cooper are wanderers, and that the two of them have been gathering info from other wanderers on 4.6. Cooper also seems to be a hacker. So why were they gathering information on 4.6? Did they volunteer to assist the M.E.G? Did they get impatient with the M.E.G investigating the Glitch and tried to take it on themselves? Did they not trust the M.E.G and think they were hiding something? Or are they from a completely separate faction/small settlement of people, who were independently researching the level and decided to share their research for the greater good?  
Furthermore, how do the “reports of finally finding the level again after 12 years” fit into this? Whose reports are they? Are they from other wanderers? From leaked internal documents? From a public announcement by the M.E.G, B.N.T.G, etc?  
I would work out more of the backstory between Cooper/Steve and the M.E.G, and the timeline of who has learned what about this sub-level.

- Another thing- this is a level that was known to previously exist, and then disappeared. Did the M.E.G know about the level before it disappeared the first time? Or was it only known to a small, unaffiliated group of people? Who wrote the original file that Cooper recovered?  
If the MEG knew about 4.6 and wrote the file, then they would have a section on the page giving a brief history of what happened. Ie. “We originally logged this level in 2012, then it disappeared and corrupted all data associated with it. Wanderers Cooper and Steve generously volunteered their findings in the effort to recover the original file.” Something like that, at bare minumum.  
They would probably also include a note like “this document was last updated in 2012, so we’re unsure if the level has changed since re-appearing. We don’t recommend you go there until we know more (and it’s a bad idea to go there anyways, so don’t), investigation is ongoing", etc etc.

- Another thing that the MEG would probably say is that this level could disappear again- they have no idea how stable it is. They also probably have no idea why it vanished in the first place. They would acknowledge both of these things.

- Where are Cooper and Steve having their conversation? A chat forum on the Backrooms’ wifi? If they’re hackers, and trying to find out about 4.6 under the radar, would they have established an encrypted chat space? What about adding some metadata to their posts, to indicate where the conversation is happening?

- *"Contact a Nearby M.E.G Employee for Access codes to this file."*  
I think it would be more realistic to say "Contact Trial Testers" (the M.E.G reasearch division), or the Archivists, because there's no guarantee of M.E.G personnel standing around in a given place to answer people's questions.

Level Features

- *"Walls can randomly crumble into the ground, showing the block void where the wall used to be. The same thing happens to the floor without warning, and this can be insanely dangerous if this happens on a tile a wander is standing on."*  
Are you familiar with [Voidstone](object-50.md)? Check this out- this has been canonically established as what exists behind the walls and floors. In which case, maybe specify that a person can no-clip into a void through the floor.

- The exit difficulty is 4/5, yet two very straighforward exits are given- the entrance floor tile and the graffiti. Are these exits unlikely to work? If so, that should be stated.

- *“The level used to be nice and clean but still glitchy, but now the office is trashed with broken monitors,lights,glass,and cabinets.”*  
How do people know what the level used to look like?

- *“A report was stated that a wander tried moving backward but instead noclip into the void.”*  
Into a void within the level, into [The Void (wiki)](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/the-void), or into the [Blue Channel](blue-channel.md)? This location should definitely be linked to its corresponding page, to avoid confusion. (Wiki's Void and Fandom's Void are not the same thing. The Blue Channel would probably be the Wiki equivalent to the Fandom Void). Of course, this level can have its own void space- but that needs to clearly stated.

- I don’t think the graffiti needs to be its own section. If these are the only two graffitis known in the level, they can just be included with the rest of the level description. If the graffiti is going to have its own section, it should be more prevalent throught the level and described in way more detail.

Page Design/ Misc

- Maybe style the page so that Cooper's file looks like an actual file that's been recovered and included. I'm not a CSS wizard, but there are plenty of existing templates and people who could help with that.

- I would change the page header- get rid of “Neophyxian” and maybe replace it with the default “Backrooms- You’ve Been Here Before.” A quick wiki search doesn’t turn up any articles about the Neophyxian, so it might be dead. (But Neophyxians predate my involvement in the wiki, so I would double-check with someone who's been here longer.)

- The credit to the original photo set is a nice idea, but unnecessary since those images aren't actually on the page. They have plenty of credits elsewhere.

Further Ideas

I think the concept of the glitchy positioning and unintended movements could be developed further. What if it wasn’t just movement that people can’t control properly? What if trying to speak results in the wrong words coming out? What if eating something sends it to the wrong place inside your body? (Or what if things in people’s stomachs can glitch back out?) What if material possessions wont stay in pockets/backpacks, and travellers lose the precious supplies they carry? What if you tried to pick something up, and grabbed something entirely different on the other side of the room? What if your joints or organs misaligned? What if this is why the level and its data disappear- neither can maintain a stable position for long? (That is, both the physical level and bits of data move?) There’s a lot to play with- I think developing this phenomenon and giving it more of a central role on the page could really set it apart from similar concepts, and build this sublevel’s personality.

Thanks for getting through my long crit! Keep it up!
