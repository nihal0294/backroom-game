Which, to be fair, I'm not entirely opposed to? I mean, we are essentially a writing platform that can have any tone and any theme, which I've always enjoyed. However that doesn't mean that there are a few select things which I don't like if not done right, lol

This *just* feels like vanilla fantasy shit that could be put into some kind of TV show or movie. I even had a game with a friend where I read to them an explanation of what Pantheon is, minus the names of characters that we knew were from The Backrooms, and attempted to see if we could tell if it was still in a realm of being related to The Backrooms, which unsurprisingly, we couldn't.

Obviously, what "The Backrooms" is differs from person to person, but to me the idea of some fantasy gods all looking like they're pulled straight from the same time period with the same generic fantasy goals and wishes whilst all being humanoid in some way(which yes, I get their avatars, but still) isn't it for me, hence the downvote.

It could very easily be my general distain for generic fantasy talking, I am usually one to call the genre rather bland and generic if it's not done in an interesting way, but I honestly don't like the concept of The Pantheon, and honestly it looks really out of place when put in comparison to some of the other material on-site

this page's concept isn't that bad ngl (and i wouldn't downvote the page if i just didn't \_personally\_ like the concept) but the execution is poor.

my biggest issue is with the last page with stretch.

the idea of the gods being weak to iron is really smart, and it was already put in place before. and you've connected **iron** to **the constructions of humans** which i think is extremely smart!

the issue is that it has no subtlety.

for example, this didn't need to be re-stated - the previous pages should show this.

> The pantheon's prominence and importance to The Lost as a whole was self-apparent, judging by what information had been deciphered so far.

and then it… explains the concept straight up:

> There was a disconnect in the narrative, and quite an interesting one at that. Why was this? Well, Overseer-A did have one theory in particular. There was evidence, although scarce, to suggest that a downfall of the gods had occurred long ago. Most specific details of this event were lost to time, but one remained consistent throughout the few surviving documentations: iron. This material was suggested to be the catalyst of the gods' downfall.

this is not very good, because the idea is one that you should be able to figure out on your own. if you read all the pages, you should be able to connect those dots without being handheld into the answer.

the excitement that you had when you first had this idea came from a connecting of dots - you should give that to the readers; this is how to make an *engaging* story.

> Although it was only a theory, it was nonetheless compelling.

this line doesn't actually give the reader doubt as to wether stretch was right or not - but the reader can tell it's *supposed* to do that, so the reader feels patronised.

uh that's all i guess
