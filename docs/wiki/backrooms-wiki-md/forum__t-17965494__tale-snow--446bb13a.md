**Sensitive Content:** Possibly mild descriptions associated with dissociation - IDK? Dead relative

**Brief Description:** Only two thoughts persisted: *Cold. Forward.* Finally, I was more a machine than a man, and home, the place I had sought to find, fell out of my mind.

**Critique Focus:** Prose, dialogue, expression of themes.

**Additional Notes/Remarks:** Experimental, unlike any page I’ve written yet. I began with a limspace but the story went in a rather odd direction and I’m unsure whether this is even Backrooms-y enough.

> Finally, I was more a machine than a man, and home, the place I had sought to find, fell out of my mind.

this line isn't effective to me as a stinger because it just reiterates the monotony previously described in the paragraph. in general, i can already tell this piece is probably a little cliched just because of the fact that it launches straight into bombastic descriptions of time and space without giving us an anchor in the form of either a personal connection with the narrator (even a single line would suffice) or some novel spin on the presentation.

> and the way you sometimes stared at me, like you knew what I had been too shy to say. Though of course, there had never been anything to say—and the words I never wanted to say anyways would remain perfectly unsaid.

it's ambiguous what you want to go for here: my immediate instinct is some kind of romantic interest, going off of the Young Adult feel to the prose and TS at large, but i'm not sure this is effective if it's supposed to be that? i also don't know if i fuck with keeping it ambiguous - as i said earlier, as i'm reading this thing, i want to look for some novel hook or detail, and while this is definitely an attempt at that, it doesn't work for me because i'm not sure what this is supposed to contribute, and it's not as though the physical scene or internal monologue is shifting to try and give extra depth to said line.

"the words I never wanted to say anyways" also just feels awkward to me as a platitude. i'm sure there's a better way to express the idea of not \*wanting\* to be emotionally close to someone you know

> the north wind blew, freezing drops of ice

i think if you want to use "freezing" as a verb here you're going to have to set that up as a feature of the prose immediately. this one just comes off as a typo this late into the piece.

> It was somehow familiar. Why did I think I knew this building? I felt a sense of belonging, wondering what it would have been like to grow up here.

you should try to slim this down: protostalgia doesn't need three sentences to express :v

> And then laugh a few times, too few times, before having to step inside in black clothes, walk around the emptied floors, and listen to him cry from hearing the echoes of footsteps where there had been echoes of laughter.

i like this sentence, very well constructed with its connections and contrasts between joy and sorrow: i will say though that it comes a bit out of the blue because it's a quite sudden shift to literary tone from an intentional monotonous and slightly boring/austere tone. i'd probably try to replicate this sort of a sentence construction earlier in the piece.

> and oh, I was already off.

is this an editing artifact?

> surrounded me, but the

remove comma

> dead but for us three: me, you, and your husband.

okay? interesting development although i do think you could stand to exposit and try to use this as a device earlier in the piece; refer to that sort of alienation one gets when your friend can't \*possibly\* give you the same amount of attention that you give them, yknow? or if not that, sumn else

> Or maybe everything felt too real, and you could see things for what they were, no longer as a part of an universal machine but as just disparate things that had been brought together by a madman. It felt like I could choose to do anything, like rules were words shouted into the wind by someone far away, someone not present and who would never know. Often things simply happened to me, but for now I was making the choices, and this scared me.

this doesn't really work for me because i just don't know enough about this character to really care about their worldview shifting. much like the comment i made earlier, a more personal buildup to this revelation (which should probably also be rephrased since as it is it doesn't feel too punchy or emotive) would probably help this out.

> Yet it was again if another man had taken over and said something sensible, to hide the fact that nothing made sense. Would this be the case forever? The questions asked to me would be answered by some man who would never let me speak and say what I was really feeling. Was this not worse than lying? And worst of all, was this man not me? I could say anything, but would choose to say something, which was nothing. Even when I had felt it, moments ago: The choices were mine to make.

slim this down: there is no reason that it should take this many words to describe derealization, especially if you're not ascribing some poetic image to the feeling. this also applies to the dialogue afterwards describing Jack's troubles: not only is this not how people talk, and not only does this not feel realistic enough in a segment that is ostensibly more grounded than the slightly surreal opening, but it just takes too many words for too little concrete, new information, especially since as mentioned before the prose isn't trying to be particularly beautiful.

> "Well, I had a crush on her. Never told about it to anyone before. Sorry, this is so awkward. You deserve better than to hear me dump all my problems and secrets on you."

also seconding concerns about realistic dialogue to me. people don't just exposit information they've been trying to hold in like this: they go in slowly, using metaphors or images or comparisons to try and prime the listener for the troubling info, and especially since he's bringing this up as an injured friend to Melissa's \*husband\*, i just don't buy this as the delivery where this info would be given; hell, i don't really buy the romantic connection in general, since out of context the conceit of this piece lies much more in the idea of rehabilitation and the comparison to drug addiction and relapse than in the actual interpersonal dynamics at play here. if anything, that sort of a grander scope should be much more emphasized here: not that you can't try to build a solid character, but i do think you should keep the aim of this piece in mind.

  
overall: the idea of the piece is good, and i do think that as a metaphor for solid, reliable help - i personally took the relationship between the snowstorm and relapse to be intentional - it works: i just don't think the actual prosaic execution of the piece works because it doesn't really do anything to hook the reader.

even if the piece is short, i do think it has to distinguish itself in some notable way, and i also think that you have to decide what you really value in this: do you need to have this vaguepost about potential romantic attraction, or could you refocus the piece to more carefully outline this metaphor you want to go for?

the actual level itself i didn't comment on because it just feels kind of incomplete at the moment - you get the sense that Jack feels kind of stupid, kind of silly for the slipup and confident that he will not repeat it (or even if he does that he can escape) - but you don't get a broader look at anything which i feel is kind of wasted potential

that's all my comments, a basic critique but hope that helps ^^
