# Unnumbered Level - "Catwalk"

rating: +47[+](javascript:; "I like it")[–](javascript:; "I don't like it")[x](javascript:; "Cancel my vote")

  
⚠️ Content Warnings ⤴

Info

Heavy mentions of self-harm, alcoholism, and transphobic related trauma.  
Huge thank you to and for the critique and polishing <3

[Close](index.md)

You were not invited, but alas, you are the main act.

The stage calls out to you. This isn't another set of hallways, no familiarity, this is a catwalk. It exposes every bit of you to those who choose to watch, and they all chose. Strobing lights sweep across your figure, illuminating you as you glide along the glossy floor.

You weren't invited to this party, this catwalk.

Yet here you are, stepping into it anyway. Your heels click in rhythm; the skirt’s colors match your accessories, every detail meticulously chosen. With each step you take here you feel the weight of your own body. You feel your chest too flat, your shoulders too wide, and your muscles stiff from years of correcting yourself and clenching so hard.

You feel the tension in your voice when you speak, the irregularity of it compared to your appearance. You are aware. Too aware of your own mannerisms, down to where you rest your arms.

They are here for you.

So you will walk. You will continue to walk for miles, feeling no thirst, no hunger from days without food, and no tedium from this organized chaos—a chaos you can’t quite put your finger on. When you look behind you, the runway looks the same as it does ahead. The same smooth, white marbled floor, leading to darkness and more runway. No end, no beginning. It is dimly lit and quiet, your ears are filled with nothing but your own heartbeat pounding in your chest. Each step taken sounds a loud click on the smooth catwalk floor, equal and balanced.

Walk.

The soles of your feet ache horribly, but expectations press down—you have to be the best.

Before this, before you fell into this immaculate perversion of reality, you were a person. A person with aspirations, hopes, dreams—all of those emotions that made you human, made you in touch with reality—were yours once. You were a performer. Until this moment, it seemed the only thing you existed for was the amusement of others, to entertain.

Your tone, criticized by your mother for being too feminine, you altered to make the world accept you. Your demeanor, style, interests, everything—was shaped by others **so you could be the boy God intended.**

You told yourself you didn't care about the way you dressed yourself.

But you cared.

You really did.

You cared in the way you lingered too long in certain aisles, eyes pressed against different accessories and products. You cared about the way you kept glancing at fabrics and silhouettes as your gaze immediately darted away with shame. You often made mental calculations in your head whether something you liked could've been worn "ironically", sometimes for just a single moment of authenticity, of being.

You didn't want to wear dresses, not at all. You just wanted to feel like dresses shouldn't endanger you. To feel like you could wear whatever you wanted, and not be punished.

The runway isn't narrow enough to scare you. It is spotless. No dust. Nothing. Just the glances of the unmoving observers, and the sound of your skirt suspended in the air.

Flop, flip, woosh.

Move.

You shouldered more burdens than you can count.

Instead of living as who you were, or who you are—you spent the first half of your life being someone you weren’t. You never got to experience the love and community other kids did, because who would want to be seen with a queer like you? Instead of living as who you were or who you are, you spent the first half of your life rehearsing a role you had to play.

Every inflection, every response, every word you spoke drew the same question: Why do you talk like that?

You talked like that because you were a child. You talked like that because it felt natural. Something resonates within you, whether it be discomfort or understanding, something accepting.

You exhale, deeper than usual this time, and really think.

You hope when you die, God forgives you for what you made your life. In those pauses between thoughts, there's an understanding that he maybe knew what you were going to be. Now, it's as if the audience turns solemnly, almost moving with empathy rather than curiosity. It's as if the runway gets thinner as you go on. Not dangerously thin, but just enough to make you aware of your balance. Balance is not the only thing you're aware of. You have to be perfect, even if you don't see what you keep walking into.

There is no sound but the clacking of your heels as you move, unsure but diligent. It is quiet, muted, tense.

Serve.

Hiding yourself, bottling your desire to be authentic, only bred resentment: of who you are, of what others wanted you to be, of the woman you knew you could become. You trapped yourself in a cage, yet you always had the way out.

You wanted to rip out the hair that grew on parts you were unfamiliar with. You were very good at being a boy. You studied it. You learned it the way some kids learn piano or the cello—through repetition, correction, embarrassment.

You learned how long to hold eye contact.

How wide to sit.

You lowered your voice until it felt as if you were swallowing dirt.

You lost your voice the first few weeks you did that. Then, you got better and better. What saddens me isn't your struggle with identity, but why you think you had to punish yourself for being different.

The catwalk stretches in a seamless strip of polished white, reflecting the lights below to the sides like a mirror. You hope there would be someone else walking this with you, to feel less distinguished, but the audience's gaze remains locked on you.

How quaint.

Stride.

Because deep down, shoving your identity in a suitcase and throwing it away didn't do anything, it didn't change who you are.

You remember the boutiques. Passing through them with your parents, pretending not to look, you couldn't take your eyes off them. They had the kind of style, the clothing, the garments you wish you could be able to wear. What you were told you were from birth was never the person you wanted to be.

The name you were given never matched your drive, your determination, your ego. There are still things you are learning. How to accept joy without being suspected, how to let yourself be seen without performing femininity like how you thought you were supposed to, and how to forgive the boy you were trying so hard to be.

The flashes of camera clicks are blinding, but feel cathartic on your skin. You feel their attention settle over you, heavy and precise, as if your outline has hardened into something permanent.

Strut.

Every old family photo you saw was heartbreaking. What stared back at you was a terrified girl, learning the world she had to live in and how she could survive.

There was a time you felt a kind of envy you didn't have a word for. It wasn't about clothes this time. Not really. It was about ease, about comfort. About the way girls would touch each other without it meaning anything. How they could exist without apologizing for being soft, how they were allowed to be emotional without it being regarded as defective.

You convinced yourself you were just attracted to them. That was easier to admit. You can confess desire, you couldn't confess your longing. You feel hot in your dress, the place is weirdly warm.

Your legs continue moving down the catwalk path. You haven't heard a noise beside the constant cameras. It's calm. One ramp on each side. You do not move out of it, you choose not to.

You continue forward.

Sway.

Your coping mechanisms weren't the greatest, either. No amount of pain you inflicted on yourself would ever confine the feelings you had about who you were. No amount of alcohol could drown out the crying, the begging, the bad thoughts. They flooded you. You felt as if you had to distract yourself of your essence to progress.

That inner voice would never go away, and ultimately, you exchanged your life for some peace and quiet. For a break. A place where nothing existed, and nothing mattered. The spotlight feels hot on your face, occasionally shining through the crowd and finding its way on you.

Your hips undulate with perfection down the runway floor, fit with loose cords and wires.

You don't trip on them.

You can't.

Pose.

Every single moment of your godforsaken life was a catwalk.

Every time you stepped out of your own comfort to exist in a space, to communicate, socialize, be yourself—you walked. You walked, and you walked, and you walked until your legs couldn't take it anymore.

Until your mind was overwhelmed with the laughing from the audience, with the faces of disgust you were given, with the acceptance you never even got. The world cast you out because you were different.

And so, you hated the world. The audience looks with intrigue as they sit on black, velvet chairs just to observe your movements. At times, you can almost hear the faint brush of the fabric against their clothing, disrupting the silence. Heat pulses under your skin, sudden and violent.

And, just for a moment you’re unsure whether you’re blushing or burning.

Lean.

Get back on the catwalk. Walk more. Pretend more. Spend eternity in this limbo, and finally listen to that screaming, agonizing voice in your head that wanted to be let out. So she can be herself. You are brave, even when the world says you're not.

My beautiful girl, always.

Your heels keep clicking on the glossy floor, but this time sounding hesitant.

You realize it's impossible.

You will never conform again.

There won't be a time where you can please everybody's wishes.

You are you, forever and always.

Strut, walk, pose.

Sway from side to side, without a care in the world.

You walk so elegantly.

Be free.

Like the girl you were always meant to be.

Show these motherfuckers who you are.
