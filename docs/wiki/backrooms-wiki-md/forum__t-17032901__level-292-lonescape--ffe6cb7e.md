Regarding Adam's message at the beginning of the wiki, what was "real world" and "outside world" was left open. I'm going to assume that these expressions refer to The Frontrooms. Still talking about the message at the beginning of the wiki, Adam talks about "wanting to catalog the Level as 292". But if he was going to catalog the Level, why would he send the message to MEG? Wi-Fi is not mentioned at any point, so this would be like a hole in the story.

Now I'm going to talk about the inputs and outputs tab. The Level entries mention The Frontrooms as a possible entry, and I think that's really cool. However, the Level does not have any output, which makes it very overpowering. My personal opinion would be to either remove the entrance through The Frontrooms, or add an exit to this Level, but one that would not be easy, still maintaining the Level's biggest danger: extreme difficulty in exiting it.

Still on the entries tab, Adam mentions Level 39 and urban things. It is simply impossible for him to theorize an entry through Level 39, and from what the wiki suggested, Adam entered here through the Frontrooms. How did he come across the concept of Backrooms, or even what MEG and Level 39 were? He reached this level directly through The Frontrooms.

I found the concept very creative (the theme of the page being windows was very good). The part where the buildings randomly change size was really cool too. That's all for now. This is my first review, so, please give me feedback on my review. Did she help the page? How i can be a better critic?

Overall:  
Adam does not seem at all panicked and only a little concerned about how he got sent to the Backrooms. To me, it seems that he just immediately goes to "investigate everything" as if he was an emotionless detective. This significantly reduces the depth of the character and what people would feel for them, and due to the page being centered around the character, detracts from the whole article.

My solution to this would to be to extend the page backwards, all the way back to Adam's initial entry to this level. His reactions should be scared and confused. Having him explore most of the level before finding the computer would give readers a chance to see his reaction to everything, besides "this exists". When he does find the computer and contacts the M.E.G, seeing his relief at knowing he's not alone would also be an enjoyable conclusion to this section.

I would point you to [this page](the-withered-rose.md) for an example of how someone entering the Backrooms so abruptly would respond (although, don't copy everything exactly, alter it to fit this page better).

These issues are more minor, but could also do with changing:  
When you refer to a website called "The Backrooms", I think what you mean is the General Public Database.  
Does "I found this file on a computer" refer to the same computer, or a different one? The latter doesn't make much sense to me (why not use the computer with the file to contact the M.E.G?) but the way it's written implies it.  
"Haunted or not" should imply that there's something stranger about the forest than rivers and fruits. Given how Adam's character is set up, it could work well if he came across something paranormal while exploring it.

You might also want to run this page through a grammar checker, like Quillbot, since I've seen a lot of incorrect word usage in some sections.

Re: Level 292 - “Lonescape”

20 Feb 2025 17:25

Hello!  
I gave this and the previous crits a read.

## **Concept**

The concept feels strong. I like the POV of someone isolated in a level that they haven’t figured out how to escape yet- it leaves us wondering if they’ll manage to, or if the MEG will eventually attempt to save them. (The forest path that *might* be an exit, but is so infinitely long that Adam hasn’t managed to walk it yet, is fantastic.) The idea of dead end levels has been explored, but I haven’t seen a totally isolated level like this one yet. I really like the idea of a strange “pocket” of the Backrooms that isn’t necessarily connected to the rest- that you can no-clip directly into a dead end from the Frontrooms. It leaves us wondering if some parts of the Backrooms are completely inaccessible from each other, and if there are entire other worlds that none of our factions and characters will get to see. A thought-provoking page is good.

I also find it interesting, and refreshing, to have a protagonist who actually doesn’t mind being there. The Backrooms are often written about as isolating and horrifying, but endless emptiness can also be peaceful. I think it would make this page richer to develop Adam’s character further, and have him talk more about why he likes being in this level. He was a university student. What if he was slowly breaking under the pressure, the constant work, the expectations to succeed, the busyness of modern life- and falling into the Backrooms was actually a welcome escape? The first real peace he’s had in years?

I want to ask- have you partly written this character based on yourself? Would you love to be in a place like this? If so, tell us more about why. Open this character’s heart for us to see.

## **Content**

Questions I had while reading:

- *"What I do know is that I was able to access a website called "The Backrooms,"* I agree with BlueSkies- change this to “General Public Database”

- *”But it's to inform you that I've fallen into a strange and lonely place.”* Suggestion: *"But it's to inform you that I've fallen into a strange and lonley place- I think you call it a level?"* I would add this- it’s more believable, because the guy has read the MEG database from afar and is probably a little unsure about using their terminology.

- *”Leaving all that aside, the place can also be summed up as… a building island in the middle of a forest ocean.”* Move this sentence to the opening paragraph, where Adam talks about going to university and entering the Backrooms. The introductory paragraph of a level should give a clear, overall picture of what it is- this sentence provides a good visual.

- Also, take more time with the introductory paragraph, and the story of how Adam got here. It lacks detail, and that makes it feel unrealistic. Which university did he go to? What was he studying? What class was he packing up from when he no-clipped? Tell the story with detail, to create a real-life scenario.

- Describe the lighting and the sky, in the part of the level with the building. Are you imagining it like the photograph- dull and overcast? Is it always the same time of day, or is there night? Does the sun ever shine? Is it ever windy? Give us more sensory details about the environment here.

- *“I can't describe much, as it's just a forest in the same twilight cycle.”* This is confusing- is the forest in perpetual twilight? Or does it cycle through night and twilight, with no day?

- *"Although I can say that the temperature is a bit low, it can return to its room temperature at any time.”* Also confusing. Does the temperature cycle according to the time of day? Or are there just random temperature changes, for no known reason?

- *"Haunted or not, the forest here has a pretty comfortable vibe and is one of my favorite places here.”* If the forest has a comfortable vibe, why would Adam think it might be haunted? People usually only think a place is haunted if it feels creepy or uncomfortable. Maybe Adam thinks it could be haunted because other people have died here- but this is just a theory, and he doesn’t necessarily feel it. If so, say that outright.

- *”As an added note, there are some fruit and berry plants, both near and far. There are also some streams and lakes, so there is never a shortage of food or water.”* I would add something like “I don’t recognize any of these berries, but I’ve been eating them and so far haven’t suffered any ill effects.” Adam’s investigating the level so that the MEG know what they’re getting into, if they try to come here. So he’ll be clearly saying what’s safe to do and what isn’t, to help them out.

- *“…psychological phenomena that are original to the person.”* A) this should be “unique to the person.” B) how would Adam know this, if he’s the only one there? He has no other people to compare experiences with

- *“Someone else who fell into this level will have their number defined in the same way.”* Again- how would Adam know? He can guess this, but he can’t know for sure. He could only say “I wonder if this number will be different for everyone who comes here.”

- *”Although near the building, you can also hear some nostalgic music…”* What kind of music? What does Adam find nostalgic? Is it MIDI music like the ambience on the page? Does it remind him of anything specific? Give more detail on this.

- *"Throughout my research around the landscape, I have come across notes containing different messages."* Be clearer that he’s finding notes in the forest and the buildings. How is he finding them? Are they randomly pinned to trees? Lying on the ground? Left on tables?

- *”My theory is that the place I am in has some kind of connection with other people who are also trapped here, and thanks to the papers in the building, they can communicate or leave their last words.”* This is a bit too vague- a theory needs to be specific. Be specific about how, why, or when he thinks other people are trapped here. Suggestion: “I don’t know if there’s anyone else here with me- but I don’t think I’m the first one. My theory is that these notes are from previous visitors. Maybe this is how they communicate their last words.”

- *“I'll try to write my own to try and prove my theory.”* How would writing a note of his own prove Adam’s theory? He would have to wait for someone else to enter the level and find it- which means he would probably be dead. It would make more sense for him to just say “I’m going to try writing a note of my own, and see what happens to it.”

- Like I said in “Concept”, I think it would make this page even stronger if you go deeper into how Adam feels about being here. Why he likes the solitude, how he feels in the forest, how he feels in the empty building, what memories the nostalgic music brings back, why he doesn’t mind the emptiness, etc. We have this beautiful but distant, soothing but sad, MIDI ambience on the page that’s full of feeling- I’d love to see that same level of feeling reflected in how Adam talks about the level.

I like that there are little hints of how he feels scattered throughout the page. (Like *“i love the atmosphere”* on the photo caption.) It’s like he’s trying to be objective, but his feelings are still coming through. (You could lean more into this, and drop more hints, to show his bias creeping in.) But to be really effective, I also think there needs to be a section where Adam directly discusses how he feels about being here. Maybe after the part about the notes, where he says he’ll write his own message. You could segue into this with Adam reflecting on whether he’s going to die here before any one finds him- then how he really feels about being this place.

Questions to consider: Does he really want to leave? Does he actually want to see people again? Is he procrastinating on following that forest path? Or has he become addicted to the melancholy of this level? (I personally think melancholy can be addicting- I don’t know if you feel the same, and want to explore this.)

- If Adam doesn’t really want to leave- or at least, if he feels peaceful being trapped here- this will affect the opening message on the page. To make sense, it may need to go something like this:

*“I should ask you for help. I need to get out of here- I know it isn’t good to be alone forever. But it’s strangely peaceful- I honestly feel like I have all the time in the world. And besides, I haven’t learned enough about the place yet- I don't think it would be fair to ask you to look for me.*

*So for the moment, I’ve decided to investigate this place myself, and send you everything I learn. You can assess this and draw your own conclusions. Please help me when you’re ready- if you can.”*

## **Language**

- *“The temperature that runs is one that comes from the **twilight** climates that usually come from the Frontrooms…”* Do you mean “temperate climates”? Or do you mean the cooler temperatures that happen around sundown?

- *“Both its exterior and interior seem to be completely abandoned and empty except for some **writers**, along with papers and pencils.”* Do you mean “typewriters”?

- *"It's a perfect natural habitat for any animal; however, there isn't any life form.”* Maybe this is being pedantic, but trees & plants *are* life forms. Maybe just say it would be an ideal habitat for many animals, but there aren’t any.

- There are more language issues besides these- SPaG errors, confusing sentences, and awkward wording. But language edits are best to do very last, after the page contents have been smoothed out. Edit the page contents first, based on the suggestions you've gotten, and re-work Adam’s emotional involvement throughout the page. Language can be the focus of a later crit.

## **Format / Misc**

Nothing but good things to say. The windows 98 theme and the MIDI ambience work very well together- it creates a good, aching sense of a place that's familiar, but weirdly placeless. If you wanted, you could include a small detail where Adam mentions that the computer he's writing on reminds him of the old computer in his house as a child (assuming he grew up in the early 90's).

Alright, thanks for getting through my lengthy comments! I love being immersed in this page, as a reader- I'm excited to see what you can do to make it even more emotionally compelling.  
Cheers.
