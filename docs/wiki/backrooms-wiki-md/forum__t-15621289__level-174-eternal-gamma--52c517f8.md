It hardly feels believable to me. Somalia feels like a Sue, Andrew feels like the annointed bash receptacle. I'll explain why it felt like this to me.

So… If you decide to send people to this level before even knowing how to enter to ensure you aren't incinerated, I don't and can't buy that some angry person walking into youur office and talking at you - because it did feel like Somalia was talking **at** Andrew to me - for a bit under five minutes (I read it out loud to time it, starting from where Somalia starts trying to get Andrew to shut down the base) then immediately striding out as soon as you make verbal noises that indicate that you'll give them what they want - actually changes your mind.

I bring up the fact that employees were apparently being incinerated because Somalia uses that as a talking point for why the base must be shut down. I am aware that elsewhere in the article, it's states the base's entrance is safe. That's another reason for the -1. It's a continuity error? Or something? Because if it's not the case that MEG employees are being turned to ash, it can't be a reason to shut the base down or even alter practices. It's just … typical backrooms level has an unsafe entrance in addition to a safe one but we use the safe one and tell people about unsafes so it's fine situation?

**The basic issue I have is that regardless of whether there's context that would make this conversation make sense outside the article, it does not stand up for me when taken on it's own, and I think articles being able to stand on their own is necessary for a novote or better. Articles that need the support of other articles to exist don't pass muster for me outside of the edge cases of chapterised tales and offset articles where the reader is clearly being told that it's a work in a sequence.**

Another issue is that the end of the discussion tells too much and shows too little. We are told Andrew's mental state. We aren't shown it via Andrew's actions; in fact, thoseactions do essentially read to me like it was shut down because Somalia ordered it. "Spur decision" makes it sound like whimsy shut it down, not a cogent reason: and coverups are fine but if there is real regret on Andrew's part, it would be better conveyed through some method other than a description directly telling us how hurt Andrew looked.

Another issue is that analogue geiger counters are almost stupidly easy to make, and cloud chambers (another low-tech detection system) are not much more difficult. But that's a lot more forgiveable because "backrooms fucky-wucky physics" is so goddamn common that it's very reasonable to assume "oh there's some complicating factor that makes a low-tech geiger counter and cloud chamber fail". And I want to be reasonable.

Another related issue is that the documents from the base should be more than gamma emitters if they're contaminated at all.

There's further stuff that's problematic. Grammar, word choice, etc. But I don't see solicitation for concrit above and beyond the amount encouraged by the site admins to make a downvote qualify as "polite" so I'll abstain.

An interesting article bogged down by unnatural dialogue and odd plot decisions.

To illustrate the issue:

- nuke level danger.
- bad overseer sends redshirts into nuke level.
- this is bad.
- overseer's cute gf comes in.
- "andrew, this is bad."
- "ok sweetie."
- the end.

What's the takeaway here? Any real focus on the disfigurement and injuries caused by radiation is mentioned clinically and in passing, without giving the reader any time to dwell on the tragedy. If you're not interested in tackling the horrors of nuclear weapons, then what's the point of a level with a mushroom cloud when any other general threat would've sufficed? This isn't what I'd call a lighthearted topic, and it's strange to me that here, it's used as a backdrop for…playful banter between ocs?
