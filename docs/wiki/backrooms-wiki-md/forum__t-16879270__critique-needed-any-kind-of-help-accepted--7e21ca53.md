Here is the Level Page:

**Level 907 - The Unescapable White Room**  
**Difficulty**: 4  
**Entities**: The Partypooper  
**Entrances**: Killing an entity (50% chance), being in Level 12 for 907 hours  
**Exits**: 1% chance of noclipping to Level 977, 99% chance of noclipping to the void

**Description**  
Level 907, "The Unescapable White Room," is a single, blindingly white room with no discernible doors or windows. The walls, floor, and ceiling are seamless and indistinguishable from each other, creating a disorienting and claustrophobic environment. A faint hum pervades the silence, enhancing the eerie atmosphere. Time seems to lose its meaning here, leaving wanderers trapped in a perpetual state of unease.

**Entities**  
The only entity present is "The Partypooper," a humanoid figure with a perpetually bored expression. He approaches wanderers with an eerie calm, uttering unsettling reassurances like, "You must accept your fate here," and cryptic hints such as, "At least you're not stuck with Him." His presence adds to the oppressive nature of the room, making escape feel even more impossible.

**Escape**  
The only known method of escape is to noclip through a wall. However, this is extremely difficult due to the seamless nature of the room. Success is rare, with a mere 1% chance of reaching Level 977, a level that is slightly more dangerous than Level 907. The far more likely outcome is a 99% chance of falling into the void, a fate from which there is no return. Rumors persist that The Partypooper's cryptic whispers hold a key to this impossible escape, though none have deciphered them.

**Entrances**  
Entrances to Level 907 are mysterious and unpredictable. Killing an entity has a 50% chance of transporting a wanderer here. Another method is enduring 907 hours in Level 12, a feat that pushes the limits of both endurance and sanity.

**Discovery and Recommendations**  
Level 907 was first documented through the fragmented and haunting notes of a wanderer. These notes suggest that the level tests the limits of the human psyche and resolve. For those trapped here, maintaining a fragile calm and documenting experiences may provide clues. Engaging with The Partypooper cautiously might unveil hidden pathways to freedom—or deeper into the abyss.

**The Final Days of Joshua Hill in Level 907**

**Discovery**  
Joshua Hill's fate in Level 907 was uncovered through a small, worn notebook found by another wanderer. This notebook contained the final, haunting entries of Joshua Hill, detailing his encounter with "The Unescapable White Room."

**Joshua Hill's Notebook**

**Day 1**  
*The whiteness is blinding. It’s all the same, stretching on forever. The silence is maddening, broken only by a strange hum.*

**Day 3**  
*Met "The Partypooper" today. He told me to accept my fate here. His calm voice made my skin crawl.*

**Day 5**  
*Tried noclipping today but hit solid rock. The whiteness is oppressive. It’s getting to me.*

**Day 7**  
*Whispers are telling me to give up. The Partypooper watches constantly. I can’t escape his gaze.*

**Day 10**  
*Tried again to noclip. For a moment, I felt hope, but nothing. The Partypooper, looking bored as a statue in a desert said, "At least you're not stuck with Him." "What? What do you mean?" I asked. He walked away calmly, but his face showed a flicker of pain.*

**Day 12**  
*The whispers are clearer: “Noclip… the wall…” I don’t know if I have the strength.*

**Day 15**  
*One last try. If this fails, I’ll accept my fate. Running at the wall now…*

Joshua was writing before he made his final attempt. As he ran, he glanced back and saw the Partypooper looking away, whispering, "You're welcome." It felt like it wasn't meant for him.

**Epilogue**  
Joshua Hill’s notebook was found near a wall. There was no sign of him, only the sterile whiteness. The Partypooper was seen nearby, watching with a bored expression. Joshua’s fate remains a mystery, a chilling reminder of Level 907's horrors. Wanderers should heed his words and approach Level 907 with caution. The whispers continue, echoing Joshua's final thoughts: “Accept your fate here.”

If you have the critique I need, please contact me.
