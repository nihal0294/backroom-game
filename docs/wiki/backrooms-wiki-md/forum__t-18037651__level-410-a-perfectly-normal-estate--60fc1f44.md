while i do think this has a good plot and shines in its best moments, i also think this needed to kill its darlings and focus on what mattered most. why was there a need to include the poetry describing the protag's sense of fucked up time and fucked up self worth when this is beaten to *death* through the notes and later screenplay? why does the opening reveal *all* of the events and respective feelings surrounding alba's view of george's abuse, when this could be used piecemeal, distributed throughout the piece to actually intrigue the reader and give this piece more of a sense of progression? why does the screenplay not focus more on bonnot's view if it is the perspective we havent seen yet, the one which would fully define the story and environment on display here?

i remember seeing the opening — vangie sent it tome for crit right before i stopped having the will or time [for now!] to fully LBL and dissect shit — and i thought the piece was going to be *very* short. i say this becuse it gets to a wider point: the pacing here is simultaneously too slow and too fast. we are dropped right into the middle of the action, something that's typically good but here results in an opening that exposits a *lot* of info, especially for a piece which has the theming and tone of being cryptic [theming i refer to the literal CSS theme, tone i refer to the epistolary format and non-obvious loredumps] and vibes-based. the emotions — the self-abasement, the infatuation, the gross desire to be dominated and worthless for someone who is so much better than you, the worthless piece of shit — are there from the start and they are never switched out or particularly expanded upon. sure, alba eventually leaves, but right up until the end there isn't any hint of his loyalty fading until it's absolutely necessary for the plot.

i also felt like the piece was too slow because the actual descriptions of what's going on are distant vignettes, and as mentioned before, they rehash too often the feelings already developed. the opening, as strong as it is, makes every note afterwards about alba's servitude and bonnot's grace and alba's twisted love feel *redundant*.

i personally think the technical side was a little bungled but not too importantly. for starters, nothing happens with the physical symbolism established at the start. you would think white roses or golden grain would play more of a role here than they really do, and perhaps they should have given the otherwise barebones choreogrqphy of the play. there's a couple SPaG errors, sure, but what really took me out of the page was the pacing in the end. it made me wonder when the page would end, so,ething that is *not* good when your piece is less than 4000 words.

it isn't abjectly terrible — i think some of the imagery with alba and bonnot is pretty well-set, and i think the lore here is clearly well-thought-out — but i do think as it is right now i wouldn't want to upvote. idt this should be off the site per se but it feels kind of bland at the end of the ex0erience from the slow/constant physical-mental pacing, even if — again — i think this piece is good once it catches its stride. as much as we joked abou it, at the end of the day this does kinda feel like midslop/qualityslop: prosaically well-written, conceptually bloated and oddly incomplete.

sorry 8f this k8nda sucks, its abou midnight here >\_> hope you found this moderately entertaining and useful ^^

Wynths not to disrespect you or anything but as much as I agree with the critique (and your ultimate result as well, because I'm also novoting) you just gave right now, the summary of your position irked me in such a way that my knee-jerk reaction is just to disagree with you solely on that. I gave Vangie mostly the same critique as you did what with the pacing and all that, but calling it 'qualityslop' is 1) rude and 2) incorrect, because qualityslop refers to things that are made with the express intent of appealing to people on the basis of it being high arts without having substance. Qualityslop does not describe things written in earnest that just fall a bit flat.

Like yeah, there's definitely some metaphors here that were spun clumsily, and I definitely feel like we're caught up in the emotions of the moment rather than the actual article context of the level itself, but I also feel like you're failing at least partially to sympathise with the characters. Why would we need the perspective of bonnot when bonnot is clearly the abusive one in this dysfunctional relationship, and this article is focusing on Frank's feelings in the relationship rather than acting as a character expose? Why does his loyalty *have* to fade away for him to leave being realistic? Speaking from experience here, you can be loyal till the very end and still decide to leave the moment everything bubbles up all at once — relationships are inherently irrational what with them being tied to the already irrational human condition. Why is the feelings being rehashed the critique here when the hammering-in and mulling things over is normal and expected with any kind of romantic splitting? If anything, I feel like the failure of the article if anything is that the constant reminder of how serious these feelings are is not substantiated by an equally developed and hammered-in end; like you said ultimately, the pacing is slow first and too fast at the end.

I don't mean to be an ass in how I respond to you obviously, but I think you could have done more effort to understand how different people act in love, and ultimately to deliver your 'blow' to the authors with more cushioning. After all that critique, summarising your feelings as 'qualityslop' and then saying they hope they find the critique useful just feels backhanded and unnecessary when the rest of your critique is largely valid. You could've just rehashed your feelings as a conclusion without the added twitterspeak
