# Abruptly Ended Respite

# **Chapter 4**

### Abruptly Ended Respite

  
  
  

This was a basement. Reggie had seen a lot of basements recently, but this one was better than the rest.  
Located some distance away from the main hub of [level 11](level-11.md), it was a cool underground oasis of calm. platinum-painted cinder-block walls, warm fluorescent lights, lacquered wooden flooring. If basement-dwellers had a hierarchy, this was where kings would live. Or so Reggie thought — it was probably just him trying to justify why they had been sitting there, for nearly three hours.  
  
This was Haven, both in name and function. It was a place where Reggie could go, that was safe, at least for now. The only area where danger didn't lurk in abundance.  
  
If you looked away from the room's structure, the place was barely furnished, mostly consisting of utilitarian shelves and tables of plastic and stainless steel. There was one oasis of colour however — a coat rack built like the rest of the furniture. It was filled with Ghillie suits in a spectrum of colours from almost-black industrial to white tundra, taking in green forest and urban grey on the way.  
But camouflage was not always appropriate for every mission, and so Reggie mostly wore a sleeveless navy jacket with dark dress trousers — not fashionable, as he would be the first to admit, but it was practical while still maintaining some of his outmoded style.  
  
But still, he was resting, nobody could judge him for that, considering the actions he had done that day. In approximately twenty-six minutes the guard shift at Thebes would change, and they would discover that the large crate of electronics just couriered from [level 502](level-502.md) was misplaced. If they had some kind of psychic circuit-board locator, they would have found it in the very basement The Catalyst was now chilling in, having pinched it some few hours earlier.  
  
Well, not entirely resting, he had to admit. At this exact moment, he was trying to unlock a cheap padlock, the sort the U.E.C. used.  
Reggie fiddled with his lockpicks, trying to wedge the various interior parts. He slid out the saw pick that he was fumbling in the slit, and tried a simple diamond pick. He recalled that they had been quite good at locksport, back in [the world](the-frontrooms.md), but his dexterity was crippled, even with the complex prosthetics they were now wearing.

## Useful Object File:

### Custom Helix Claw

![A illustrated blueprint of a connecting sleeve in a double helix shape with labeled parts, along with a cut off figure showing its proper wearing position.](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/the-catalyst/Helix_Blueprint)

A digital blueprint of the connector segment of the customized claw.

The Helix Claw is a customized version of a regular claw, carried by The Catalyst.  
Instead of relying on friction to stay on their arms, like regular claws, Helix Claws have a complex double-helix shaped sleeve that connects the hand to a belt system around their chest. The sleeve is made of flexible plastic, and so can bend in almost every direction without breaking, helped by the unusual shape.  
  
The hand segment is also different, consisting of a metal hand with rubber fingertips and knuckles, instead of the usual corrugated pincers. The hand is motorized, responding to wrist movements similar to high-grade human prosthetics.  
  
The two sections together give The Catalyst better flexibility and grip than an ordinary human, and they say when they have completed preliminary testing they will release all the plans, including material requirements.

He lightly pushed up one of the pins, and quickly applied more torque.  
The shackle popped out, making the padlock fall to the ground from where it had locked itself to a ring in the table.  
  
Reggie sat back in the dining chair that would have smelled suspiciously of pine resin — had that sense not been removed during their transformation. They carefully removed their left fist, and opened a bag of peanuts with their right. He hadn't been through a matter transference beam recently, but it still helped to get some protein in his diet. Oddly enough, it didn't matter what they ate, husks would never gain or lose weight, or show any signs of deficiencies. Neither did most poisons fatal to humans affect them, nor did diseases in food. This was likely due to their relations to Partygoers — after all, what kind of apex species could be stopped by a bit of arsenic?  
  
He looked over at the folding table stacked with loot. There was all sorts of double-ill-gotten gains here: Modern guns, with ammo to boot; various computers, from bulky laptops to slim phones; and advanced technology, including subtle implants and bugging devices.  
Past the stacks of comparatively worthless items, lay a couple of yellowing parchment rolls. They were written in some kind of Latin, and so Reggie, being monolingual now, couldn't ascertain how important they were. Reggie's eyes strayed over to the [Art Museum](level-222.md) brochure.  
  
'It's not like I have anything better to do,' considered Reggie, 'and anyway, I'm sure [Castellos](leo-castellos.md) wouldn't mind protecting them.'

As Reggie reached his rubber-tipped hand towards the brochure, a sudden noise in his room of peace caught him off guard.  
It was a notification from one of the many burner accounts he was connected to — an entity rights sympathant called Ruby, who was helping out the movement via leaking M.E.G. internal reports that could be useful to him.
