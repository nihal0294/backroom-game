To start, I'll say that I think I like this page enough to upvote it. It's an obviously much more conceptually interesting expansion of the original without making it a completely different concept and I think it does both the prior concepts of Level 3 and the additional lore built after it some justice. I really liked some of the ideas here, especially the hyper-intelligent entity stuff, that's really cool.

My main points of critique come in 2 points:

- Point 1: I think this page is bloated.
  - One, in terms of general prose. There are a few points that need not be stated and points that are repeated that could be easily condensed much shorter. I'll illustrate this point with the following 3 sentences:

Level 3 is an extremely dangerous old electrical facility that is full of some of the most rare and valuable resources The Backrooms has to offer. It is the easiest way to acquire both rare and common resources in the entire Backrooms as well. Most groups of interest in the Backrooms consider Level 3 to be their highest priority when it comes to research expeditions given the rich abundance of otherwise rare objects that can be encountered on this level with relative ease.

  
While the core of these sentences are different, they all *also* repeat the same point of Level 3 having rare and valuable resources. This kind of repetitiveness is quite clunky to read, especially for wiki-clinical-esque prose, which ideally should prioritize or at least value some level of succinctness. Let me rewrite this part:

Level 3 is an extremely dangerous old electrical facility. It possesses some of the rarest and most valuable resources the Backrooms has to offer, while being the easiest way to acquire them. As such, a large majority of groups of interest consider Level 3 their highest priority when it comes to research expeditions.

  
Now, this may not seem like a particularly large change, and in isolation it isn't. But this is present all over the page, and over an article this long, with this much info to take in, it can really drag. Tightening up prose is important for pages like this that are largely exposition because reader retention is low and focused on the next bit of interesting info to keep up rather than the prose itself.

- (cont. of point 1)
  - Two, in terms of the details presented here. For some of the same reasons as above there are a decent amount of things here that I feel you could have cut for the sake of brevity. There's a lot about the bars, and this is repeated multiple times with multiple expansions; there's a lot of stuff that alludes to Icarus, and every single part about Icarus is followed up by at least one paragraph of what can be summarized as "we don't know anything about any of this or what causes it"; Base Gamma and its defense systems are talked about in extreme detail, usually to some effect of or in an attempt to illustrate how intense, extensive, and thorough it is. A lot of this is somewhat redundant, and I know it's for worldbuilding and elaboration, but I like to think readers can fill in-between the lines a bit, and in a way it can feel more "alive", to get the feeling that a world extends beyond the border of what is written. In addition to this, I know the frequent lines about how we don't know x and y are to build a sense of mystery but there is value in creating mystery by omission; we don't need to be told what the MEG doesn't know because we already know or at least can tell that the MEG doesn't know anything about this; in some cases it's more interesting to just be told these details raw and for us as the readers to think on and draw connections ourselves.
- Point 2: The Icarus stuff. Now, this point is shorter, mostly because my view of this may be affected by the fact that I have read the Icarus Procidens page (and partially because it repeats a bit of the above point). As a whole I can say I like the details of how Level 3 is affected by Icarus' presence and other lore details: purpification is weird and cool, architecturally dissonant sanctums are an interesting idea, and I liked how the notes were written. But I also think the Icarus stuff kind of feels *hamfisted* after a certain point, especially with how much the text spends, I guess, "gawking" at all this mysterious art and these strange things and how we don't know where they come from and etc etc. Repeating the statement of the previous paragraph, there is value in not outright telling the reader what to think, and I think the large amount of speculation and exposition on these Icarus-related things can be shortened or removed to create some negative space and to let these details have some space to breathe if that makes sense.

Overall, I can't say I disliked it, but I also can't call it my favourite of the main 12 rewrites (though not my least favourite either). Sorry if any of this was unclear, rambly, or otherwise not a pleasant read for one reason or another.
