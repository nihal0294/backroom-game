First off, I think it's worth stating that **you cannot use random images from the internet reliably in your pages**. I know, it's disappointing, but it's true. You need to make sure that your pictures are from reputable sources and have compliant licenses. You cannot just get a random photo or illustration from Twitter/Google and claim them as being under the [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/) license. For a lot more of an in-depth explanation, you can look at these pages:

- [https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/image-use-policy](image-use-policy.md)
- [http://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/component:license-box](component__license-box--969e0688.md)

If you still are adamant on using Google, you can go into the image tab and click on tools and then usage rights to sort by pages that are compliant with the Creative Commons license the site goes by. This isn't very consistent, though, and may only really show you ~20 images that don't exactly align with what you searched for. As a good list of reputable image sources, though, I would recommend taking a look at one of these websites:

- <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Main_Page>
- [https://unsplash.com/](index.md)
- [https://pixabay.com/](index.md)
- [https://www.pexels.com/](index.md)
- [https://search.creativecommons.org/](index.md)
- [https://www.flickr.com/](index.md)

With that out of the way, there's another major thing that needs to be pointed out, and it's that **negative levels no longer exist**. They were actively removed by the higher ups of Tech Support, so you can't really try and re-implement them into the site. This isn't a negative thing, though. The draft could easily work as a regularly numbered level.

> Level -7 is the -7th Level of the Backrooms.

The use of this traditional phrase is one that's falling out of use and is actively being phased out within newer drafts. Although you're not barred from using the expression, I would recommend removing it. Down below are several links showing that the phrase is being discouraged from use, all originating from the Backrooms Tech Support Discord server:

- [Example #1](https://discord.com/channels/695664941247627274/1194841402652229682/1195169918388281364)
- [Example #2](https://discord.com/channels/695664941247627274/1208550345459114004/1208934895242444850)
- [Example #3](https://discord.com/channels/695664941247627274/1180651714081198100/1186063011643920435)
- [Example #4](https://discord.com/channels/695664941247627274/1175854712873041981/1177006198801842206)

> Reader discretion is advised when reading this as level -7 has strong themes of gore

When creating a content warning, you should make sure it's easily discernible from the page itself so that the average reader doesn't just skim past it thinking it's the regular "This is Level blank of the Backrooms…" If you want a clear way to demonstrate this, you should look towards the site's [Wikidot Syntax](wikidot-syntax.md) guide, as it provides a lot of good tricks you could add to your draft to make certain parts more obvious. I would personally recommend using a block quote.

> only of a house with 3 stories. The exact size of these stories are

To avoid repetition of the word stories (which may sound odd when used too often within a short period) I would replace the second use of the word with "floor".

> "hallways" of the level are known to change but it is approximately 1km².

Using specific systematic terms like "1km²" doesn't really benefit you much in this draft. I would suggest that you keep a level of vagueness within your page so that you don't over explain things. This is not the SCP Wikidot, so it isn't wise to write like it is.

> and the hallways physical

Remember to make "hallways" a possessive term using an apostrophe.

> rooms that appear to be from a house complex.

The Backrooms consist of a lot of urban structures, but saying that they "appear to be from" implies that it has some parallelism to the real world. I don't believe this to be the case, so would instead say something like "rooms that form an indoor housing complex."

> around a singular room also known as the dining room.

The word "also" doesn't quite fit here, so I would remove it. To avoid awkward repetition, I would change the first use of "room" to the word "location".

> **AVOID THE DINING ROOM AT ALL COSTS.**

There are a lot subtler ways of hinting that a part of your level is dangerous without saying so in bolded, strike-throughed text. I think it would do you well to try a more simple approach at things here, as this really stands out. Something as simple as, "Even though this level may lead you towards its center when going through the hallways in a linear manner, you should avoid doing so as much as possible. John's dinner is murderous." could communicate that effectively.

> There are NO bases, outposts or communities on this level and living here is extremely looked against as it is almost certain you will get caught.

If there are no outposts here, you don't need to dedicate an entire header to communicate that information. You can just bring this up in a stray sentence, or entirely commit it since it's a pretty obvious thing to assume.

> « Level -7 | Level -7 | Level -7 »

I'll assume this was done for a "unique" effect, but if it wasn't, make sure to correct this and properly list the levels.

> It takes the appearance of…

Generally, this phrasing is really unclear a lot of the time. If the level you're writing is just a set of hallways, say it's that, not that "it looks like it's a set of hallways." It's redundant and not concise.

> rooms found in level -7 are that they

Whenever you have the word entity/object/level/phenomenon followed by a numerical value, you need to capitalize it. You would say, **"This level, Level 852, is pure hell."** but you wouldn't say **"This Level, level 852, is pure hell."** I'll ignore this issue throughout the rest of the work assuming that you work on it.

> they are shrouded in dark mist but the level itself is not dark.

This is probably a good time to bring this up: there are a lot of little grammatical errors in your draft that can easily be fixed if you put it through a spell checker. ([Quill Bot](https://quillbot.com/grammar-check) is my favorite for this.) You need to add a comma after "mist" because "the level itself is not dark" is an independent clause that you tacked onto another of its kind, so it needs a conjoining term. If you have a lot of these grammar errors riddled throughout your page, critics will be less likely to look at it, so you should aim to fix as many as you can before you seek them out. You are not above a spell-checker; everyone is better with them. I'll be ignoring all the non-obvious grammatical errors going forward.

> The darkness is real here. I CAN FEEL IT.

A lot of the times, strike through text in pages is rarely used well, and it falls into the pit of being very cliché when added. I'll come back to this at the end of the draft and see if I carry the same feelings towards it.

> This physical lack of light

Just use "darkness" for brevity.

> usually floats around the basement

If you said "usually floats in the air of the basement" you would be clarifying things a lot more.

> the walls but whatever you do

A comma is needed before "but".

> as it will alert "Johnny" (the entity that inhabits this level) to your location.

I'd prefer if you said "as it will alert the central entity of this level to your location, known as Johnny." as it removes the need for the parentheses, but that may just be a personal want.

> Johnny has been known to start

You can just replace has been with "will" since this is a confirmed thing.

> sprinting at an inhuman like pace to your location.

Is it "inhuman-like" or just inhuman? If it's the latter, then you can just say: "at an inhuman speed towards your location."

> The sound itself, however is more like strange,

Remove ", however" as it is not needed.

> in an unnatural and liminal way.

You can't really just attach the word "liminal" to describe things and expect it to make sense. That word is typically used to describe locations; just saying unnatural will suffice.

> Presumably a photo of the level

I would add punctuation here, even if it isn't traditionally a full sentence. Most captions do the same.

> hallways and rooms in this level like to shift and distort

You can drop the "like to" and just say that they shift and distort.

> Like I mentioned earlier, the hallways

This wording is getting weirdly personal. You can remove "Like I mentioned earlier," and carry on with the sentence as normal.

> you are not looking which makes it extremely

Add a comma after "looking".

> Johnny: Not much is known about

If this is the only entity present in the level, then you don't really need to include "Johnny:" so it would be best to remove it.

> The first 2 floors have three different "zones" but the third floor has only 1 and this zone is known as the "endzone" of the level.  
> The "endzone" is a massive room that spans about a kilometre in all directions and the only thing that has been found within this zone is a white glowing door with a small keyhole in the side and the occasional staircase leading down to floor 2. The rooms in level -7 are empty and plain with only the occasional bed, chair or table scattered around each one (save for the dining room). These furniture items are your only way to hide if Johnny comes so know where they are. Like I mentioned earlier, the hallways and rooms in this level like to shift and distort when you are not looking which makes it extremely difficult to map out the level. The room changing can also come to your advantage when evading Johnny however as it is known for the levels to change around him. Do not lower your guard though as the room changes can also cause you to run into him.

I'll be ignoring the rest of the entity section for the next couple reasons. This is feeling video game-y in a really negative way. You're really forgoing good storytelling and opting to style your draft over what you might see if you watched a "Backrooms Youtube Shorts" video. It's gimmicky and it lacks any depth. This is a writing site, so you're expected to hammer in your intended emotional objectives, like making something horror-oriented, which is what I assume this draft is. If you want to tell something scary and evocative, this isn't how you do it. Focus on why you're writing about what you're writing about; why is Johnny the focal point of this page? If you can get to the root of why you're writing this, then you can hone in on specific things you want to accomplish instead of leaning towards what the average person thinks a Backrooms page is. I would really suggest that you drop the different sections of the level (as they are now, at least, because of how flat they are presented) and expand on what you're trying to tell the reader with your use of Johnny. Create a unique creature (one that isn't solely written based off an image) and then base your level around it, catering to its flaws and strengths. As it is now, this level is a maze with SCP-096 thrown into it.

> ~ [[\*HaloIsNotMyName]]

Add "user" and a space after the star symbol.
