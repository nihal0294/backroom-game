Sorry for the wait!! I have done a full SPaG, format and concept critique. I will go through each section in that order. But before we start I thought I’d mention that there’s some exposed CSS at the top of the page.

Anyways, enough CSS and into SPaG

“escape that hell-hole alive.” - “hell-hole” doesn’t need a hyphen and should be one word.

“I know ya'll want to document every single bit of every level to help others, but I recommend to protect the lives of those in your organization to leave this place alone. - A few things here. Number one: the first half of this sentence (“I” to “others”) is quite rough-worded. My first few times reading this I kept tripping over words (could’ve just been my dumbass being unable to read after a long day). I feel it could have something to do with the repetition of “every” (I am aware the repetition was used for effect). Number two: The second half of the sentence (everything not mentioned in the first half) is missing two commas, one before “to protect” and another after “organisation”. Why do these need to be here? To mark a subordinate clause. Everything between the two commas could be taken out of the sentence and the sentence would still make sense. However, if the in-comma section was left on its own it wouldn’t make sense, classing it as a subordinate clause.

“see any lit up sections” - “lit up” should be hyphenated.

“school, head towards it.” - Dependant upon how forceful you’re being with the imperative “head towards it”, there should be an exclamation mark if it is truly crucial to go towards the light. If it is just some advice, it should remain as a period.

“thus the name it is addressed by non-M.E.G.” - This sentence looses the majority of the articles flow, due to it being hard to read. I would rephrase it to something similar to ‘thus the name given by non-M.E.G.’.

“It contains little useful resources” - The quantifier ‘few’ would be more appropriate than “little” here.

“some of which have been undocumented” - The article up until now has been in present tense. “have been” is past tense. I would recommend changing it to either ‘which are undocumented’ or ‘which have been left undocumented’. My personal preference being the first.

“However, sometimes one may come across gyms and cafeterias.” - Using the word ‘occasionally’ would sound more professional compared to “sometimes”, despite “sometimes” still being technically correct.

“chipped paint on the walls,” - The phrase “on the walls” is unnecessary here and should be cut.

“from various periods of time back on Earth.” - The phrase “periods of time” should be shortened to just “periods” or you could alternatively say ‘eras’ instead of the phrase “periods of time”.

“with them upon entering the level.” - “Upon entering this level” should be cut from here as it has already been used earlier on within the same sentence.

“feeling traumitized, although experiences” - Misspelling of “traumatised”.

“always bad enought to account” - Misspelling of “enough”.

“for the amount of emotional” - A more appropriate quantifier to use here would be ‘number’ rather than “amount”.

“A study is on-going on the” - “ongoing” is one word without a hyphen.

“than another one, it would” - “one” isn’t needed here and should be cut.

“Hallways have yellow-cream colored walls,” - “yellow-cream coloured” needs a second hyphen between “cream” and “coloured”.

“Nothing of value has been found as of yet in these lockers, but they can be used to hide in when being chased by an entity, who due to their lack of intelligence, may not be able to open one once closed.” - This sentence is unnecessarily lengthy. I would split it in two at the point where the concept changes from being what’s inside the lockers, to hiding in the lockers.

“are green-painted brick-walled rooms with chalk boards” - Two things: number one, after “green-painted” there should be a comma. Secondly, “chalkboards” should be one word.

“and are often accessibly through large” - Misspelling of the word “accessible”.

“Old-fashioned desks are scattered throughout them, partially bolted to the ground, which is wooden in classrooms.” - Not really sure what was meant by the end of this sentence. Please reword it.

“and crushed soda bottles that do not have labels.” - This part takes away from the flow of the article. I would recommend a reword to something such as ‘and crushed soda bottles without labels.’ as “that do not have labels” is *clunky*, if you will.

> I’ve decided to add a little mini section here to discuss the SPaG and concept cross within this sentence.
>
> > “Nothing of use can normally be found in these gyms, but can be a place to circle around certain entities and slow them down if being chased.”
>
> SPaG: I’m not a massive fan of the wording here. The phrase “circle around” sounds like you’re trying to escape an enemy in a Roblox game. I would reword it to ‘manoeuvre around’ or ‘loop’. The first being my preference.  
>   
> In terms of concept, how would a gym slow down an entity. Gyms are open spaces made for running around and playing sports in, hence I do not understand how it would slow oneself. Unless you’re referring to treadmill and weight gyms, but previously stated, it were said the gyms are “open spaces” and they “have bleachers”.

“Cafeterias are more likely to be smaller than Gyms and have bench-tables to eat at.” - Three things here. One: “more” makes the documenter seem uncertain. I would cut this word. Two: “Gyms” should not be capitalised. Three: “bench tables” are two separate words and do not need a hyphen.

“other areas in the level.” - Based on what multiple other critics have told me: “*You are not “in” a level, you are “on” it.*“. “in” should be “on”.

“But if one is lucky, there may be vending machines in the cafeteria that will include bottles of almond water inside them which are retrievable by breaking into them.” - this sentence contradicts being unable to get consumables as previously stated. Please reword this paragraph to avoid contradiction. Also, this sentence is relatively lengthy. Please try to shorten it.

“level presents is the lack” - “is” should be “are” due to their needing to be a plural verb form (“is” is singular).

“lack of a provided source of light, … One should, if they are aware they are about to enter the level, make sure to have a carryable source of artificial light. Having said light is crucial, as most entities in the level will shy away from light, due to them not being used to it.” - Three issues here (two of which are format issues that will be mentioned later). “if they are aware they are about to enter the level” I would move this sentence fragment to the start or end of the sentence, rather than embedding it as you would with a subordinate clause.

“These creatures stay in rooms and do not normally exit these rooms unless they detect potential prey.” - This sentence is yet again *clunky*, if you will. I would reword it to be sweet, short and simple. Something similar to this ‘These creatures stay in rooms until they detect potential prey.’

“As said above, there are little resources” - As said above, ‘few’ would be a more appropriate quantifier than “little”.

“to be found in this level” - “in” should be ‘on’.

“working chromebooks with wi-fi accesscan be found.” - A couple mistakes here. “Chromebooks” should be capitalised as it is a proper noun. There should be a space between “access” and “can”.

“Safe almond water to drink can” - It should be ‘for drinking’ rather than “to drink”.

“recommended one stocks up on” - “stocks” shouldn’t be plural.

“almond water bottles due to their portability if one finds a cafeteria.” - “Almond Water” needs to be capitalised, “if one finds a cafeteria.” is unnecessary and should be cut.

“as their names imply, to show where a wanderer has already been.” - I would break this up a bit more. This section seems quite rushed, with all the ideas quickly crammed in (sorry if this sounds a bit harsh, I’m just trying to help). I would go back and refine the resources section. For this sentence in particular you should show the usage as its own thing, in its own sentence as the name doesn’t imply the use as the statement suggests.

“for an exit of the level.” - “of” should be ‘from’.

“There is a possibily of finding a chromebook cart somewhere in Level 6 with a large amount of undamaged chromebooks” - A few issues here. One: “possibly” should be ‘possibility’. Two: both instances of “Chromebook” should be capitalised. Three: ‘number’ would be a more appropriate quantifier than “amount”.

“chromebooks inside them that will already be logged into.” - “Chromebooks” needs a capital ‘C’. After “inside” put a period, and remove “them”. Reword what’s left to ‘That are already logged on’.

“speculated there are multiple chromebook carts” - Capitalise “Chromebook”.

“theory hasn't been proved.” - “proved” should be ‘proven’.

“the level and do not have” - “and” should be ‘that’.

“For instance, there have been reports of shadowy figures of people with blue lights flashing faintly in them that hover throughout the level, simply observing, and of strange, box-like creatures that can sometimes be found in the corners of rooms or in lockers to ambush people exploring the level and trap them inside it.” - This sentence is far two long, needing to be broken up for multiple reasons, including length and rushed description. Go more into detail with the descriptions instead of skipping through, missing commas and adding multiple ways to interpret the entities due to clear lack of separation within the description. I would go back and remake these brief descriptions of entities. Making each one flow well, well punctuated and with set interpretation.

“While these reports are unconfimed,” - Misspelling of “unconfirmed”.

“infested with entiites or has” - Misspelling of “entities”. I would also add a comma before “or”.

“through traveling dwelving deep into” - Two things. There is a misspelling of “delving”. Also delving isn’t needed here and should be cut.

“the level were in.” - This should be ‘the level they were in.’

“Going to deep in The Ocean” - The wrong “to” has been used. It should be ‘too’.

“downward in the water,” - “In the water” isn’t needed as we know that if you’re in the ocean of level 7 you are in water. This phrase should therefore be cut.

“gym of this level.” - “of this level.” isn’t needed as we know you’re referring to this level.

“Sublevel of Darkness” - Everything here should be lowercase.

“It is possible to chance upon a janitor's broom leaning up against a door marked simply "Utility".” - Please reword this, I don’t understand what is meant by this sentence.

“the Piped-lined Halls” - All of this needs to be capitalised or lowercase, not just some one, some the other.

“chromebook and pressing the last closed page will not lead to said page; instead, it will lead to a google search for "Vacation".” - Three things here. “Chromebook” and “Google” should be capitalised. “Vacation” should be in quotation marks rather than speech marks.

“Some of said blood has been tested and is found to not belong to humans and the age of which has ranged from being a few months to several years old. Why it has not dried is unknown.” - The “and” after “humans” should be replaced with a period. A better way to word “the age of which has ranged from being a few months to several years old.” would be ‘the blood’s age ranges from being a few months to several years old.’

Moving onto format!!

“lack of a provided source of light, … One should, if they are aware they are about to enter the level, make sure to have a carryable source of artificial light. Having said light is crucial, as most entities in the level will shy away from light, due to them not being used to it.” - All of this is stated in a previous section. To avoid repeating yourself I would reformat so there is only one instance. Also, try to be consistent with paragraph spacing. After this paragraph the spacing between the next differs to the rest of the article.

“Some are unique to the level, while others are present throughout the Backrooms, such as Hounds.” - Make clear what entities do and don’t have documents. This section could come off as hounds being undocumented.

For the “Entrances and Exits” section, rather than using bullet points for one, use them for both or neither.

For the “credits” section, and the “authors” section both of the collapsible tab titles go off the page on mobile, causing issues including: being extremely zoomed out and the page jolting up and down.

For the “credits” section I would still use a license box otherwise you’re creating more work for the staff helpers and staff members adding license boxes to every other page. I also noticed you stated you don’t know what it should be licensed under, the license should be “**CC-BY-SA 3.0**”. If the images aren’t under this license you will need to find new images to avoid CC strikes.

Finally concept!!

The level concept is good but can be expanded upon.

To start with the positives, I like this spin on level six. It adds a lot more to what would normally be an empty level. I also enjoy the work of the desolation canon.

Onto improvements: I noticed there is an exit using the Chromebooks. Perhaps go more into detail about that in the section or at-least mention it. I would also like to see a report on how such a long winded, low chance of finding with no guidance type exit was found. Maybe a M.E.G. log or a BNTG report, maybe even a forum post by whoever found the exit to fit with the canons theme and the post at the top of the page.

Another improvement would be the entity descriptions. I feel these were rushed and could’ve had more detail and greater concepts than a box and yet another humanoid entity. Please refine this section as I already stated it was missing a lot of punctuation.

Sorry if I missed a few things. I spent over an hour writing up critique only for my google to crash and wipe it (I died a little inside lol). Overall I think this is a solid article concept, there’s room for improvement and I’d be willing to do a second revision. Anyways that’s all from me, BlockHead out, cya!!
