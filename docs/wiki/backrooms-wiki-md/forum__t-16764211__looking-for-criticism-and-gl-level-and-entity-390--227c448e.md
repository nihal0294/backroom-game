My response to this is coming from you asking me to help you with the formatting and licensing of the draft, so I'll be assisting you with just that. If you would like me to touch on other topics, like conceptual or grammatical flaws, please don't hesitate to let me know.

**If you've created artwork for your level, you can take many different routes in terms of how you go about licensing it.** First off, this website works off a Creative Commons license system. This means that any image used on a page **cannot be copyrighted**, meaning that it cannot have exclusive use. If you make an image for the page, you are acknowledging that it may possibly be used elsewhere. However, how you get credited and cited for its creation is where you get a lot of freedom. All you have to do to actually apply a specific license to your image is just state the license next to your image somewhere. Here is a list of licenses that, when used, allow others to replicate, alter, and use your image without crediting you:

- [Public Domain](https://fairuse.stanford.edu/overview/public-domain/welcome/#:~:text=The%20term%20%E2%80%9Cpublic%20domain%E2%80%9D%20refers,%2C%20trademark%2C%20or%20patent%20laws.)
- [Free Art License](https://artlibre.org/licence/lal/en/)
- [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/public-domain/cc0/)

The following is a list of images that can be used and altered by others, however, you will be credited whenever it is used elsewhere:

- [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/deed.en)
- [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/deed.en)
- [CC BY-SA 2.5](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.5/deed.en)
- [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0/deed.en)

As said in the Image Policy guide, "You may use original artwork or photography, but you must attribute it to yourself, as well as release it under CC BY-SA 3.0. This is just as simple as writing in your attribution that you are releasing it under CC BY-SA 3.0. You do not have to provide any additional proof that the image was created by you. Note: If you take a photo of a copyrighted piece of artwork or media, this cannot be used as it counts as a derivative." This is pretty clear in terms of what you can do with your artwork, so I don't think I need to elaborate on it any further.

**The formatting of the draft is largely fine**, but there are some minor things I would like to touch on.

- The image used in the Survival Difficulty class looks really unpolished and low quality, but I'll assume this is just a placeholder. If it's not, find a replacement image as soon as possible.
- "People cannot make contact…" Linking Entity 1 is really odd here, unless there's some greater reason for it.
- The collapsibles are entirely unnecessary. I would personally remove all instances of them. For the "endings" section, you can just use centered (or normal) headers to title each one of them.
- The use of emojis and wing dings (EX: 👌︎♋︎♍︎🙵❒︎□︎□︎❍︎⬧︎ ⚐︎💧︎ 📄︎📂︎) can be really abrasive to those who use screen readers and will make it very difficult for them to read your page. Due to accessibility concerns, I would remove these.

That's all in terms of helping you with formatting. The level itself looks fine (appearance-wise) so I don't need to harp on that much. After fixing the aforementioned issues, this should be fine on that front. Again, **I won't be helping you with any other type of critique because you didn't request it.** If you need those services from me, please let me know in the future.

The issue isn't it not being translatable. If an individual with a visual impairment or an inability to read effectively tries to use a text-to-speech add-on to read your level, they'll reach parts of it where the screen-reader just says: okay hand sign, cancer zodiac sign, scorpio zodiac sign, etc… which will be very annoying for them. It's obviously not an enforceable thing, but it would be great if you could make sure everyone is able to view your work.

**I'll be honest and say: I didn't read Entity 390.** However, that should be a fine inclusion. In-universe, the second cluster (and all that comes after it) exists, they may just not all be cataloged yet. But if you really want to emphasize that she comes from an unknown location, then you shouldn't just try and explain it away as her being from Cluster II. Give the reader some red herrings that convince them that she doesn't come from where she says she does, such as in the FAQ section of the draft.

As for the table of contents, **it can be a good inclusion, but don't go overboard with it.** If you have 30 headings that the table of contents link to, then it'll look very obtuse and long giving your draft an unpolished appearance. If you take this approach, have around ~6-8 headings at most.

Also, unrelated to all of that, I forgot to actually provide you the Image Policy guide. Here it is below:

- [https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/image-use-policy](image-use-policy.md)

This level is certainly unique. I haven't seen many people attempt this take on gamifying the Backrooms. There was [Level 191](level-191.md), which covered shooters, but this page is doing an RPG. In particular, I liked the opening line. It acts as a better hook than a lot of levels (and is a bit cheeky).

There are some things that I do have issues with though:

1. **The theme is unreadable for me.** The yellow on purple is hard to read, which means your page is hard to read, which means most people are going to downvote your page because it's unreadable. It's cute, but form must follow function. I recommend checking the contrast here: <https://coolors.co/contrast-checker/112a46-acc8e5> and adjusting things so it's easier on the eyes.
2. **There is *a lot* to get through.** There are at least 8 characters, 4 endings, 20 locations, 4 objects, and countless mechanics. That's at least 40 different things the page introduces to the reader. When writing shortform fiction (and by that, I mean things that are 10,000 words or shorter), there should probably be 3 or four.
3. **This page is not a story.** It's an infodump. On this site, pages don't have to follow the traditional narrative format of prose with dialogue, but there should still be a story: rising tension, climax, resolution. In terms of tension, this page has the same amount throughout the page. I lost interest in reading around… paragraph 4? There's nothing in this page to keep my attention once I get past the first paragraphs.

Overall, the biggest issue with this page is that there is too much information and no focus. My advice would be to answer this question: What is the Backrooms story you want to tell with this page? Remember, we're moving away from, "Place That is Weird," so you may have to adjust your draft a bit.

First off, I recommend using forum notifications here: [http://notifications.wikidot.com/](index.md). It'll make your life a lot easier on the forums.

Secondly, it is an unfortunate truth that during critique, you're going to get contradicting opinions about your piece. People are going to bring different ideas to your piece, and your job is to filter through and figure out what is and what isn't useful to you. If my advice isn't useful to you, I'm not offended if it's not used.

That being said, I'll probably just repeat the question I said earlier: what is the story you want to tell? The current format may be fine for others, but it didn't do it for me because I didn't feel any emotional connection. I would like to see an overarching theme to the page. I don't see one at the moment. It's just a game guide, which by design, does not lend itself to themes, narratives, tension, or anything of the like.

I would suggest this:

1. Cut down the amount of "things" I have to remember. Focus on maybe just six.  
2. Weave a story through the page. It may be a game guide, but beyond that, what else does it have? What greater point do you want to make about the Backrooms? Why does this page exist? Why is it written as a game guide and not a different format?
