Not very believable

22 Apr 2022 02:21

I'm not sure if I'm missing something, but even thought I like a lot of this, the main premise just doesn't feel very believable to me. This ritual that has devastating effects on the entirety of the backrooms consists of mixing almond water with cashew water? There isn't even any intent or verbal components involved, it's just mixing (presumably) any amount of the two substances to completely shake all of reality.

Almond water is like, the single most common liquid in the backrooms, just behind *actual water*, maybe even ahead of it in some areas. I know cashew water is significantly rarer, but almost everyone who *does* happen to come across it is almost definitely going to be carrying almond water with them. Then all it takes is for them to accidentally mix a single drop into that cashew water and this big deal doomsday omen ritual is just completed. So how exactly had this ritual not been done by complete accident decades or centuries before The M.E.G even existed?

I mean, this article treats Cashew Water like it's no big deal, which its own article confirms. And to top it off, the article for Level 178.1 literally tell you to throw bottles of almond water into springs to convert it into cashew water. The fact that somehow nobody accidentally mixed almond and cashew water during that process is less than unbelievable, I would expect this ritual to be performed every time someone bottles cashew water.

You want to tell me you have to combine almond/cashew water with something like (the late) oracle where the substance is rare, coveted, and almost entirely controlled by The M.E.G? That I could believe, but cashew water is worth less than dirt most of the time, and the process for creating it literally involves throwing almond water around to transmute it. For this to be remotely believable I'd have to believe that this article takes place in an alternate canon where literally nobody (except maybe the lost) lives in Level 178 or 178.1.

To fix this all you'd have to really do is add a tiny thing that states you have to intend to perform the ritual, or that you have to say a certain thing or something. As it stands, I feel obligated to downvote, but there's enough here that I like that it barely saves it for me, even though the article doesn't really grab me in any meaningful way.
