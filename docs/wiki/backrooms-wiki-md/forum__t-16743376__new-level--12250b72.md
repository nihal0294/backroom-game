The biggest issue with this draft is **formatting**. You may notice that your draft doesn't look very similar to pages posted onto the main Wikidot site, and this is not just an issue with the sandbox website, but instead how you laid things out. Down below I'll list some issues that deal with formatting and how you can make your draft look more appealing. Beyond that, you have a largely coherent piece, but you should try and work on how you describe things. You can't sum up the entirety of an alien planet's physical features in just a few paragraphs, yet you do just that and move onto talk of the civilizations and the entities and societal constructs.

The easy fix here would be for me to tell you to "lower your scope" and try to handle an easier to write concept, which wouldn't be the worst advice. However, if you do want to pursue this concept, you should keep in mind that **writing too much is always better than writing too little**. As it stands, I don't have a clear enough picture of the level by the time you start to move onto bigger things, so add a lot more to the physical description.

> The charted region is 'The Continent' which is made up of 6 geographical regions as listed below:  
> 1. The Moutains  
> 2. The Valley  
> 3. The Plains  
> 4. The Desert  
> 5. The Forest  
> 6. The Coast

This way of organizing things stretches out the text a lot vertically which looks odd; this style of listing things out isn't really needed, you could just say something like,

> The charted region, widely recognized as "The Continent," comprises six distinct geological regions, each labeled according to its most prominent features: mountain range, valley, plains, desert, forest, and coast.

---

> The Continent is huge in area, about 2 million km².  
> Further, the time on this planet is a little different.

Don't space out these two lines; it makes it look weird.

---

> The uncharted territory is beyond the Coast and is across the sea.

Saying "the coast" or "the valley' doesn't make it a proper noun even if it's an established section of your level, so don't get into the habit of capitalizing the terms.

---

> 1. The Town: The Town is the first place that a wanderer usually spawns in. It consists of 5000 members and is spread in an area of 1500km² with 6 districts. Each wanderer is allotted a cottage and shares it with 3 other people. The wanderer must also choose a responsibility that he wishes to perform within The Town, if he wishes to stay. The responsibilities are: Craftsmaship, Records, Defense, Farming, Hunting, Quarrying, Lumbering, Storage and Communication. The Town has a temperate climate with warm summers (about 20°C) and cold winters (about -10°C). The precipitation includes rain in summers, amd snow in winters. Hail is rare and the total annual precipitation is a comfortable 150mm. This is because The Town is located in the most habitable region of The Continent, i.e- The Valley region. The town has no system of currency and is leaderless, yet well functioning. The Town is built with walls around that also serve for defense. Transport and communication within The Town is made using the help of 'Nelvun' entities that resemble deer-horse hybrids, and foot trails have been made all through The Town with woods around. The Town also celebrates festivals like Christmas, where all except defenders gather in the Town Center for merrymaking. The Town uses oil fat lamps to light the nights. The Town is also home to the bigger of the two M.E.G bases of The Continent. The districts are as mentioned below:

I think it's pretty easy to see how oppressively this is structured; it's a huge block of text and you fail to use the "numbered list"[1](javascript:;) correctly. Try both breaking this up into two or three paragraphs, and see if you can write this section without actively lining out sections of your text with a header or other identifier. If you can describe these areas with smooth and continuous writing it would do a lot in terms of readability. As a take-a-way, **don't rely on headers**. It won't take your writing from not transitioning well. (Apply this feedback to anything else mentioned in a numbered list. It's a bad practice that won't help you develop pacing and flow.)

Conclusion:

Overall, this is obviously a very flawed piece as a result of several issues. Going forward[2](javascript:;), seek out your critique in this order: formatting, conceptual, grammatical help. Doing it like this will help you a lot, as you'll be able to focus on broader issues easily without having your feedback be scattered.
