Great image and I really wanted to like this page, but several things don't feel right to me personally:

- "Wanderer" has long been adopted as a simple moniker for "humans in the Backrooms." In universe, it'd be illogical to name a whole entity species the exact same way. Like imagine if the Wretches were called Humanns and you'd have to specify verbally "Oh I saw a group of Humanns, aka the zombie entities, aka spelled with 2 n's" lol
- The capitalization dinstinction works exclusively on text, as long as it's not in all caps or starting a sentence, but *not at all* in spoken language where it would be even more important to be able to clearly distinguish what you're talking about. This is a MEG article, aren't they supposed to ensure minimal confusion in their catalogs?
- Must drink almond water to survive -> but that will eventually turn you into an inhuman entity -> is the message here that you either die a human or live on as something else? Solitary, selfish, emotionally stunted = not human? Idk, to me that's just another side of humanity, born out of a survival instinct when facing a foreign environment.
- This seems more fitting as a Phenomenon, as it *transforms* someone by drinking Almond Water over a potentially looong period of time, but still retains most human properties (I'm thinking of the Infinite Wanderer)

anyway that's just my opinion😭

2:00 am binge-read session

11 Feb 2025 18:49

I love the tone and world-building implications here, but I have a few issues with the presentation.

The elements I liked:

- I like how the article seems to take jabs at how humans acted in interview logs spawning from the early writing of the site. From the trade of one bottle of almond water for information, to the standing aside and watching someone die, this page cleverly takes inspiration from familiar tropes and spins a believable in-universe explanation for their existence.
- The removal of the inevitability of becoming a Wanderer. This adds a lot of sweet tension to the latter half of the page, as the M.E.G. scrambles to find an alternative to their cure-all food source. It feels like both a pressing conflict at the climax of this article, and an invitation for others to fill out that part of the lore. The previous iteration also had the issue of removing the agency of the wanderers who became Wanderers[1](javascript:;), which this page thankfully remedies by making it avoidable, albeit difficult.
- How subtle the transformation into a Wanderer is. Everything behavior-wise is left unclear; it's not actually that far off from the baseline of ordinary human selfishness and snapping under the pressure of the situation. You could go halfway through the article and still question whether the condition exists or not, which I think plays to its strengths? The uncertainty benefits the slow build of dread as you reveal that most people become Wanderers in a decade. The only definite thing biology-wise are the alterations to the eyes, which, I would've preferred if it was also left ambiguous (like the eyes of Wanderers being more susceptible to reflect weirdly under flashing cameras, i.e. the red-eye effect). But that's neither here nor there.

The elements I disliked:

- Wanderers. Seriously, imagine shouting, "I saw Wanderers around [X] level!/Oh my god, is that a Wanderer?/I got attacked by a Wanderer!" I would strongly advise a similar alternative like Wandering Syndrome (with people affected being referred to as The Wandering or something). Also, consider translations of this page into languages without capitalization.
- The interview feels a bit corny and falls flat because we actually don't know Doctor Daniels prior to his transformation. In my opinion, it's like killing off a character in the very log they appear. The reader doesn't get the time to bond with the man Daniels was prior to getting afflicted with the phenomenon and starting to believe generic Wanderer stuff.
- And speaking of which, some people above mentioned that this article might work better in the phenomenon category. I don't know if I agree with that. On one hand, wretches, another phenomenon that converts humans into entities, is listed under the entity category. On the other, wretches are indisputably inhuman monsters, while Wanderers reflect a preexisting side of humanity forced out in extreme circumstances. So ultimately, I believe the choice is up to you, and what aspects of the concept you would like to highlight for the reader.

Overall, I'm rating this a -1[2](javascript:;), on the verge of a +1.
