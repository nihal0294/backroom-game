***New York City***, January 7th 2000

---

*Shove, push, sway, thud.*

You know, for a "new millennium", shit is exactly the same. It's cold, grey and miserable. And y'know what? So are the damn people. I can vaguely remember New Years, drinking gallons upon gallons of spiked punch at the work get-together I sat at the corner of, Truman timing that godawful Doctor Who movie so its New Years celebrations matched up with ours, and the gradually worsening smell of sick and cigarettes. It wasn't fantastic, but it left me optimistic that this year would be different.

Boy was I fucking wrong.

Seven days in, and I already want to kill myself, and I'm clearly not the only person who is already sick of the new year.

*Some* people would tell you, "Oh, the new century doesn't actually start until next year" like a nerd, but I don't think 2001 would be any better for New York, let alone anywhere else.

After a long time standing on my sore-ass feet and trying to keep away from the people 500 times my size at each stop, the train came to my station. With haste, I stepped out before anybody else could. I think I nudged some child out of the way on accident, but *really* if you're bringing a little boy on the NY Overground during weekday rush hour that's entirely on you. Making sure to not make a fool of myself, I took the stairs to the street level slowly, making sure not to slip. And I didn't! So how about that?

Once out of Harlem 125th Street, I was right onto the road, glancing at the sights around me. It was the same as the rest of Manhattan, but considering I live just east of the Hudson River in Riverdale, it was a fair bit different from where my house is. People also bother to grit the roads and pavements, so I don't have to spend every single step trying not to fall on my ass.

It was clear Harlem was changing; the shops had new owners monthly, and previously abandoned locations were now thriving with shops. I *do* feel a little bad about it. It's pretty clear that a lot of this "improvement" is just… gentrification. White people moving into a predominantly black community, usurping it with ~sudden funding~ and making things *technically* better from a capitalist point of view, but also a lot worse for the people who actually live here.

Part of me feels bad about perpetuating that, since, y'know, pale white guy, but also I *really* need my job. Riverdale taxes aren't cheap. Part of me could ponder about how willing I am to let my ethics slide for personal benefit, but I know if I did that right now, I'd miss the turning for the office. Perhaps that *also* feeds into my liquidy morals, but perhaps I really just don't want to be fucking fired.

I stepped through the door of *BoringCorp*, for that is definitely its real name, and switched my brain off. With my mind thoroughly ready for the final working day of the week, I brushed my feet of sleet and strolled through the empty foyer, casting a side-eye at the company logo on one of the walls next to a plant that no one had bothered to water or throw out.

On my way to the lift, I flashed my ID card at "Becky Sands", the woman who worked behind the desk. It still surprises me just how much *everyone's* name sounds like it was ripped from a sitcom or other type of comedy about an office. As much as I'd watch something like that, it really makes me feel like I'm perpetually stuck in a stereotypical hell. Which, hey, I wouldn't be surprised.

The elevator chimed as it hit my floor, and I stepped into the office space. As much as I wanted to turn off more of my brain to deal with the next 8 hours of phone-call-based hell, I think I'd literally die or turn into a lobotomy patient, and I'm not at that level of desperation. Not yet.

"Hey." Truman called from his office cubicle, his eyes momentarily meeting mine. He had his cubicle phone to his ear, so it took me a moment to realise he was talking to *me* and not the idiot on the other end of the line.

"Hey." I said back, almost in the exact same tone as his. Ironically, despite his name correlating to that movie I wish I got to see in theatres, I swear he has *no* self-awareness of anything going on whatsoever. I've listened to my idiotic and kinda racist college friends ramble about the conspiracy theory on "FaKe PeOpLe", or whatever. I don't believe in it, but I can *absolutely* make an exception for Truman. I've always wanted to toss something at his head and see if he'd notice, but also I don't want to be written up by HR if he *did* react. Whether or not his reaction would be because he's not on autopilot or because I finally awoke him from it is a whole different thing. Man, I'm sounding too much like my old friends.

I got a "hi" and two "yo's" from Mirabel, Jacob, and Robbie respectively, but nothing more. I swear, everyone here is as idiotic as Truman in this shithole. I mean, it's a well-built shithole; we haven't had a heating failure in at least 8 months (new record, probably), but it was still a shithole. A fact I was all too aware of when I sat down and stared at what would be the next 8 hours of my life.

---

The first three hours went alright. It was soul-crushingly boring. I'm glad I switched off for it, but at least it passed relatively quickly until lunchtime. I got up and subtly stretched, not really wanting to draw too much attention to myself, and sauntered over to the break room in a disorganised bundle with the other fellow idiots of the office.

While some people queued up at the microwaves to reheat food or sat around the tables discussing whatever was happening in the world of football, I stayed off to the side drinking coffee and leaning on the vending machine. *Some* people, though I won't name names, decide it's a good idea to stink up the place with tuna sandwiches (I chose to stick to just ham). Some of us can read a room, Grant.

As I ate, staring across at the other co-workers, my mind wandered. Bad thing to do at work, I know, but with nothing interesting going on, I can't help it. I glanced at the people talking to each other, and despite the fact it sounded pretty meandering and basic, I… well, I don't know, I felt a little envious. Not of the conversation itself; I'd rather kill myself than talk about the weather or whatever, but just talking to someone actually seems alright. I do miss having friends, and I miss having *good* friends even harder than that. Not had those since graduating college in '97.

Perhaps I could enjoy talking about the weather if it meant I got a little bit of social interaction. Perhaps that's what they do. Are *they* as switched-off as I am? I mean, sure, they have lives outside of work, but I always assumed they were as dull as I see them now. Though even if they are as dull as dishwater 100% of the time, just talking to them about anything may be nice.

Do I approach them *now*? Well, immediate instinct says "yes!" all written in a massive sign circled with blinking lights like the Vegas strip or a crappy motel. My body, rather annoyingly, refuses to move.

Then the clock struck, my phone buzzed against my thigh, and suddenly break was over. People were filtering out, and I was stuck just watching them. Honestly, I'm a little dumbfounded at what actually happened just now. Perhaps I haven't put enough of my brain into stasis yet. I'll make short work of that. Complex thoughts and *BoringCorp* (real place) don't go together.

---

I'm not sure what I did, but the next few hours passed… longer. The hours from the start of my shift to lunch are actually *shorter*, but still. It really felt as though I was stuck in a loop of nonstop phone calls. Ironically, the only thing that saved me was my brain refusing to fully turn itself off, the thoughts of my own loneliness permeating through the brief respites between calls.

As our branch of the company I refuse to even call by its proper name all clocks out at the exact same time (and only sociopaths would do overtime on a fucking Friday), I was able to watch everyone progressively file out of our floor, leaving me behind. I know what I just said about the whole sociopath thing, but in my defence, I'm not doing overtime, and I couldn't help it.

I *wanted* to go with my co-workers, at least *one* of the silly bastards, but I stayed completely rooted to the confines of my cubicle. Tracy was the last one to file out, and once the door was closed, I became acutely aware of the humming fluorescent lights above me and the loud sound of the AC. No other sounds were present; no one else was in the building. It was just… me.

I really don't think I've ever felt so alone. I left quickly. At least the outside was filled with sounds so I didn't feel as isolated. Even if the people outside *are* idiots. Heh. Yeah.

***New York City***, January 8th 2000

---

*BEEP. BEEP. BEEP. BEEP. BEEP. BEEP.*

I have no idea why I'm making these sounds at 11:38 in the morning. The alarm already went off hours ago, *and* I already hit it with 20 million hammers to make it shut the fuck up. I mean, come on, it's a Saturday. Why do I still keep alarms going on days I don't have to do anything?

I think it's the alarm's rude awakening that has kept me bedbound this long, and ruminating through my previous list of excuses through the weekends of the 20th Century… This definitely isn't the first time I've made that assumption. That must mean it's true! Hell yeah. Problem solved.

I had wanted to try and get a bit more productive this year, and yeah, if I wanted to be a nerdy little shit, I could cheat 2000 and try to be a bit more productive in 200*1*, but also… no. Life is absolutely *fine* right now, and there are absolutely people who would kill to be able to sit in bed and do nothing on a Saturday. I give my change to some of them on the street, for fuck's sake.

When I realised that part of this anger was because of hunger, that gave me the sudden strength to launch out of bed and make my way to the kitchen. One quick sandwich later and I felt… unfortunately exactly the same. Though *really*, expecting a fucking ham sandwich to completely fix your mood is like expecting a sloppy one-night stand to turn into a blossoming romance and marriage. Not that I'd know, mind you, but still.

After raiding the fridge for a few more things (I somehow hadn't eaten my slice of cake yet from the New Years party, fuck yeah!), and still not feeling *particularly* great, I moved to the couch and turned on the television. Hey, the remote was in an easy-to-find spot, that's a new one. Maybe the universe is trying to give me an easy victory. Isn't that a quaint thought.

I flicked through a few channels, although since I only have Freeview nothing caught my eye. The news is as depressing as ever, "Third Afghan Civil War" this and "a British woman reaches the South Pole for the first time" that. Like, it's stuff that's *probably* interesting, but holy shit, I don't want to be depressed and bored simultaneously. Not any more than I usually am, that is.

After enough idle surfing, the last of my efforts gave way, and I stopped on some rerun of a football match from the previous year. Wishing to watch literally anything else, my thumb gave one last jerk and spasm to swap to an infomercial network, which was at least a little better. Not that I'd ever buy whatever "Quick‘n Brite" is, but it was better than watching dummies kill themselves for a British rugby ball. They don't even *use* their feet for fuck's sake.

I began to zone out. Hell, I probably took a power nap to show that alarm clock it doesn't control me. When I was awake, I decided to have a look at my phone. Not much was really happening with it/ Really, the handheld versions are pretty pointless, and with a lack of messages, voicemails, missed calls, and frankly *anything*, I began to scroll my contacts.

Unfortunately for my boredom, that didn't last long. I passed Dad, "Buffy", Marc Work, Mom, Tracy (Barista), Simon, and then the final named contact was Truman's. The others were numbers I'd never named, so who knows who they are.

I got up, since not even becoming one with the couch felt particularly appealing, not when there's supposed to be New Years energy energy. I took a walk to the window and peered out of it, gazing blankly out to the street in front of me.

Don't get me wrong, Grosvenor Avenue is not a bad little road, and the fact that Riverdale is a nice pocket of suburban in the Urban Hellhole that *is* New York is nice, but it's hardly a place that screams interest or excitement. Arguably, the layers and layers of snow have made it worse. Gone is the even slightly interesting greenery, blue skies, flowers, neighbours, and really any sign of life; replaced instead by… white fog and white ground and white absolutely *everything.*

The only good part about this weather is it's the perfect climate to drink a hot chocolate, but also I really cannot be fucked to actually *make* one.

God, what is *wrong* with me today?

---

Somehow, the hours passed. Really, it felt like they passed without me, but either way it got to dinner pretty damn quick, and just like at midday, hunger compels me.

After a brief struggle with the plastic, the cardboard shed itself like paper-fucking-mache beforehand, the premade pizza was free of its packaged confines and put right into the oven. Obviously, I made sure to preheat it, I'm not making that mistake… again. After setting the egg timer for 11-ish minutes, then I… well… then it's now. And once again, there's *nothing* happening.

*Tip tap tip tap tip tap tip tap.*

If I do that for long enough, my fingers are gonna start to hurt. Best not.

Eight minutes.

Sure is a lot of nothing going on in the backyard.

Five…  
…minutes.

Did I remember to get eggs? Wait, I don't eat eggs. What the fuck am I talking about? Where'd that come from.

Four…  
…God I'm bored…  
…minutes.

What's the mitochondria for again?

Three minutes… and thirty seconds.

God, I'm surprised I've not killed myself yet, I've been waiting for so long.

I should really stop making jokes about that, I'm gonna start taking myself seriously. If I'm not already-

Ding!

After what felt like an age, the little egg timer finally reached 0 and let the entire house know about it. Thank fuck, honestly. If it took any longer I think I would've died of boredom.

That's two death jokes in a row. Is that bad?

Eh, who cares. It's time for pizza.

---

It's really quite incredible how TV can have so many shows, a fair few for free, running for 24 hours a day, and have *none* of them be any good. Once upon a time (okay, about 5-ish years ago), I would've been entertained by literally fucking anything. Of course now, in the magical 21st Century, there was *nothing* good. How does that even happen? How does television become terrible when I arguably need it the most?

With the pizza already finished, I decide to slowly get up and peruse through my strange brew of both VHSs and DVDs. God, reading titles is a slog, *especially* when you have titles on both formats. Seriously, why did I keep buying VHSs *after* I bought a DVD player? What stupid idiot does that?? Well, I mean, this one does does, but that's besides the point.

Something strikes me as interesting, or at least I find something I read more than a single letter of without completely gazing over it. *Jurassic Park*, bought by my Dad about a year or so after seeing the film in cinemas. God what an experience that was, and god what a terrible sequel. I hope they don't make another one.

After a few motions with the aged case, the lovely cassette, and the equally antique player, the film was on and my ass was sat. Ah. Snacks. Oh well, I was already sat down, I really don't think I can move. I can't be bothered to, I mean. It's not like I'm *stuck* to the fucking pseudo-Lazyboy. it just feels like it. That's very different.

---

You know, I really remember this film being better.

Okay, *no*, that's absolute fucking slander against one of the best films ever. I really don't know how to explain it. It's just… I remember it being more enjoyable. It's great, abso-fucking-lutely. I love the effects and the characters and the action and the subtle themes and stuff. It is, without a doubt, one of the best films of all time. I'm just struggling to sit through it.

My mind keeps wandering to places, but not to anywhere in particular. I can't sit still; I can't concentrate. Even when doing something I love I still feel bored and… sad. I've contemplated putting on something else, since maybe I'm just feeling sad my dad's not around to watch the film with me (it is very much a film we'd watch together), but I can't find the strength.

For some films, I've made the same excuse about my Dad. Judge Dredd, The Crow, Pulp Fiction, you name it. We watched those films together in theatres and VHS, but really the only film I can *actually* justify not watching because of my Dad is Independence Day. We wanted to see it, but he died before it came out. The others I'm just lying for.

Do I do that a lot? Do I always make excuses for everything? An excuse to not enjoy this, an excuse to not watch that, an excuse to not talk to the others at work. I mean, they're idiots, right? Only *I* turn my brain off. I have to match *their* intelligence, not the other way around. Unless it's not the other way around at all either.

I mean, the only *other* way it would work is if we all (somehow) decided to collectively switch off our thinking when we started the job. But that would be silly. I think. I mean, sure, I did it on my first day, it's what you do when you start a job. But surely not *everyone* does it.

Without me even realising it, I had my phone flipped open and in the palm of my hand, scrolling through the list until I reached the final named one. Truman. I just know for a fact that if I wait too long, I'm going to second-guess myself, realise what I'm doing, and put the fucking thing down. So, unfortunately for me, it was only another moment before his number was dialling.

That regret hit like hose water when the phone began to dial, its tinny vibrations almost making me leap out of my seat in surprise. I held it in my hand, a pit growing in my stomach.

Please don't pick up.

Please be busy.

Please be cooking or shopping or relaxing or cranking it or *something*. Just let it go through to voicemail.

Fuck it, I'm hanging u-

"Hello?"

"Yeah, hi. Um, Truman, yeah?" I asked like an *idiot*. Of course it's him, I only got his number at New Years! He KNOWS this!

He's very confused why I called him, and he checked to see if it was definitely me. That I know, at least. We had a little meandering conversation, just a very office-like back and forth that didn't really go anywhere at all. It was dull and boring, and part of me wanted it to end. Fuck, part of me almost hung up on him, and *god* I wish I could do that at work.

But no. That's not what I'm going to do, and that's not what I did. Really, I don't quite remember how it got to the point it did, but after a bit more interesting chatting (I learnt he lives in Brooklyn, grew up in a place called Henrietta, and lived in Ireland for a while!) I gave him a proposal.

"Listen, um, I know it's *very* short notice, but like… I've got nothing going on. Do you want to go into Manhattan and get a drink?" I asked him, rambling.

"Eh, I don't drink." Truman said, immediately destroying all my hopes. Gee, thanks, dude. From somewhere, though, I still had some enthusiasm.

"What about just my place? I live in Riverdale, I can cover train costs." Wow, good going, as if I'm not broke.

"Oh, no need… but, uh, sure? I like that idea." Truman replied, giving me great relief at not having to pay subway fares and great excitement at actually having something *happen* tomorrow.

We kept talking, and it was… actually nice. Eventually it wrapped up, of course, but I enjoyed it. I have something to look forward to, and I've not had that in a while.

***New York City***, January 9th 2000

---

God, my place is an absolute fucking tip.

I've been cleaning it for most of the day. It's past midday now, and man I have never been more thankful for my own anxiety. After Truman actually agreed to actually come over, I'm still not sure how the hell I pulled that off. We chatted about what time to come over. Of course, I'm a do-nothing little recluse, but he doesn't need to know that. I was worried I wouldn't be able to clean the place in time, or that I'd sleep through my alarm and have to open the door to greet him in nothing but tight boxers while scratching my ass. Unfortunately, that would not be the most flattering thing I could do. Not in tight ones at least.

So, instead I was up at 7:30, and here I am *now* at 12:30, still sorting out the fucking living room. Seriously, I should use my vacuum more often, because at least cleaning things gives me something to do.

Midway through reaching the little handheld vacuum right under the couch, the doorbell went, vibrating through the entire house. The sudden shrill made me jump so hard I hit my fucking wrist on the underside of the couch, so now *that* hurts like hell. Whatever, let's not fuck this up any further than I already have.

"Hey!" I said, almost *immediately* using too much enthusiasm the second I opened up the door. What a great start! Surprisingly, though, Truman doesn't seem to give much of a crap.

"Hey, dude. You've actually got a pretty nice spot." Truman commented, smiling at me before taking a little step back to admire the rest of my house. I imagine, living in Brooklyn, he's crammed in a tiny apartment or on a row of little terraces.

"Uh, yeah, well… I got my Dad to thank for it. You gonna come in?" I asked, to which he gave a chuckle. After some further back and forth, he was in.

Don't let me fuck this up.

---

"*Fuck, dude, your hands are bony.*" Truman said with a deep chuckle, taking the Fanta Citrus I'd offered him. It had been a few hours now, and somehow I was yet to fuck things up. I took my spot next to the guy, cracking open my own Fanta Black Cherry.

"Are they?" I asked, flaring out my hand like a girl looking at her nails.

"Yeah, dude. You're *mega* thin." He said lightly, taking a swig of his drink, which I also did a moment after.

"So are you, we work in an office." I replied with frankly sharp wit, setting my can down between my legs. My comment seemed to make Truman laugh. That felt pretty good.

"Touché, my man…" He said, sitting further back with a loud sigh, really getting quite comfortable on my couch. "So! You watch sport?"

"Uh… no." I said simply, momentarily glancing at the television.

"Good choice, me neither," he commented, sharing my glance. His eyes wandered, spotting my shelves of films and television series, and… well hopefully nothing else is on there. I didn't get time to sort it before he got here. "You a big film buff, then?"

"Heh, well… sort of. It's about the only thing I spend my money on." I chuckled, rubbing my knees anxiously. Truman slowly got up, leaving a little him-shaped impression on my couch cushions, and went to go have a look.

"You don't mind if I skim through, do you? Sorry, I'm curious." He asked, stopping just short of the shelves and looking over his shoulder at me.

"If you care enough, sure?" I said, choosing to get up too. Truman's attention went back to the shelves, and I have to admit I was following his eyes *very* curiously. I'd had people around sometimes, albeit not in years, but seeing someone take an interest in my stuff was interesting. I really can't explain it, but it's giving me a bit of love for the collection again, even if some of the stuff I've never actually watched.

"Back to the Future, The Goonies, Predator… Predator *2*. You really like your action stuff, huh?" Truman commented, crouching down to read the list of DVDs in the block just below his chest.

"My Dad did, yeah. Most of these are actually his. *Especially* the older stuff, he's been a home-film fan since you could buy them in shops." I replied, watching him parse through some more titles, muttering some ones I assume he found interesting under his breath.

"Does he buy them for you or something?" He asked, having absolutely no idea of the nerve he tripped. I chuckled instinctively, clearing my throat.

"No, uh… I got them when he passed." I explained, taking a moment to make sure the tone coming out would be appropriate.

"Oh! Dude, I'm sorry." He said, righting himself up and looking dead at me. It would've been uncomfortable were it not for his genuinely apologetic expression.

"It's fine. It was almost four years ago, I… it's not recent." I said, feeling guilty about making *him* guilty. At this point we'll have a spiral of ants guilt going in the next two minutes.

"That doesn't mean it doesn't suck? He must've thought you were a hella good son to let you take all his movies?"

"Heh, my mom certainly didn't want them, and we watched them a lot too. It was a big thing we did most nights." I explained, picking out the Jurassic Park VHS from the collection to feel.

"Sounds sweet… How'd he buy you the house, then? No offence, but if he's dead…" He trailed off, his face growing even more guilty. It made me chuckle.

"Oh, um, *huge* fucking will. He worked in disaster relief up North for years and got a fat-ass pension from it. I got most of what was left and used it to buy a house outright." I explained, continuing to fiddle with the case idly.

"Dude, that's smart. I bet my old college would've taken any big sum of money I got to pay my loans. Well done on getting something comfortable." Truman said. Of course, he didn't need to know that my own college had tried the *exact same thing*, to which I told them to fuck off. Probably not the most legal thing I could've done but oh well. Wait-

"You went to college?" I asked suddenly, the sound waves from his mouth *finally* fully registering and turning the cogs in my dusty, decrepit brain.

"Is that really so surprising?" He asked, looking befuddled at what I'd said. Honestly, dude, you and me both.

"I mean, I suppose it shouldn't be, but like… you work at-" *I hate saying the company's name I wish it burned forever.* "It's sort of the most… dead-end uneducated job you could ever have, second to shovelling pig shit. Not trying to be rude, but-"

"Oh, dude, you're not. You're absolutely right." Truman said, breaking my rambling off, thankfully. Really, I don't know where I was trying to go.

"So… Why work there?" I asked, leaning my arm against the bookcase.

"Are *you* college educated?" He asked, his tone a mix of genuinely curious and one that seemed like he was leading to something.

"I graduated 3-odd years back." I replied.

***The Habitable Zone***, April 2nd 2000

---

Surprisingly, my first level transfer without any kind of group with me or to go back to went well. That was nice. I have to admit, every time I cross worlds, I worry I'll get caught in that same void. I still have nightmares about it, and the thought of ending up there plagues me a lot. Weirdly, I haven't always felt the need to sleep or eat; I feel weaker sometimes if I don't do either for long periods of time, but I've never passed out or starved to death yet. I'm sure that's fine.

Now I'm in the Habitable Zone, apparently once called "Scene-01.3" according to the professor, but no one's used that name since the late 90s. As far as I'm concerned, this place is just "*the Habitable Zone*". Simple enough. I will say though, for a place that's "habitable," it's pretty fucking empty.

At least, it felt like it was for hours.

After near misses with entities, getting lost, doubling back on myself, and almost ending up in some nasty-looking world, I ended up with some people. Just a small group, they called themselves something like "the Early Home" or something like that. I don't fully remember details anymore, I block out most information that I know damn well won't make a difference when I talk to someone.

Speaking of.

"Oh, shit, new person." A guard at the Early Home had said, standing guard at a doorway with a pretty rudimentary wooden spear that had bits of tape strapped across it. Really, I don't think that would even be able to kill *me*, and I'm… probably not strong. I'm not actually sure.

"Am I encroaching on some territory?" I asked, holding my hands up to show the trembling man I had no ill intentions. It really didn't seem to help much with his nerves, poor fucker.

"No, no. We're peaceful," he explained, flexing his hands on the weapon. "if you're looking for somewhere to stay or live, as long as you don't have any weapons, I'll let you through."

"I'm completely unarmed!" I had said pretty jovially. If he's barely going to remember I exist, I may as well have some fun with it. Comically, I inverted both of my pockets and opened up my messenger bag. He stared at them, already forgetting quite why he was, and then awkwardly nodded and sidestepped so I could enter. On my way in, I think he forgot half of what had happened but tried to play it off cool, so he escorted me to the others of the group.

"Who's this?" A woman named Casey asked. I didn't request her name or anything; I never do anymore.

"Casey, this is… I don't know. Someone who wanted to come in, I think." The man said, shrugging. Casey got up off her little pillow on the floor, one that encircled a little antique oven that others were also sat around, and approached the two of us.

"You think?" She asked, regarding him before she regarded me. She studied me up and down, but her face didn't come to any real conclusion.

"Yeah, I mean, they showed up, we talked for a little bit, and now I'm escorting them in."

"Did this, err, person say why they're here?" I get made the third wheel a lot. At this point, I keep a mental timer to see how quick it happens.

"Probably. They said words, I think." Classic.

"Well…" Casey started, finally turning to face me again, "why *are* you here? What's your name?"

That second question's easy. I just blurted out the first funny thing that came to my mind. I've come to learn I can literally say anything, and they'll air over it. It's incredible. I think I said something like ice cream or fat wank. I don't remember.

"Right, that's a… good name, probably. How come you're here?" She asked, airing over the stupid shit I said as expected.

"I ended up here, and why not?" I shrugged. Again, I can really say anything. As long as I say it in an upbeat and friendly tone, they think I'm being a nice guy. I could call them an asshole and accuse their entire family of being witches and whores, but I don't *actually* want to do that. I can do silly jokes as a one-off, but these are still people, and they literally can't know any better.

Anyway. We went back and forth in our conversations, not really speaking about much at all besides the name and intentions of their group. She offered me some food, which I took, and then she offered me some more later which I took, and then she offered some more even later, which I… turned down. I figured I'd be nice. She seemed totally unaware of the last two times she'd offered me food, and I think these poor fuckers need it more than I do.

They explained some deeper stuff about their history I forgot, they offered a home which I turned down, and they offered a map of nearby worlds which I accepted. The Aiding Stragglers already gave me one, but this one is a homebrew, so hopefully it has some new locations on it. It *almost* felt like a home, you know, all sat around a cosy fire while people talk to each other.

But it began to creep in.

The loneliness, the isolation, the fact people were looking through me like I wasn't even there, and any little chimes or quips I had were met with (at best) a chuckle. I wasn't there, let's be honest.

Eventually, someone started asking Casey if the leaders of some group, called Aelita or something, had stopped by with any updates on good scavenging locations. I took it as my little cue to slip out unnoticed, as it seemed everyone began to pitch in with calls that they didn't need the help or this, that, and the other. I didn't really care. I called out that I was running late and needed to dash, but it didn't register. I'm not surprised.

Of course, I could've left any time I wanted; I was basically never there, but it felt a little bit more human to slip out when people were distracted. And I *am* a human. People keep saying that, and it's what I see whenever I look down. I think. I can't actually remember the last time I looked down.

---

Upon rerunning my little stint with the Early Home for the billionth time in my head, other thoughts were coming to me. Thoughts of Truman, the other employees, and my living family. Had I been forgotten by them, too? Would they remember me if they saw me now? Does this effect *only* work on people I don't know, or is it universal? Do they remember my past? Would they think I'm still missing if I could somehow get back? If they *do* remember me? Maybe they can tell me who I am, because these days I'm not sure.

Sometimes my mind drifts to Truman, my one-day friend. Someone who I barely knew, then knew a little bit, and now… I'll never know again. He was cool. I miss him. Maybe he'll fall into the Backrooms at some point too. Who's to say?

Hopefully by then this dumb little effect will have worn off. The professor and Hannah both agreed, after I eventually got them to remember that I exist even slightly for a few days, that it may wear off. Of course, their claim is unsubstantiated, and I think they said it either to be nice or because they had no idea what was going on. It was probably both.

Still! This can't last *forever*, right? Either it'll wear off or I'll die, and both of those would be sweet, sweet mercies from what's happened to me.  
   
   
. . .  
   
   
I remain hopeful. I bet it'll be gone within the year.

***Spire of the Past***, April 2nd 5,000,000,053

  
   
   
   
   
   
   
   
   
 

Time passes on further and further.

For most, the acceleration of time is gradual but steady. The more years one is able to prosper, the faster the subsequent years get, with every next epoch feeling a little shorter than the last.

For an immortal, it is the same; only the years become shorter… and shorter… and shorter.

Eventually, a decade may feel like a blow of dust, a century a stone skim, a millennia but a single exhale…

Time passes on faster and faster.

An age passes from one eye to the next.

A terasecond breezes past like the wind.

A megaannum occurs during an inhale, a petasecond on the exhale.

Eventually, a galactic year feels like nothing, and one day neither does an eon or a kalpa.

What started out as a typical example of the species has now become an echo of something nobody knows the origin of.

Nothing stays the same. All becomes dust. Each memory feels incongruous and completely incompatible with the others.

For the lucky, the end of everything includes the end of them. To be violently ripped and melted in the final death throes of ancient creation is a mercy to the opposite.

A mercy to what actually happened to one.

Somewhere, somewhen, in the nothing where something used to be, there is a presence. A lonely presence. A figure that represents what there once used to be, but not even *it* knows how long ago that was.

Once upon a time, in a unit of measurement never conceived nor remembered, this figure travelled somewhere no one else ever has. When unwillingly crossing the boundary from [one collection of everything](the-frontrooms.md) to [another](blue-channel.md), their physical body transported itself to a [metaphysical plane](level-586.md). Its matter was alien to the realm's information and, as such, was expelled to [a more appropriate place](level-2.md).

On the way, however, something was swapped. Part of the information on what the ancient figure was changed places with information already stored in the metaphysical plane. Something even more ancient, something forgotten, something powerful, something inconceivable. Something no one could remember. It grafted to them, rubbed off on them, and they became one.

That place was long gone now, and the figure's original fragment along with it. Now there was nothing, nothing but that figure.

A figure who only wanted a friend.
