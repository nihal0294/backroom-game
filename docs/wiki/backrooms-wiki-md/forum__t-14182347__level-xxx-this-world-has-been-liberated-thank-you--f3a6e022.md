Level XXX - "This world has been liberated, thank you."

30 Aug 2021 09:36

**Page Type:** Backroom Level

**Genre (Optional):** Tragedy

**Page Layout (Optional):** The page will have a brief introduction to the level itself, but then will enter into the entries of the journal found in the room, which is what the main focus of the level will be on.

**Elevator Pitch:** The level itself is just a small, dark, wooden room, with the light of a single candle illuminating a pedestal. But on that pedestal is a book which tells the story of what this level truly used to be, and only by reading the book in its entirety can you leave the level.

**Central Narrative:** The narrative of this level lies within the pages of the book. The book is a journal belonging to British naval admiral Francis Crozier, who, after his ship and his crew got trapped in the Canadian arctic, wandered to try and find civilization (NOTE: This was how he, unknowingly, entered this level of the backrooms, as wandering in despair for a certain length brought you to the level naturally from reality, but you can no longer enter the level that way). While wandering, he apparently came across an elderly man, who looked like a native, and he handed Francis this book, telling him to journal his travels, as his story would be one for all time. Francis just accepted this as some sort of native custom thing, and wrote down who he was, what happened, and where he was now.

Eventually, he arrived at a small settlement, filled with about 10-15 people, dressed in similar clothing to the elderly man he came across. Their faces looked… familiar, to Francis, but he couldn't say who they were. Their skin was quite grey, and their faces, stuck in a monotone look of misery. Francis was quite disturbed by this, but still, they offered him food, water, and shelter, so he took their offer regardless.

He couldn't see the sun, as the world (level) was covered by a thick blanket of grey cloud, but still, it was enough to tell when nightfall cameabout, and so he went to bed. But, when he awoke the next day, the group was moving out of the sleeping area without explanation, pickaxes over their shoulders, and still just as glum. Francis followed the group to wherever they were going, they were trudging along quite slowly so it was easy to write and follow at the same time. Even though he tried to ask the members of the group what the hell was going on in every language he knew, there was 0 response.

Eventually, Francis and the group arrived at the base of a large alpine region, where many entrances to what seemed to be mines opened. Many other beings came from other parts of the land that looked just the same, with these same recognisable, but not too recognisable faces plastered on ghostly grey and glum bodies. Just outside the mines, Francis noted some other being. Made of stone, it had the appearance of a gargoyle, but they moved and were dressed in clothes similar to Francis’s. They didn’t take any note of his existence, or at least that’s what they seemed. They sat upon perches in the outside world. Francis was incredibly confused at the situation, and as to why these gargoyle beings were copying him, and so, he ventured into the mines.

Lit by only the flicker of lanterns upon the walls, he couldn’t even tell what they were mining. It didn’t seem like anything. They just seemed to be digging aimlessly, overlooked by those damned gargoyle beings. He was originally contemplating just leaving, and continuing on his journey to try and find aid for his crew still presumably stuck in the far, far north, but then he heard something. Deeper into the mines, a scream, and the sound of something like gravel falling.

Francis ran to where the noise came from, and found a large hole in the ground. Taking one of the lanterns, he saw that one of the ghastly beings had fallen down a drop, but the Gargoyle overlooking them just screamed at them to keep digging. His voice, it was the same as his own, but, deeper. It was hard for Francis to describe. With a lantern, Francis jumped down the hole, it wasn’t too deep so he would be able to get back up. Setting it aside, he attempted to tend to the wounds of the humanoid. He originally reached down, trying to find something on his person to bandage it up, but, while fumbling in the darkness, he accidently cut himself on his knife through his sleeve. But, after finding nothing, he opted to just take one of his gloves off, and wrap it around one of the cuts of the being. It was strange, as they weren’t bleeding at all, just many wounds despite them being obviously very deep.

But, as soon as his glove, covered in Francis’s blood, life seemed to return to the beings skin. Even though it was dark, Francis could see that, but he also saw something else. Its face. It was the face of one of his crew members. His breath was drawn from his body, as the being slowly started to become more human. It gasped for air, its eyes grew wider, and it started calling Francis’s name. But, before he could reply, the gargoyle came to the hole, and, with some form of blade, threw it directly at the being, killing it in an instant.

Francis’s last entry of the level described him leaving the mines. When he exited the mines he looked at one of the gargoyles. Just as where he had been cut on his sleeve, on the sleeves of all the gargoyles, a tear was visible in the exact same place. Francis panicked internally and so he decided to screw it and flee, as this was all too disturbing for him. But, when he came to one of the mountaintops, he noticed another one of the beings, just sitting there. He went to try and see its face.

It was the face of Jane Griffin, the wife of his expedition’s commander, Sir John Franklin. And then, like a wave, it hit him. By walking out on his comrades, he was leaving them there to freeze to death, and their families would forever suffer for it, as would all his peers back home. But he knew, he had a chance to be forgiven, and repent for his sins, and that was by healing the world around him. His blood, it brought life to this world made of his mistakes.

And so, after cutting himself multiple times, and completely dousing his jacket in his own blood, he lets it go to fly in the wind. He is incredibly tipsy and lightheaded, and it’s hard to write due to the lack of blood, but it’s never written down what happens next.

Only after reading the book in its entirety does a door appear on the wall, which allows you to progress to the next room.

**Hook/Attention-Grabber:** I believe the level's name will be a good attention grabber. "This world has been liberated, thank you." (or something along those lines), and the first thing people see when clicking upon the page is a note or a plaque or something like that which says

"*We have been freed from our bonds. Our lives, wasting away no longer they are. Francis's doing, his sacrifice, it freed us, and we are forever grateful in our rest.*"

"*This world has been liberated, thank you.*"

On top of that, this level's uniqueness of the level's entry telling more of a story of the level rather than just a level itself may also keep the reader's attention as well.

**Additional Notes:** I do feel like this is just too drama drama drama. Like it sounds good in theory, but the events in the journal just feel very over-dramatic. I fear that may be a pitfall if I am to write this level. Also, this indeed somewhat skewing off of the "normal" backrooms template, might make it collapse, but, then again, this is really the only way to implement it if going off this idea.
