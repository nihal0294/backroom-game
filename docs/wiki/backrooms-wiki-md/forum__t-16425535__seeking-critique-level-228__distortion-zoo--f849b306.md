> Level 228 is representative of a zoo that slowly distorts the longer you are present. All exhibitions and cages within the zoo are empty although food placed into the troughs are eaten and the water gets consumed. To get around there is a dirt path extending to every enclosure and around the perimeter; Level 228 is circular in shape and surrounded by a wall extending infinitely upwards.

**"Level 228 is representative of a zoo…"** You should change "is representative of" with "represents". I honestly don't know if both forms are correct, but it sounds 10x better the way I said it.  
**"All exhibitions and cages withing the zoo are empty…"** Change "exhibitions" to "exhibits". The exhibition is the whole zoo, and the exhibits are the animals, aquariums, etc. that you would find inside the zoo. Also, I would recommend removing "and cages".  
**"…although food placed into the troughs are eaten and the water gets consumed"** delete "and the water is consumed" and instead place "food and water placed into the throughs gets consumed", that way you can make a shorter sentence.  
\*\*"Level 228 is circular in shape and surrounded by a wall extending infinitely upwards. " Just remove all that and instead embed it at the start of the description, because it just feels very out of place. Why? Because until now you were talking about the interior, the exhibits, etc, and now you get to something that should be at the start of the level description.

> All of the enclosures represent those of those that would be seen within zoos inside the Frontrooms. The floors are made of bark with branches going from wall to wall of the metal bars. Other exhibits are plant life and vines beyond a grass wall; some are even fitted with small ponds. It is not uncommon or difficult for a wanderer to get lost as a lot of areas look similar.

**"All of the enclosures represent those of those that would be seen within zoos inside the Frontrooms."**. Too much repetition with "those of those", two "those" aren't needed, you can write only one.  
**"The floors are made of bark with branches going from wall to wall of the metal bars."** I don't want to be rude here, but I don't even know what you meant by this. Just rewrite this part completely at this point man.  
**"Other exhibits are plant life and vines beyond a grass wall; some are even fitted with small ponds."** You mean like… what do you mean by this? You should define plant life in a foot note or right there, like are we talking daisies and roses or vines and algae? Try to answer that question.

> After three days on the level humans will begin to slowly loose their senses;

Change the "on" to "in" and the "loose" to "lose" (loose mean not firmly or tightly fixed in place; detached or able to be detached.)

TO THE ENTITIES WE GO!

> Interacting with the facelings can result in having a friendly and interesting conversation; occasionally finding out a piece of history to the zoo or some information about another level.

Replace the semicolon with a comma.

**"there was demons;"** The correct form is "there *were* demons", also, once again, remove the semicolon and replace it with a comma.

> Officer Ben Murphy seems a little scared but tries to keep a straight face.

Remove this whole part. You shouldn't just tell the readers outright that the characters were scared to make something scary. And it just feels very… out of place, so to say.

Hello, hello! Welcome to the second revision! You have improved a lot since last time, but there are still some mistakes here and there. Let's see:

> Level 228 is represents a zoo that slowly distorts the longer you are present. The perimeter is circular in shape and surrounded by a rough concrete wall extending infinitely upwards. All exhibits within the zoo are empty although food and water within exhibits is consumed. To get around there is a dirt path extending to every enclosure and around the perimeter. There is however, a gate toward the bottom of the zoo. The zoo’s workers enter and exit through it at will, yet no human has ever managed to walk through the gate or even see what is beyond it.

**"Level 228 is represents…"** There may have been a misunderstanding last time, I meant replace everything at the start with represents. The form "is represents" is completely wrong, you should remove the "is" from there.  
**" All exhibits within the zoo are empty although food and water within exhibits is consumed."** I would recommend replacing the second "exhibits" with "them" because there is a very observable repetition, so it doesn't sound very good.  
**"There is however, a gate toward the bottom of the zoo."** Firstly, generally, you place a comma before and after the "however", but in this case, you should remove it completely. Why? Because "however" usually signals a positive part of something negative, or a negative part of a positive. For example: "Marie bought some ice cream**, however,** it will melt very fast.". In your case, there is no positive, or negative.  
**"The zoo’s workers enter and exit through it at will, yet no human has ever managed to walk through the gate"** Look! This is a great place to insert a "however" instead of "yet no human…" you can say ", however, no human…" by removing the "yet" and replacing it with a "however".

Now, a general tip that I place in the middle because I wanna and that I should have told you about from the start, please add a description of a security office. It doesn't have to be long, but the wanderer has to know what he is looking for when he is looking for a security office.

Didn't see any problems with the entities, TO THE LOG WE GO!

> Okay so Mr Maxfield you claim to have discovered a new level.

I… don't know what to say about this part. Even though it doesn't seem right… I don't know how to fix it. I think that you should say "Hello, Mister Maxfield" (notice the comma) instead of "Ok, so, Mr. Maxfield…", it just feels a lot more professional.

> It said come down to the daisy town z-zoo.

"Daisy town zoo" should all start with capital letters because it's a name. And, you know, names usually start with capital letters.

> Alan Maxfield was deemed to not be in the right state of mind to be interviewed further. After the interview he was transported to Level 11 where he now lives in MEG Base Omega, after being found unfit to care for himself.

Add this whole thing as a note after the "Tape End", because it's still *technically* part of the log. Also, add a comma after "after the interview".

Welp, that's all I can think of right now. As said, you should add a description of the security offices and maybe you could gather some history about the level from the Facelings and place it in a table, but I understand if you don't want to do it, and I'm not pushing you to do it, but the security office description is a must. Can't wait to see the next update!

Hey man! Firstly, sorry for the late reply, had some work to do. Secondly, this will be my last review of the page, last revision, last everything. After you fix everything I tell you, I recommend either:  
A) Make another thread for greenlight  
B) Edit this thread's title into a seeking greenlight thread  
Pick whatever you want. Also, when this gets posted on the site, please add my name into the "Critiques" section or in the comments of the level, and also send me a DM when this gets posted. Thank you in advance! Now, let's get to the final critique  
Also, I will be talking a lot about Oxford comma, so in case you don't know what those are, here's a link to a Grammarly blog about it [here](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/what-is-the-oxford-comma-and-why-do-people-care-so-much-about-it/)!

> Level 228 is a large zoo, completely devoid of animals. It’s maintained by the workers and security of the zoo. Don’t stay too long, a lot of time in this level will have serious negative consequences.

You don't need this, believe me. The part before the description just says which number the level has. Also, when you use "rd", "st", etc after numbers there is no need for an apostrophe between the number and the "rd", "st", etc.

> It is not uncommon or difficult for a wanderer to get lost, as a lot of areas look similar.

Instead of saying "it is not uncommon", you can just say "it is common"

> If a wanderer does find themselves lost, they can find a security office and ask an Aiden for help or they can find the nearest worker and ask them to take them to an exit or to the main gate.

Instead of saying **"they can find a security office and ask an Aiden for help or they can find the nearest worker and ask them to take them to an exit or to the main gate."** you can just say "they can ask an Aiden or a worker for help, or to get them out of here." to "compress" the sentence and make it easier to read.

> There is a day and night cycle in place which typically lasts 16 hours in Frontroom’s time.

In my opinion, you should remove the "in Frontroom's time".

> they will get an increasingly stronger migraine, high temperatures and hallucinations.

Instead of saying "high temperatures" you can just say "fever". Also, we love Oxford commas, so why not add one before the "and"? (:

> Wanderers descriptions on these hallucinations vary.

You should add an apostrophe after "Wanderers" because it is plural possesive form, so the apostrophe comes after the word itself.

> Some of them report seeing regular entities, others see undocumented entities to their high school bully.

**"others see undocumented entities to their high school bully."**. What do you mean by this? You should formulate the sentence in another way, at least this part. Like I get what you mean, you mean that some people see their highschool bullies in the cages as hallucinations (right?), but the way you formulated it is weird, to say the least.

> Then, after a week, the wanderers will no longer be able to feel pain, hear sound or see in colour.

You should just stay "the wanderers will no longer be able to feel, hear or see in colour" instead of "the wanderers will no longer be able to feel pain, hear sound or see in colour." to remove redundancy. Also, the Oxford comma makes it's comeback, add it between "sound" and "or".

> Cages will start to become longer in length, the ground will randomly spike upwards, as if it were a glitched piece of simulated terrain generation.

Once again, one of the sins of writing, redundancy, remove "in length" because it's useless. Also, instead of a comma, recommend adding a "and" between "longer" (we removed the "in length") and "the ground"

> Some enclosures will clip together and the hallucinations will start to seem malformed: missing limbs and having dented bodies.

Instead of saying "missing limbs and having dented bodies", which seems very wrong to me, you can say "and the hallucinations will start to seem malformed, having missing limbs and dented bodies"

> Often their voice will no longer sync to the movement of the apparition’s face, if they still have one.

You should add a comma after "often".

> The wanderer will be forced to wander the grounds until they eventually pass on, usually because of dehydration or starvation.

Instead of saying "The wanderer will be forced to wander" you should find a synonim to "wander" and replace the word with it to avoid repetition and, possibly (I don't know if it works in this case), redundancy.

> Level 228 has small security offices that are randomly scattered around, there is no real pattern to how they generate.

Instead of a comma add a dot between "around" and "there".

> One thing they all have in common is there is only one way in: a heavy iron door, around 7ft high and 4 inches thick.

Instead of a colon, you should use a comma.

> However, there is generally the same items:

You should replace the "is" with "are" because the subject, "items", is plural so the clause, infinitive "to be", should also be plural, "are".

> An Almond Water cooler, a mini fridge with seemingly infinite food, a desk with a double monitor PC, displaying a live security feed.

Remove the capital "A" from "an". Also, you should add a footnote describing how the food supply is infinite. Is it spontaneously reffiled after you close the door? Or does it just go on forever? Or maybe it's something else completely, only you know. And, finally, instead of using a comma before "a desk", you should add an "and" with an Oxford comma before it. You should also remove the comma before "displaying" because it is not needed.

Now, my favourite part of this whole page, the history. I just wanted to say, great job, I literally clapped IRL when I saw how you wrote the interview with the faceling and all that, but there are, still, some grammar issues that cannot be overlooked! Let's get right into it, shall we?

> I don’t tend to dwell on the past, but, we did it once have animals.

The way you phrased it isn't very good. I recommend saying "I don't tend to dwell on the past, but, we did have animals once."

> The Boss just tells us we can only be here once a day, in shift hours only. We don’t question him on it otherwise he stops paying us until they see fit.

You used male pronounce until the last few words. As long as "The Boss" isn't gender-neutral or gender-fluid, make sure that you use male pronounce.

> Good afternoon Sir.

Firstly, the "S" mustn't be capitalised. Secondly, add a comma between "afternoon" and "sir".

> I’m not the best person to ask but as far as I’m aware this was a normal zoo once.

You should add a comma between "ask" and "but".

> We were the main attraction of Daisy Town and this place was jam packed every day!

You should add a comma between "Town" and "and". Also, "jam" and "packed" are united by a hyphen ( - ).

> Our boss tried to get us to ignore everything; how could we when all of our customers disappeared at once.

Instead of saying **" Our boss tried to get us to ignore everything; how could we…"** you can say "Our boss tried to get us to ignore everything, but how could we…"

> What do you mean by they were agitated?

You should place "they were agitated" between quotation marks.

> they snapped at people who got too near;

Just say "who got near" instead of "who got **too** near". Also, replace the semi-colon with a period.

Also, why did you suddenly go italic? Was it like to underscore whispering or an outside force? I just wish to know, you don't have to change anything.

> Or can you show me to someone who has?

Remove the "to" from there.

> Listen, you can’t tell the Boss, or anyone for that matter but I decided to stay on site for a few days.

You should add a comma before **and** after "or anyone for that matter"

> On the fourth morning I woke, the office seemed different.

You should add a "and" after the comma. Also, you should add a comma after "morning".

> So it was distorted?

You should add a comma between the "So" and the "it"

> I noticed I could put my limbs through other limbs.

This part is just… unnecessary in my opinion.

> I ran over to 538.6284 and asked if she could see what I saw.

To avoid repetition, you should change "what I saw" to "what I witnessed", or something similar.

> Only when the Boss reviewed the security footage with me did I notice the taser prods in my upper abdomen.

What does the boss seeing the footage has to do with getting tased? This sentence is weirdly formulated, so I reckon a rewrite is needed.

> Aiden wouldn’t fight me back even though I kept hitting him. Instead he kept increasing the voltage on his taser.

There are 2 punctuation mistakes here:  
1. You should add a comma between "back" and "even"  
2. You should add a comma between "Instead" and "he"

> After that I don’t remember anything; on the security footage however, I fell back on the floor, passed out and I kept harshly vibrating.

You should add a comma between "that" and "I". Also, another comma is needed between "footage" and "however". And you should also remove the "I" from "I kept harshly vibrating", because you already imply that this is on the first person with "I" from "I fell back on the floor".

Welp, that's it! This was a nice ride for me at least, I hope same can be said for you. If you have the time, could you please rate my overall critique, taking into consideration SPaG and Idea Critique, on a scale of 1 to 10? Could you tell me how can I improve as a critique and, consequentially, as a writer? Thank you in advance.
