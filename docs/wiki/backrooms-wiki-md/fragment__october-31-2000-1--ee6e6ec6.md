# My Home...

# My Home…

*"Honey… Honey, wake up. It's Halloween! Dad's downstairs getting the decorations out. We found the old bats you like."*

"Mrng… In a bit mom… Give me five minutes."

*"Of course, honey, Dad and I will be waiting for you. He says he's taking us to the farm today to get pumpkins from Farmer Richard this year."*

"Really!?"

*"Hehe, yes, really, honey, now get dressed and meet us downstairs. We will be waiting for you.*

I remember my house. I remember it well, a one-story house in the suburbs of an American city. I don't know the city or the name of the town my home was in, but I can recall what happened in that house. I remember all the holiday parties, all the family gatherings, the family feuds, every moment that house felt like home. Even the times I had lain down to sleep, the feeling of home was always with me.

The beeping clock slowly stirred me from the slumber I was in. I woke up in my old house, in my old bedroom, in my old bed. Like I never left. I lazily flopped my hand to the blaring sound, feeling around for the round, deafening alarm. Once the beeping menace was silenced, the muffled tune of Halloween music gradually filled my ears as I pushed myself up from my warm bed. I remember my way around my house; in the back of my mind, I know where everything is, but everything feels so alien to me- nothing is fully here. I walked down the hallway where the music was coming from. To my left is the study, which holds the family computer with wires running back to a nearby printer. Several bookshelves hold books that I have never read, with trophies and art pieces I do not remember. The music led my attention back to the kitchen. White faux-marble counters covered with plates of different Halloween sweets and pizza boxes filled with various toppings. There were some other food items, but I wasn't hungry at the moment. Making my way out of the kitchen through the other side of the counters and across the median island, I looked around the dining room. The tablecloth is patterned with the same Halloween colors I see every October.

My brain is still waking itself up, still recalling everything.

**"Well, good morning, sleepy head!"**

"Morning, Dad! Mom said we are going to go get pumpkins from Farmer Richy today!

**"We sure are, champ! Now come on, we don't want to be late now, do we?"**

Has my house always been this small? I could have sworn my parents' bedroom was right next to the living room, and that laundry room was the same. Yet it isnt there. Where is it?

Wait, what else was I going to do…?

Oh! Right! My family was always excited for Halloween! A time for our nearby family and friends to come around, hang out, and even help my parents and me with decorating the house. The decorations were all those tacky, cheap plastic bats and window stickers of pumpkins, bats, and skulls, to name a few. There were always orange and black streamers strung up around the ceiling.

"Wait. What about everyone else? Are they supposed to be here by now?"

**"No, not till much later, when the sun sets."**

*"And don't worry, we will be back in time before they get here."*

"But what about the rest of the decorations?"

*"Don't worry, we'll get those finished once we're home, love."*

Not to mention all the pumpkins that were strewn about the house. I know my parents always got pumpkins from the local farm around Fall, big or small; it didn't matter what size. There was never a shortage of jack-o-lanterns for us; they always had the carving tools in a box in the kitchen just for the occasion. But there were none to be found anywhere around the house. Maybe they forgot to get them this year? Probably. I’ll still go out to town and get them. They won't be far, maybe the farmer will let me borrow their wagon.

I always liked riding in Dad's car. I can't place it; it was always so fun. Just being in the back seat, looking out the window, watching all the houses and other cars pass by. Especially now that it's Fall, seeing all the orange and brown leaves fall on the pavement, being blown by the passing breezes. Seeing everyone outside enjoying the nice weather, from the other neighborhood families playing with their kids on the lawns, to even the old couple a few doors down, taking their daily walks. It was nice seeing all of this, even if it was through a very quick glance. It was very nice.

I wish it would last like this all year.

After opening the front door, I was met with a chill breeze from the outdoors. Shivering slightly, I made sure that I had closed the house door so that Mom wouldn't get on me about leaving the front door open again before bounding off into the neighborhood.

The neighborhood is always so pretty during the Fall. The falling autumn leaves from the trees, the cold outside air, and the neighbors putting out their Halloween decorations, it's always so nice.

"Are we there yet?"

**"Not quite, champ. We just got out of the neighborhood!"**

"How much longer then?"

**"Just a few more minutes. I promise."**

  
I don't recall what the neighbors' houses were like; I just recall there being one- or two-story tall, Americana, if you will. The sidewalks were always worn and uneven; it was easy to trip over the concrete blocks if I wasn't careful. I managed to catch myself a few times while I was walking and taking in the outside, save for the more affluent part of the neighborhood, a gated community. I never really went over there, no reason to honestly. I didn't have any friends who lived over there, and I never really felt welcome by those kids both outside and inside school.

Outside, neighbors' lawns were decorated with hanging lights, inflatables, and their own pumpkins. Yet, I never saw any of them put out those decorations; it was all just out there, untouched besides the fallen leaves and natural weather. I remember the family next door, the mom, dad, daughter, and the family dog, too. But I can't remember their names. I wish I did so I could actually talk to them outside of a hello. Or maybe even go up to them and greet them properly. Yet I couldn't, no matter how long I stared or how hard I tried to force my mind to speak their long-lost names, nothing came.

Maybe Mom remembers their names. I can hopefully ask her if I get home in time, if she is even home, that is. I didn't see her this morning, but she should be home before the kids start coming around for candy hunting time.
