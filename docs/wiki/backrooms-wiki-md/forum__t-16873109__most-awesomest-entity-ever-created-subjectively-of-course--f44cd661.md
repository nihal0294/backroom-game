Sorry that this critique is much later than it should've been.

## Format

This is my first impressions from just looking at the page. There are no paragraph spaces. Instead, there are 2 very big paragraphs. This is most likely to be off putting for people reading.

Do you intend for this page to be part of the Decay canon or are you using the theme for aesthetic reasons?

For the subheadings, there is no need to put a colon after them. The underlining works fine as it.

## Description crit

I love the first sentence. It works really well as an introduction to the page. I really like the tone established in this section too. I don't have much critique, just some wording recommendations.

> Hardened cover over an insect's wings that fold out when in use.

Due to the footnote's placement in this sentence, add "typically after "cover" to avoid confusion when you follow it with the mention of the Beetles not having wings.

> While beetles do possess elytra, they cannot fly, as they do not have wings

I recommend swapping the order of the second part of this sentence. Something like "While beetles do possess elytra, they do not have wings meaning they cannot fly". This is because them having wings is directly relevent to the first half of the sentence and their inability to fly is simply a consequence.

Just also wanna say, one paragraph is fine here. It wouldn't make sense to break this up so the format here is good.

## Behavior crit

This is where the page gets a bit less strong but there's a lot to work with.

The first problem is that it isn't very well organised. Points are loosely connected but still follow each other without much transition. This is mainly because of the lack of seperate paragraphs but there's a few seperate points that would be better together. I am going to critique this based on the order I think this section should go but you don't have to follow my advice in this exactly. You decide what you prefer.

Open with how they behave when they are not hunting. Do they have a hierarchy? Is there anything else that they do which are reminiscent of Frontrooms beetles? Is there anything other than blowing up that is completely different to Frontrooms beetles? How social are they with each other? This doesn't have to be long but it gives an idea of how they are straight away. This would also be a good place to include areas they typically inhabit.

> While hunting they will keep to the shadows until they find prey.

This seems a bit too obvious of a choice. I know it seems odd me bringing it up here but I think it could be built into a bigger point. Do they prefer shadowed areas as a whole?

> It should be noted that Beetles pose a real, tangible threat in areas with dense foliage where the ground is not visible due to their small size and ability to hide.

This can be moved to this paragraph. I think this sentence could be reworded too. It's very obvious in what it's saying rn. First of, the opening "It should be noted" isn't needed. Instead, treat this as a fact that's being stated. "where the ground is not visible" can be removed too as you're hanging too much on the location and not the entity. This is a really interesting point so don't get held back. "ability to hide" I think would be worded with something like "reputation for hiding" or "tendency to hide". Something like this is a less obvious call to their intelligence and instead presents it as something they do because of their intelligence.  
Also, finishing this paragraph on this sentence is a nice way of leading into their hunting behaviour.

> Beetles are predators that hunt in swarms.

We have just been told that they're predators in the sentence before. This would be a better opportunity to use the footnote in the main body of text instead. Something like "Beetles hunt in swarms that can reach up to thousands in numbers."

> they will attempt to enter any opening into flesh

What happenings if there are no openings?

> This process will continue until the creature expires from either internal bleeding or the swarm runs out of beetles.

The "either" is in the wrong place "should be after "until". I don't think "from internal bleeding" is a needed addition either. There's lots of ways this could kill something and lots of things they could kill so it's best to leave it open.

> The remaining swarm will then proceed to consume and reproduce inside of the carcass until eventually repeating the process.

This sentence is kinda abrupt. Instead, I recommend making the carcas th subject. Open with something like "If a carcass is produced" or change the previous sentence to say that the process usually continues until the creature expires and start this sentence with something like "The resulting carcass" (If you choose to do the latter, have a sentence after this and the next sentence saying about how they'll alternatively run out). "The remaining swarm will then proceed to consume and reproduce inside" will need to be reworded if you choose to open with the carcass but the words used are fine so they only have to be changed to make grammatical sense. Instead of sayig " until eventually repeating the process", end with a reference to what makes them decide they're done with a carcass. We can infer that this is a process that is repeated but we don't know what makes beetles decide they're done with a carcass.

> Beetles are unique in the sense that they attack both wanderers and entities.

This isn't really unique but still important information.

> Beetle swarms will ignore a wanderer if they are already being pursued by an entity and instead pursue the entity

Does this also apply to other entities being attacked by entities?

The part about beetles and levels is a bit of a tangent and overexplained. It could be cut down a lot.

> Beetles naturally avoid any entrance into any sort of level, and reside on the level they are rendered in.

Remove the first "any" and reword to "entrances" so you don't repeat any. "rendered" is an odd word to use here. It has heavy connotations with coding/gaming due to modern use. It's fine to use words that have different modern connotations but also I'm not sure what you actually mean by this? I recommend replacing it.

> This is likely to avoid entering a sparsely populated level.

This sentence can be removed. It's repeated later and doesn't fit in here.

> The only way for beetles to spread is through deliberate transportation via travelers or random no-clipping.

Honestly, I think this would be better without the random no-clipping. It makes them a bit more unique. I can't think of any other entities who do that. This is just an opinion thing tho and you don't have to change if you don't want to.

> Beetles will immediately self-terminate upon entry into a Sublevel, or Enigmatic Level where entities are not present. This applies even if there are wanderers on the said level. This does not apply to Normal Levels.

This seems like a strange addition. It's good to not overexplain things but it doesn't make sense for this to not also apply to normal levels. If you wanna keep it, let me know why in a reply just so I make sense of it.  
Otherwise, this is three sentences that could be cut down to one. Something like "Beetles will immediately self-terminate upon entry to a sublevel or enigmatic level where entities are not present, regardless of if wanderers are also on the level." As you are specific with where, you don't need to add the sentence about normal levels.

> Beetles also tend to avoid areas of high human/entity population, despite their ability to easily kill and consume any wanderer in the level they inhabit. The reason the beetles avoid high population density areas is unknown, but it is believed that the swarm would diminish faster than it would reproduce.

This sentence is too obvious. It's literally just stating their abilities. As there's uncertainty around this, this would be a good time to word this like "Beetles have been observed" to imply a lesser degree of knowledge. These two sentences could be combined by removing "despite their ability to easily kill and consume any wanderer in the level they inhabit" and "the reason the beetles avoid high population density areas is unknown," then rewording so the two connect more smoothly.

## Biology crit

This is really long so I'll give less here for now. I'm not a fan of the firesalt choice. It makes sense if you approch this purely scientifically but it loses some of the Backrooms aspects. I personally would prefer this be removed. It makes it feel like you've just taken a real world animal and combined it with a Backrooms object rather than creating a Backrooms interpretation of a real world animal. You don't have to remove the firesalt completely if you don't want to but I think it should, at the least, play a lesser role.

That's all from me for now. I really like these guys. They're a lot of fun and feel pretty classic but the writing is up to date. Feel free to ask any questions or start a discussion and let me know when you're ready for me to check the page again. I really hope this hasn't been left too late because I like these guys a lot.
