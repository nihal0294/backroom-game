This will be a quick summarization of my collective thoughts on this page and how it tries to execute its themes; I will be turning a blind eye to any induvial nitpicks I have with specific sections of this work, as I feel going over so would not benefit you as a writer.

When I read an emotional page, I expect two main things:

- A person/object to identify with.
- Emotions to align myself with

It is for these reasons that I believe this text failed to evoke anything in me. On the topic of the first point, I don't really know who this page is centered around. And, I don't mean *who* in terms of a name or a background; I instead mean it in a "this person has no depth or identity to them" way. Early in the text, you have the chance to establish a character with this singular sentence:

> The time I fell off the swing after my brother pushed me too hard, the first time I electroplated copper to a quarter, my high school graduation, the day I met my first girlfriend, when I moved into my first apartment.

But, as is emblematic of the rest of your work, I'm not really told anything personable here. Everything here is especially generic and not very unique events in one's life. *Barring the "the first time I electroplated copper to a quarter" section, when critiquing this, their background as a physicist was known to me, but trying to communicate that here through this tidbit doesn't work. It seems odd and non-descript.* **There is no person here.** I couldn't tell you this person's hobbies, what type of friends they like, what their motivations are, who they align themselves with, their mental state, or really anything about the person because I'm not really fed anything here. This person is a vessel for which you communicate this specific phenomenon through, and it doesn't feel like anything beyond that.

As for my aforementioned second point, the emotions that you want me to feel aren't evoked strongly enough to actually tell me if they're intended or not.  You want me to feel nostalgia, fear, sadness. You want me to relate to the character's longing for their life before the Backrooms. But, you don't put a lot of work into actually shoving these feelings down my throat. You kind of just go "Oh, I need to make the reader feel nostalgia, so I'll include reminiscent descriptions here, and then go back to my clinical parts to tell the reader what is actually happening."

And, as a sidenote, god, I hate the clinical parts of this page. You do not need to explicitly/bluntly tell the reader everything that is physically occurring to have them understand things. Ambiguity is something that you need to learn to work with; people coming out of your page with multiple interpretations is absolutely amazing. If you dropped all the clinical sections here and really dived into who the speaker is as a person and why they're seeing what they're seeing, you suddenly don't need to explain a whole lot and you can just do away with the explanations you've laid out for the reader.

I'll end it there to try and keep things concise (because if I don't I'll end up ranting about the hidden potential of this concept, which doesn't *really* exist due to all the hope I have for it being based off of "you can make this experience emotional" which can be done with, like, 85% of all concepts) but on a read through of your revisions, I was honestly pretty disappointed with what I saw. I probably should have been a bit more blunt with what I was thinking at the time, but I believe things could have been executed much better. This will be a down-vote until further deliberation makes me (most likely) change it to a no-vote.
