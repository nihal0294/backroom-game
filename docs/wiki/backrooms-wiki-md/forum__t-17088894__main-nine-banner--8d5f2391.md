Great concept; but not execution

11 Apr 2025 10:21

Aight so I have a few things to talk about here,  
The idea to incorporate the main 9 into the banner I like very much; but the way you went about it is a bit *eeh* for me. Here's why:

The letters are very barely visible and are a literal accessibility hazard imo; I can't read them without taking my eyes out in the process.  
Maybe giving them a full border would've solved said issue.

Tess's pose I like very much; but they way it's shaded looks a bit weird to me; especially since the line borders inconsistently switch from lighter tone to darker tone throughout the whole body and it looks a bit confusing and off.

The head is peak tho, I'll give you that, I like it very much.

Now the idea to incorporate the background into the text is not bad; but very badly executed. It personally took me a good minute to notice that the exit sign even said "The". And the graffiti saying "You've been here before" is very unclear and I only noticed this as I'm typing, as well as being another accessibility hazard. Not enough contrast at all.

Also the background image itself is very low quality compared to the rest of the image and I can't help but think that you sourced it from somewhere online without crediting it. It also causes unnecessary quality contrast on the art itself.

The word Wikidot at the bottom is very tiny and spread apart that I also only noticed it while writing this; which this is like the 4th or 5th time I'm looking at this. I think giving it more space would've solved this issue.

Overall, while I think the idea is good and there was a valid attempt to make something here; I can't let this be our banner for a multitude of reasons, especially being a serious accessibility hazard and not something you'd put on a visible-to-everyone public banner at all.

Re: Great concept; but not execution

11 Apr 2025 21:00

Hello. Thanks for the Critique and the insight. I might be able to explain some of the choices you mentioned:

- As for the letters, I don't see too much of a problem with them. The black and white colors for their shadow effect is based on what lets the letters best pop out, so they can be readable. I attempted to make the letters visible at different brightness levels and sizes, but I apologize for not considering how it might affect people with visual impairments.

- As for my inclusion of the site mascot, Tesseract, the reason the line borders are not consistently darker is because Tesseract is shown as a piece of graffiti on the wall, rather than being 3-dimensional.

- And as you pointed out, the "You've been here before quote" is not in full visibility and the text saying "Wikidot" at the bottom is quite small. These are a purposeful choices, as I did not want to overload the image with too much frontal detail, and the inclusion of the quote and the wikidot text serve more of a purpose as easter-eggs for those who look closely at the image, rather than details that a person would pick up on their first glance at the banner.

- As for why the background is low quality, it is because I had to zoom the image in from how I originally made it (a zoom in of around 20-30% or so), because originally I had made the "backrooms" text too small, and I had already merged the layers for the art piece, so rather than reconstruct everything I chose to enlarge the entire piece. Another thing that might make the background appear low-quality is because I applied a texture layer to it, which may have merged as an image rather than as a vector (this decreases the resolution for the texture). But I can assure you that the background is self-made.

All that being said, I think that maybe the reason the piece seems off to some people is that perhaps they are viewing it in mobile, rather than on desktop. In hindsight, I have only made the banner knowing how discord appears on my browser, and I do not know how the banner might appear on mobile or on the desktop app.  
Of course, I appreciate the critique, and how you brought attention to how it might be an accessibility issue for people with visual impairments.
