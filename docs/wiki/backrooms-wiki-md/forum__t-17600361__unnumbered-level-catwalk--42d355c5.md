I think the biggest retort to this message of "Why should this level exist?" is the simple question: "Why not?"

We don't necessarily need levels or entities or stories within the Backrooms. Kane Pixels didn't *need* to make his videos on YouTube. And looking at your history, you didn't *need* to make a meadow-based level. After all, we already have those.

But that's not the point. The point is creation. The point is expression. The point is to take someone by the hand and to invite them to explore and ponder a new point of view. For this level, it was the feelings and emotions around being trans.

The problem aside from this kind of "critique," aside from it insinuating that the emotions and feelings of being trans are not "worthy" of having a level dedicated to them, is that there's this assumption that the act of creation and sharing isn't a worthy cause on its own. This kind of gatekeeping does nothing to make the author better, and usually, just serves to boost the critic's ego.

To your credit, you at least acknowledged that it was well-written and didn't downvote it, but I highly encourage you to look within yourself and question why you said anything at all.

Despite my fellow colleagues being cordial and kind about your message, I personally don't feel that you deserve this sort of kindness.

You question why a level needs to exist, which already undermines your critique. As there is a reason for such works to exist, especially works that depict realistic damage that is done to the mentality of trans people, especially trans women. While the wiki is based on writing for a fictional liminal space, there is still an aspect of realism and mental anguish in the foundation of the backrooms; trans affirmation and struggles can be a part of that. Just because it is the central point to ONE article doesn't mean it is indicative of a whole canon or an entire storyline. There is no trans or LGBTQ canon, only articles, characters, and storylines that have small sprinkles of said topics or are wholly based on them, like this article. Pain is universal to the backrooms, which includes trans affirmation and trans hate, and thus makes this article a valid piece to be in this wiki.

However, if I may get personal, you bring into question why there should be anything related to trans affirmation in a community that is built by trans and gender nonconforming people. And you yourself state that you are LGBTQ as well, which is also built by trans and gender nonconforming people. If you are American, which I am assuming you are, then you must know that you have the right to be who you are now due to trans people. Stonewall was started by a black trans woman (<https://www.si.edu/stories/marsha-johnson-sylvia-rivera-and-history-pride-month>), the spark that ignited the queer rebellion and the start of LGBT rights in America. The very rights you have as a cis LGBT person are thanks to trans people, trans women, black trans women. And you are insinuating and questioning why trans stories should even have a place to stand with other LGBT stories is hypocritical and harmful. Just because it doesn't fit your view on what the backrooms are and what stories should be told doesn't mean it shouldn't exist. Trans stories and articles deserve the same right to be told as any other LGBT story on this site.

Because without the T in LGBT, you would not have the same right to even be sharing your existence in a place like this.

Do your research, listen to trans voices, learn where your rights came from, and don't be transphobic.

Sincerely, a trans person.
