Project Providence: Report

Project Providence, our largest intelligence-gathering operation conducted against multiple major groups, has been an unparalleled success. As a matter of fact, it was a far greater success than we had initially thought possible. Due to Backrooms Robotics taking significantly longer than expected to patch the security exploit we were using to control the Robovacs, we were able to use them as a distraction for several months rather than a handful of weeks.

As you are aware, Project Providence was originally intended to be a brief operation. Peculiar Robovac behaviour would serve as a brief distraction for security teams, allowing for our infiltrators to plant bugs within a handful of key locations. When Backrooms Robotics did not quickly patch the bug we were using to control the Robovacs, I made the decision to expand and alter the scope of Project Providence.

We increased the amount of active Robovacs, creating a greater distraction and allowing for bugs to be planted in even more locations. In addition, we also took advantage of the confusion to implant more infiltrators within major groups. Security efforts being focused on investigating Robovacs actually made things easier for our operatives. Agents implanted within security teams could justify their presence in conference rooms and other sensitive locations as "checking for Robovacs".

After Backrooms Robotics patched the security vulnerability we were using, Madame Holmes proposed that we release a portion of the information gathered to the public. I proposed that we claim that we gathered the information using the Robovacs, as it would hopefully distract from the true source of the information and falsely convince them that the risk had passed. Madame Holmes agreed. This deception has been largely successful in convincing not just major groups but most wanderers that the Robovacs were the threat. Outside of a handful of conspiracy-related forums…

I paused writing my report to Sub-Sector Colombina for a moment. Out of curiosity, I had browsed the Nightstalkers forum to see what they had made of the entire affair. I was relieved and almost disappointed to see that they had fallen for our deception just the same as everybody else in the Backrooms. Some had noticed the inconsistencies, but it was just a handful of people.

I jotted down the usernames of those who had noticed the inconsistencies nonetheless. They might potentially be useful to our recruiters. One of the users - EyesOpen - demonstrated a level of technical aptitude that could potentially be of great use to us. As for the rest of the people on the forum, they had proved themselves just as easily misled as everybody else.

As I started writing the report again, my mind drifted to memories from my childhood in Level 11. There had been a pair of street magicians. A small crowd would always gather around them, watching the tricks they performed, trying to work out how they did them, and laughing when one of their tricks went wrong - which they did at least once a show. With nothing better to do, I decided to follow them around and watch every one of their impromptu shows.

I eventually came to notice something - every one of the "mistakes" they made seemed just as practised as any of the other tricks they performed. You wouldn't notice it if you watched just one show, and you probably wouldn't notice it even if you attended multiple - not unless you were obsessively paying attention to every detail of their performance like I was.

The next thing I came to notice was that there weren't two magicians - there were three. I only noticed it when I found myself in a spot where I could watch the crowd rather than just the show. The third magician wasn't part of the show; he was in the audience. While the crowd stared at the two magicians in the centre, focussed entirely on their tricks and trying to prove themselves smart by working out how they did it, the third magician was shifting through the crowd, carefully lifting wallets from pockets.

The entire scheme was quite similar in mechanism to what we pulled off with the Robovacs. The Robovacs were the first and second magicians, performing a flashy stunt to capture the attention of the marks, while our infiltrators were the third magician - stealing secrets while everybody's back was turned.

… the success of this project shows that the best way of keeping attention away from our covert operations is to draw the attention somewhere else. Flashy and highly visible stunts capture everybody's attention, allowing us to work unobserved.

Signed,

V. Saxon,

Director of Espionage, Sub-Sector Pantalone
