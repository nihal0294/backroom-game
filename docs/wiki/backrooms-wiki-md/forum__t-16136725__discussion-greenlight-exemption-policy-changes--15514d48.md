## This is a thread for discussion regarding the topic in the title. Please avoid going off-topic as much as possible.

It’s been some time since [Greenlight Exemption](greenlight-policy.md) was introduced to the site, and it may be time to adjust its policies as the site grows and changes. There have been suggestions to remove exemptions entirely, to increase the requirements for it, and to keep it as-is. **Please discuss your opinions on the Greenlight Exemption policy, and what changes (if any) you would like to see made.** This is not an official vote, but rather a place to discuss the topic. We highly encourage users to stick around and see what others have to say after you’ve posted your thoughts.

A quick FYI, as a lot of acronyms and terms used in discussions about greenlights are very similar:

- GL - greenlight; permission granted to post a page to the wiki
- GLer - greenlighter; users who can give out greenlights
- GLT - greenlighter trainee; users training to become greenlighters
- GLE - greenlight exemption; the policy allowing users who fulfill certain requirements to post to the wiki without a greenlight)
- Greenflame Initiative - a test for users considered to be good greenlighter candidates without long-term training; separate from the greenlighter trainee process
- Greenflame trialee - users being tested under the Greenflame Initiative

And a link to the [Greenlight Policy](greenlight-policy.md) for reference.

I like the idea of greenlight exemption. I think that as long as we have some type of greenlight policy, we should also have an accompanying exemption with requirements. I also think we’re at a point where the combination of greenlighting + the crit checkmark system in the Discord server is making the posting process faster and not as much if a struggle as before. GLE should have its requirements raised, both for page count minimum AND vote minimum.

Currently the requirements are 3 pages of at least +15 with adjustments made for tales and contest entries. I think we could adjust to the following:

- 5 pages minimum meeting the below rating requirements
- +30 for non-contest standard pages (levels, entities, objects, etc)
- +15 for non-contest tales
- +40 for contest standard pages
- +20 for contest tales
- Joke pages, translations, essays, hub pages (including Groups pages), and co-authored pages of any type do not count toward usual GLE
- Translation-specific GLE remains the same (3 pages of at least +15, limited to translations only)

“Good grief, aren’t these numbers a bit high?” Yes, they are. This is by design. The idea is that for someone to gain Greenlight Exemption, they need to show above-average skill at writing because they need to show that they can post a page with less critique. I’ve purposely listed high numbers here, but I wouldn’t be opposed to numbers a little lower than that.

That's all I really want to highlight for this, but that's my suggestion! Slightly higher expectations, an application system, and a periodic review of those who are exempt!

I think that the idea of reviewing GLE is a wonderful idea; if someone does get GLE and it turns out bad, it'll be a far more limited problem. We should do it every year, since the reviews would take a while.

---

On the other hand, I think the idea of having an application system is a pretty bad idea. What else would they be reviewing? SPaG? Ratings do that just fine. I think that the system of looking at how highly rated an author's pages are is the best way to keep it about how much the readers of the site enjoy their work.

---

On what said, I don't think it's a lack of patience on display here. I think it's that the 9-crit + gl crit system involves so many people (up to 11, 9 critters, 1 gl-er, and the original author) and their schedules — and for good pages, sometimes later crits seem pretty pointless — that it can feel like you're waiting around for no reason. It's a massive hassle that people only agree to participate in because:

- They REALLY want to post work on this site in particular (even though Fandom has less hassle than this)
- They will only have to endure it thrice (or you want to make it four times and that's fine)

These pieces of patience don't last forever! Remember that this 10 day timeframe Pedder talked about was not quietly patiently waiting, it was him contantly applying crit and waiting for new crit, because he wants to get the page done before it's been a week and a half.

Also, a review on current GLEers is a great idea! But I would just make sure that they live up to the new standard, not scrutinize them subjectively.

The one issue I take with this is, on what grounds should GLE be revoked? Personally, I think it should only go as far as having a certain amount of GLE-posted pages fall into deletion, or in cases of suspected GLE abuse. Furthermore, I also do not believe that GLE should be applied for. It should always be automatically granted when the required numbers are met, and only evaluated if the GLE author is brought under scrutiny. This prevents bias (intentional or otherwise) and additional work for Staff/GLers.

I don't mind the idea of a revocable GLE. That does seem reasonable and fair. Here's my take on it:

Anyone who does not perform well in a given period can have GLE taken away from them after going through a review (Given how long writing can take, I'd say somewhere from every 6 months to a year).

This review doesn't check activity, but rather, the performance of their last several pages.

- If someone has not posted any pages in the timeframe for the review, they are given a pass due to having no pages to review (They did not write any poor pages, so they get a free pass for that period. People sometimes have busy lives and other interests, and not having time for writing should not impact their writing capabilities).

- Having all well-performing pages that meet this standard also passes.

- If they post several pages that perform lower than a given quota/standard, then they have GLE revoked and must publish another set of pages through the GL process that meet the standards that originally granted them GLE. If the standard is 3 pages at +15 or higher, then they must meet this standard again, taking into account tales and contest entries of course. The same goes for any new standard.

I personally like a strike system. Some poorly performing pages is okay, but too many strikes in a review period, and you're out! Not every idea is liked, and that's okay. Authors need \*some\* room for error. Of course, this will take into account tales and contest entries, so the exact quotas for each page that determines these strikes may vary based on the kind of page. It could be three strikes, two, or some other number. We do want to make it fair enough to allow for some error, but not so strict that people who are qualified frequently lose GLE due to a few mistakes.

If someone nearly reaches the strike limit during one review, there could be some kind of trial to allow them to keep their GLE, but need the next few pages to be signed off by a GLer before posting (This is just a once read through and approval/rejection. Not an in-depth critique or anything). And of course these pages must be successful to continue keeping their GLE. If the pages fail, the GLE can still be removed next review. This means staff cannot outright remove or give GLE outside of meeting or failing the requirements, but they can deem individual cases to need a push to improve quality through this GLer sign off system.

I suppose you could always enforce this signoff system, but I think that unless someone shows that they need a push towards improvement, it's not strictly necessary. Doing it as I suggested above also gives authors who prove themselves a little more freedom to do as they please. Critique and a GLer signoff is always an option, but only a requirement to those who show they need it.

If keeping track of who needs a GLer signoff is a concern, then you can have some kind of list or document for that, or even announce the results of reviews publicly, much like you do for page greenlights.

As for the standards to achieve GLE in general, I don't mind increasing it a little.

My suggestion would follow in line with Eden's suggestions quite a bit:

+20 for non-contest, non-tale pages

+15 for non-contest tales

+25 for non-tale contest pages

+20 for tale contest pages

A slight increase for contest entries, and a slight decrease for tales. Contest tales become the normal page rating. And of course, other page types might get their own values to meet, depending on how many people read them.

This also acts in a way as a compromise between all the differing opinions I've seen so far. It restricts the staff biases and prevents staff abuse, and it allows people who put the hard work in the right to GLE. It can work whether you alter the initial GLE requirements or not, and it also allows a way for staff to give a push to those who need it. You can also use the GLE tag for pages to help determine which pages are used for the reviews to revoke GLE, and which ones to review to grant GLE. I like the idea of the tag.

Overall, this system is fair. It isn't too strict, and it allows room for freedom and growth.

I am also fine with the system remaining as-is, but if people deem it to need change, then these are my thoughts on that.

Noting what Princebun said about people having patience issues when '10 days' is an 'extremely fast amount of time', I would like to offer a rebuttal. Many users don't contact a greenlighter until they have finished the draft(I'm referring to forum critique, not discord), and have posted on the draft critique forum. Sometimes, even after they believe they have 'finished' it, they wait for regular users to critique it, and then contact a greenlighter. What I'm trying to say is, is that often, a writer has already worked on the article, and is ready to move on to the stage of writing it with a professional's advisment, and the estimated 10 days plus the time spent writing the article does drag on. I personally would already have at least three articles in total posted to the wiki since I've joined of decent quality if I weren't restrained with a long time period waiting for *someone* who has the permission to and knows properly how to come look at my article and point out flaws for me. However, that being said, I realize that these 'greenlighters' are people, and they have their own lives. But that's not what we are discussing. I just felt the need to say that.  
Regarding the actual policy, I feel it should stay the same. There is the old saying 'If something isn't broken, there is no need to fix it'. I think this fittingly applies as the policy has worked out well so far. The policy is mainly in place to get users who aren't skilled at writing continued help as their producing more articles, yes? It's been effectively doing it for awhile. Raising the already high number of votes wouldn't really help with that. I want to also point out that votes are user-based opinions, and don't necessarily point to 'Oh, this person is a great writer because they met the required amount of upvotes for these three articles!'. Some users downvote articles just because they feel like it, or just disagree with one small thing. And just because a user produces high-quality articles with a reviewers help doesn't mean they're good at writing without one's help. Also, some articles receive less attention (or more attention) than others, especially in contests because there are so many other entries, because another/the author is well-known(No offense to you, Mr. Goerman [You do produce great pieces], but you're the perfect example)(applying this in or out of a contest), because of how new or less-known the author is(both in AND out of contests), or because the article is just overlooked(both).  
I suggest that the amount of articles require be kept the same, but there be a quality check or some kind of test those who have greenlight exemption to make sure they are still producing good articles. If they fail, or aren't, they have to produce a certain amount of articles under the green light system that have to reach a required amount of upvotes, just to make sure they're still in the swing of things after getting GLE. So yeah, pretty much seconding Mr. Crafter about that.
