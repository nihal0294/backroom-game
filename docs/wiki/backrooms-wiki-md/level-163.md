> *"Vertigo is anguish itself. I am on a precipice, and I understand that the possibility of throwing myself over exists."*

— Jean-Paul Sartre, *Being and Nothingness* (1943)

---

![A view over the concrete edge.](http://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/level-163/163main "I peered into the void of her eyes,")

**Level 163** manifests as an expansive void containing various high-rise apartments suspended within a thick, seemingly endless fog. It is unknown where the abyss of the level leads to, if anywhere.

Due to the distance between the skyscrapers, one is unable to travel further than the building one initially appears on (designated the "main" building"). Theoretically, if one managed to enter **Level 163** with something to keep themselves in the air (such as a hang-glider or parachute), it would be possible to explore the other buildings, although there are no records of whether this has been done nor if it has been successful.

All entrances to **Level 163** are done through a singular elevator located in the center of the building, serving as the sole entrance to the level. Upon exiting the elevator, the doors will shut and it will rapidly descend downwards, never to be seen again. Besides the cubical structure containing the elevator, various AC units, and electrical equipment, the roof of the building is empty. The floor of the roof is composed of solid concrete.

The air of the level is cold; a constant, audible breeze flows through and between the skyscrapers, chilling the level significantly. The wind's strength is strong enough to blow small objects off over the ledge, but not strong enough to force a person over. **Level 163** is devoid of any other meteorological phenomenon such as rain or snow.

Whilst within the level many wanderers have reported sensations of the desire to jump off of the main building. Such desires are easily suppressible, although one should take its presence into account when approaching the edges.

The fate of those who fall into the void of **Level 163** is unknown. Although it is commonly assumed that jumping into the abyss would lead to one's death, many suggest that the level is an offshoot or extension of [The Blue Channel](blue-channel.md) and that one will enter it after falling for a certain amount of time, although this claim is unconfirmed.

The void yearned for me.

The void was one lacking a disguise, as one would not be necessary. It possessed neither an alibi nor a veil; I could see where the concrete met the fog and the fog met the void. It was universal, surrounding every corner, each crevice.

The void called for me not with a demand, rather, it asked me slowly with courtesy. It made a request, one that could be denied. It wanted me to escape the concrete, something I myself would want to do. It spoke concisely, and yet it discoursed with sinister nature advice on how I should make the leap. It spoke as if something were to be hidden within the fog that would necessitate concealment from me. I stepped towards the edge, attempting to ignore the guidance given to me, although despite this I still subconsciously took it into account.

I repulsed instinctively from the fringe of the concrete, my blood flowing towards the center of the structure. The words the void gave looped around once more, and I approached the edge again. Each new cycle of approaching and deserting the edge yielded the same result; never moving forward but never pushed back. The void spoke softly, asking me to come closer again. I reasonably disdained from listening, for fear of the void attempting to trick me.

And yet I was bewildered. My eyes were compelled to peer downwards, locking eyes with the void. The prior sensations from before were suddenly cleansed upon my realization. I had no other choice of where to go, nor did I have a decision to make; I was fighting my own thoughts while the void smirked on the sidelines.

Peering over the edge dissipated the nausea of vertigo; my eyes focused on nonexistent points on the horizon, attempting to gauge the distance, yet a horizon I could not find, nor could the distance be determined. I looked back into the concrete, attempting to get another reference point on how far I would be jumping. I held my breath, found a clear path, and I ran.

![An image of the abyss.](http://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/level-163/163dive "And the void stared back, gazing blankly.")

I dove through the void, and I lost myself within it.
