Regarding the use of sensitive topics in writing (cw suicide and gore talking)

12 Jul 2023 13:46

> DISCLAIMER THIS POST IS ABOUT SUICIDE AND GORE USE IN TS WRITING SO IF YOU DON’T FEEL ABOUT TALKING ABOUT THIS YOU ARE FREE TO PASS!
>
> I'd also like to remind you that the French Internet and French culture are very different, that the members are ESL and come from very different writing cultures. Please don't take certain members' responses personally, as this is more likely to damage your argument than anything else. Also, link parts that you seem to have misunderstood, to avoid misunderstandings. No insults to any member will be accepted in this thread.

Hello, this post is co-created by several members about the use the suicide in TS writing, using phenomenon 99 as the primary example since it started the conversation. Some people expressed issues about this particular page and the mindset of suicide writing.

First, in this particular page, the subjects have been poorly handled in some people’s opinion. Indeed, the use of gore is very cliche and not expanded at all, just serving as a way to induce violence in the writing, but not in an original or interesting way. The presence of mass murder, with everyone dying, is a very manga-like thing, with an emergence of a cliche blood god that kills everyone because of madness and murder. It is not really coherent and just feels grandiloquent for the sake of it, but badly expanded. The same opinion can be said about the use of suicide. Indeed, its use is not really important nor obligatory, because the article work with just casualties. If someone dies, they come back to life with some consequences. You don’t need suicide for that, this is something passive that does not require the use of such a touchy subject. The page can feel like an excuse of a page to write about this particular subject.

Moreover, the page portrays suicide as something that can be glorified. Indeed, the rise of such a powerful, incredible superior being thanks to the sacrifice of many, many people is such an important part of the page that it begins to be seen as something glorious and positive. This is not something that is visible on the main page, where the part shows that wanderers should not commit this act, but the second and third are too positive in the way the writing is set.

Thirdly, the age of the author should be taken into consideration. Indeed, the way suicide is portrayed and used by someone that is very young and not very experienced with such a topic leads the page to be very fragile in its presentation. We are not trying to appear as superior in any way, but instead expressing concerns about how the way someone that is not very experienced enough with gore and suicide is detrimental. At younger ages, people are more inclined to talk about those difficult subjects in a clumsy way. This is something that can be seen with the cliche aspect of the page. This is not a generality, but this is something that has to be taken into consideration, which leads us to the next point.

With the suicide and gore elements used here, the page should have gone in a more deep greenlight process in order to make sure writers use those topics well. The gravity aspect needs to be handled with care (for the offsets, mainly), and this is not the case here. This needs to be taken well. That means important people need to handle such cases. Since staff is the instance that is here to make sure rules are respected, the approbation of a staff member should be involved to make sure the subject is used in the correct way (and if absolutely no staff can, pause the page for a moment since it’s such a sensitive topic). This could also be talked with several greenlighters because they also know how writing should be handled.

Lastly, some people wanted to talk about the disclaimer part. if it is indeed a way to present whether a page is sensitive or not, not all people see them the same way. Some people don’t care, some people think that should not be that bad because of the nature of the site… Also, the page can be talked with no filter, which piques the curiosity of people, who will read the page and not feel well about it. This is why this should not be an excuse for freewriting such a topic with no restriction (as this is the case here), because it should still be handled with extreme care, as this is, by human reasons, not something that will stop that many people (morbid curiosity). Putting a [list of suicide crisis lines](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suicide_crisis_lines) could also be beneficial and would be more useful than just a simple disclaimer.

I can’t speak on the actual portrayal in the page since I was only able to read a short section, so I’ll touch on the parts that I can comment on.

> Thirdly, the age of the author should be taken into consideration. Indeed, the way suicide is portrayed and used by someone that is very young and not very experienced with such a topic leads the page to be very fragile in its presentation.

Yes, younger authors have less experience with writing and more likely to bungle respectful portrayal. However, I hold issue with the idea that a teenage writer should be prevented from writing about a topic like suicide. Just because the topic is uncomfortable doesn’t mean that teenagers don’t have experiences with it. Suicide and suicidal ideation affects an extremely wide age range; it’s a deeply disturbing idea, but there are teenagers who are suicidal and/or have experienced loss because of suicide. They have the right to put their feelings into writing, and they have a unique perspective on the topic. There is no reason why we should be preventing young users from writing the topic for the sole reason of age.

> if it is indeed a way to present whether a page is sensitive or not, not all people see them the same way. Some people don’t care, some people think that should not be that bad because of the nature of the site…

I heavily disagree with this point. I read this page as a draft before Ratif posted this. I saw the trigger warning, did my emotional preparation, and I stopped reading when I realized it was too much for me. The purpose of a trigger/content warning is to **warn** a user of the contents. It does not hold the responsibility to decide for the user whether the content should be read or not. If someone ignores a content warning, they do so at their own risk. It falls upon **them** to stop if the page is triggering or too upsetting to continue with a good state of mind.

> Also, the page can be talked with no filter, which piques the curiosity of people, who will read the page and not feel well about it. This is why this should not be an excuse for freewriting such a topic with no restriction (as this is the case here), because it should still be handled with extreme care, as this is, by human reasons, not something that will stop that many people (morbid curiosity).

Again, I heavily disagree with this. A reader’s morbid curiosity is not an excuse to blame the writer if a work has proper warning. Like I said above, there is a message of warning placed there by the author, and if someone chooses to continue it is **at their own risk**. I stopped when I realized the draft was too much for me. I didn’t blame Ratif for showing me an upsetting draft. I said “I can’t finish this one, it’s too much for me” and Ratif accepted that.

Other users’ lack of good sense in discussing a page’s themes is also not the writer’s fault. when people were talking about this page uncensored and without spoiler tags in the middle of the main channel in the discord, that was the fault of **those people** for lacking good judgement in openly discussing suicide without any sort of spoiler tagging to hide the messages.
