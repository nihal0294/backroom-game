I have a qualm with the language that's been used.

> > …but to no avail, the windows came up unbreakable.
>
> > This expansive hellscape we know as Level 927…
>
> > HOWEVER, as calm and lively as the level seems, you need to head to airport terminal 27 or 48 before the level instantly changes into night.
>
> etc…

Yes, this is a "clinical language" issue. Language that is too emotive will detach readers from the document, and in the end, this is a fictional catalogue, so please write it using language more suited for a catalogue.

12 Aug 2024 20:42

Thanks for the review! I appreciate it. Just wanted you to know, though; that I used second person writing because there was no other way to explain what I wrote without making it too confusing. I just wanted to *twist*, (if you will) the boundaries of what we'd normally consider a catalogue. Very few Levels in the Backrooms use this level of writing. However what I've noticed is- that all levels written in second person "**spoke**" to me in a way that feels unique. It makes me feel like someone is actually explaining the level to me, (like Broogli), and adds another layer of immersion. I used words like "you" and "your" to inform the reader what one should do when they get sent here. I am definitely aware that this style of writing is less descriptive, but I want the reader to be able to "**imagine**" the details of the level. I'm not trying to force this upon you, just wanted to inform why I chose to write in this style. I'm glad that you got the time to tell me your thoughts on this level, and took the time to read it. Have a nice day!
