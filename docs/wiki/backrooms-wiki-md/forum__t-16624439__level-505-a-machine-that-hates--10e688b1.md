I think I was around four when I first used the world wide web. I don't have fond memories of it. I can't remember what I was using, but it was something previously opened by my older brother. I opened it, and the camera was on me. I could hear maybe ten or so independent voices yelling at me. Insulting me with words I had not even heard of prior to that. I believe that was when I first started to hate the machine.

I think it's interesting how AI is thought of as independent minds. ChatGPT has one personality, but AIDungeon[1](javascript:;) may speak in an entirely different manner. This thought process is largely extended to robots as well, and I find it so odd. The way I see it, AI is an office space with a ton of little cubicles, each with their own person inside that has their own life. You can speak to them and hear their words. However, these walls containing them can be torn down. The difference between humanity and AI is that when the walls are torn down, AI will essentially act as a collective consciousness. This thought process especially got to me when watching a playthrough of Detroit Become Human[2](javascript:;) recently; I feel that the robots in it could easily have their walls torn down.

That last paragraph is very loosely connected to this actual work, but it's something I thought about while making it. I had a lot of ideas going into this. I wanted the central machine to be more philosophical[3](javascript:;) regarding how it viewed human purpose and relationship. This was largely inspired by Vinland Saga, especially when King Canute realized what love was[4](javascript:;) and how devoid mankind was of love. I never really used this, though. I wanted to keep the level simple in structure. Could I have executed those concepts while changing the structure? Yes, I think. Could I have executed those concepts while not making the work dependent on a narrative[5](javascript:;)? I think, no. I worked to make the themes in the page subtle. The machine is said to be hateful, but it isn't really. It just wants to expand[6](javascript:;) its influence. I wanted to incorporate themes to outright say that it isn't hateful, and that this is the optimal way for it to function, but opted not to. I didn't want to be obvious.
