Horror literature can never really be as scary as real life because you don't have to face it every day without fail. Even if you really have nothing to be scared of, that fear - the fear that you're just going to endlessly circle the cycle of eat-work-sleep-repeat - is very real, probably more real than any of the monsters we fill our time with here writing about.

This page, deciding to grab that kind of mundane somberness by the shirt, makes a bold play. A play which I think pays off in the long run. The monochrome gradient over the screen, the different perspectives of the letters, it all works to such great effect.

It's a page which will stick in my memory far moreso than most of the contest entries here, and for that it gets a **+1**.

also, i find that this article just didn't grab me at the conceptual level not only because the fear of 'oh, im nlt doing enouh…..' is portrayed a hell of a lot, to the point of being a trope — more than that, the frame of reference that tries to goad the reader on by referring to how they *should* be feeling actively irritated me. it feels as though the page is more concerned with being special and memorable than it is with being a piece with a definite point like it's trying to be: a layer of irony that i only wish was exploited here.

it's not impossible to write the fear of suburbia, but one risks being as dull as the subject matter when they do. this fell on the side of dull for me, at the end of the day.

It'd be a +1 in another life (format)

06 Jul 2026 20:00

Novote for now. Might change whether or not I can eventually understand entirely what's going on here.

For what it's worth, I understand completely what themes and feelings the level is trying to give, and for the record not only do I have no problems with that, but I like it! It's a story about the mundane, about being and living in the mundane, and hating it. Dreams and ambitions kinda end up the same whether they're achieved or not, cus when it's all said and done and you're an adult living your life it can get… dull. and repetitive. Samey. Boring.

But where this fails is, unfortunately, it's format. I *think*, from what I gathered in the page, that the Monochrome is a place you go to when you feel like this. Probably from the Frontrooms, since apart from the story with the teacher's wife this seems to mostly take place on Earth. And to a degree I understand that, but I also can't help but feel like that's the weakest part about this page. *That it's a level.*

I would argue the Backrooms itself is already a setting to convey this idea enough. It's an interesting interpretation on why you end up in there. Life gets monotonous and boring, and where you do? Not somewhere to bring you to salvation, but a place where that feeling is personified. I think I understand that the Monochrome *isn't* that entirely, cus like the pets are there and stuff as is in one of the final letters, but that brings me to my next point.

What the hell *is* the Monochrome? I leave this page knowing a lot about the type of people who end up there, how to get there, and what it represents. What it looks like, however, I have no clue. Not that you need to do a boring and bland "Level Fortnite is the Fortnite+1 of the Backrooms and resembles the tilted towers" garbage, but if one of the many, many notes in this page actually relayed a crumb of detail about the place, that would go a long way!

While I can understand the creative choice to withhold this knowledge to a degree, all it did was cause issues for me as a reader. I couldn't visualise anything going on, and the entire time I was just asking "okay but what is this place like." This *is* a level page, after all, you'd expect there to be some exploration or explanation of the level in question. Again, clinical fluff is not needed for that, but just *something*. Unless I really misinterpreted parts, which I'm open to being corrected on, I didn't get any of that.

The actually interesting overlapping stories of people who knew each other, had similar woes, and ended up in the Monochrome (presumably) kept me intrigued, and I think they're the best parts of this page. You know how to write sad people and, to a degree, portray that sadness of the invitable monotonousness of life. I just wish other important parts of this page were given love, too
