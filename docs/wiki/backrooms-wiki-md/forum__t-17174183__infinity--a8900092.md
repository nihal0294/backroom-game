02 Aug 2025 04:37

While I can appreciate this piece's message in its most basic form, I think that the choice to show this message by lifting and repeating several other pieces in a sort of artistic plagiarism hinders this. Yes, it may be the case that revealing all of what you read before to be a tribute to other pages on the site is clever, but I don't think that making that *choice* on a metal level is satisfying to me. The message conveyed here, in my eyes, could have been better conveyed by making something that *is* original, and then revealing the influences it has; skip the Backrooms page and go right to the Wikipedia source, or hell, just draw from multiple pages.

Another issue: I personally felt that the pieces were completely disunited, and that the conclusion to this raised more questions to me about the author's mental framework of their work than the actual finished product — more specifically, it makes me feel like I'm being presented all of these little ideas because *you* personally liked them, and that the end was written as a cop-out to justify the rest of this tale.

Technically, it's fine enough, though I thought that the accountant's diary in Offset 2 was unrealistic and wasn't using the right voice and tone for what it was portraying. I also think that the introduction could have been edited to have a more definite hook.

In terms of the message which you clearly want to impart here, I am unimpressed, and that message is what makes this thing work.

After all, at the end of the day, the whole message imparted is that we can be *hopeful* that future generations will carry our ideas on and mutate them into their own proper works of art. The keyword here is *proper*, and I think that the distinction of still making art that would be completely fair and sensical with little outer context was lost here with this one.

TLDR: I like the boldness in both the message and the way it's expressed here, but disliked the actual implementation of the plagiarism; the execution wasn't enough to make me put a +1 on this.

Interesting read, Wynths. Though honestly, I find it curious that the strongest criticism you make is precisely what the work is intended to provoke: a reflection on reinterpretation, artistic heritage, and the eternal return of ideas.

Calling it "artistic plagiarism" is reducing a meta-narrative exercise to a mere copy. Not everything that is repeated is theft; sometimes, it's homage. Sometimes, it's deconstruction. Sometimes, it's exactly what's needed for the reader to stop and say, "Wait, I've seen this before."

I'm struck by the fact that you're asking for a work to defend itself "without external context," when the whole point is that nothing in this story exists in a vacuum. It's a love letter to ideas that mutate, recycle, and transform. If that's not "own," then I don't know what is.

But hey, as you said, we all read from our own mental frameworks. Maybe this one didn't resonate with you, and that's okay.

The good thing about all this, at least, is that the great thing about art is that it can be brilliant even when not everyone sees it. 😉

There will always be beauty in articles like these, and I know you'll learn that one day.

having gone through the source links, i've changed my mind. the offsets, while I do think that each one is individually executed a little worse than its predecessor, *do* take liberties with the concept at hand — RED is about the birth of war and hatred for your fellow humans as a concept, and Offset 1 is about the living being at the center of the universe.

but, having said that, I think i'll only change to a novote, because in my eyes, the execution is just a little off for Offsets 1-3.

as mentioned earlier, I think that the voice in the journal in Offset 2 isn't very good (not even the voice for Level 800.1 is but that's a separate article), the threat and pacing of Offset 3 is a bit too confusing and muddles its own development, and Offset 1 just doesn't have the same *beautiful* imagery that I wish to one day copy (lol) in my own work. Only Offset 4 bucks the trend — I think it takes its source material in a radical new direction, and it executed it just as well, if not better[1](javascript:;).

also, as a sidenote, I think that the fundamental struggles being different for Offsets 1, 3, and 4 kind of undermines the message of the piece? like, yes, they are formatted in the same way, but they cover different things from their root material, which means that they aren't *really* drawing from the source in the same way that Offsets 2 and 5 do. I dunno — it's a matter of pedantics, so I don't really think much of it, but it seems like the theme of hope and creation should actually have been inserted more superficially, and the core of these works should have been left intact. but probably doesn't matter.

so, yeah. I think, after sleeping on it, that it *is* fine in its current state, but could be improved by the author.

(and remember: Beauty is in the eye of the beholder!)
