I get that this is supposed to be minimalist, which is why I’m novoting instead of a downvote. My rule for rating things is to judge them for what they are, not for what they aren’t, and I know this isn’t supposed to be something very evocative or flowery. It’s minimalism, it’s simplicity, and every word is purely emphasizing what this page is at its core.

However, I do expect pages to still get *something* out of me.

I think the problem I have with this page is that its use of minimalism removes the gravity of the concept it presents. The generalization of the main character makes this feel hollow to me, not all-encompassing. I get that you are trying to make this applicable to any reader, but in doing so, this made it feel… empty in a way? To me, it isn’t reminiscent of something that recognizes the complexity of suicide and how much of a universal human experience it can be, instead it comes off more as careless for what the subject is experiencing.

This feels more objective than personal, and because of that, this didn’t do as much as it could’ve for me.

I think that this would’ve benefitted more from providing more characterization to the main character. Even if this is a second-person story that is supposed to be about the reader, I think it could have retained its minimalist feel while still providing something that the readers could latch onto more and feel more empathetic towards.

Overall, I like what this is and what it is at its core, but I think that it could’ve done more. Because of that, I’m novoting.

My main gripe is less centered around the main themes of the article and more around its execution. While reading I felt that the page did not exactly deliver the final bits well. It feels as though I could perceive so much more from these lines, yet the page decides to give up on it. It's like wanting to charge your phone to 100% and only stop at 70%—it still *works*, but it is not the same thing.
