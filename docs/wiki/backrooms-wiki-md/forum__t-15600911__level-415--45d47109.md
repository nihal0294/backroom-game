> Level 415 is the 416th Level of the Backrooms. It was discovered by an expedition team on April 15, 2009.

This formatting is a little outdated. Unless this little intro excerpt actually adds any meaningful information that isn't provided in the proceeding paragraphs, I recommend discontinuing it always as a rule of thumb. Also, "Level" isn't a proper noun unless referring to a specific one.

> Level 415 is a huge modern library with a total of nine floors, the height of each floor is about 2.5 meters.

Replace "is" with "being". Alternatively, you could swap the comma for a period, but that would disrupt the sentence flow unless you did some switching around.

> Its area appears to be infinite, or it may be a constantly circulating space.

Instead of saying "its area", which is a roundabout way of just referring to it directly, just say "it". Also, I understand what you mean by the second part of the sentence, but it isn't very apparent upon first glance. To simplify and allow the reader to assess thus possibility on their own, I'd opt to just say that it's probably infinite and leave it at that.

> But it will become more and more unstable as it goes deeper.

What is "but" opposing? Usually, you say but after saying something related to the proceeding sentence, but whether the level is infinite or not doesn't dictate its instability. The sentence is also extremely abrupt and out of place, as well as poorly expanded upon. What *about* the level gets more unstable? I recommend removing this sentence and saving the exploration of this phenomenon for a later part of the article.

> The ceiling is equipped with fluorescent lights, which will flicker continuously, and emit a buzzing sound similar to the fluorescent lights in Level 0.

Firstly, the second comma is unnecessary. Secondly, the word "continuously" poorly describes the lights' functionality; "frequently" would word much better as a substitute, as continuously implies that they don't stop flickering ever, which I don't think you intend. Thirdly, I recommend substituting ", which will" for "that" to further improve fluidity.

> Listening to the sound for a long time may cause the wanderer to have physiological reactions such as dizziness and nausea.

Who is "the wanderer" here? I recommend substituting it for "one" just to make it more natural.

> Any method of blocking the ear from hearing sound is possible.

This is worded very strangely. It's also unnecessary as a footnote, as it can be inferred that when sound = bad, no sound = good.

> Any outside light sources, such as flashlights, lanterns, and windows will provide no light in this level.

This seems a bit random. Why don't they provide light? It just seems like a random feature crammed in just for the sake of it. It doesn't fit with the paragraph either, and interjects rather unnaturally. Also, windows wouldn't represent an external light source when speaking about things that come from outside of the level.

> Most personnel in Level 415 reported a distinct smell of mint in the air, although the source of the smell is unclear.

Personnel of what? The M.E.G.? Please, do tell. Or change your wording to avoid confusion.

> In some cases, the smell is very strong.

This doesn't really add much of importance to the page, so I suggest that you chop it.

> The number of books on the shelves and the quantity of hostile entities gradually increase with each floor, as higher floors tend to have more books and entities than lower floors do.

The part of the sentence following the comma is literally almost the exact same as the part preceding it. Chop it.

> Thus, if one cannot locate the signs indicating the floor number, wanderer can to easily determine which floor they are on.

The English in this sentence is a lil' broken. "wanderer" —> "the wanderer". "to easily" —> "easily". How can they determine the exact floor they're on based on entity and book count alone? That's not very realistic, so perhaps state that they can **guess** the *general* number rather than **determine** the *exact* number. Also, this the first I've heard of these "signs". I recommend explaining what they are before this sentence.

> Items such as drinking glasses, backpacks, gloves, etc. are often found in Level 415's trash cans; it is understood that these items have been around since records began.

Records? Records of what? Specify that you mean records of the level to clarify.

> Its source is not yet known.

This is quite obvious from the tone of the previous sentence. This should be chopped.

> Wanderers trapped in Level 415 often use these items as props. Once taken, it will be replenished at 4:00 am each day in an unknown manner. Level 415 sometimes appears to have consumed a portion of almond water, but cannot detect human signs such as fingerprints, saliva, etc.

This whole excerpt could use a good deal of rewording to ease confusion. Remove the first sentence since it doesn't make sense, unless you had greater plans for it and I failed to assess its meaning. "it" —> "the items". "an unknown" —> "a random". The whole last sentence doesn't make sense whatsoever, and could use some clarification/explanation.

> The radio speakers in the Level 415 will play some relaxing music throughout the day, there seems to be an unknown phenomenon that affects many of the wanderers, making them feel "relaxed" and "pleasant".

Remove the first two instances of "the". Remove "some". Replace the comma with a semicolon, and proceeding the semicolon, indicate in some way that the phenomenon is connected to the music just to make it clear.

> Some of the more open centers usually have one or two cyan sofas, one or two newspapers with or almond water in a paper bag can be found on the benches.

What is a "center" in the case of this level? You seem to indirectly refer to them in text and in the image captions, so it'd be nice if you could convert them into a stable definition. The English in this sentence is pretty broken, so I suggest rewriting and rereading it aloud.

> Level 415 does not have any windows, but occasionally there is a blue wooden door on both sides of the corridor, marked "The Frontrooms", often homeless people hear the voice of people in the city through the wooden door, but wanderer in the level The wooden door cannot be opened. Any vandalism of the door is void.

I think mentioning that the level has no windows could fit in a better spot than this, as the paragraph's topic seems to have more important things to revolve around. I can't really understand much of what this sentence details; try using a grammar checker to lead you on the right path in that department. I'm sure you don't mean that homeless people randomly hear voices.

> The books on the shelves are of different sizes and colors, It is worth mentioning that the books of Level 415 are made of non-combustible paper materials, And Level 415 books have a peculiar feature: opening a book will get an unusual effect, but it may also be accompanied by certain side effects, and it is harmful to the wanderer, and even kills the wanderer

Uh. Ok I'm starting to see a trend here. Before, your sentence structure was pretty good and didn't seem to house many issues. It seems to have fallen apart about half way through. I once again advise that you use a grammar checker, but I'll give ya a few tips about frequent errors; after a comma, don't capitalize words that aren't proper nouns; as usual, you only really capitalize words apart from proper nouns when starting a new sentence wholly (like, after a full stop). Additionally, try to avoid using commas as frequently as you are, as that makes the sentence very choppy and unintelligible. Commas should act as the natural pauses one makes within speech, so try to structure them around that. There are a few rules about them otherwise, but those are more technical than the basics.

> One notable feature of Level 415 is the ability to convert specific items carried by people coming to the level into other items with the same characteristics, these same characteristics are usually associated with composition, shape, color, etc, but occasionally there are items that do not have any of the same characteristics at all. When wanderer leaves the level with a transformed item, the transformed item is restored to the original item. The most important thing is that the items that are converted are different each time, We conducted an experiment on this feature:

This part includes many of the same mistakes that I pointed out previously. Additionally, this feature doesn't seem to be very thematic and rather very random. Slapping a bunch of independent features and phenomena together isn't what makes a good level.

Also the "remark" section in the log things confuses me a bit.

> On the ceiling of one of the atriums, there is a blue stained glass skylight with a length and width of about 15 meters, which is not provided by other atriums.

The wording in this is a little unnatural. "not provided by" —> "uncharacteristic when compared to". Also, when referring to a type of building in a level by different names, it can get a bit confusing. Try to stick with one name.

> As such, this atrium containing the skylight was considered by wanderers to be the center of Level 415.

"containing the skylight was" —> "is". How can there be a center to an infinite space, though?

> The night sky of Level 415 can be seen through the skylight, but it is also the only way to observe the outside view of Level 415.

Replace the second part of the sentence with "and only from the skylight".

> Anyone visiting Level 415 will get here first.

This would be a lot more useful to mention earlier on in the article. Also, try to reword this to sound more natural and refer to the location directly instead of just saying "here".

> Level 415 occasionally has a cart with few books.

This sticks out randomly from all of the other paragraphs and doesn't seem quite that important to mention.

> In view of the particularity of the environment at this level, this list displays the characteristics.

Worded strangely. Rephrase:  
"In view of Level 415's peculiar environment, this list displays each floor's characteristics."

The list doesn't really make sense to me. Don't you state that the level's floors extend infinitely at the beginning? If so, then why only list up to the 9th floor? Additionally, you frequently mention hostile entities but never give a list of examples of such entities that reside here.

> Continue walking in any direction at Level 415, and there is a high probability that you will come to the office.

Avoid referring to the reader directly in this style of writing. It ruins the atmosphere. (Don't use terms like "you" and such.) Removing "you" from this sentence will come with the consequence of having to rephrase it, which was already on the to-do list considering that there are a few grammatical mistakes within it anyways.

Gonna skip over the special facilities and entities in terms of SPaG and leave then for concept and format crit.

> It is made up of the first people to discover this level.

Remove "it is".

What's the point of the book club? What do they do? If you can't answer these questions in detail, you should probably remove them from the page due to their cannonical unimportance.

> Around 65 operatives permanently stationed here.

Add "are" before permanently.

> Gather resources.

This isn't a clear or full sentence/bullet point. You don't properly convey what this means.

> This is the seventh main base of The M.E.G.

That's a pretty big and important thing to boast considering how many bases there are. Maybe check with someone important before cannonizing this.

> Likely set up to collect resources.

Remove likely. Be more certain.

Alright, I finally finished SPaG.

Moving onto formatting;

The way you've formatted your article is rather sloppy to be fully honest. Sentences are misplaced and scattered throughout unrelated paragraphs, and divs are separated from their relevant paragraphs, making it difficult to make sense of most of the article without reaching for some info. Try restructuring your sentences and slapping related info into paragraphs. Make sure sentences within any given paragraph are consistent with the topic, and make sure there isn't an apparent disconnect within the flow of sentences in any given paragraph to create a pleasant reading experience. To achieve this, the best advice I can give you is to group your sentences by topic as you read them aloud, placing them in their own paragraphs and getting rid of the bumps in flow while remaining versatile in punctuation.

Also, your entrances and exits are formatted weirdly. Try presenting then in the form of full sentences rather than in a combination of list format *and* full sentences.

---

Concept;

In terms of concept, this isn't all that stable. The environment is somewhat original for the backrooms, which is good; however, in terms of its other parts, it fails. You've added many features that don't work well together, which is the opposite of what your goal should be; making all of your level's features synchronize in terms of theme and mood helps to maintain your page's "voice", and keeps it unique. Throwing together the many random concepts that came to your mind has inhibited the growth of this page, making it hard to critique as a whole due to its many holes.

The book spiders are about the only unique thing consistant to the theme that you present. Think about what you're trying to present with your level as a whole, and represent your ideas side-by-side in a way that they connect and bounce off of each other to enhance your environment.

---

Execution;

In terms of execution, unfortunately, your page also falls short, in my opinion. I can't exactly tell what your environment looks like; I can't picture it in my head. This is likely due to the confusing and weirdly-formatted description that you've presented. It's an easy fix, however, as you can change it alongside your other mistakes. Make sure none of your info conflicts, and make sure that what you're describing makes sense. Detail every part of your level clearly and vividly; make sure one can envision your level without the assistance of images. This is the first step to creating a good execution.

Otherwise, your page could use some shining up in terms of general originality in terms of ideas.
