### Object Number: 2-FR

**Mural Meringues** are the second Backrooms object of the French-speaking list. They are uncommon food sources, with varying reliability as a consumable depending on the level where they’re found.

## Description:

  

![Missing alt text.](http://fr-backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/objet-2-fr/Meringues)

*A Mural Meringue from Level 4*

**Mural Meringues** are meringues that can be found inside the walls of different architectural levels. They’re located randomly and without any apparent logic, although they’re more generally found inside hollows and/or fragile walls. They have currently been found in [Level 1](level-1.md), [Level 2](level-2.md), [Level 3](level-3.md), [Level 4](level-4.md), [Level 11](level-11.md), [Level 15](level-15.md), [Level 33](level-33.md), [Level 283](level-283.md), [Level 2.2](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-2-2), [Level 4.4](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-4-4), and [Level 38-FR](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/niveau-38-fr).

**Mural Meringues** are, in most cases, fit for consumption, and their taste, texture, and toughness depend entirely on the level where they’ve been found. **Mural Meringues** possess the same nutritional values as their Frontrooms counterparts when declared by the [M.E.G.](the-m-e-g.md) as edible: given the high quantity of sugar they contain, it is unadvised to base one’s entire diet on them. Some can be toxic, maybe even deadly, and can cause irreversible psychological issues if consumed in excessive quantities.

## Identifying Mural Meringues:

  

**Mural Meringues** are easily recognizable; they are generally found as a piece approximately 5 to 8 cm in length. However, in some rare cases they can also be found in clusters. They possess a different taste, color, and texture depending on their level of origin. Here is a table of the different types of **Mural Meringues** found in different levels of the Backrooms:

## Discovery:

  
  
**Mural Meringues** were discovered during an exploration of Level 2 by Francophone wanderers in 2001. While they were exploring the level by advancing in the same direction, a pipe exploded and leaked its usual liquid as well as lemony meringues. The pipe was obstructed by the agglomeration of meringues, blocking the liquid and causing the explosion. After they contacted the M.E.G. about this discovery, numerous cases of meringues trapped in the walls were reported across the Backrooms.

The origin of **Mural Meringues** has been confirmed to be [Level 38-FR](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/niveau-38-fr), following its accidental discovery by a francophone wanderer. All information on this subject and the report of its discovery are transcribed on the level report.

## Vanilla aroma: Not so obvious!

*A lot of people make the common mistake, after finding a Mural Meringue in Level 1, of trying to combine the vanilla aromas of Almond Water and the Meringue! Every professional on the matter is already laughing out loud about this idea, so I prefer to give a warning to the dissidents of basic culinary knowledge:*

*Combining aromas with too-obvious similarities is a very bad idea, especially if it’s a drink and a food item! To have in the mouth a liquid and a solid with the same taste makes everything bland and saturates the taste buds! Would you have found it delicious, as a child, to eat an orange and then drink a glass of orange juice? Vanilla is no exception to this rule, and it is important not to make this beginner’s mistake so you do not end up unsatisfied, criticizing the poor Almond Water by doubting its quality!*

  

## Our tips:

  
  

*So avoid this stupid combination and think about diversity! Think young! Don’t put two scoops of the same flavor into your ice cream! Go back to the classics: vanilla/chocolate of Level 11! Or, if you can afford it, cross the barriers of novelty and choose even more fanciful aromas!*

  
  

## Inner beauty!

*The place of Level 2’s meringue in the Backroom’s quality dishes was the object of fierce debates, and few supported the cause of slimy pastries. A rumor even spread about its usefulness as a glue: this is, of course, a shameful web of lies! Level 2’s meringue is hiding some deeply hidden qualities, and this is what we are going to see right away!*

  

## Our tips:

  

*Avoid eating it as it is: the melted meringue will stick to your mouth and saturate your palate with its violent acidity! This slimy layer covering the core is several millimeters in thickness and yet is more than useful to season another meringue!*

- *Grate the sides of the meringue with a straight-edged object such as a knife (avoid using an object made of wet wood or rusty metal) and continue to scratch the surface until you can get the entire acidic layer!*
- *You can use the acidic layer to, as previously said, season other meringues: even if this isn’t accessible to everyone, covering in this way a meringue from Level 283 provokes an explosion of flavor in the mouth, blending perfectly the fanciful note of cherry with the eccentricity of lemon!*
- *Once the acidic layer is collected, let’s now attack the rest of the meringue: if you scratched it enough, you could now see a sugary ball with a deeper shade of yellow, emitting a strong scent. If this is the case, you’ve got numerous options: you can eat it immediately as it is (simplicity is often a very good choice) or cut it in two to reveal a melting heart, containing a very fluid liquid, that can be collected to give a boost to your Almond Water!*

  
  
  
  

## A culinary intruder? Maybe not!

  

*When discovering for the first time the salty meringue of Level 33, you most likely believed it was some sort of joke and licked your fingers covered in salt to ensure that the taste was indeed the one of chips!  
Unsuspecting people have certainly seen in this gustatory message that this meringue was a special case, an anomaly in the meringue family, or have seen here an opportunity to taste chips again. For informed people who were fooled by appearances once but read this article soon after, it’s likely a small satisfied smile that accompanied the discovery of the salty pastry! As I will explain below, the meringue of Level 33 is far from being the sick and misunderstood little sister of the sugary siblings!*

  

## Our tips:

  

*Even if the advice I’m going to give you seems taken right from some obscure manuscript about dark magic, it is possible to transform the salty taste of the meringue into a sweet taste! Yes, you read that right! Even though it seems at first like a claim as eccentric as that of the Backrooms' linearity, it is a recently discovered fact!*

- *To start, take your meringue and rub it delicately with your finger! If it doesn’t crumble upon contact, then it is of good quality, and you can apply the following advice without transforming it into an amorphous and spongy mass.*

- *Take an object that can contain a liquid, such as a glass, and pour in Almond Water.*

- *Place the meringue in the glass and let fifteen minutes pass. Once you waited long enough, you should, after taking it out of the water, find yourself in front of a vanilla-flavored meringue with a soft and delicate texture: the Almond Water dissolved the salt and softened the surface of the meringue to give it its well-known taste!*

- *If, after reading the beginning of these tips, you had the reflex to, like every good wanderer, chuckle at the thought of wasting a precious glass of water by dissolving the salt in it, you are mistaken! Almond Water’s properties allow it to conserve its vanilla taste when you dissolve salt into it (this information isn’t coming from me, but our very esteemed Nehemias, to whom we owe this discovery!).*

## Honey for everything!

  
  

*Level 2.2’s meringues are known by enthusiasts as dripping some sort of honey similar to that of acacias in the Frontrooms! Though considered by everyone as a delicacy, one shouldn’t let themselves be fooled! Attracting at a glance, one shouldn’t forget that meringues aren’t soft madeleines and aren’t suitable for every type of culinary assortment!*

  

## Our tips:

  

*With one additional ingredient, you can prepare yourself a delicious little cough syrup!*

- *Take a small quantity of Almond Water and pour it in a glass (you can pour it directly in your mouth if you don’t have one) and then pour the honey in the water while stirring for a few seconds!*
- *You can also consume the meringue emptied of its honey by using the bottom of the glass of water! The vanilla note of Almond Water goes perfectly with the aroma of the honey and will titillate your taste buds!*

  

#### Bonus tip:

  

*If you can afford it, you can heat the water or add a few drops of lemon to give it a better taste!*
