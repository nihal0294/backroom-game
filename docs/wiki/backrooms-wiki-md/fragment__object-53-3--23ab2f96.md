Rain gently placed his phone down for a moment and stroked Lucius's hair between the cat ears.

'You awake dear?' he asked.

'Mrrowwr,' replied Lucius gently.

Rain softly pulled Lucius up to a more comfortable position to talk.

'I, uh, know I'm not supposed to ask,' started Rain, wondering how well this would go, 'but I was wondering if you could help a friend with something?'

'What do you want me to steal?' Lucius replied promptly, lowering his fluffy demeanor.

'Um,' Rain hesitated, the directness catching him slightly off guard, 'a-a seal skin. Research object, I think. In Thebes.'

'Any more info you can give me?'

Rain wordlessly passed over his phone.

Lucius skimmed through the chatlog and the article, saying nothing until he passed it back.

'Shouldn't be too difficult,' he said, 'Any idea where I can meet this friend of yours?'

'hang on,' replied Rain, 'I'll ask.'

'Fine, I'll just call in for leave.'

'Will it really take that long that you need to take a day off?'

'I can't work for the whole day, dearest,' he said, kissing Rain gently on the cheek, 'I can't cut into time with you to do this sort of thing.'

Rain giggled.

'I love you too dear,' he said, turning slightly to kiss the boy on the lips.

Lucius got up and crossed the room towards the analog telephone.

It was a nice piece of kit.

Lucius refused to use any kind of cell phone — due to how easy they were to track — and so the Benefactor had sent him a Bakelite telephone.

It was a cigar brown, and instead of a rotary dial, there were 13 buttons for the 13 callers; the 12 Months and the Benefactor.

For whatever reason it had an 03 button, which presumably called itself.

Mr. March had never dared to try and see what it did.

He stared at it for a moment, mulling over who to call.

Mr. September was probably the right choice, he usually dealt with leave.

He reached out towards button "09".

The phone started ringing before he could touch it.

That seemed to be happening a lot today.

Mr. March dragged the handle off its hook and pressed it to his ear quickly.

A voice that gave nothing away started to speak to him.

No real tone, no age nor gender.

This voice seemed synthesised, with its three combining tones of low, high, and mid.

The Benefactor was calling.

'Your request for **one** day of paid leave has been granted.'

'Thank you sir,' Mr. March replied, making sure not to show any weakness.

'Your loyalty to the Agency has been noted, **03**. Thank you for you service.'

The line went dead.

To any normal person a call like that from your boss would be extremely strange, but it was best to discount such things with the Benefactor.

Working for the [Akmē Espionage Agency](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/the-aea) had always been kind of weird.

'Dear, I got a response from Niall,' said Rain across the room, 'He says to meet at… you'd better look at this yourself…'

Mr. March looked at the string of numbers.

'eleven-dimensional coordinates,' he said, mostly to himself, 'More useful than an address, certainly.'

'Will you go?' asked Rain.

'Of course, it doesn't look too far,' replied Lucius, 'I can't deny your requests, dearest. I suppose I should get dressed now.'

Lucius proceeded into the bedroom and removed his black-and-white maid outfit.

While extremely cute, it wasn't very useful for any kind of infiltration.

Every part of his uniform — which wasn't really a uniform since every Month had a different one — was top-of-the-line.

Perfect black dress trousers, with a crease you could shave with.

Well-fitted light grey button-front formal shirt, with cuff-links in the shape of the A.E.A. logo.

Polished Derby-style shoes, looking nice but still packing absurd amounts of metal protection, from sole to toe.

Custom-made waistcoat, in a black diamond-print pattern.

Two ties of the same pattern, one normal, the other clip-on.

A similarly-patterned suit jacket, with a wide array of pockets for concealment.

Finally, a real leather briefcase with [more carrying space than would be expected](object-87.md).

Mr. March put on all the layers.

He pulled up his collar and clipped on his tie, putting the other in a pocket.

While proper ties were nice, he didn't feel like being garrotted today.

He decided to put on a layer of makeup in the bathroom.

While *technically* it gave no practical advantage, a big part of the A.E.A. was looking the part.

You had to look perfect, carry yourself like the talented young agent you were.

It was no job for anyone who didn't value style over substance.

Mr. March proceeded out and picked up his briefcase.

As he walked towards the main door, Rain ran up and gave him a big hug.

Lucius leaned up and they kissed, tongues touching for a moment.

Then he was away, strolling out of his apartment and towards his target.

---

Getting into Thebes was easy.

The guards walked set routes and the jumble of connected buildings was big enough to avoid every last one of them.

Like most groups in the Backrooms, the U.E.C. did not have very good locks.

Everything was going according to plan up until Mr. March entered the research division.

'I thought you were off today?' asked a feminine voice behind him.

Mr. March turned around as if on a turntable, facing the somewhat familiar speaker.

The lady he was now facing had long ash blonde hair bordering a pale face that could probably be described as attractive if Mr. March had any interest at all in women.

Her bird's-eye pattern suit was crisp and clean, and she was holding another A.E.A. briefcase.

'Ah, Miss June,' Mr. March recovered, 'I was not expecting you.'

'Likewise,' June replied.

Despite her generic and conventionally attractive looks, Ms. June was quickly proving to be an effective Month, with quick wits and a talent for adapting to almost any kind of mission.

The only reason she wasn't on the same level as most of the other Agents was because she lacked full experience in a lot of the A.E.A's operations.

'I'm not here on official business, to answer your question,' said Mr. March eventually.

'So then why are you breaking into a hostile area?'

'Eh, just some re-thefting. How about you?'

'Stealing some kind of super-weapon that the U.E.C. is developing,' replied Ms. June promptly.

'I'm going to have to report this conversation to the Benefactor,' said Mr. March sternly, 'internal information is to be distributed on a need-to-know basis, and needlessly sharing important mission details is a breach of…'

Ms. June had gone white.

Mr. March laughed.

'You're not actually reporting me?' asked Ms. June in confusion.

'I could, but no, just keep it in mind in future,' replied Mr. March, still giggling, 'Don't share unless you have to, simple enough.'

'Well fuck you too I guess.'

'Please don't.'

'Fine, I get it, come along if you're coming, you can help with this,' said Ms. June, striding ahead into the main research areas.

Mr. March scurried along behind, glad for the chance to not be the one navigating.

---

The U.E.C. weapon lab was about what they expected.

It looked like a high school science classroom but with slightly more high-tech water pumps.

Ms. June sauntered over to a glass case in the centre of the room and examined it critically?

'Is this it?' she asked, 'This just looks like a standard-ish [Pyroil](object-15.md) flamer.'

'You think?' said Mr. March, leafing through a couple of files left haphazardly on a desk, 'greater range, massively reduced weight, and a new fuel source. This is far from standard.'

'That makes sense,' mumbled June, 'We may as well get on with stealing this.'

She reached towards the case and the jumble of pipes and valves inside.

'Hold,' said March, his intensely calm yet sharp tone stopping June in her tracks, 'High-value research objects are always stored in cases with pressure transducers. Touch that and we'll have an alarm on our hands.'

June looked closer and saw the buttons on the edges of the glass.

'How do we proceed?' she asked tentatively.

'They look like BR-PT-4.5es…' replied Mr. March slowly, putting his case down on the floor and reaching inside up to his elbows.

He dragged out a small cardboard box and rifled through its contents.

Eventually, Mr. March slid out a set of pressure plates.

He compared them to the ones on the secure weapon case.

They were an exact match.

'Hm, I see what you mean,' said Ms. June, staring at the two sets of plates, 'But how does this help us?'

'Instructions,' said Mr. March, pulling out a thick sheaf of papers from the box, 'Maybe they have something on how to disable them.'

'Yeah, but…' began Ms. June, taking the booklet from the boy and tearing it in half.

She jammed the pieces of paper under the glass, over the plates.

'…You could just do this,' she finished, pulling open the case to silence.

Mr. March boggled, staring at the open case with a combination of horror and respect.

He began to say something, but his mouth clamped shut as Ms. June glared.

'Very well done,' he said eventually, composing himself, 'I suppose simple is best.'

'Well put,' replied Ms. June, grabbing the flamer and shoving it in her case, 'Where next?'

---

The other lab they checked looked pretty much the same, except somehow more busy.

While the flamethrower research needed to have its own room for safety, biological research could have the same room for a dozen projects.

A rack of petri dishes sat on top of a pile of pH test strips, books on mythology mingled with books on fish, the whole room was a mess of discontiguous disorder.

Ms. June stared at the disarray.

'People work here?' she asked, perplexed.

'Not at the moment,' said Mr. March, gazing at a timetable on the wall, 'Currently, B sector research is on holiday, so some deep cleaning can be done. It seems the custodians haven't got this far yet.'

'Ech, let's just get this done and get out,' said Ms. June, picking her way across the room towards a stack of glossy security cases.

'This one I believe,' said March, selecting a box marked "Object-R-53."

He went through his case again to find his lockpicks.

Ms. June checked her watch.

The malachite-backed square face told her it was nearing 3 pm.

No time to lose, then, the flamethrower was supposed to be delivered by four.

'Is that really necessary?' she asked quickly.

'I don't see any other way into this case than picking it, do you?'

Ms. June wordlessly picked up the case and hit it against the sharp metal edge of the nearest table.

The thin grey plastic buckled and quickly shattered along its melted seams.

The box fell away into two jagged pieces.

Mr. March stared again, seeming more contemplative this time.

'I'm really not doing very well today, am I…' he said, more to himself than to June.

'Why are you trying to steal a bear skin anyway?' asked June, carefully easing the fur out of the broken case.

'It's a seal skin,' corrected March, 'and the reason is because it's magic.'

Ms. June glared at him.

'You could just say you don't know, I'm not a child.'

'No, it really is magic.'

Ms. June diverted her glare towards the skin, willing it to reveal its secrets.

'It doesn't look magic,' she said vaguely, then stopped, 'Would I even be able to tell?'

'Unlikely,' said March, taking the fur from her and slinging it over one shoulder while he opened his briefcase.

'I knew I would find you here!' cried a voice from the door, clearly reading from a script.

The figure was silhouetted against the light, but March could still recognise them.

Lucius had gone to Niall's flat to pick them up with Rain once.

He recalled a permanently miffed middle-aged lady in a lab coat that had been there that mostly matched the person before him now.

Given that, it was safe to assume this was Loretta.

'I refuse to let you leave with that without!…' she stopped her intonement, realising that neither of the people before her was Niall.

Momentum halted, she seemed at a loss for what to do next.

'You're not-' she began.

'nope,' replied March, 'Even Niall wouldn't be stupid enough to walk into an obvious trap like this.'

'Then who-'

'I doubt we have to explain ourselves to you,' said Ms. June, 'we ought to be leaving, be sure not to tell anyone about this because they won't believe you.'

She made towards the door, March in tow, but Loretta drew a shortsword as they approached.

The pair of rogues stopped.

The researcher had clearly had some training, they had a good stance and held the sword firmly, but it probably wouldn't help.

Mr. March pulled out a fork.

---

It always surprised people how effective cutlery was in a sword fight.

Spoons were a good option, as were table knives.

Steak knives were pretty good for stabbing, and tongs were decent at disarming.

But nothing could match the sheer versatility of a regular stainless steel table knife.

Mr. March pirouetted at the end of his four-pronged sliver of metal, defending but never attacking.

He dodged backwards, widely dodging a misguided lunge and using his "weapon" to slam the sword into the ground, creating a large gouge in the linoleum.

'Enlighten me,' said March to the struggling Loretta, 'why did you try and stop Niall leaving by pinching his skin?'

'He… took… my… CHILDREN!' stuttered the scientist, eventually dragging the sword from the floor.

'That makes sense,' replied March, dodging another swing like a fighting game character, 'I wonder if he would describe it differently.'

Another lunge.

'One last thing,' said March, 'How did you even know where the pelt was? It should have been well-hidden.'

Loretta said nothing and swung again.

Mr March dodged again, grabbing her sword arm and slamming it against the wall.

The blade clattered against the ground.

'Answer, if you please?' he said politely.

The lady tried to struggle against his grip, but his thin appearance belay restrictive muscle like iron bars.

'I found it for him,' she said, poorly trying to keep her boiling rage hidden, 'And we hid it together.'

'Very good,' replied March, letting her go, 'we shall be off.'

He turned back towards Ms. June, who had been waiting unsure for the entire battle.

'Come along,' he said brightly, making his way out of the room.

Behind him, Loretta tried to pick up the sword silently.

The result sounded like a brass band, but the two agents seemed not to hear.

She sneaked behind March, and prepared to strike.

He turned quickly and gently tripped her up with a foot.

She tumbled to the floor like a large barrel of tomatoes.

Her sword seemed to fall upwards, spinning through the air in a damning trajectory.

It landed directly in Loretta's skull, splitting it like a slightly smaller *sack* of tomatoes.

Mr. March stared blankly at the mess of gore.

'oops.'

'FUCK YOU MEAN OOPS‽' yelled Ms. June, 'YOU JUST KILLED A RANDOM SCIENTIST!'

'In my defense that was not intended,' said March calmly, like he was trying to explain that he was late to work because of a traffic jam, 'and were actively aggravating the situation.'

'Do you act this way every time you kill someone outside of the schedule?'

Mr. March looked exasperatedly at Ms. June.

'What is the point of agonising over every single issue we have?' said March slowly, 'We make mistakes like this, if we don't move on we'd never get anything done.'

'Surely leaving corpses around compromises our position?'

'Not really. No cameras, no fingerprints, no identifying information. While there is evidence of some kind of struggle, they are more likely to suspect an internal dispute than our organisation.'

Ms. June took off her gloves for a moment and stared at her featureless fingertips.

She had only had her prints removed last week, and it still stung like a tiny toffee hammer on each finger.

'I get your point, but…' Ms. June faltered, trying to think of a reasonable way to explain morality to an assassin.

She gave up.

'Okay, let's just go,' she said eventually.

They went.

---

Ms. June wandered the streets of Level 11 like a regular accountant, with no suggestion that she had done anything clandestine that day.

Mr. March had bade her farewell when they exited Thebes together.

She had completed her special delivery to her current client at one of their drop-boxes.

She was now heading back to her apartment.

Ms. June headed up the long spiral staircase to the penthouse the continual spinning making her feel like she was in a massive washing machine.

She unlocked the door with an expensive-looking brass key and a long combination code.

She walked hesitantly through the frame and reset the series of security measures behind her.

Hoping that what was about to happen would not happen like it always did, she edged out of the entryway.

Seven [Blub Cats](entity-29.md) pounced on her in unison like an artillery barrage made of explosive kittens.

After several minutes of spherical struggling, Ms. June was able to fill up their bowls of almond water.

Ms. June flopped over onto her black sofa — energy spent — staring up at the projected starscape on the ceiling like it was something more than dancing lights.

As she always did in these liminal circumstances, her thoughts strayed to other people.

It was strange to say, with all these cute creatures and nice coworkers, but she was lonely.

She had always known working for the A.E.A. would mean she couldn't see people as often, but she had never expected it to be this outcasting.

It was dangerous to let other people in on your life when it was fraught with violence and secrets.

Getting into a relationship with a Month was the equivalent of going cave diving with box jellyfish.

She wondered how Mr. March was able to hold down a boyfriend without getting him killed.

Then, her thoughts flipped along another path like a salmon, and she wondered what March was doing now.
