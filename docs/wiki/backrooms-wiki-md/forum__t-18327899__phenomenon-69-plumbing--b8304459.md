Mh. There are things about this that I enjoyed and others less so. Here's me rambling and overanalyzing in an unorganized manner:

For starters, props to you for establishing a "clinical report" tone and format that actually works. Does it read a little like budget SCP? Maybe, but I think it translates the sort-of investigative but still amateurish tone it'd expect from self-styled "researchers" in the Backrooms. There are nitpicks I could point towards, such as the third footnote which I would have written differently — probably something like "The reason for this is unknown", but this is down to personal preference since this Wiki isn't particularly strict on how to properly write a "scientific" page.

Now, the humor is a little hit-and-miss. It's juvenile; poop jokes; reminiscent of the sort of comedy you'd read in the heydays of the lolFoundation era; you get the idea. It made me snort, but I also groaned upon reading Test 69-075, which is the equivalent of that meme with the middle-aged woman wearing a sack of frozen foods on the head going "I'm so crazyyyyyy". I also think you lean a little too hard into the jokes in a way that stretches the limit of whether this qualifies as a mainlist comedic page: To \*me\*, comedic mainlist page should still feel a little grounded into some sort of believable reality, like someone would have truly written something a report of that sort in a "serious" manner. It's debatable whether the page successfully remains believable enough even with the suspension of disbelief. There are other bits I would have also removed, such as the crossed-out sentence in Test 69-038 and the very last two sentences in Test 69-105. In both of these cases, I think the jokes are made a little less funny by insisting on the joke and thus reducing the effectiveness of the punchlines. I did however enjoy the little plot twist at the very end, very cool demonstration of how to use the "Show don't tell" principle central to clinical pages.

I might seem nitpicky but truly it's because I respect the effort, it's rare for me to enjoy at least elements of a clinical page here, and you've managed to do so. It speaks of your capabilities as a writer.

Hi! Thanks for the feedback. I'll give my thoughts here based on the (admittedly brief) writing process of this.

What I agree with:  
1) The last sentence of the last test is definitely something I mulled over a bit before including, and with what you say in mind I think I'd rather remove it (which I will do once I have actually finished writing this, lol). The crossing out thing is something I'd like to keep even though it 'hammers it in' a bit, because I thought it would be funnier to have the implication that someone tried to brush it off onto the janitorial staff before someone else intervened and said 'hey, maybe don't punish innocent bystanders for something we specifically told the offenders not to do'. Still, it's a good critique.

2) And I definitely think I let the joke ride a bit sometimes. It was partially intended because, as you correctly pointed out, this is all juvenile poop jokes and I wanted to hammer that in. In truth, I only planned (before writing) two parts of this article, namely the first part which was my answer to 'what happens to poop in the backrooms' and the ending joke, which was what everything I wrote here was ultimately building up to from the moment I asked myself the poop question (in truth, the very immature origin of the ending punchline was 'wouldn't it be hilarious if there was something just eating all the poop in the backrooms'). The scope of the article was to answer the worldbuilding question more than it was to tell jokes, but it ended up evolving that way as I wrote it (because there are only two ways to write faeces: with intense rigor and seriousness, or to take the piss)

What I object to:  
1) Third footnote: I figured it was just funnier to do the same thing as the first offset. It's purely a comedic choice and I know it's not the correct way to write it (same goes for the other footnotes).

2) To my knowledge, lolfoundation refers to the quasi-flanderisation of otherwise rational characters acting comedically and irrationally for the sake of being wacky. My rationale for the tests and how I approached writing them was to envision the three types of interactions that would happen in the hypothetical scenario where your boss tells you to investigate a set of magic toilets. A) Serious tests meant to advance science, B) Unintentional tests that reveal some truth (like the love letter or the cocaine pouch), or C) In a wholly unserious manner because we are investigating magical toilets. God knows, if my supervisor at work told me there was a magical toilet in the building, I would definitely try some stupid shit in the name of science, and I figured the contrast between 'please keep this academic' and 'I will set the toilet on fire for science' was funny in and of itself. In fact, I tried to tongue-in-cheek my way to acknowledging this in writing by the end with the whole 'these tests are getting redundant, can we stop them?' bit. I'll accept that 075 is a bit cliche, though I figured that when keeping realism in mind, there's always someone who will take the fun stuff too far and screw it up for everyone. But I figure that's just my own sense of dry humour that makes the note funny for me — the groan is deserved.

3) I only disagree vehemently with the suspension-of-disbelief point, specifically because I think a lot of comedy articles on this site follow the trend of absurd and random humour and I take some issue with that. This is forgivable for the joke articles to some extent, but I think a lot of serious comedic articles miss the mark by dragging those absurd scenarios for too long. This is obviously a difference in how we perceive humour, so I won't die on that hill of course, but I figured we could use some bureaucratic humour to shake things up a bit because I think that in an ARCHIVAL site, where we generally write DOCUMENTS, there is a big lack of dry, institutional humour that comes with 'what would happen in a real life workplace' if you get what I mean. Not that I haven't stretched the poop jokes out a bit, or some of the meta humour, but I figured not every piece of formal writing (I loathe the term 'clinical') on this site has to treat the Backrooms like either a high fantasy sci-fi world or grimdark survival situation. Sometimes things are nonsensical and a bit exaggerated for the plot, and I don't see an issue with that PERSONALLY. But with that said, whenever the word personally is involved you always have to accept that subjectivity follows shortly thereafter, and it all comes down to taste anyway.

I hope I've responded to everything here — big thanks for actually taking the time to seriously analyse the poop article and to comment on the contents in such a way, and thank you for holding me in the esteem that you do. I hope my future shitposts (pun intended, please groan one last time) improve upon my misgivings in this one!
