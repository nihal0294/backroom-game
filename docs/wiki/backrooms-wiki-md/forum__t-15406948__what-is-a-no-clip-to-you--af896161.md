I guess it would be similar to what the two users above described.

No-clipping should be like walking through something as if it were not there, or walking through thin air.

I remember a game I played a fuckton of times when I was a small demon skulldog. SuperTux. Basically a Super Mario Brothers clone, a 2D side-scrolling platformer with a built-in level editor and a penguin as a main protagonist. Very popular among Linux users. I loved making stupid levels on that thing running on my EeePC 301 (Oh, the memories!)

The level editor lets me do something very interesting, straight out of the box: I could place objects in the same "dimension" the penguin was, allowing Tux to interact with them like normal blocks and whatnot (Middleground [?]). But there was also an option to place blocks in the **Foreground** or the **Background** - objects placed in either of these would not act as a physical object for Tux, and only were there as something you could see in the back or in the front of Tux (and the rest of the level and its tiles and blocks and whatnot.)

The **Foreground** would be closer to you than Tux was (2D games are weird to explain); or, in other words, any objects there would cover Tux - yet the penguin could not interact with them, and only walk or fall **through** these objects in the Foreground.

This is what I imagine no-clipping as.

I also wonder if the Backrooms could be accessible through an extra dimension, explaining how we just "fall" through things . We don't. We just stumble across a fourth dimension. Hell, an *n*-th dimension. And then we travel through it erratically. For how long or towards where, that's probably going to depend on our movements, or our luck.
