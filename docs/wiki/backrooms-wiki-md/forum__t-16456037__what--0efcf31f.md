"Apparently, 1000dumplings is back, even though I gathered that they were gone forever."  
The only thing I can really adress is this. I don't think 1000Dumplings ever left the Backroom Wiki as a user, they just stopped being active on it. Like, they've still been a member of the site, but stopped being active. They posted on a rewrite of their article, which makes sense for them to respond to. It is nothing to get excited over. It doesn't mean they will start writing again or even posting again actively. I think at most we could probably expect them to post reviews to rewrites to things like Level Fun and Partygoers(which is going well, from what I'm seeing, but I'm not going to indulge on spoilers).  
"I keep seeing people referencing some kind of drama that everyone else is aware of *except for me*."  
I don't know the details of it either, so you're not alone. I'm sure there are others as well. I've already said what I think it is that is going on in reply to your contact staff post. I think part of the drama that pushed Plant over the edge had to do partly with the contest shitposts, but I wouldn't know what else.  
"Immense changes on the wiki"  
Being one of said users being overwhelmed, I'll start with the wipe out of the entire rewrite team due to a staff vote, which now moves the power of rewrites over to greenlighters. While excited by this prospect, it is just as overwhelming, since it is a monumental job on top of the already difficult work of critiquing user's pages. As much as I hate to say it, it would have been a bit more considerate of staff to see it also as putting more work on the few active greenlighters available, and not just getting rid of a duty for staff. Perhaps they should have asked all greenlighters their opinions before making this decision. I mean, you made a decision that would affect a group of people *without* asking their opinion. Imagine how that group of people may feel to not have their opinion consulted. Especially when they may have several questions on how all the changes would work. It just leaves me frusturated and some-what lost on what to do.  
Let's see here, what next…  
Now, then there's the license box stuff. While this was implemented several weeks (or even a month, I don't quite remember), it still is an irritation to add to a page, especially since the license box won't work for any other license than the BY-SA 3.0. I get that it's for legal purposes or whatever, but that doesn't change the fact that it is annoying. Plus, at the bottom of my screen is a thing that says, "Unless otherwise stated, the content of this page is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 License". Unless a person wants their page to be licensed something other than that (and I don't think staff should make it required to have the most recent version of the BY-SA 3.0 as the default and just let people say what the license is if it something other than the one below), then they shouldn't have to include the license box. Simple as that. But it's a required component, and the license box is automatically set to BY-SA 3.0, and there isn't anything I can do about it, and it frustrates me. This is kinda just me flooding out my anger at this in one post that has been gradually building up since it was first introduced.  
Alright, the contest. Contests have been around for quite some time. Nothing about except the general concept of the contest and "**suggestions**" for this contest has been changed from the previous ones, yet for *some* reason, it shook up the wiki like no other contest has before. I personally did not like the subject of it, and found that the suggestions were taken up to self-interpretation on how much of an impact they would have in deciding whether a page was "classic" enough or not. Then there were the "protest" articles. I have nothing against said users who made the two articles, but people's responses to these articles were dissapointing. They just saw "Oh, these articles are garbage" and dismissed them instead of actually taking into account their purposes. I was guilty on that one on the first article. But then, upon seeing the second, returned to the first one and honestly readdressed it. I didn't see anyone else doing that. They still dismissed it, even after seeing the second(which they dismissed as well). Now, obvously, I've been joking around some about the amount of downvotes on said article, and may have somewhat encouraged the behavior I'm clearly criticizing without meaning to, now that I look at it, but all the same, I think people should have considered these people's opinions better.  
These are just my thought spilled out.
