Hey everyone! This is the first writing collab I’ve ever done. Me and are very proud to present the new and improved rewrite of Almond Water! Do you remember that magic sanity juice from a while back that nobody liked? Well guess what, it had a glow-up! It’s no longer magic nor a potion, but now an answer to the biggest plot hole in the Backrooms: “how do people eat?”

Me and max worked really hard on this, and we hope you enjoy. Thanks to and for Crit.

Edit: Hey, so just gonna address some things I’ve seen.  
- The overseers are teenagers here. They noclipped into the backrooms at 16–17 years old. Of course they’re gonna be immature idiots, that was wholly intentional, though I see now not very popular of a decision. They’re in their 20s by the time the Meg becomes a full fledged group. This was confirmed by the author of the Meg himself.  
- I apologize for the SPaG errors and bad flow. I probably should’ve gotten more Crit on this. I figured 2 crits would be sufficient, but it seems not. It came from the fact that I had just finished writing Icarus and figured all of my own criticism was just me being too harsh on myself.  
- The description is as much detail as I could possibly put into it. 5 paragraphs. Some articles don’t even have more than 1. If that’s still not long enough then I’m not sure what is.

I apologize for going mostly inactive writing wise and leaving the discord. Hopefully this is not my final article and I still have it. If this changes your vote, great, but if not, I understand. For those who did enjoy the article, I’m glad I was still able to provide something you liked. For those who didn’t, I’m writing this because I just figured I’d at least break my silence after 5 months. I should’ve probably put more effort into this than I did. Sorry.

This rewrite is certainly better than the original….

but it's still got problems.

I'm just gonna go through and list off every problem I caught within this article along with a little explanation of why its a problem.

> is a very useful object that can be obtained on most levels of the Backrooms.

This introductory statement pretty much just restates what we already know from that first blurb.[1](javascript:;) Why not instead of starting it by restating something we already heard, introduce a piece of new information?

> obtained on most levels of the Backrooms. It is a set of metal thermoses filled with clear liquid.

This sentence contains a problem that is present throughout the article…bad flow.

> suggest that all of the bottles may have suddenly appeared one day through unknown means

Another issue this article has is it's persistent use of "the reasoning for this is unknown". I understand not wanting to explain literally everything about an article but at least trying to offer even the mildest amount of explanation beyond what ends up feeling like an excuse goes a long way in improving articles, at least in my opinion.

> though it is always possible that these are just tales and not fact.

This sentence does practically nothing and would honestly be better removed.

> Most of the bottles are usually slightly damaged or worn with marks, such as dents or scratches in the paint.

I *believe*, although I'm not sure, that there shouldn't be a comma before such as.

> All of the thermoses are roughly 1’ tall, and they are always filled to the very brim of the bottle, with each bottle being able to hold anywhere between 2–4 cups of water.

Considering they are all about the same size….2 to 4 in terms of cups of water is a very big difference.

". So when you are told to get 3 cups of water, for example, that means 3 x 8 fl oz, which equals 24 fl oz to 3 cups." Using this as a metric, (1 cup is 8 fluid ounces), that's a difference of 16 fluid ounces. That would def be large enough to be noticeable[2](javascript:;)

> The contents of the bottle inside vary somewhat in taste, though all resemble water in both texture and appearance.

This could easily be shortened too "The contents of the bottles vary in taste, but are otherwise identical to water"

> is safe to consume by default

The by default here could also be removed.

> however, certain levels can have an anomalous effect that makes the liquid cause symptoms similar to cyanide poisoning to the drinker.

This concept feels completely random and underexplained. It comes out of left field, gets a mild explanation of how certain parts function but….why do certain levels cause this? Including it in the article would imply its a significant enough amount to make it worth mentioning but why would so many levels have this effect? Is it just the ones that are dangerous?

I also worry using something like this may cause an…effect on the way people write levels. This is unrelated to actual criticism of the article, moreso I'm just foretelling the fact I feel like making the almond water in your level "dangerous and actually kill you instead" will become a huge trope so people can show how scary and dangerous their levels are.

> The general rule of thumb is this: “When in doubt, boil the water.”

this feels like a tone break. > on for the average, healthy person. Despite being the first object ever discovered by The M.E.G., it is still unknown exactly how this is possible.

same issue I have in other parts. Just uses "this is unknown".

> traces of things

Things is a tonebreaker and shouldn't be used.

> this does not appear to alter the flavor or taste as it would be expected to.

Generally clunky wording. Could be just "this doesn't alter the flavor or taste" Using stuff like "appear to alter" adds unnecessary wordiness when it is completely uneeded and adds no new information to the statement.

> mixture

Solution would be a better word in this situation.

Adding suspicious here is almost completely pointless. It impacts the tone because "suspicious" is not clinical in the slightest and saying just researchers would read better in general.

Shouldn't this be placed before the mention of different colored bottles, since it goes back to a topic mentioned earlier?

Disappointing

12 Sep 2022 23:43

After a very thorough first read and a rather brief reflection, I've come to the conclusion that this piece is simply to lackluster for me to care in any meaningful regard, especially considering that this object is going to likely represent the fundamental aspects of an object in the future.

So, with that in mind, let's start with the proponents, shall we?  
- I did enjoy the change from a universally positive object to an object with potential for backfire. I do wish this was expanded upon more, rather then being only from external modification. However, as it stands, the level of which potential tampering may occur for Almond Water is adequate.  
- There's a storyline explaining the origin of the M.E.G. Quite fitting for a first object in an index created by them; I also found the justification well enough to be considered.  
- Changing it from a liquid to a sort of "liquidized meal" is intriguing, an excellent way to explain the lack of deaths in levels like level 0.

Now, as for opposition, my list is somewhat longer.

I feel as though this article is divided into two major parts: the description and properties, then the discovery. I'll be going into discovery first, as it is more important, then I'll touch on description and properties.

For a discovery log, the story works, though I will admit the concept behind it is substandard at best. Though an origin story is good in concept, relating that origin story as a facet to explain why the Overseers are currently alive to a specific entity feels… misattributed. Rather then relating their survival to a specific entity attack, I think this was a missed opportunity to play into survival analysis and how the Overseers play into death by the elements. The mentioning of low rations could definitely have been played into more (I mean, two weeks on granola and stale water sounds unrealistic to me, think of the nutrients they were missing).

Okay, sure, that aspect is more nitpicky and the current story revolving around Skinstealers works to an extent. I suppose a more objective criticism is how g-d awful the dialogue itself reads. These lines do not feel as though they come from any humans mouth. Overseer A only fills in the role of being someone that Overseer C can speak to during a time of distress while Overseer B just… gets hives? From… what? The usage of hives during the discovery log was the most cliché usage I've seen for a while. It's not even the obvious cliché that I mind, it's the fact that hives only emerged as a plot point and nothing more (hell, there wasn't even an attributed reason as to why she got hives in the first place, and as far as I'm concerned, hives could have been replaced with any disease and would've fit because the exact symptoms were not mentioned at all.) (As a side note having Overseer B do all the worthwhile exposition in one giant text block whilst the rest of the log is one liners and someone literally dying is… it feels lackluster and rushed)

Now, as for the description and otherwise, it was honestly okay. Though boring and monotonous at some parts, it is a description. There really isn't much to say beyond the grammar is kind of bad and the flow is meh.

I suppose this feels more like two seperate articles over a single standalone one which fails to encapsulate the true potential objects as a whole can have. Much more work could be put into it and yet there wasn't. This is why I will be downvoting.
