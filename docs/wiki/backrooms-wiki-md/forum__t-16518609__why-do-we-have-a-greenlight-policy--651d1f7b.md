Re: Why do we have a Greenlight policy?

13 Dec 2023 01:23

You mean allow coldposting?

Unfortunately, there are many bad drafts in the Sandboxes, some that have incredibly rule-breaking content. In addition, going through critique *does* make your piece more polished. As a community, we've decided we want high quality writing on the site, which means writers need to get critique. Greenlighters should not be denying Greenlights because of personal bias (but if you feel like this is happening, share with me a screenshot so I can bring it up with staff).

Also, deleting articles is a bit of a time-consuming process for staff. The less we have to delete, the better.

But generally, this is what the critique process looks like:

- You write a draft, send it to someone, and they look at it.
- They'll tell you what is and what isn't working.
- Then, you revise it and send it to someone else.
- They look at it, and the process repeats until the piece is ready for a Greenlight.

This process (on the forums) will take as long as it needs to. I will say you will get more critique in our Discord, so check that out if you're able.

I know I'm basically repeating what they're saying but there are numerous reasons to not just allow people to post whatever onsite. I think one thing a lot of people forget is that we're not S.C.P., we're accessible to younger audiences which also sometimes can mean a lack of self-control.

- Rule-breaking content is a pretty large reason why to not allow people to just post whatever.
- Additionally, quality control is a major factor here.
- As a sub-point to the last one, the number of articles that could be produced yet not have to abide by quality control is far more tedious for staff.
- Shitpost articles could be far more common and again, tedious for staff to clean up

This isn't even covering close to everything. With all of this being said, we also have a clearing process that kinda skips the GL system on our discord which I would highly recommend you take advantage of.

As both Anime\_Roar and BlueSignet have pointed out, crit is a time-consuming process that shouldn't be rushed. Get crit, improve, ask others, and then go to a GLer, if they deny, rinse, then repeat until you do get a GL.

Re: Why do we have a Greenlight policy?

13 Dec 2023 02:19

For context, the “this page does not exist” notice used to not require a password to post. It just told you that you needed to get a Greenlight. People broke that rule, leading to a few coldposts. Deletions aren’t just as simple as deleting the page; they need to be logged on the Staff Oversight site, and the page source needs to be sent to the original author. Doing all of this on mobile devices is *awful* because of how much text copy-pasting it entails and how easy it is to accidentally lose text. There was a noticeable issue in how many coldposts weren’t getting deleted in a timely manner, and the passcode was added to stem this.

Now, given that there was a slowdown even dealing with just the people who broke the rules, how many pages would need to be deleted if everyone could post? The amount of time spent just on logging deletions would go up wildly, and we don’t have the staff for something as tedious as that. If pages have a minimum expectation of quality, why not stem the posting rate to ensure those basic expectations are met rather than chase down an ever-growing pile of work?

Checkmarks are another way to get pages posted to the site, but this is hosted through the Discord server. If you’re interested in receiving a checkmark clear, feel free to join the server to see how it works.

EDIT: I forgot to address this earlier, but if a Greenlighter doesn’t like a draft for reasons like their own subjective preferences (enjoying levels over objects, disliking format screws, etc), they’re expected to be clear about that with the author. Most will suggest reaching out to a different Greenlighter instead if they feel like their own preferences is getting in the way of properly critiquing your draft.
