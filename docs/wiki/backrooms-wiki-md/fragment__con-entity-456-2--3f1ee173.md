*At 24:04, 3 Frost Boars, apparently belonging to the same squall, appear from the forest. One approaches the drone and proceeds to both sniff and poke at it with its snout. The drone does not move during this period. Presumably finding the machine to be of little interest, the Frost Boars quickly move on and out of frame. 1 minute later, the drone begins to move again.*

*At 27:47, a burrow becomes visible from among the treeline, with several Frost Boars standing around it. Slowing its speed, the drone cautiously approaches the mouth of the burrow, drawing the attention of one of the boars. The drone comes to a complete stop as said boar begins to move towards it. The Frost Boar begins repeatedly sniffing, circling, and gently nudging the drone, before leaving to return to the other boars. The drone begins to move again, and at 31:09, it enters the burrow.*

*As the drone descends further into the tunnel — beyond the range that the light outside provides — the footage abruptly switches from a traditional camera recording to that of an infrared camera. This has the effect of replacing the otherwise obscuring darkness with a dim blue-purple haze, illuminated by the warm bodies of the Frost Boars in the burrow. There appear to be at least 16 such boars visible, all milling about a large entrance chamber that the drone now sits inside. Many of the boars present appear to be piglets, and a nursing mother is also spotted in one corner. The drone begins to survey the area around itself, observing the squall and its behaviour. Multiple Frost Boars approach and interact with the drone, but — as in previous instances — quickly move on.*

*Pressing further in, the drone begins to move towards the far side of the entrance chamber. As the drone nears said far side, several small openings become visible on the burrow walls, evidently leading to deeper passages within the burrow. Inspecting one such passage, infrared radiation from a Frost Boar appears on the video feed, indicating the passage leads to a deeper chamber within the burrow. However, the drone does not enter the opening, instead tracing the outside wall of the entrance chamber, and peering down each opening it passes.*

*Eventually, the drone encounters an opening which does not appear to lead to another chamber. Instead, this opening leads to a passage which gently slopes down for a great distance; no end is visible on the recording. Stopping completely at the sight of this, the drone proceeds to turn to enter the passageway, before slowly rolling down the tunnel under gravity.*

*The tunnel extends to an extraordinary depth; the drone travels down it, uninterrupted, for 2 minutes straight. Nonetheless, an end does appear, which the drone encounters at around 39:53.*

*The end of the tunnel is visually unremarkable, simply being a both flat and featureless wall. The drone stops directly in front of it, records the oddity for a few minutes, and then — slowly — drives into it.*

*The camera feed cuts out completely, and the video shows only a black rectangle for the next 20 minutes.*

*When the video resumes, the drone appears to be in an entirely different tunnel, this time sloping upwards instead of downwards. The walls appear to be made of stone rather than soil, and — just as before — the cramped tunnel stretches upwards as far as is visible. The drone begins to ascend the passage.*

*The ascent takes substantially longer than the descent, but due to the effects of gravity the drone moves so slowly that the total distance traveled is actually much less than the that of the previous tunnel. At 1:03:02, the drone emerges from the end of the passage, into another large entrance chamber. This entrance chamber bears no resemblance to the previous one seen in terms of topography, but does contain similar features, such as passageways on the sides of the chamber, numerous Frost Boars, and piglets. Switching to the visible light camera, the drone locates a presumed exit passage, identified by a soft glow of outdoor light.*

*The drone slowly makes its way to the exit passage before beginning to climb, and — at 1:05:37 — exits the burrow.*
