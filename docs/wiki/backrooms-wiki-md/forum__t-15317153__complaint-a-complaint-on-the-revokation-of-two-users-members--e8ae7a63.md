Vulgar language itself is not against the [Site Rules](site-rules.md). Language that belittles users *is* against the site rules, and this is applicable whether or not someone uses vulgar language.

Epic’s swearing was directed in frustration toward the reasons for downvoting. It might not be the most polite response, but I find it hard to not sympathize with Epic for being upset after being accused of vanity for doing something fully encouraged for this contest, and being told that the article was so bad that it should be deleted. The fact that no foul language was used means very little when the spirit of the comments directed at Epic were belittling, aggressive, mocking, and in general completely uninterested in constructive criticism.

Personal attacks have little moral high ground against foul language. Foul language has a time and place for it, and this site is a casual environment where foul language isn’t inappropriate. I could tell someone that their article is “fucking amazing” and “the best shit I’ve ever read” and this would be fully within acceptable language for the wiki. If I told someone their article is “a waste of time” I would be disciplined for belittling language regardless of me not swearing.

I suspect that a cultural difference is at play here where foul language is treated much more seriously by CN, but I find it unfair that you and others claim unfair treatment due to Epic’s foul language and ignore the nature of the comments made toward Epic. Regardless of a lack of foul language, Koden and valler made comments that are blatantly rude and cruel. They (and others) should reflect on why their behavior was inappropriate.

If they want an apology from Epic for swearing at them, they should also apologize to Epic for their comments. Civility and respect goes both ways. If their expectation is that they will be spoken to respectfully, they have no right to treat Epic disrespectfully (and in a way that explicitly breaks site rules, as well).

As for Nate, I agree that staff members need to keep their cool. Staff need to be prepared to de-escalate situations, especially when a high probability of different cultural expectations exists. Koden and valler were probably upset about Epic swearing and them, and Nate likely made them feel more resentful with his tone. Nate was righy to call Koden and valler out for their comments, but going in guns blazing was the wrong approach from the start.
