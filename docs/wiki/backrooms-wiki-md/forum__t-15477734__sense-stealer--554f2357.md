[Here's my take...](javascript:;)

I'm really pissed right now because I had a great spag post ready and the site froze. I'm just going to tell you what I saw and move on. I apologize in advance if I sound rude, I'm just really annoyed with the situation.

> Therefore, the wanderer will see its own body through the senses of the creature.

"Therefore" isn't needed. Also, say that Wanderers report seeing themselves from the enty's senses. "creature" isn't quite clinical enough. 'entity" is perfectly acceptable.

> This effect also work on any recording devices regardless of their position.

"work" should be "works" for proper subject-verb agreement SVA.

> This anomalous effect also affect the mental of the attacked also sharing emotions and point of view.

"affect" should be "affects" for SVA. You could probably say"mental state". That's more clear to the reader.

> Thus, any sentences or thoughts is made from the point of view of the creature.

"Thus isn't needed. Say that wanderers believe that they experience the entity's thoughts while under its influence.

The example must be written in a complete sentence.

> Additional side effects may include loss of equilibrium, loss of directions, spontaneous laughter or crying.

You could say "dizziness" instead of "loss of equilibrium". Both have the same meaning.

> Theses attacks often end with "partial devouring" or "lucky escapes".

I belive you meant "of".

> "A partial devouring" consist of the wanderer being caught and waking up in a strange conditions.

"consist" should be "consists" for SVA. "in a strange conditions" has to be changed. This can be done in two ways. Delete "a" or replace "conditions" with "condition".. Pick one.

> Black liquid had flown from all the sensory orifices including the eyes.

Change "had" to "will have". Place a comma after "orfices".

> All 59 testimonies of wanderers being "partially devoured" relates the feeling of having lost an essential part of themselves

Is the part physical or mental/emotional? State it if it's the latter. If it's the former, change the partial devouring, as humans cannot survive without something essential, like a lung.

> They're almost guaranteed to "partially devoured" wanderer.

"x% of wanderers have been "partially devoured", a high chance in most entity attacks."

> Thanks to the anomalous side effect, the rare survivors can attest that the entity is an extremely malevolent one.

The effects make survivors rare. How do the effects prove that it's malevolent, if at all? Also, "hostile" is a better word than "malevolent", as it fits the clinical tone better.

> They sometimes play with its more fragile preys like a cat.

A simile is far too personal. Also, detail this specific behavior.

> The 23 testimonies attest that the entity always start its attacks in places with good lighting.

"23 of the testimonies attest…" Use "starts" for proper SVA.

> The person managed to escape. Yet, no one saw the group afterward.

"The wanderer managed to escape, but no one reported seeing the group afterward." is a good rewrite.

> The 14 "lucky escapees" have all one point in common. They all at one point or another managed to seal their senses after distancing the entity.

Just 14? I thought there were 73 testimonies. Also, wouldn't they tell you how to seal your senses?

> The means of this sense sealing vary according to testimonies from accidental knock-out, meditation, prayers to more extreme method such as using a flash bang or even permanent self-removal of sight.

"method" should be "methods" as you list more than one method afterward.

> Attempts to harm the creatures have all failed, leading even researchers to question whether this attacker has a physical body.

Not exactly clinical. I don't think it's needed.

> A last yet concerning property of the entity had been found based on the 73 testimonies. Only one person never heard of the creature before the attack.

Put a comma after "yet".

> This is highly unlikely, because during the time of the 40 first testimonies, the knowledge wasn't widely spread.

You don't need the first comma, but keep the second.

> Attacking the entity, because it's useless

"Attacking" should be "Attack". Don't attack the entity. "because it's useless" isn't clinical, but you could strike it through to add some extra spice to the article.

In your last two sentences, you forgot to add a period at the end of them. Please do that.

My biggest issue that really annoyed me was the inxonsistency in testimony numbers. Forst, you say there are 73 testimonies. Then, you say "all 59" testimonies, meaning there are only 59 testimonies. For the 23 in patterns, I believe you meant to write 73. It's 54th, not 54rd. That inconsistency between the forst mention of the 73 testimonies and the 59 testimonies is annoying me right now.

It's really early, so I'm going to turn in.

First, I want to say I like the idea: an entity that takes over its prey's mind, & torments its senses. This is a hard idea to convey, yet if you can pull it off it will make for a truly terrifying creature.

I wonder if it would improve this entry were you to invent a character who performs the interviews & research that comprises this profile. Say Dr. X (who may/may not be affiliated with the M.E.G.) undertook this study as response to a close friend/relative/loved one who was "partially eaten" by this entity. Such a study would serve to provide proof there is only one such creature without needing to explain the calculations of times & places that led to this conclusion. (I suspect there are too few people in the Backrooms to support an extended academic-level discussion of this monster's qualities.)

But a few criticisms. One is the image of a cat playing with its prey. I've had a couple of cats who have done exactly this — I watched them — & it's not so much a demonstration of cruelty as it is a puzzling mixture of innocence & sadism. (As in, "How can a cute little kitty torment a mouse to death?") But it does sound as if this entity has a sadistic side.

Another is the term "sealing the senses". That just doesn't sound right to me. There must be a better way to express this idea, but it's not coming to me at the moment.

A last point — not really a criticism, & perhaps this just shows my ignorance of how things are done here — is that I wonder whether the M.E.G. has the staff & resources to provide psychological/psychiatric help. That is a field which requires lots of training — I wonder just how many mental health professionals end up in the Backrooms — & access to sufficient amounts of psychoactive drugs to help people. (Just how much Prozac in found in the Backrooms in a given time frame? More than the amount of antibiotics? Or killer marijuana?)

I'm just throwing out ideas here, some of which I don't know if they've been adequately discussed, & solutions found which all have agreed to.

Keep at this article & good luck with it!
