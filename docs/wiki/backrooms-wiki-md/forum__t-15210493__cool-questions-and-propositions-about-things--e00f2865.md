TL;DR  
A no clip is either our eyes render distance, like in Minecraft, and looking away from a no clip point while running backwards allows the user to go in, or we can assume the [Backroom Basics](basics-of-the-backrooms-a-guide.md) has the same no clipping points in the backrooms. The first option is just…well, confusing, and the second option brings up the question about why some corrupted levels can't be escaped, assuming the "Interacting with corrupted objects."

[Long.](javascript:;)

[Wow so long.](javascript:;)

A no clip, by video game definition, is navigating through a game environment with collisions disabled, so as to be able to pass through solid walls, etc.  
There's a way to do this without cheats through different methods in different games, such as camera clipping or the pause, save, load, repeat method in Fallout 3 and others(I think.)  
I think the backrooms method is through high speed. Basically, imagine a game that renders stuff(Such as Minecraft), and imagine you have bad wifi, slowing the render speed. You can temporarily walk on the void before Minecraft renders it in. I imagine it to be our own eyes to be the render distance/speed, and successfully noclipping requires you to be backwards running.  
Either something complex as that, or we could go with the [Basics of the Backrooms](basics-of-the-backrooms-a-guide.md) ways of entrance, and assume that the same things are in the backrooms, explaining why some levels have a specific spot.

  
In terms of items, I assume that as soon as you stick a item in a no clip area, it either fully disappers in reality and goes into the backrooms, or it takes you along with it.

Also, you can't noclip back into reality. Well, maybe, but authors don't really acknowledge it. It would make a cool tale to have entities noclip into the frontrooms.
