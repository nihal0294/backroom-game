# A Demonstration of Nonviolence

# **Chapter 2**

### A Demonstration of Nonviolence

  
  
  

A few months later, on [Level 11](level-11.md). Reggie had not yet become resigned as had been predicted — if anything, the fires of rebellion were stronger now than ever. They had spent the last few weeks getting together like-minded Husks and even a few sympathetic humans for a peaceful protest in front of [M.E.G.](the-m-e-g.md) base Beta, in an attempt to remove their Entity classification. Over forty had turned up, just to hold some signs and stand silently in front of the building — no shouting, no waving, that would be seen as too dynamic.

---

'How are you doing with the signage?' Reggie asked Halley, who was methodically zip-tying placards to some of the protester's arms using her dexterous human hands.  
  
'All going well, Reg,' she said, finishing off the last in line, 'That's everyone — we all have a card now.'  
  
'Let us start then,' said Reggie, and waved the group to start walking.  
  
As the blue mass started to move forwards towards Beta, Reggie quickly sauntered towards Marvin at the edge of the crowd.  
  
'I didn't think you would actually go through with this,' said Marvin as they approached — he was carrying a sign reading "Equal Rights for Equal People!"  
  
Reggie Laughed. They had floated the idea of this little demonstration when they were on one of their resource trips through the Backrooms. At the time they were on [46](level-46.md), and deserts contracted the mind wonderfully, a bit like a crippling disease.  
  
'I guess I'm just determined,' replied Reggie, as they strolled round to the front of the crowd.  
  
As the demonstrators continued along the wide street, Reggie paid attention to the other humans that did whatever it was people were supposed to do around here.  
He saw the way MEG guards closely eyed them, checking for weapons from afar.  
The way couriers took a detour when walking around them. The way regular wanderers turned back into the alleys they were going through to find a different route.  
The way people in the taller buildings were peering out windows, hoping they weren't a threat.  
  
After what seemed like a month and a day of marching, they finally reached the entrance to the base, which they stopped exactly one meter in front of — they didn't wish to block anyone from getting in, that would be seen as obstruction, and therefore violence.  
The world became a very strange place once you consider the worst interpretation of every action.  
  
'Do you think we're having an effect?' asked a Husk called Olivia, still stoically waiting in the crowd.  
  
'Have a look at the first floor offices,' said Reggie without moving, 'I think they're getting distracted.'  
  
Indeed, the researchers seemed to be congregating at the windows and staring at their placards. The sun stayed steady as a rock while the protesters stood, doing nothing but hold up their signs and wait. After around an hour had passed by in the temperate street, some mid-level administrator came out and worriedly looked at a clipboard filled with questions.  
  
'Excuse me,' they said nervously, before remembering basic courtesy, 'Terribly sorry, A.E Atkins assistant researcher, at your service sir.'  
  
They glanced at the clipboard for a moment.  
  
'Could I speak, for want of a better word, with the leader of this group?'  
  
'We don't really have leaders,' said Reggie, stepping close enough to the sweating assistant for them to hear, 'But since I set this up, I suppose I have to talk to you.'  
  
'Uhh okay,' said the irritating young man, fiddling with a paperclip attached to the documents, 'What does your group want… like, specifically, from the MEG?'  
  
'We want to be treated as equal people in the eyes of your organization,' said Reggie forcefully, 'Under our current designation as entities we have next to no rights, even though we are as sapient as everyone else. I'm not sure the MEG can remove all social ostracism we receive, but changing our Entity page and hiring more Husk staff would help.'  
  
'I, uh, don't know whether they can do that.'  
  
'Just go,' said Reggie vaguely, 'Relay that back to your overseer. Until you have a solution for this, we shall be waiting.'  
  
Atkins hurried off, and Reggie rejoined the frozen crowd.

---

After a few more hours of expectation, Reggie was getting vexed. There was no sign of any other MEG associates, and now even the regular wanderers were starting to ignore them, pushing around the crowd like water around a stone to get into the base. Reggie stood their ground as a wanderer strolled a little too close and gave them a dirty look.  
  
'Something's wrong,' said Marvin worriedly, 'I can feel it in my water.'  
  
It wasn't a completely reasonable explanation for anything, but Marvin's instincts had never been wrong before - he had saved them from a [Wyrm](entity-42.md) attack once, just through feeling the invisible lines of energy which all pointed towards a bladder they no longer had.  
In any case, the number one rule of the Backrooms was to trust your gut, and this seemed to qualify.  
  
Reggie decided to entertain this idea, and surreptitiously surveyed the street around them.  
It looked normal enough, up until they looked towards the skies, and saw the rooftops.  
On one of the roofs next to the street they were on, some black-clad figure was setting up a curious contraption of wood and what looked like bungee cord.  
A firesalt slingshot, it had to be, he had seen the design before.  
  
'**Disperse!**' shouted Reggie in a psychic shriek that could even be heard by some of the humans around.  
  
But it was too late. As the group bolted in different directions, the firesalt ingot landed in the center of where the crowd was, shooting fire across the street. The warning had helped somewhat, however, as only a dozen of the protesters were hit by the dancing lights. They all knew that they couldn't be helped, the slingshot was already pulling back for another shot.  
  
There was a procedure for this eventuality, although nobody had expected it to be used in quite this fashion. It wasn't too hard to follow, you were just told leaving the level at the soonest possible point and avoiding hostile groups. Reggie ran into an alley, in the direction they knew would be a [Level 1](level-1.md) exit - there were many consistent ones near Beta.  
As they entered the thin corridor, they heard voices from the other end.  
  
'Located 68 instance,' said a deep voice, muffled somewhat by breathing gear, 'Squad, engage.'  
  
Then there was a slashing sound, and a psychic scream from the Husk that had just had it's arm violently amputated. The four U.E.C soldiers were pushing them into a corner, carefully blocking all escape routes with their long blades. Another arm was severed, with another more vehement cry. Then the supposed leader tore open their adversary's chest flap, flinging a vial of orange crystals into the wound.

---

Reggie stepped into the cleft of a wall and listened to the chatter as the unthinking soldiers checked the scorched corpse to see if they had actually vaporized the malformed [centipede](entity-134.md) with their fiery attack.  
  
'Sarge, I don't remember them screaming like that before,' said one of them.  
  
'You were fighting Partygoers before,' the sergeant replied, getting out more vials and tucking them into various vest pockets, 'These are Partypoopers, although they are almost as hazardous.'  
  
'It matters not,' intoned one of the younger members of the group, 'They are all contemptible varmint sent to harass honest working men.'  
  
'Easy lad,' said the sergeant, 'We all know the doctrine by heart. You don't need to spout it every battle.'  
  
'If we lose sight of our moral obligation all is lost,' the petulant youngster argued, 'We cannot let the evil entities win!'  
  
There was a moment of distraction there.  
Reggie artfully spun out of their hiding place and upended the closest man - who was guarding his end of the alleyway - with a swiping arm.  
They did not rouse again, the force of their fall knocking them unconscious.  
The other three noticed the movement, and started to prepare their weapons.  
  
'Eradicate the enemy!' the propaganda-obsessed one cried.  
  
Reggie said nothing. They just shattered their sign onto another man holding a machete, the thick cardboard forming large chunks.  
Then they swung their arm into the guy's stupid armored face, letting their noodly weight carry the man to the floor.  
  
They stepped over the sleeping form and ran the propagandist into a wall by the shoulders. The boy managed a valiant effort to shove Reggie away, and they cannoned into the last man, who was desperately trying to get his stuck machete out of it's holster.  
He was just able to get it out and slash vaguely at Reggie as the latter struck him hard in the chest, flinging him into a pile of bins, while Reggie only received a comparatively minor cut across the face.  
  
'Stay away!' screamed the final terrified soldier, as he frantically threw a bottle of clear liquid at the advancing shade.  
  
Reggie was too slow to dodge, and the glass shattered as soon as it contacted their shoulder. Searing pain engulfed them as the acid's acrid touch melted their blue epidermis. It exposed the red flesh underneath, looking like crimson blood in the half-light of the alley. Nothing remained under that mind-ending pain except incoherent rage. Reggie removed their attacker's head with a single strike. As the rage subsided, it was replaced with an emptiness. Remaining here would not be a wise move, and so Reggie left with speed.

  
  
  

### Pain…

### Betrayal…

### Regret…

…These are the emotions Reggie felt as they sat in a dark corner of [Level 1](level-1.md) trying to pull themselves together.  
  
They stared at their own reflection in a puddle of condensation. The burn didn't hurt much anymore, it had reduced itself to the dull ache similar to a fracture, but the skin was unlikely to grow back - for now they were stuck with unsightly red splashes across their shoulder and face. The scar was annoyingly ugly too - it had missed both eyes but was still another sensitive area to look out for.  
They slouched back against the wall, and would have cried if they hadn't lacked tear ducts and eyeballs for doing so. Reggie knew they shouldn't have killed that guy, even if he was an almost irredeemable U.E.C. employee. Violence wasn't the way, they knew, but…  
Reggie stared at the inside of their head. Was it really such a bad option? They thought some more about the morality of violence.  
  
'Some people can't understand discrimination unless it's against them,' thought Reggie, 'If the U.E.C. cannot see what harm they are causing, I will unleash it back at them, see what they do about that.'  
  
Reggie's phone started vibrating, so they opened it up to see a notification from the protest Chatroom.

Back on [level 48](level-48.md), the enumeration was not going well. Halley and Marvin had a printed charter of all the protest members, and they were checking their status off one by one.  
  
'Uh, Olivia Peterson?' asked Halley, looking up at Marvin, who was holding his phone with a MEG report open.  
  
'Corpse found near base Alpha, death by burn-related complications, they think.'  
  
'Matthew Walters?' asked Halley  
  
'Didn't turn up to any of the rendezvous sites,' said Marvin.  
  
'So I'll just mark as missing,' said Halley, 'and that's them all done.'  
  
'What's our status?' asked Marvin, surveying the columns of names and colours.  
  
'Six alive, twenty-nine confirmed dead, eight missing.'  
  
'Hateful, isn't it?' said Reggie, who was standing motionless in front of the open balcony, the sunset turning his Cerulean skin red.  
  
Marvin, sensing the tension, slowly edged up behind Reggie and gently placed his hands on their shoulders.  
  
'It's okay,' he said soothingly, 'We can rebuild.'  
  
Reggie shook him off with surprising vehemence.  
  
'It's not okay! Forty-Three people, exercising their moral rights of free speech, were reduced to six in less than an hour, and all you can do is say that we can rebuild. And then what? We just get killed again, just asking for our rights?'  
  
'We cannot do anything more drastic!' said Marvin, backing away slightly, 'the only thing more effective would be violent, and as we all know, violence is not the answer.'  
  
'What of guards? or police? Should they have their weapons taken away because violence doesn't solve anything?'  
  
'Well,' said Marvin vaguely, 'That is allowed, I think.'  
  
'So violence is only the answer when it reinforces the status quo?'  
  
Marvin stuttered at this, realizing he had argued himself into a corner.  
  
'This is now the third time I've lost everything I ever lived for,' said Reggie, slightly more calmly, crossing back over to the balcony again, 'The first time was when I ended up here. I left behind a decent job and a loving boyfriend in favor of a hellscape of decay; the second time I lost all my friends and my cushy scout job because I was attacked by a demon; and now I've lost almost all my friends again, because of a nonsensical quarrel. Do you think I'm going to let this happen to anyone else?'  
  
Marvin started to reply, but Reggie quickly continued their defense.  
  
'You may be fine with allowing the current state of affairs to continue because of the threat of retaliation, but I am not. You can stay defending dynamic stagnation if you like, but don't expect me to take part.'  
  
Reggie crossed to the door, and lightly turned the shiny handle.  
  
'Goodbye, at least for now.'  
  
They shut the door, and hurried down the path to the barn that would lead them to 10.  
  
Marvin sat back down, and held his featureless face in his flat hands. Halley sat beside him, placing a thin hand around his shoulders.  
  
'What do you think he'll do next?' she asked.  
  
'I don't know,' replied Marvin, 'Reg is not predictable in the slightest. There's no good way out of this, and I don't know if he's is going to help matters, or simply hinder the movement.'
