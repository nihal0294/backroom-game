Me and some of my friends have done an investigation on a Robovac and found that they're not very well suited for spying. We got our hands on a mostly-intact Robovac (somebody had ripped out the debris storage container but the rest of it was still in good shape) and took it apart to work out what was going on.

[@BNTG4life](javascript:;) was right, Backrooms Robotics hardware is tough to understand even if you're pretty experienced with hardware. But we managed to get a rough idea of what most of the parts did. The parts that are relevant are the cameras and microphones. Neither of them would work well for surveillance. The built-in camera is pretty terrible. It outputs low resolution video in black and white. I'd say that it's probably the bare minimum needed to allow a Robovac to navigate using visual data. You could use it to get the layout of a base, but not much else. As for the microphones, they're configured to pick up ultrasound- probably for communication. Those microphones would be really bad at recording human speech.
