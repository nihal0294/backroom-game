# The Farm...

# The Farm…

**"Okay, kid, before we go and get our pumpkins, I'm going to need you to come with us to the orchard. We need to get apples for Mom's desserts for tonight. We don't want what happened last year to happen again."**

*"But I was okay! I'm a big kid, as you say!"*

*"Honey, the farmer found you crying while being chased by one of his hens in the barn."*

"But it chased after me!"

*"Didn't you also have one of her chicks in your hand?"*

"I just wanted to pet it!"

**"And you got pecked for it. And grounded."**

"Hmph…"

*Mom just sighed. She knew I could be a handful, even if I meant well.*

*"Still, if you come with us, you can get first pick on an apple."*

"Okay!"

Pushing myself up and over the community's picket fences, rows of apple trees gently held several ripe red fruits that were crying out to be picked in their branches. These trees were sectioned in neat rows all across the flat terrain of the field. I could even see the pale farmhouse from the furthest row in the orchard. It wouldn't take me that long to get there from the fence, maybe a couple of minutes of walking, and I would be at the barn doors in no time!

Speaking of the fence. I could have sworn I was just in the neighborhood, just before the fence of the orchard. Was it always this close by? It should be a bit of a longer walk to get here from my house, yet it's only felt like a few minutes. How long was I walking for?

Have I eaten today? My stomach is feeling empty. Hopefully, the farmer won't mind me taking just one apple from the tree. I remember when my parents would have me go out and pick apples from the farm to make into apple fritters and cider. It would always be so delicious! But none of that cloud beat just picking them off the tree and eating them from the source. Even now, it still tasted so fresh and ripe as I bit into them. I feel the same energy I got from them in my childhood trips to the orchard.

I remember when the farmer would give me and some of the other kids some spare apples he had harvested from his orchard. When he let us watch movies on the barn wall with the old projector, he was always so kind. But I could never remember his name, even more so now.

I think my brain is starting to clear up a bit now. I'm starting to remember a few more things now. Maybe I was just hungry and couldn't think straight? I don't know, I know I'm starting to feel more alive right now because of the apple.

**"Okay, I think that's all the apples we need."**

"So does that mean it's-"

**"It's pumpkin time!"**

"Yay!"

Sadly, when I got to the barn, the doors were closed and locked, and I couldn't slide them open. The worn paint on the doors showed where previous attempts to open the gate had left scratches. Even when I tried to open the doors, they still didn't budge. There were no other entrances to the building's interior besides the high-up windows and the roof entrance. While I could get in there through the windows and roof, I don't think it would be very useful when I have to push the wagon back out, so that idea was a bust. Luckily, while I was looking around for an entrance, I managed to find a wheelbarrow on the right side of the barn. It was a bit old and rusted in a few places, but it still worked and moved with a slight squeak on the wheel.

[![Barn_edit](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--resized-images/fragment:october-31-2000-2/Barn_edit/medium.jpg)](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--files/fragment:october-31-2000-2/Barn_edit)

It's better than nothing!

No, if I remember correctly, the pumpkins the farm gives out are usually in the middle of the corn maze, near the north entrance of the orchard. Or at least good-quality pumpkins.

*"Honey, are you sure you want to go in there by yourself?"*

"Yeah, I'm sure mom! I'm a big kid, I got this!"

*"Very well, honey, if you need us, we will be close by, okay?"*

"Okay mom, but I got this! I'll beat you both to the center!"

**"Whatever you say, champ. Just be sure to call out if you get lost… Again."**

  
Hopefully I don't get lost again…

Grabbing the wheelbarrow from the side of the barn, I pushed it towards the south side of the farm, spotting a few hastily made arrow-shaped signs pointing towards where the supposed maze was.

The corn maze was a yearly tradition for the neighborhood; every October, the farm planted one. Or is it made? How are those mazes even made? I just know they just pop up around the middle of October, on the north side of the farm’s property. Doesn't really matter now. I need to get the pumpkins; the sun is already past its peak!

Rushing past the many trees in the orchard, I reached the corn maze's entrance, a makeshift wooden plank arch. There were signs hammered into the sides of the arch; the top of the bend was a large horizontal sign reading “CORN MAZE” in red. To the right side of the vault were the rules of the maze, written in the same red paint. The rules say to stay on the path, not to cut through the corn, not to pick the corn, not to eat the corn, not to throw the corn, not to run in the maze, and to have fun. The left side of the arch held a sign with a painted image of a pumpkin, with the words “BIG PUMPKINS IN THE CENTER OF THE MAZE! GET TO THE CENTER TO GET YOUR OWN PUMPKIN!” painted above it. That’s what I'm looking for! I just need to make it to the center and get back out again!… If I can remember my way out at least.

[![Corn_edit](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--resized-images/fragment:october-31-2000-2/Corn_edit/medium.jpg)](https://backrooms-wiki.wdfiles.com/local--files/fragment:october-31-2000-2/Corn_edit)

"MOOOOOOM! DAAAAAD! WHERE ARE YOOOOU?! I-I CANT FIND YOU!"

"Honey, we're over here! Remember what I taught you! Stick to your left, you'll find us soon!"

"OKAY!"

I did end up finding them in the end. I wanted to be brave for them, to prove I was that "big kid" I always wanted them to see me as. But in the end, I was still a scared little kid, calling out for Mom and Dad as all children do. Yet, that day, I remembered what they taught me, not just the maze cheat code trick, but that they were always looking out for me. Always there for me. Always just a few steps behind me.

I love them for that so much.

  
Entering the maze, wheelbarrow in hand, I noticed the corn stalks towering overhead, not able to see over them. The beaten dirt path below me and some fallen ears of corn were the only indicators that I was going the right direction. Taking turn after turn, I only hoped that I was going the right way. It's easy for me to get lost in mazes like this, my sense of direction being thrown off with every dead end I hit.

Was it always this twisty and turny? Or was I just never good at beating mazes?

Luckily for me, my lack of pathfinding skills was not too much of a detriment, as after my tenth, eleventh… fifteenth turn, I managed to find the center of the maze. I expected to see maybe a few pumpkins, some in a small pile, and that would be it. What I didn't expect was a mountain of large pumpkins, the tip of the tower teetering on the edge of breaching the tallest level of corn. Good lord, there were so many pumpkins; the only problem now is getting the right one from the pile without it all toppling over and crushing me. I managed to find one that wasn't too firmly lodged in the rest, able to wriggle it out of its place and put it into the barrow. Repeating this process two more times, pulling on some of the gourds to make sure they weren't a foundational piece, then throwing them in the wheelbarrow. By the time the pumpkins had been picked, the sun had begun to set farther, the blue sky above slightly darkening. Guess it's time to get going again. Luckily, the cart had made a track in the dirt path, so I could simply follow it back to the exit and save myself some time!

"Now that we have everything, let's go home!"
