M.E.G. Rework Discussion: Structure

12 Jul 2023 18:03

So as part of the rework of the M.E.G., I want to give the group a new, defined structure that will help the group make more sense and be much more functional given how big it is. This will be the present-day structure going forward. Of course, it will have evolved and changed much over time before reaching this point, looking very different in the earlier years.

New M.E.G. Structure:

Oversight Council /// Overseers & Underseers

- 5 Overseers are responsible for managing the 7 Sectors of the Major Explorers Group.

- Composed of the Overseers at the top of the chain of command, and the Underseers directly beneath them.

- Can give orders to any level of the group’s hierarchy, and can veto orders lower down in command (basically they have the final say in things, and have the power to do whatever they want within the group, including make changes to the structure if necessary)

- All decisions made by this Council are made by majority vote, so that no one individual can have complete power over the group.

Sectors /// Chief Directors

- Each Sector carries out an essential function of the group.

- Each is headquartered at one of 7 major bases (Alpha on Level 1, Gamma on Level 3, Omega on Level 4, Beta on Level 11, Delta on Level 230, Epsilon on Level 283, and Zeta on a level yet to be determined)

- Each Sector is led by a Chief Director, who manages the branches within their sector, and report to/takes orders from the Oversight Council

- The only group of people that make multi-sector decisions is the Oversight Council, and under normal circumstances, one sector does not have any authority over others.

- The Sectors have complex relationships that aid each others’ functions and allow the Major Explorers Group to run smoothly.

- Chief Directors can form and disband Special Teams and Outposts in addition to managing the branches. These are assembled for a variety of reasons, such as for carrying out non-vital functions of the group, conducting operations, or for emergency circumstances. These allow the Sectors a degree of flexibility not provided by the branches.

Branches /// Directors

- Each Sector is split into multiple branches, each of which manage specific areas within the Sector (ex. In an Exploration sector, there could be a cartography branch).

- Each branch is led by a Director, who reports to/takes orders from the Sector’s Chief Director

- Branches will only contain Teams with vital functions to the group (former divisions and regiments, as well as new additions). Special Teams will remain separate and instead report to the Chief Director.

- Directors can form and disband Teams within their branch as they deem necessary.

- Branches themselves are stationary within the major base the sector is headquartered in, while Teams can be sent out to wherever they need to be, depending on their purpose.

- They replace the old Regiments and Divisions (giving the group a much more fleshed out central command structure).

Teams /// Outposts

-Directors can form and disband Teams as needed.

-Teams within branches report to / take orders from their branch’s Director instead of a Chief Director.

-Outposts and special teams, such as Level-specific ones, are an exception, reporting directly to their respective Chief Director.

-Outside of highly unusual / extreme circumstances, Teams and Outposts never report to the Oversight Council directly.

-All currently existing Teams and Outposts will be assigned to a Sector, but will maintain lore compatibility

The goal behind giving it this structure is to better reflect the goals of the group and give it more identity. It will also take it away from the militaristic and SCP-like aspects that the old Major Explorer Group had. What are your thoughts on this?

I never used the word squads. I kept all the old terminology, save for divisions and regiments. We've been debating over what to call the sectors for ages, and this is the best we have so far, since a lot of other words make it sound like a government or religious, or disaster relief group. There really aren't a lot of words for a central system like this other than those contexts, which surprises me.

All groups that big need a central ladder of authority. If a group this big has no central structure and authority system holding it together, it'd fall into chaos pretty quickly. I just couldn't think of any other way for a group this big to be manageable and also make sense. The point of this system is to keep the vital functions of the group independent, but still working together as a group. No one part of the group can mess with the others and cause issues, but they can coordinate things and cooperate with each other to achieve higher goals and keep the group going efficiently. The specific functions of the sectors will also take it away from a military focus, once we decide on what the sectors will be (that's a discussion for later).

I'm open to ideas, but I can't see any other way of this making sense. The shift away from a militaristic focus is more in the way it functions than the names, since a lot of the old names were militaristic.

The group has a LOT of functions to deal with, and it just isn't possible to break them down into just three general groupings (I've had some in-depth discussions to people about this). Seven is already a stretch, but I can make that work. The point is that the group has grown to be HUGE, and it is going to have thousands of people across the many bases and outposts (at least hundreds per major base)

But to clarify on the reason for seven sectors. One, this is not a small group (it's the largest group there is, so it makes sense to have a larger structure at this point), and placing it under a smaller scope defeats the point of this project. I want there to be plenty of room for anyone to contribute lore (even to the main structure of the group), without having to just make up random outposts in other levels. This actually has it make more sense from a lore perspective, and gives way more options than just more outposts. Two, there are many categories of functions that can all be grouped together nicely, though they aren't able to be condensed into as few as three. These aren't set in stone, but just some ideas for the sectors.

Possible Sectors:

- Exploration (mapping, discovery of new places, sending out rescue missions. That sort of thing)

- Resource Management (Resources, determines how things are distributed, trade, and the production + storage of various resources. Also deals with food and other essential resources)

- Communications (Postal work, news, radio and internet communications/maintenance, etc.)

- Transportation (Deals with the logistics of transporting things throughout the Backrooms)

- Conveyance (combining transportation and communications, since postal work is transported communications, and each on their own wouldn't necessarily be as big as some of the other sectors)

- Records (which deals with documentation of information, as well as the Archives and the online database)

- Education (Suggested to me quite a few times. This would teach wanderers important information, such as stuff that would help them survive in the Backrooms, and perhaps even train new M.E.G. volunteers and members)

- Human Relations (deals with other GOIs and people not affiliated with the M.E.G.)

- Entity Relations (deals with entities found in the Backrooms, including hostile, neutral, and peaceful ones)

- Defense/Protection (Pretty self-explanatory)

- Relations (combining Defenses/Protection, Human Relations, and Entity Relations, this idea just puts the three into one. Probably the best option This would handle relations with other GOIs and communities, as well as security and defense-related functions. Be it human or entity, this is the sector that would deal with that, be it peaceful, neutral, or hostile.)

- Research (Do I even need to explain this one?)

I think the ones that combine several make more sense than the individual ones. Otherwise we'd have WAY too many sectors on our hands.

Lastly, I originally had it in mind as more of a disaster relief group, but not everyone agrees with this idea, and after hearing why, I do agree that it is a bit more than that at this point. That is certainly part of what they do, but a big part of it as well is exploration and research on the Backrooms. Some see it as disaster relief, some see it as a research group to document information, some see it as a government-like/militaristic group. I do want to remove the government-like/militaristic parts as much as possible, with the exception of existing names and lore, but I think I can mix the research and disaster relief into the group both.

Okay, having thought about this for a couple days, I still have some issues. However, it really depends on where you are in the process of writing this. If you're at the end, then I suppose the reworked structure you've presented is fine. It's not my favorite, but I'll live. However, if you're still at the beginning and haven’t started writing, then these are my issues.

### The M.E.G. is a Bureaucracy

There's nothing fundamentally wrong with a bureaucracy. However, this makes the M.E.G. even more like the Foundation (who have a lot of horror coming from the indifference a bureaucracy inherently has). The good things about a bureaucracy are that it's stable, it has a clear chain of command, and clear responsibilities for every role.

However, its strength is also its weakness: an organization like this has a ton of middle managers. At minimum (if every sector has 3 branches and every branch has at least 4 teams), that's at least 84 managers, not including the Underseers (whose role I'm not clear on). Communication between just the middle managers is going to be a *nightmare.* Throw in the Overseers and the foot-and- rank members, and that nightmare gets worse the more people there are. (Ask me how I know). There are going to have to be meetings to make sure everyone is on the same page. And if there's not meetings, there's going to be *emails.*

Getting anything done is going to be slow, especially if it comes from the bottom. If a member on the bottom of the hierarchy wants to make an overarching change, they're going to have to go through all of those 84 managers. Sure, the member could just contact the Overseers themselves, but in a bureaucracy, the people at the top will *never* be able to have a personal relationship with people at the bottom. There's just too many people, especially if the M.E.G. is as big as you say. So why would the Overseers listen to a random nobody whose qualifications they have no idea about?

This isn't a problem in our world, where things are relatively stable. But in the Backrooms? Where things can and have gone to shit very quickly? An organization like the M.E.G. needs to be able to react quickly. The more democratic a bureaucracy is, the slower it's going to be… which means that in order to react quickly, the M.E.G. will have to become more militarized to stay alive and reach their goals.

The other issue that's going to pop up is the issue of information silos. Realistically, the people in one branch of the organization aren't going to know what's going on in another branch of organization. That's by design, right? Each branch has their own purpose they're going for. However, this means that important information that one branch *needs* to know might be locked away by another branch, making communication slow and reacting to emergencies harder.

I can easily see the M.E.G. being crushed under their own weight when written this way.

#### Solutions

So like I said earlier, there's nothing wrong with the M.E.G. being a bureaucracy. All of the issues above could *maybe* make for an interesting story (if anyone has the skills to make the ins and outs of bureaucracy interesting).

But it should at least be scaled down. People are going to tune out if they have to keep track of 84+ characters as a reader or as an author. Personally, just thinking about keeping all of this straight is turning me off from using the M.E.G. in this iteration as an author. There's just so much to keep track of. Seven sectors with each sector having 3 branches? That's 21 unique things! Imagine being a new reader with no background knowledge of any of this. The way the organization is right now, the M.E.G. page is going to throw at least 100 items at someone.

Cut one layer out. Condense Branches and Sectors into one single layer. Then the structure is as follows:

Overseers  
Underseers  
Branch Leaders  
Team Leaders

It’s still a lot of middle managers, but it’s more manageable (heh).

OR

Eliminate the hierarchical structure completely. There’s a lot of information out there on different ways of organizing groups. I’ve found a list here: <https://www.lucidchart.com/blog/types-of-organizational-structures>

I would suggest the team-based structure personally. You would keep the Overseers, but then everything is organized by teams trying to achieve various goals set by the Overseers. This is a structure used by Apple, so I doubt you’ll have to worry about this structure not being able to support a large organization. This article goes into more detail: <https://communicationmgmt.usc.edu/blog/team-based-structures/>

This also drastically reduces the number of things a reader/author has to worry about from 84 down to 5 (just the Overseers). Which leads into my next point.

### This is too much information

Here's my fundamental question I think that illustrates why I have issues with how it's presented: Why is it important that I, the reader/writer, know any of this?

Having stuff this detailed *is* important when you're a solo writer. The reason for this is that as a solo writer, you have to know these things. You have to know so the internal logic of the story makes sense when you're writing.

However, the corollary with that is too much detail can bog you down when working with other people. More detail is fine when you're working with a small group of people. But on this site? All it's going to do is trip people up. The more detail you put, the more things are going to be "canonized," the more people are going to nitpick at inconsistencies.

All this information makes using the M.EG. hard to use. Which would be fine… if the M.E.G. wasn't the default POV. As much as people say it’s not, it really is. The default option should not be difficult to use.

Also, as someone who’s new to the community, I’m turned off by all this. I don’t see anywhere where I can contribute. All the holes are filled in. This doesn’t feel like something that I can contribute to. This feels like someone’s personal GOI, not the community’s.

I honestly wouldn’t get too bogged down in all the little details on how the M.E.G. is structured. What I think would work better is to narrow the M.E.G. summary down to 3 or so sentences:

- The M.E.G.’s primary goal is to find an exit to the Backrooms.
- Their secondary goal is to take care of people in the Backrooms.
- They have 5 Overseers who direct teams to fulfill these goals and others as needed.
- They have 7 main bases across the Backrooms.

Any lore or any missing gaps can be filled in by authors according to what they think the M.E.G. is. They may become a military group, a disaster relief group, or a research group, but ultimately, it’s not going to matter what they really are. They can be whatever the author needs them to be. That’s the beauty of having a no-canon policy. Other authors will fill in the holes.

The more you define what they are, the more narrow their scope becomes, the less mystery and intrigue the group has.

This obviously is all my personal opinion, but as a newcomer to the Backrooms, I hope you consider my point of view.

I also really want to hear others' opinions on this as I feel like I'm dominating the conversation.

Okay. There are a lot of points to cover here. Let's go in order.

1. The M.E.G. seems like a Bureaucracy.

It is absolutely not a government. All work here is entirely voluntary and there are no laws that govern people under their care. Anyone can leave at any time with no obligation to stay, and people around them and even within protected communities can live their own way. They stick together through common interests and a strong resolve. Working together and staying together helps them survive and achieve far more. Technically, even an overseer could just leave if they felt like it. It's just that none of them want to let the group down.

As for managers, there are never more than 4 managers in any chain of command (think of the top down as roots branching out in many directions) . You don't report to the leader of another team, and team leaders do not report to other sectors. It only goes up. If someone has an issue that needs to reach the top, the worst it would have to do is go through a team leader, a director/chief director, and then an underseer or overseer. In most cases, you can report issues directly to someone higher though. They DO listen to people's concerns. Due to the Backrooms internet, reporting issues and higher ups administering changes is extremely simple to do. The specific layers of the hierarchy are to divide up the management of the group to different people and keep it from becoming an overwhelming burden to anyone. Before, the 3 overseers handled the concerns of literally thousands of people on their own, as well as controlled every single aspect of the group. Every team, every outpost, etc. That's messy.

As for the higher upside not being able to form relationships with the "lower" people, that simply isn't true. Even before this, the overseers were known to often hang out with people they met in the major bases, and some of their best friends were members of teams way lower down in ranking. They talked a lot. Unlike a government, they do believe in their goals, and are not going to get lost being power-hungry or anything like that, Kat is very careful to pick good people in the new system. They value everyone, and they just assign people to specific areas based on their skills and interests to help the group out the most, and to give them the best chances at succeeding. And because they care, they would listen to concerns from anyone in the group, and even from outsiders, just like an ideal version of HR.

The group's numbers are so big that you need a giant system to hold it together. The sheer number of managers and layers is necessary. The obstacle of relaying information is actually pretty easy to get around when they use every resource at their disposal, like the Backrooms internet. Also, sectors DO communicate constantly. They can know what's going on with each other. They just can't command each other. It's arranged so that they need to work together (a member of one sector just can't order around or discipline members of other sectors. They should each handle their own jobs. An exploration director telling a member of records stationed at the exploration sector to go on a dangerous exploration mission would be bad). Records provides information to the other groups, resources provides equipment, supplies, food, and more, conveyance maintains communicaton equipment and helps with logistics, and so on (Just as stand-in examples). If one failed to perform their duties or had a problem, it would become apparent pretty quickly, and that could be reported to the higher ups to be resolved in a timely fashion. These sectors have teams stationed in every base so that if a sector itself fails, those smaller teams can keep things going everywhere else until the situation is resolved. It is a structure that resembles a Bureaucracy, but it is something unique. Any large-scale structure for any group would eventually resemble some kind of government structure eventually. But it's just that, a resemblance.

Lastly, you mentioned it being democratic and it needing to be flexible. It is very flexible. Directors and chief directors can not only directly and quickly form teams to handle emergencies or unexpected situations that arise, but they can quickly change up operations in any layer they are on or below them to deal with the emergency. If someone reports an issue to a manager, that manager can deal with it immediately. People CAN report issues to a higher position. The reporting to the next level up is for results of their work. Emergencies and genuine issues can skip layers of reporting. As for it being democratic, it's actually more like a kind of republic. They will listen to concerns, but the people in charge make the decisions, with a checks and balances for every layer to ensure no one abuses their power. If a branch acts up, a sector or oversight council member can deal with it. If a sector messes up, an oversight council member (or the whole council) can fix that, and if an individual in the council tries to abuse power, the other members can shut it down by majority vote.

Overall, my point is that this system allows it to be functional and efficient, while maintaining the human values of the M.E.G. It may resemble some government structure, but it's far from it

2. Why should the reader or writer know any of this?

Well, this is all so we can keep consistency in the stories. The ideas I'm proposing now are a road map of the overall concept. Not what readers need to all know immediately, or even authors. They can pick up the pieces over time if they need. In most cases, you'll never have to mention a branch, sector, director, or the Oversight Council. In most stories, you can simply have it revolve around a character or team/outpost off doing their own thing in their own lore. All of this stuff is the bigger picture, but individual stories can still flourish under it with no need to worry about canonizing everything. It works with all existing stories like these. There is plenty of room to add your own lore, and to be creative with this stuff. You can reference the main structure, imply it, or leave it out entirely. It's up to the writer. And headcanons can omit it, or include it.

There is PLENTY of room to contribute. Not just in the ways I've just mentioned, but I purposely did not create my own characters for the directors, chief directors, underseers, and even most of the new overseers. I want community members to make those characters and add their own lore and backstop to the group that way. When all these pieces come together from many different people, it will feel much more like a community-built project. I'm just laying the foundation. You can make teams or Outposts like before, but you can also flesh out the bases, the many managers, and even the Oversight Council itself!

I won't make the GOI page descriptions nearly so complex, and I'll leave a lot of details out to allow other pages to explore the specifics. The way I'm splitting the timeline into a few parts even allows canons to keep using the old M.E.G., the new one, or have their canon M.E.G. evolve along with this one. Technically people can still orient their versions of the M.E.G. towards research, disaster relief, or military. The large structure acting as a template and much variety leaves room open for it for it.

3. The M.E.G. are the main POV.

This is only this way because the community sees it this way. In reality, this is one of many groups, and you can choose to write from any other perspective. I own the group's page and many related pages, so I can do as I like with them and take the M.E.G. in my own direction. I still do want to have a lot of community involvement and make it into something that they are proud of using, and I know this won't be east. I've already spent a year discussing these plans with others. Heck, the seven sectors structure wasn't even my idea originally. It was a suggestion given to me, and I just expanded on it with help. As a community of authors, we made something new that we all liked.

I really have considered many angles and views. People's differing canons, how people see the group, how it's considered the main group, logic and lore consistency, simplified explanations for new readers (which we will make), how people can contribute, making it differ from a government, military, and SCP etc. There was a lot of thought put into this. I'm not discrediting your thoughts. I just want to know your opinion knowing exactly the kind of thought was put into this, and now that you know pretty much the full picture, give or take a few things.

I've also ironically had quite a few complaints about the M.E.G. being too vague in lore, structure, etc. I aimed to fix that too with this, by giving them a clear history and structure.

And again, this is NOT what will go on pages. I'm just seeing what people think of the concept. Most of it will take a long time to reach a page, and it will sit in the M.E.G. notes to work off of and slowly expand the group's lore. The community will help with that big time. Bureaucracies aren't governments. They are an organizational structure defined by having a clear chain of command, clearly defined responsibilities, and a set of internal guidelines that members follow. Most governments are bureaucracies, but not all bureaucracies are governments. Most corporations have a structure that is similar to the M.E.G. in its current iteration. They have different names, but looking at the structure, the M.E.G. is a bureaucracy.

I can see you've put a lot of work into this. You've taken a great deal in trying to see every angle. At this point, you probably should just start writing. There's a point where world building and getting information is just procrastinating, and a year of planning is a long time of not writing lol :P

I think I've gotten my points across. Hopefully they're helpful when you move to the next stage of writing! Good luck!
