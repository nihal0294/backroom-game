Overall, I think this is a fairly ok level lacking substance that make it stand out from the other countless levels, but to be honest, I wouldnt expect much from someone's first article (if this is your first, if not, I apologize for my incorrect assumptions).

Anyways. Now, I'm no greenlighter nor have I posted an article, but I felt like I've read enough articles to be able to crit decently well. I'm bad at catching SPaG errors, so don't depend on me on that. If you wish, do take this with a grain of salt though.

Aside from what I talked above, I can still spot a noticeable amount of SPaG errors even for my grammar-deprived brain. For example, go to paragraph 2 second sentence, and see if you've spelled 'shelve' right. There's also a consistent lack of space between a period and the next sentence in the 'Engineer' section plus overuse of periods.

Aside from grammar issues which I am not gonna go too in-depth into, there's a couple things that I would like to point out:

> The number of books on the shelves will gradually increase with the change of floors. The higher the floor is, the more books there are on the sneleves. You can tell which floor you are on by the books on the shelves, or find by the signs. And the number of harmful entities gradually increases. So the higher the floor is, the more dangerous it is.

This could be rearranged to:

> The number of books on the shelves and the quantity of hostile entities gradually increase with each floor, as higher floors tend to have more books and entities than lower floors do. Thus, if one cannot locate the signs indicating the floor number, they can look at the books on the shelves to easily determine that.

Putting related information (such as the elements associated with the change in floors) together can increase readability.

---

> An Engineer is like a librarian.They are also active entities at the Level 415

This could be changed to:

> Engineers, just like Librarians, are active entities on Level 415.

---

> Level 415 is a huge modern library with a total of nine floors, the height of each floor is about 4 meters. And the central hall appears every 50 meters.

I'm honestly quite puzzled when I saw this sentence. What is the central hall? What do they look like? What do they contain? This is nowhere mentioned in the article despite being brought up farther down the article, and in fact, I couldn't find any solid explanation on the structure of the library itself besides that one lone picture. Consider writing something that explains the layout and the various parts of the library (libraries doesn't look all the same!) so readers doesn't get hella confused while reading some of the later parts.
