Okay, there's a lot, so I've put it all into the collapsible. I'm copying and pasting my critique from the google Doc I've made, so if something's unclear, let me know.

**Decoded Text**

> Multiple tentacles of flesh burn into your cloth

Change "cloth" to "clothing"

> hard, crackling bone.

Keep it somewhat parallel with the previous sentence structure: "and cracks your hard bone."

> Your sockets bore and burn with a shooting pain.

While I'm a big fan of the description, maybe take it from a 10 to an 8. You're not really losing the vibe if you tone it down just a touch.

> You cannot scream over us.

Just a nice line I liked :3

> until you rust, too

wouldn't say the main fear of your page is rust. When I read it, I'm getting more of an assimilation vibe. Rust happens when iron is exposed to oxygen and corrodes. Nothing about the description is corrosive. The flesh monster doesn't do anything to the body itself. It tears the body apart and adds it to itself, if that makes sense.

So I would say, " Until you succumb too" instead here.

**2nd Page**

> Buildings outwardly depicting themed small businesses, food-related establishments, and a variety of other structures typically found in a residential uptown are to be encountered most often in the sub-level.

Change to "Buildings outwardly depict themed small businesses, restaurants, and a variety of other structures found in a residential uptown."

> These buildings advertise a manifold of products. Signs and billboards placed in-front of buildings advertise food, convenient goods, hardware, and many other products.

Change to "These buildings also advertise a manifold of products, such as food, convenient goods, hardware, and other products."

> No building has

Add "However" at the beginning of the sentence.

> The rooms of buildings mimicking

Change to "interiors"

> don't rust like we have

Again, the overall fear of you article isn't rust. It's assimilation. Perhaps… "succumb" is a better word?

> The lampposts shed plaque,

Not plaque. It doesn't feel right. Pick a different word or extend the body part metaphor more. For example, "The lamppost shed fluorescent light like plaque upon the sidewalk; each one glows dimly in dense fog from afar."

> The damp air concentrates heavily in the night-cycle of the sub-level, and, in a phenomenal circumstance, forms dense fog.

Change to "The humidity is more concentrated during the sub-level's night cycle and often forms a dense fog."

Change to "Before"

> Most reports stated of the night-cycle beginning abruptly, during a time when Level 11.2 was in a constant day;

Change to "Most reports stated that the sub-level suddenly started to have a night-day cycle. Until that point, Level 11.2 was in a state of constant day;"

> Due to this, it can be said the night-cycle of the sub-level is only relatively new to the sub-level, itself.

Remove. This is implied by the previous sentence and is therefore redundant.

> continue to until reason is provided.

Remove the "to." Change "reason" to "an explanation" and "provided" to "discovered."

> 13 hours4. The night-cycle lasts for precisely three hours.

So the total comes to 16 hours in a "day?" Just double-checking that's on purpose and not a math mistake.

> The abrupt change in the color

Change "The" to "An"

> The smell of ozone and rust intensifies at night.

Change "rust" to "metal." It'll make more sense with the actual themes of your page.

> Breath and heartbeat fill ears to the brim as you walk further to get away from yourself.

Just commit to second person with this sentence. "Your breath and heartbeat fill your ears to the brim as you walk further to get away from yourself."

> your footsteps are leaving imprints in the tissue on the pavement.

Logically, that doesn't quite make sense. If something is made of tissue, I wouldn't really question why I'm leaving imprints. It's quite squishy after all. Maybe a different question would fit, like "why you've chosen this path of flesh and concrete."

> The quiescence bore you,

Change "bore" to "bores"

> in every step.

Change to "with every step"

> Wet, burning, rusting flesh

Pick a different adjective. Maybe "sloughing"

> it is advised wanderers hide in garbage bins. it is advised wanderers hide in garbage bins

Remove the first instance of the phrase

**> The Meanies**  
Eh, I don't think these guys do much still. It would be better if they had died suddenly or something. Truly show the threat of the Emporia

Alright, that's everything! Also, you've done the offsets a touch wrong. You're going to want to put your encoded message on its own offset or else it'll show up on every page. Read the [Offset Guide](offset-guide.md) again.

Sorry it took so long! Some staff duties happened, and I had to put critiquing on hold. Post here if you have more questions!
