# **Chapter 6**

### Record Straightening

  
  
  

The brighter the light, the stronger the shadows it creates.  
  
Up above, in the infinite city, the sun always shone, and the lights always blazed in base Beta.  
  
In contrast, the darkness in the basements was absolute. It wasn't often that people ventured down here, so the electrical boxes across the shady concrete walls were permanently bathed in shadow. Dust drifted across the empty space from unseen breezes.  
  
Today, it was not the wind that stirred up the dust in the cavernous rooms. A metal talon reached out for a slightly open cover.  
  
Reggie surveyed the electrical panel. They didn't want to cause any issues with containment, so they carefully checked each wire before flicking off the switches for each circuit in turn. First the security cameras, then the electronic locks at the basement level, then the lighting for floor three.  
  
The panel clicked closed, and the dust clouded up again as The Catalyst stalked out.

---

'So, you mean you leaked internal information to an accused criminal?'  
  
The moral defense was not going well for Ruby.  
  
'Don't people have a right to know when misinformation is being made about them?' she countered, trying not to sound as if she had worked out this conversation in advance.  
  
'It's standard protocol not to share unreviewed information outside of the organization, in case it becomes actual misinformation,' replied Walter Smith, his swivel chair remaining rock solid as he talked.  
  
'But surely they would know about their history better than we would, right?' said Ruby. 'I thought they may be able to straighten out the inaccuracies.'  
  
'It's not exactly a reputable source, though, is it,' Smith replied, 'any firsthand information has to be collected through logged interviews.'  
  
'It could at least give us a starting point that we could back up with verifiable evidence,' replied Ruby.  
  
'But we ought to-'  
  
The lights in the room turned off, the darkness cutting through Walter's sentence like an axe.  
  
'The power again?' rhetorically asked Smith, as if this was a common occurrence.  
  
'Should we tell the janitors about this?' asked Ruby.  
  
'I'm sure they will have noticed already, they're usually pretty proactive,' replied Smith, silhouetted against the slowly opening blinds.

---

The sunlight from the perpetually bright outside filtered in on a room not unlike the one lit with fluorescent bulbs mere moments ago. The main difference was the stylish renegade leaning in one corner.  
  
'I hear you're working on a person of interest page about me,' said The Catalyst, stepping out of the shade, 'I also hear that much of the information contained within is untrue.'  
  
They stepped closer to the central desk and the paralyzed senior author.  
  
'You wouldn't happen to know anything about that, would you?' Reggie asked, in their uncanny voice that reached the brain without going through the ears.  
  
'H-How did you get in here?!' stammered Smith, reaching for the telephone.  
  
Reggie pinned the handle of the phone to the base with a metal hand, before sliding the whole device out of the way.  
  
'Through the door, like everyone else,' they replied, 'please — If I were meaning anything harmful, I would have done it already. Just tell me what I need to know.'  
  
'If you've seen the article, then you should know that I have no power over the sources.'  
  
'Indeed,' replied The Catalyst, 'I simply wish to know how you know this. I find it suspicious, it's not standard protocol to restrict authors to a single source.'  
  
'I got an email just after receiving my initial orders,' replied Smith, 'I suppose you should know that too, what with all these leaks.'  
  
'Hey, it's not like I've done anything wrong here!' wailed Ruby defensively.  
  
'Calm yourself,' said Reggie, raising a hand, 'You did the right thing at the time.'  
  
The Catalyst turned to Smith again.  
  
'Do you still have these emails?' asked Reggie, 'May I see them?'  
  
'They're still on my computer,' said Smith, gently tapping the rectangular monitor, 'But you turned the power off, didn't you, so of course I can't access them.'  
  
'The lighting is on a different circuit to the appliances,' said The Catalyst, opening up the login page, and instantly proceeded to the desktop, 'and you haven't even set a password. I don't think that's protocol either.'  
  
Smith did an impression of a ceiling inspector and Ruby looked smug. Reggie eventually found the correct email in the overflowing inbox, and scanned it disdainfully. The prose was about what you would expect from high-ranking M.E.G. personnel, full of buzzwords and odd formatting. The digital signature was correct too, even down to using the correct file type for the logo, a mistake amateurs often made.  
  
Reggie focused on the email correspondent. The name was correct, "Administrator-R-Locke," in the standard order of rank-initial-surname, nothing wrong there. The domain name looked right at first glance, "M℮G.bck," but a cursory inspection showed this not to be the case. Reggie smiled inwardly — it was the only way he could.  
  
'This isn't from the administrator,' said Reggie, turning away from the screen.  
  
'How?' asked Smith, 'It has all the proper signatures and addresses, How can it be fake?'  
  
'Have a look at the web address it's from.'  
  
Walter glared at the displayed letters.  
  
'I don't see anything wrong.'  
  
'Look at the E,' replied Reggie, zooming in more.  
  
'Is that some kind of larger lowercase E?'  
  
'U+212e,' said Reggie, 'also known as the estimated symbol. That is not a regular e.'  
  
'So… we've been following the orders of someone else?' said Ruby, who had been uncharacteristically silent recently.  
  
'It seems that way,' said The Catalyst, shutting down the computer.  
  
'What am I going to do about this?' asked Smith, pacing around the room in an agitated manner.  
  
'Contact the overseers,' said Reggie, getting up and checking the windows, 'Tell them you realized it was a spoof account when the orders didn't line up with protocol. Leave my involvement out of it.'  
  
'Wouldn't that be lying?' asked Ruby.  
  
'I honestly don't care,' said Reggie, opening the window, 'Just get the article written properly. Contact me if you need an interview or something.'  
  
'Wait,' said Ruby, 'Can you answer a few questions now, instead of later? Just so we have something to work from, you understand.'  
  
The Catalyst hesitated on the windowsill for a moment.  
  
'You may ask one question.'  
  
'Why do you call yourself The Catalyst?'  
  
'A catalyst in chemistry speeds up the rate of reaction,' explained Reggie, 'It can also be used figuratively for social or environmental change. I am trying to achieve both. Eventually, the U.E.C. will see that what they are doing to sapient entities such as myself is immoral and wrong. Whether it happens now, or in many years time, I don't care, all I want to do is make sure that it happens sooner rather than later. Thus, I am a catalyst, speeding up a reaction that may take centuries to achieve.'  
  
'Do you… think it's working?' asked Ruby carefully.  
  
Reggie stared into the abyss within, and saw it stare back.  
  
'No.'  
  
He vanished out of the window, with nobody below seeing him land.  
  
Smith stared at the open window for a long time, before eventually picking up the phone, and dialing an internal number.  
  
'Can I speak to the overseer? It's about falsified emails.'

---

Reggie sat just outside of the window, positioned so that they could hear but not be seen.  
  
As they heard the phone conversation inside, Reggie felt better than they had for weeks.  
  
They had both ensured disinformation wouldn't stain the M.E.G. database, and brought the perpetrators one step closer to the light.  
  
The Catalyst swung to a nearby ledge, and proceeded across the rooftops back towards Haven. As the sun beat down above and the crowds swam below, Reggie headed home.
