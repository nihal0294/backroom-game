I think the issue was suggesting that we should simply forget the classical themes and style and move on to something else. The (now deleted) article especially seemed to imply towards a certain type of articles and themes.

…Which isn't ideal. While I do agree the classic writing style was often poorly executed[1](javascript:;), the classic concepts are to this day a core component of this collaborative writing community. Changing writing styles because a part of the community deems that it is time to depart from the core concepts of this wiki isn't progress, you progress by working on the quality of your writings, by bringing unique and interesting stories, by enriching the lore and with qualitative world-building.

Of course, bringing a new writing style to do so is welcome, but it is also entirely possible to build something new with the old. What has made "liminality" so popular and what were its shortfalls? Why are people so interested about murder monsters and what can be done to go beyond their one-dimensionality? Is the clinical tone salvageable and if yes, how so? If not, how can we create something that takes the "liminal" feeling of early levels and mix it with a more experimental writing style?

All of that to say: the worst that could happen to a collaborative community of this type is to end up writing the same thing just to follow the hype of the moment.
