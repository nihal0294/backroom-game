I remember that in [Level 75](level-75.md), they mention that the gallium they harvest is used in stuff like weapons, so I assume that that's at least one source of the guns.  
I assume that the B.N.T.G. has a pretty robust manufacturing operation, considering they're able to produce [Deuclidators](object-4.md) at the rate that they do.  
Backrooms Robotics also probably has a big manufacturing workforce given the kind of stuff they mass produce ([Lamps](object-4.md), refinement of G9hS54 into [Red Light White Light](object-38.md), etc.), so they could probably do weapons if they wanted.  
On the page for [Tournesol](tournesol.md), it mentions that the U.E.C. uses (or used) the Level for mass production of weapons, although given the nature of the U.E.C., and context given in the offset, I don't think that they were sharing those things.  
There's also the possibility that people use [Organiguns](entity-39.md), but like, are we really accepting that the M.E.G. is standardizing the use of those things? I love Organiguns, but the idea that the M.E.G. would make them standard-issue is too out there for me.

This is just what I remember, so I may be forgetting something.

Well, if you know how they work, it's not all that hard to build a simple firearm(like a slamfire pipe shotgun), and with enough practice and/or knowledge as well as some machining tools I'm sure producing significant amounts of high quality firearms would not be infeasible.  
Now, ammo could be a problem, but firesalt could be used both as propellant and as primer. It'd probably require a good bit of experimentation to get loadings right. Downside of firesalt is that it wouldn't work above 47C which means your gun either needs to just accept heat limitations or be an open bolt[1](javascript:;) weapon which puts screws with accuracy.  
A concern with using firesalt as propellant is that it could explode if you drop a bullet or magazine, which would be **bad**.

Footnotes

[1](javascript:;). This means that the weapon fires immediately after seating the bullet rather than leaving time for the barrel to stop vibrating from the impact of the bolt and the previous firing.
