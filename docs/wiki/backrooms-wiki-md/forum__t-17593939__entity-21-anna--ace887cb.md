Man, this is fucked up. And I'm all for it. How ironic, and fitting, that to a lonely, pathetic, misogynistic sack of shit like David, his ideal wife would be a mannequin… and even *that's* not enough for him. There were lots of horrific moments in this article — the frenzied counting; the food; the fact that, in the beginning of the article, as if we were in David's shoes, we could plausibly think Anna was a real person; the negative space of the parts with David and Anna in the closet (ugh); the fact that the creature very clearly and cleanly slots into the Backrooms ecosystem; the fucking *piss-drinking* (the piss-drinking actually got a cackle out of me). This article does a good job saying what needs to be said, and leaving what doesn't, unsaid.

Now, if I had one (small) complaint, it's just that the setting here doesn't feel very fleshed out. Like, while I think the direct and plain prose and the descriptions of the food do a decent job of selling the banality of this level of the Backrooms, it doesn't really sink in for me just how horrible this Backrooms level is, how lonely it makes you feel, how utterly *bored* you become after days and months and years of wandering identical hallways and warehouses. I find this important because, the way I interpreted it, that loneliness, that misery, is how the entity feeds — after all, nobody in their right mind, nobody with an actual set of healthy relationships with other human beings, would be driven to the sheer depths to try to form one with a mannequin. Without an environment that promotes isolation, Entity-21 would starve.

As an aside, I think the fact that this entity leverages the Backrooms' isolating qualities makes it more interesting, to me, than many of the other more "classic" entities out there — some of them give me the sense of "well, there's a scary dimension — it's gotta have a monster in it, right?" without much regard for whether the monster fits the setting. Entity-21, on the other hand? It fits in extremely well.

Okay, back to the setting. Despite my gripes, I recognize that I *might* only have found the setting to be underbaked because I haven't read much of the Backrooms yet, and, so, the misery and repetition and isolation that more seasoned readers take as a given from other, more foundational works they've read, is lost on me, a newer reader.

All in all, though, that complaint aside, this is an excellent article. Easy +1.
