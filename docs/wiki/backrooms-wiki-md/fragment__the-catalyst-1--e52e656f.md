# An Unlikely Reprieve

# **Chapter 1**

### An Unlikely Reprieve

  
  
  

The light of a perfect sunset in eternal stasis shone over Reggie's wrecked form as their as-of-yet unknown savior sat beside them on the long beach of [Level 48](level-48.md). The soft sand under them and the calming waves nearby eased Reggie's rapidly disappearing pain. Perhaps their rescuer noticed their ease, because at that point they spoke again, in that strange voice that didn't seem to have anything to do with air molecules.  
  
'Look, I know you must be confused about what's happened to you, about why you can't speak or move easily, but all will become clear.'  
  
The indistinct blue person drew closer, and came into focus in front of Reggie's up-staring eyes. They were a tubular, vaguely humanoid creature, sans mouth or any other facial features except two tall crimson slits where eyes should be. Their skin was a sort of bluebonnet hue, and they wore some kind of formless green hoodie over their narrow shoulders.  
  
'You were attacked by a Partygoer, as you no doubt noticed,' they began, 'and it tried to turn you into one of them. Fortunately for you, it failed, and so you are now what is known as a [Frowning Husk](entity-68.md), just like me. A Husk is a malformed version of *them*, set apart from the hivemind. We still have sapience, although we have to talk with our minds, and have greater difficulty with all tasks humans find normal.'  
  
The Husk seemed saddened by this, as if recounting some great calamity. Then it seemed to remember something important.  
  
'By the way, my name is Marvin, but just call me Marv. Now, to get you talking, in the nicest possible way of course. You may remember [Zero](partygoer-zero.md) trying to talk to you while you were… resting. Try and imagine that link again, but extend it towards me. I know that isn't a very helpful explanation, but there really is no other way to explain telepathy.'  
  
Reggie tried, harder than they had tried any spiritual quest before — not that they had attempted telepathy before, believing it to be both impossible and a waste of time. They must have sat there for hours, just working their way up from vague suggestions all the way to full words, then finally to proper sentences.  
  
'Do you think we are finished now?' asked Reggie eventually.  
  
'You'll get better at it as time progresses,' replied Marvin, 'I think we're done for now. We ought to get you up and moving.'  
  
'How do I do that?'  
  
'It's different than how a human would move. You don't have muscles which only pull in two directions, now you have full gyroscopic motion in every part of your body. You have to stop bits of you from moving so that you can have other parts move you forward. I'll get you standing up, try and move your legs like you were walking, but hold everything else steady.'  
  
The moment Marvin let go of Reggie, they fell eyes-first into the damp sand.  
  
'Sorry about that,' said Marvin as he picked their companion's stricken shape out of the dented beach.  
  
'It's not as if I wasn't already covered in sand,' Reggie said as they were dragged to their feet.  
  
This simple task also took many attempts, but after about half an hour of failure, Reggie was able to barely limp, while leaning heavily on Marvin.

---

As they toiled along the lovely beachfront, Reggie decided to ask where they were going.  
  
'My place of course,' said Marvin, 'I asked the neighbors to keep an eye on it so it should still be safe. It's not more than one or two kilometers away, judging by that radio mast over there.'  
  
As they proceeded forwards, there was the roar of pinkthers in the background, but Marvin was definite that they weren't a threat. The sun stayed motionless in the sky as they hobbled together, proceeding towards comfortable safety.  
  
'By the way, what is your name?' asked Marvin casually, 'It just occurred to me that I didn't ask before.'  
  
'Reggie, or Reg if you prefer that I guess.'  
  
'Short for Reginald?'  
  
'Nope, that is a name for decorated British officers with more hair on their upper lip than their head, not a name for a boy not expected to do much more than attack a spreadsheet.'  
  
At that point, Reggie noticed the oddity.  
  
'What is that for?' they asked, indicating the chunky claw attached to Marvin's otherwise flat left hand.  
  
'This old thing?' said Marvin, 'It helps with picking stuff up. I'm sure I have some spares at home you could have if you want.'

## Useful Object File:

### Husk Claw

![Simple blueprint of a crab-like metal claw from three angles with labeled parts.](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/local--files/the-catalyst/Claw_Blueprint)

A digital blueprint of a common design of claw

A Husk Claw is a dexterity-enhancing prosthetic designed to be fitted onto [Frowning Husks](entity-68.md). It consists of a geometric claw similar to a crab's that is attached to a flexible torus base which fits around the wrist of the Husk wearing it. They simply have to flex their hand and the claw will open and close. Exact designs vary based on cost, but they often have texturing on their edges in order to increase grip. They were invented in 2022 by an anonymous Frowning Husk who shared early designs on Backdoor, saying that they had worked on it for many months and were distributing the files in order to help others like them. They are mainly manufactured by independent craftsmen in Metropolitan areas, although [the B.N.T.G.](the-b-n-t-g.md) is looking into the economics of mass production.

---

By the time they reached their destination, Reggie was able to walk unaided, albeit erratically.  
  
'We're here,' said Marvin, stopping in front of a small luxury beachfront house, all limewashed balconies and massive glass windows.  
  
'Nice place, isn't it?'Marvin asked rhetorically as he fumbled open a windowed spruce door.  
  
Inside was an immaculate open-plan living room with kitchenette, furnished akin to the outside world, with minimalist sunset paintings and a strong Tiki theme throughout. Reggie sat down on an angular cream couch and tried to relax.  
  
'I mean, it's pretty nice compared to everywhere in this hellhole,' said Reggie, 'But couldn't it be a little less… stagnant?'  
  
'Well I have been away for a bit of time,' replied Marvin, 'You know, resource gathering, the like… I would get a job with the Veroka Farmers, but they don't hire entities for some reason.'  
  
'Why not?'  
  
'Not trustworthy, they say,' replied Marvin with disdain, 'Apparently anyone who can work a plow can be hired except me. I have to go to level 1 via 10 then 11 to get some of the crates, then make my way back through 46. I only found you because I saw a trapdoor in 46 and decided to take the risk of going through 134.'  
  
'That seems like a long route only to get a bit of almond water,' said Reggie, 'Isn't there a better way to get supplies?'  
  
'I haven't found any safe alternatives,' said Marvin, 'and anyway, I can trade whatever I don't need with the farmers for Veroka juice.'  
  
'Speaking of juice,' said Marvin, opening a tall fridge in the kitchenette and taking out a small bottle, 'Want some?'  
  
'How am I supposed to drink it?'  
  
'Oh yeah, I forgot to explain that,' said Marvin, sliding down a catch on one claw and letting it drop to the table.  
  
He used the other claw to unscrew the cap, then the non-augmented hand had something strange happen to it. The flat end split, revealing rows of carefully filed teeth inside a partygoer-like mouth. Marvin steered his mouth over the bottle, and used the teeth to grip it while he turned it so that the liquid poured into the orifice without leaking.  
  
'See, Reg?' said Marvin, as Reggie watched nervously, 'You've got mouths instead of hands, just like our hated adversaries.'  
  
Reggie stared at their hands, willing them to open, and they did, showing sharp cutting teeth within a crimson mouth. They recoiled slightly from the suddenness of their opening, but managed to collect themselves. Marvin had strolled over to stand in front of them, and offered the half-full bottle of grey juice.  
  
'You try,' he said, holding the container at a slight angle so to not spill the liquid.  
  
Reggie tentatively reached their open hand towards the neck of the bottle, and carefully put their mouth around it. It was an unusual sensation, drinking through the arms instead of through the neck, but it wasn't bad, as such, just a cold feeling from wrist to shoulder, like drinking normally. Marvin tipped the bottle some more, and nothing came out.  
  
'Well done,' said Marvin, 'You managed not to spill any. You seem to be getting better at controlling your body.'

---

A few hours later, the brothers-in-death were sitting at the kitchen island next to each other, in front of a sumptuous meal of salad. It had been foraged by those eating it, from the various edible leaves that coated this Habitable level, along with some kind of grey mayonnaise equivalent made of Veroka.  
  
'You've been very… hospitable,' said Reggie. 'Why?'  
  
'Because we're all we've got, Reg,' said Marvin, sticking his hand in his bowl, 'The only people who want to help Husks are Husks themselves. Very few humans think of us as equal to them, thinking of us as spies for the hivemind, or at worst, that we're only pretending to be passive.'  
  
'That's not fair.'  
  
'I know, but there's no changing some people's minds,' uttered Marvin despairingly.  
  
'There's got to be some way of making them see we aren't any harm,' said Reggie, although their tone suggested even they didn't believe this.  
  
'Most of us are just resigned to it. We don't do anything because any action can be seen as hostile if your opponent is bent enough.'  
  
They sat in silence some more, with the only sound being the distant surf and soft leaf crunching.  
  
'But what am I going to do now?' asked Reggie. 'It's not like I can make my way back to my place on 11 like this.'  
  
'You can stay here for a while,' said Marvin quickly, 'I can help you there next time I go out gathering, it's not a particularly hard route. You could even help with the gathering itself if you like.'  
  
'I think I'd only get in the way,' said Reggie bashfully.  
  
'It really depends whether you *want* to go back to 11,' said Marvin vaguely.  
  
'Why would I not?'  
  
'[The U.E.C.](the-u-e-c.md) has been causing trouble for us over there. You know, they think the best way to stop us from being a threat is to violently murder us at every opportunity.'  
  
'These are the people who can't have their minds changed?'  
  
Marvin giggled slightly. A rather unusual sound, considering it wasn't one.  
  
'Those are the ones.'
