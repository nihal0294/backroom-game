Ha ha, very cute and disconcerting! I would not want to come across these in the Backrooms… or would I? I like that they’re found in playful levels, and that their population increased after being noticed for the first time- as if the Backrooms is morphing in response to human experience.

Some points and ideas I can think of, related to Concept and then to SPaG/language use:

**Concept**

- I’m a little confused about what happens when a Plushling imprints on you. Do they just follow you around and try to hang out with you, or do they actually, physically cling on to you without letting go? Is this emotional or physical attachment? It isn’t quite clear.

-*“Do Play with them. They are harmless when docile, and only want to have fun.”*  
*”…any attempt at isolating or detaching them will cause them to emit an infant's cry, the likes of which will induce strong bouts of depression in anyone nearby…”*

I feel like there’s a bit of a contradiction here. Plushlings imprint on people, so playing with them seems like a good way to get imprinted. And if they imprint, they can be dangerous to remove. So *is* it a good idea for people to play with Plushlings? It seems like there’s risks to weigh. If a wanderer gets themselves an imprinted Plushling, they could be setting themselves up for trouble if they need it gone for any reason- like having to fight or flee. (This could be very dangerous if they actually grab on- what if someone needed to run, and had a little guy stuck to their leg, or clinging to their face?) Also what if someone needs to be quiet, to sneak or hide from other entities, and the Plushling’s babble gives them away? There’s also a psychological aspect- because most levels are infinite, and exits can be hard to find, a wanderer could end up stuck with a Plushie constantly vying for their attention, for a very long time. They want to play, and get upset when ignored. That on it’s own might be enough to drive someone crazy and make them snap. (Ask anyone with small children.) I think it makes more sense to advise caution about playing with them, rather than outright encouraging it.

Expanding on that, I think it could also be good to address why some people might want to play with Plushlings. After all, they're cute and innocent enough, and someone who’s been isolated in the Backrooms for a while might be desperately craving company. If a wanderer has children they left behind in the Frontrooms, it could be especially painful to ignore them. And if a child-like level is giving someone a bout of nostalgia, they might be more willing to say heck with it and engage in some innocent play. (Especially if they’re mentally far-gone.) All of these things could provide badly-needed emotional/mental nourishment for a wanderer- but like I said before, it may come with risks.

So I think it makes sense to address the possible benefits and harms of interacting with these entities. An entity in a grey area makes for rich storytelling.

-*“This has greatly reduced attacks in Level 283.”*  
Personally, I would not say this. To me it kind of feels like it’s cheapening the danger of Level 283, making it too easy and taking it away from itself. I feel like it’s more interesting to say that hostile Carnies are safe if they have a Plushling, and just leave it at that- because it introduces another factor in surviving the level. A wander could encounter a Carnie with a Plushling- but many other situations could also happen, so there’s no guarantee of safety and people have to pay attention.

- *“strong bouts of depression”*  
For the sake of accurate language around mental illness- does this mean that wanderers are experiencing the equivalent of clinical depression for a certain timeframe after hearing these creatures, or are they experiencing other negative emotions/feelings that commonly get labelled at depression? “Depression” getting used as a colloquial term is a problem- so I think this part needs to be specific about what exactly people are experiencing. If it’s not clinical depression, it should use a different term.

- I’m curious- do Plushlings ever leave their toys scattered around? That could be an interesting, if wanderers can come across random toys in Plushling levels. What would happen if a wanderer tried to take a Plushling’s toy?

- Because Plushlings can sometimes look like dolls, it would make sense for the page to include a warning about not confusing them with [entity 34 (Dollface)](entity-34.md) or [entity 173](entity-173.md) (Evocationaries- which could probably be a doll or a stuffy.) Mixing up a Plushling with either of these two could be fatal.

- What about including some study notes from Stephen Banks, and his experiences of interacting with the Plushlings? The feelings he gets from playing, ignoring, carrying around a Plushling could be very interesting to read.

**Language**

- the “cm” measurements need to be lowercase

- footnote 1 is unnecessary. “-ling” is just a common English suffix for anything small. It doesn’t come from “duckling”. (At least, according to Wikitionary. Here’s the entry: <https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/-ling#English:_diminutive>)

- *“Plushlings most commonly adapt the appearance of stuffed animals…”*  
Using “adapt” here makes it sound like the entities are taking their features on by choice, like a shapeshifter. Maybe use “Plushlings most commonly *take* the appearance of stuffed animals”

- *“Plushlings seem to inherit the mental capacity of a young child…”*  
*“Despite this, they do talk a lot and each Plushling seems to inherit their own personalities.”*  
Using “inherit” this way implies that the Plushlings are being given their personality by something, or someone. Like a creator giving them properties. Is this on purpose? If not, it would be more common just to say “they *have* the mental capacity” and “each Plushling seems to *have* their own personality.” (personality should be single.)

Alright, thanks for getting through my detailed assessment! I always have a lot to say, lol. Keep it up with the uncanny cuteness!

My biggest issue with the concept is that it's something that's been done many many times on the Backrooms to the point of being overdone. The whole 'here's something that appears innocent/good but actually is bad/harmful' (specifically this being overdone. The rest of the concept has potential). I think you could combat this by focusing more on how they can be harmful but in ways that aren't necessarily their fault. Do people make sacrifices for them, such as in giving them most of scavenged food, going on searches just to find things (like toys or clothes) for them, or even putting their own life on the line against a dangerous entity so they can escape. What about company? Will people avoid groups or others out of fear of how they'll interact with the plushling? Do they see the innocence of the plushling being threatened by the violence inherent in most groups/people? And shelter. Do people drive themselves to exhaustion building shelters they think are suitable for the plushling?

Another interesting thing is the relationship between plushlings and children. The obvious here is how children, especially under 5 year olds, interact with them/how plushlings interact with them but I also think it would be interesting to explore how adults view children when they have the company of plushlings. Plushlings really are an 'ideal' version of children, they remain innocent despite the setting demanding otherwise, they are constantly attached to their caregiver and don't seek independence, they enjoy fun activities and imagination. You could explore how plushlings effect the way adults see children. Do adults with plushlings feel pity for children who aren't like plushlings, do much so that they can't bare being around them and abandon real children to live a fantasy? Do they see children as basically being adults because now there are a new 'children' who are much closer to the idea of children than any real child could be? Do they force interactions between children of any age and plushlings, becoming distressed or even agitated at the failure to change the child, unable to escape the fantasy they're trapped in?

Another issue is that this page is really long but doesn't feel like it explores enough. There's a lot of surface level information but things could be explored more. I've brought up a lot of questions above but there's some more I think could be explored. Pick out the most important points from the current text and figure out what makes them so important. Then, work this into the page some more so it feels really expanded.

So the layout of the page is very standard for a Backrooms page. My big advice is to do your own thing. I don't think the interview log adds much. Biology can just be removed, it's really just appearance and that works as its own paragraph in the description. Discovery isn't needed either tbh. Add some subheadings to order the page some more. Maybe one on how they interact with adults, one on how they behave when alone, I'm not sure what else but whatever seems appropriate.

This is all from me for now. Let me know if you have any questions or anything you wanna discuss.
