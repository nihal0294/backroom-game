Valid! The issue with terror hotel articles as a canon is that they're designed to be quite simple through and through. All the terror hotel entities are implied to have been human at some point iirc but the issue is that terror hotel spirit dictates that you write about the monster being a monster rather than their past as a human. I also had little desire to try to break that formula with this because I didn't want to spend the gore time trying to humanise a completely monstrous creature. I like my pure evil characters a good deal lol.

The third section ends up being a bit of a slog on paper which is why I tried to be a bit detailed with the descriptions, though you're right in saying that the article's course is a bit predictable. The unfortunate thing about that is that it's necessary to bridge the more interesting parts together despite how predictable it is. I tried to make the descriptions be a bit more vivid instead though I'm conscious it just may not always land with everyone.

Thanks for the feedback!

Eh, an issue's an issue regardless of the format. But I'd like to point out that [there is precedent for showing how one becomes a Terror Hotel entity](level-5-1.md) and if you had a backstory in mind, adding it could have helped. But the start of the article is fine anyway.

I guess the ending is just a bit longer than it justifies. You do use vivid description effectively as connective tissue throughout the previous parts of the article, but once the entity starts eating the meggie near the end it's just bad — because the reader is already numb to plain gratuitous gore, and is already well aware of what's going to happen. You could have subverted it by having the MEG lass escape somehow, e.g. by some no-clipping accident, which would show something new about the hotel (because the way they react to that — either try and take further action to make sure the photography doesn't spread, or just leave it and go on with working — shows a lot about how the hotel is run). You could also have had something else unexpected to do with the mechanics of the entity happen.

But at the end of the day, "diner that really serves human flesh" is already an [established trope](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TheSecretOfLongPorkPies) and you don't really do anything to unexpected with it. I kind of had already guessed at the start that it was going to be human flesh. The vomit is cool, but then it was over.

Also, being trapped in the freezer would have been better done [from the wanderer's POV](object-18.md).

I am aware that there are articles showing the process of becoming a terror hotel entity, *buuuuutttt* I also didn't want this page to be an origin story of sorts because none of the actual entity pages are (except for jazzheads, which only briefly touch on it amongst other things because the whole point of that article is because they're hardly even monsters themselves). Considering I tried as hard as possible not to humanise Chef beyond 'he has some human mannerisms', I didn't really want the article to have an origin story — though this is not to say there's absolutely no possibility of ever writing it onsite, just not here.

I also appreciate what you're saying when it comes to the trope already being done and the wanting of subversion of expectations, though I didn't set out on doing this mainly because I wanted this article to be a character profile rather than a terror hotel exposition piece. Not to say that there's nothing about the hotel at all, because I tried to use the subpar environment of the kitchen/dining hall parallel to make le meta commentary about how the hotel is not what it seems and all that jazz. I like my creative takes but I'm also a fan of good old slasher flicks, as predictable as they are. That's why I tried to make the difference the quality and the level of description rather than the content itself, because realistically you can't make a a plain murder scene with a knife interesting considering the massive oversaturation of the genre. Like you said, it serves its purpose of being connective tissue — the real focus was the vomit if you had to ask me.

All that said though, I don't mean to try to convince you otherwise because I'm very well aware I took a gamble with the safeness of this article that didn't completely pay off. All your points are very much valid and the only response I can muster is a sheepish 'that's not the article I set out to write'. I will keep them in mind the next time I plan on writing evil cannibal monsters though.

Thanks again for sharing your thoughts!!!!
