# fragment:object-53-4

Deep under Level 11, Mr. March stalked the tunnels.

Down this far, you couldn't hear a hint of the city above.

As above, so below; ceaseless noise above, ceaseless silence below.

Yet, against all odds, there was something to be heard.

A faint echo of a voice, far off, reverberated almost into song by the curling walls.

He followed the tone to its source, and…

---

'Faaaaaatherrrr,' asked a plaintive teenage voice, elsewhere, 'can we go nowwwww?'

Niall sighed.

'Soon, soon,' he said, getting up from the floor and pacing around as his Ellis went back to scrolling on his phone.

Bailey barely even lifted their head from their book as Niall leaned on the wall beside them.

'I know this is hard for you, son,' said Niall slowly, 'but I promise we'll be out of here soon.'

The teen folded over the corner of the page and closed the covers.

They gazed at the rip in the wall where blue light was spilling out like they were observing a novel kind of beetle.

'Why did we have to come all the way down here for you to open that thing?' Bailey asked.

'Y-fixation,' replied Niall flatly.

'I… don't get it dad.'

Niall laughed.

'Just playing with you,' he replied, 'basically the higher you go in a level, the harder it gets to open a rift. I used to be able to do it at street level all the time…'

He trailed off, then snapped back with a jolt.

'Of course it also helps make sure no ordinary people get trapped outside.'

Bailey stared back at the hole.

'Will I ever be able to do that?' they asked tentatively.

'It takes a while to do consistently,' replied Niall, feeling the edges of the crack to make sure it wasn't faltering, 'but I don't think you'll find it that hard.'

'Faaaatherrrrrr,' droned Ellis again, 'someone's comingggggg.'

---

Mr. March was glad to find the source of the voices.

Despite his experience with liminal navigation, getting to specific coordinates in such confined tunnels was near-impossible.

He reached the tunnel that blazed with azure light that flowed like ink.

Mr. March barely had to take in the scene at all.

The crack in reality could be safely ignored; he didn't understand it, and probably wouldn't even if he examined it, so there was no point in observing it too long.

The children were similarly inconsequential, they looked like regular teenagers and so would probably act like regular teenagers.

The only real interest was the client.

Mr. March internally corrected themselves; Niall wasn't a client in the traditional sense.

'You came,' said Niall, inspecting Mr. March critically.

'Why wouldn't I?'

'I wouldn't have thought you did this sort of thing.'

'I wouldn't have thought you would do this sort of thing either,' replied March, gesturing to the lurid cut in the wall.

The two glared at each other for a moment, neither one wanting to take the next move.

'Faaaaaaaaatherrrrrrrr, do you always have to aura farm in front of guests?' intoned Ellis.

The tension snapped like a wooden ruler, and they stared at the teen.

'Um, yes, quite right, Ellis, well done,' said Niall distractedly, 'Can we have the skin now Mr. uhhh-'

'March,' replied March.

'Is that like a pseudonym or-'

'It's also my name. Pretty funny coincidence actually.'

Mr. March tossed the pelt over and Niall caught it quickly.

'Perfect, we can get going now,' said Niall.

'Hold a moment,' said Mr. March quickly, 'There were complications. It would be best to discuss without these youths around.'

There was a short objection from one twin, but the other stayed silent.

Niall looked grave.

'I suppose I have no choice,' he said, leading March off into a less echoey tunnel.

Mr. March twisted the dial of his watch, and a blueish glow lit up the dull cylinder of the tunnel.

'What was it you wanted to discuss?'

'Your wife is dead,' said March simply.

'Oh.'

Niall didn't seem completely surprised by the revelation, nor did he react in the usual way husbands did when hearing of their spouse's death.

'How did it happen?' asked Niall slowly.

'She tried to set a trap for you where the pelt was being stored,' replied Mr. March, condensing somewhat the trials of the day, 'She tried to attack with a sword she didn't know how to use properly, I defended with a table fork, and she accidentally cut her own head off.'

March could almost see the gears turning in Niall's head.

'I already know he is an assassin, ergo, there is no reason to lie and say it was an accident, and the absurdity of the situation makes it more true, not less,' thought Niall.

'I see,' he said momentarily, 'Not sure it has much bearing at this stage.'

'Explain.'

'Well, I have been expecting this for some time,' said Niall, 'U.E.C. scientists don't last once they become senior, they usually make some big fuck-up and are removed from their position before long.'

March listened attentively.

'After I leave, I don't plan to come back soon, neither,' continued Niall, 'she'd probably be dead by the time my boys are trained enough to come back here.'

'Finally,' Niall said, 'She is no longer my wife. To help one find one's skin is the greatest gift, and in my specific clan's culture, it is often an offer of marriage. She helped me find my skin after I lost it from a freak blue surge, which is why I went with her, but that union is now voided by her trying to hide it from me.'

'Odd system of marriage,' said Mr. March slowly, then halted in the face of a strange idea, 'wait, this doesn't mean I have to marry you now?'

Niall laughed, then stopped suddenly.

'Actually… yeah I think you do.'

'Fuck no,' said Mr. March swiftly, 'I suppose I am at liberty to refuse?'

'Of course, my clan is not completely old-fashioned, at least the last time I checked. Probably got worse since I lived with them. But yes, you're not forced to go along with it.'

'Capital,' replied March, relieved, 'I think Rain would have got upset if I got into a polycule without asking him first. Actually, thinking of him, why didn't you explain all this to him? He knows you better than me.'

'Because…' the father trailed off, 'You know, I'm not entirely sure. Maybe because it doesn't matter what you know, you won't tell? Maybe because strangers are easier to confide in? As I say, I'm unsure.'

'Eh, that makes sense,' said March.

'Anything else?'

'Well…' Mr. March wondered if it was right to mention it, 'Why is Lorretta upset at you now? She said you took her children, but your twins seem fine with you.'

'Oh, that,' said Niall as if he had been expecting it, 'I just let them take their own decision, and that upset Loretta so. You see, when we seals partner with regular humans, we don't stay forever. Our main goal is to sire children and bring them back to the clan with us when we are of age. It used to be forced, but now we tend to ask if our kids actually *want* to come. I asked a week after the boys turned sixteen. I tried to make it clear that it was entirely within their own will whether they came or not. They both wanted to stay with me and see the world. They knew they would likely not see their mother again, but they came anyway. I guess Loretta didn't want them to leave her control.'

'Leave her control?' said March, puzzled by the wording.

'Oh, yeah, I should have mentioned,' said Niall, 'She isn't exactly the best mother, nor a decent wife. She spent more time at the lab than she ought to have, and we preferred it that way, if that makes sense. I'm sure you don't need a detailed description from me.'

'Right, thanks for your help,' said March, squinting through the glare of his watch to check the time, 'Now get out of here. I need to get back before Rain starts worrying about me.'

'Yeah, you get back home,' said Niall lightly, 'By the way, it was nice to meet you. I hope we meet again.'

'I doubt it,' said Mr. March, striding off into the shade.

---

When Niall returned to the hole in the wall, the twins had proactively got out the rope harness they had made.

It was there to make sure the boys didn't have their arms torn off when being taken to their new home.

A simple harness designed to brace two teenagers against a large harbour seal.

Niall flopped around the corner in his true form.

He arfed at his two sons.

Bailey patted his father's shiny head.

They tied themselves together, and the two humans sat at the edge of the portal as the Selkie prepared to leave.

The seal leapt over the edge suddenly, dragging the boys with it.

It was an unusual sensation, travelling with such speed through the blue channel.

By some arcane power, there was no exertion at all, being dragged along by Niall felt like riding on a canal boat, rather than the hyperdrive that it really was.

The pair waited, silenced by the vast clouds of blue as they hopped dimensions.

---

I ride the lunar winds in a path I've tread before.

A long-abandoned route home, littered with the ashes of my old life.

Six dimensions pass by me at once, I narrowly sweep past a hiatus with a [rapidly-shifting world](phenomenon-32.md).

I dive into the rolling deep blue far below.

A curl of blue in the depths slides past lost cities as I row past the forgotten.

I spin through the dark, my sons like trailing comets behind me.

I veer up, out of the shade, into a new light.

My trek is almost over.

The overarching flotillas of the Selkies was ahead of me, like they always were, even back in my day.

A sprawling super-structure of traditional house-boats like a village of swaying reeds.

As I approach my new home and my old home, I feel a strange sensation.

Below the new excitement, a long-forgotten emotion.

Above the blue, beyond the land…

I am free again.

  
  
  

Fin.
