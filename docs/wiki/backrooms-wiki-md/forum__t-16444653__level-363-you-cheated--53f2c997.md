Nobody is coming.

05 Nov 2023 23:14

[PLEASE ONLY READ THIS ONCE YOU FINISH THE PAGE](javascript:;)

[Well I mean I guess I'm not your father](javascript:;)

Okay, so first up, thanks a lot for reading this page! It actually took lots of efforts to create, and I'm quite happy of how it turned out!

I also want to use this author post as a way to thanks once more the amazing people who gave me crits for this page:

- for his defining help about the page concept. This wouldn't be as great if he didn't give me feedback about the initial concept.
- for her vital first crit on both SPAG and concept. It helped me a lot in deciding how I wanted the page to be.
- for their help in the execution of the concept. Some parts were quite clunky at first, using ideas in the wrong way. Thanks to Aether, some elements of the page got changed to better serve the purpose of the article
- for her massive help on the narrative execution. Between lackluster paragraphs and weird sentences, it was through her help that the parts were developed well.
- for his massive narration expertise. Some parts really needed some important tweaks to really immerse the reader in the story. Not only this, but Graph also helped A LOT on the SPAG part. If the page feels immersive to you, that is thanks to him.

Now, I want to talk a bit about the elephant in the room. This article is a narrative format screw. Why did I choose this, even though this form of writing was discouraged (but not forbidden)? It's simple. Classicon is not a contest about a format. It was not supposed to be a clinical contest, but instead one about the use of the old feelings of the wiki. Liminality, dread, survival, insanity, human insecurities. The Backrooms, from the beginning, have been a world where we could write about the fears of mankind. Disconcerting nostalgia. The dread of the unknown. The fear of one's own weakness. All of this can be found in a particular image that was rather popular in the early years of the wiki.

![maxresdefault.jpg](https://i.ytimg.com/vi/sBTenaWAXpA/maxresdefault.jpg)

This always has been fascinating to me. The clash between the nostalgia of the empty room, compared to the fear of the unknown, the scare of what existed beyond the door. Beyond the threshold, into the darkness. Unfortunately, it seemed that the early writers of the Backrooms just didn't use this image to its full potential. As much as this sounds like a liminal purist talking… the use of entities… quite disrespected the image in itself. Yet another bland concept where a person is attacked by creatures that came from the void ahead. *So original*. But this image does not need scary monsters to create a feeling of uneasiness. It is already, without any presence, giving the watcher a sense of dread, as they witness the unknown ahead of them. there is a feeling of something waiting for your end, yet you don't know what it truly is. You feel trapped in this room, and your attention is driven only by the vast expanse of darkness. You can't go anywhere else. yet you are scared of what will happen beyond that threshold. This feeling is what I wanted this image to be used for in a Backrooms writing. Sometimes, you don't need scary creatures or even lethal environmental dangers. Sometimes, the fear just comes from yourself, from the realiztion you will not escape, and from the madness of your own despair.

This is what I wanted to develop for this image. Obviously, the original image isn't CC-compliant, as it was used and popularized through Fandom. But I found a great replacement over on [Creepystock](index.md), a really great website hosting really great liminal CC-compliant images. I actually prefer this image over the first iteration, at least for my narrative. It is taken at the moment when the wanderer notices a potential exit from this place. But they don't know what lies beyond. Unlike the original images, this one doesn't show what exists beyond the door, yet the feeling of the unknown persists, even if it's not a feeling of creep yet.

Now that I talked about the image, let's speak of the writing. As I said, I wanted, with this article, to use the potential of the original You Cheated image that I felt was wasted over bland concepts. But the original concept was about the idea of cheating in the Backrooms, so let's talk about this a little. If you have been active on Fandom, or just read their lore occasionally, you may have seen a rewrite of level You Cheated named [The Game](https://backrooms.fandom.com/wiki/The_Game) (a rewrite I wasn't aware of back when I wrote the first draft of this contest entry). But I'm bringing that up because it is using a key part of the original level concept (or at least in the designated room section): the idea of cheating. Both in the original page and rewrite, the cheating aspect has been used in a videogame way, showcasing a more digital idea of the Backrooms.

But that's not how I saw the concept. Let's talk a bit about the Backrooms overall. A massive ecosystem where humans aren't supposed to be. But, with time, humans started to noclip from the Frontrooms into the Backrooms. But they were never supposed to take part in its ecosystem. The Backrooms were a world of twisted reflection, copying Earth in a disturbing way. But every creature, every landscape, served a purpose in the overall Backrooms ecosystem. It didn't make sense to the human mind, but this place was self-sufficient. However, humans started to change this world, just like they did on Earth. Instead of entering into the ecosystem, they used it for their own gains. That's what cheating in the Backrooms is to me. A species that, instead of blending in and contributing to a virtuous yet strange cycle, instead exploits those unknown shores for themselves.

- They abused phenomenons regulating this world to disrupt the following of events, cheating death by escaping from situations where they should have perished. Without the fear of death, they developped themselves far more than they were supposed to. Humans became a parasitic species of the Backrooms.
- They industrialized resources from the Backrooms. Mass-producing items like Warpberries. We saw in the object page that those fruits were never supposed to be created on such an immense scale. By doing so, humanity majorly disrupted the local environment of Level 10. Yet again, they are cheating by using items they were not supposed to, which creates a negative impact on the universe.
- They used their knowledge to create twisted contraptions like the Hermes Devices. Those tools are very controversial even amongst mankind. They are an immoral way of getting the upper hand over traveling in the Backrooms. By horrible means, humans are disturbing how core properties of the Backrooms work, by exploiting and forcing them. Again, they never were supposed to do this, and this cheating aspect is yet again disrupting this universe.

I won't tell the identity of who or what was behind the level, and to be honest, even I don't know. But some*thing* grew bored of a species that was parasitizing the Backrooms, and endangering it for their own gain. Humans felt too… confident. Too superior. And the person you just read about, in particular, was one of them. Someone who thought with pride and overconfidence. Someone who cheated, using unstable contraptions, mass-produced food, and phenomena of the Backrooms for themselves. This person, by their feeling of superiority, represents really well how mankind in general behaves in the Backrooms. Prideful, arrogant even. They cheated and did not deserve their lives. And cheaters deserve to be punished. And while they are punished, some*thing* will gorge over their distress, feeding on how their prideful mind quickly turns into fear, then despair, as they understand they are nothing more than simple creatures that, by cheating, thought to be gods. But they aren't powerful at all, and this level is here as a way to remind them how weak and pitiful they actually are, destroying them little by little.

And everything I said, the context, the feelings… everything has to be felt by the reader. it is a story, based on emotions. And clinical tone can't give any of those. This kind of writing has pros, for writing descriptions about landscapes, groups, creatures… but it can't convey feelings enough. Narration exists to show a feeling, to translate emotions to the reader. It is an incredible tool to use when talking about nostalgic, scary feelings. And those feelings were the true theme of the contest. They were the old vibes that failed to be used well by the lackluster clinical tone of 2020. And where clinical tone fails, narration shines.

I very much hope this story succeded in conveying those feelings to you. A classic concept with a classic image and classic emotions, but revisited with new eyes and new ways to write. That's what I wanted to achieve for classicon. And I hope I did.

It is intriguing how this article, in an unconventional manner, aptly addresses the topic by "non-classical".

I mean that it doesn't conform to the classic style of a clinical tone and standard formatting. Instead, it offers a thoughtful reflection on what "classic" means, coupled with a degree of criticism regarding the present situation.

In terms of the narrative structure, humans evolve from initially disregarding space (merely treating it as a transitional corridor) to the eventual collapse of faith. They are compelled to yield to space, utilizing the "paths" it offers for exploration.

Humans continuously attempt to "bypass level" within this space, from the most ordinary no-clip -> trying items -> the Hermes Device. Almost every time, they seem to expect to succeed "as a matter of course," but the recurrent failures are akin to levels increasing their volume in their ears, proclaiming, "You cannot escape from here."

While I believe this article could potentially lead people's thoughts to be more extreme and arrogant, this demand is already nitpicking, as the article itself is already exceptionally well-crafted.

Close, but no cigar

06 Nov 2023 06:56

I've read both the article and your author's post, and whilst I can appreciate the effort behind your attempts to innovate, I do not feel like this does the original concept justice.

For starters, I'll break down why, in fact, the image RELIED on an entity to be scary. The original 'you cheated' image was two sentences long — you cheated, someone is coming. Right off the bat, you're thrown in an oppressive situation wherein you are cornered. The tiny room whose door is its only aperture is the only place you can go to via noclipping and the like, lest you want to exit out of the door into the unknown — considering the fact that someone is coming from that door, you might want to avoid doing that. As such you're left to wait and wonder what's going to become of you. Furthermore, the usage of the word 'someone' instead of 'something' means that whatever is coming from the door isn't some random bumfuck entity rules of the jungle entity. It is a person, it has some degree of rationality and personality, it knows you did something it didn't like, you know it's coming for you, and you know there is nothing you can do. You're left at the mercy of whoever this is and you're left feeling **helplessness**, which is in reality where I think the image shined and the article failed.

The feeling of helplessness in this article contrasted to the original is that the helplessness of this article promises a known death (you will eventually die of thirst or whatever), whereas the original image doesn't specify what's going to happen to you. You're left at the mercy of the thing that's coming for you, but you have no clue *what* exactly that may be.

The outside environment here is something you can go through, even if it's infinite, giving the place a sense of vastness. It doesn't feel off-limits either, just a bit scary. In the original image the outside environment could also be infinite, but due to the presence of something that is imminent residing within it, it feels much more oppressive and makes the environment seem claustrophobic even, emphasizing your trapped nature.

In this article, through omnipresent narration, you know just why you're here. You industrialized the backrooms or whatever and now you're paying the price (more on this later). In the original, you have no idea what you did. You don't know what cheating constitutes. There is an element of horror in knowing that there is some game you don't know the rules of and you just broke one of them, and you're suffering the consequences of your ignorance.

Contrasted, it's easy to see that this version of the image doesn't evoke the same fearful helplessness. It's the kind of helplessness where you can just accept your fate and find comfort in your inevitable doom. Existential? Yes. Scary? Not really. The original does a better job at creating dread; not knowing if you can make peace with the situation because you don't even know *what* you're facing. It's in your face, it's sudden, it chills you to the bones. This one doesn't do that. It's a shitty situation to be stuck in, but there's no 'unknown'. Your commentary on darkness reminding us of weakness was done very well, but you missed one crucial fact in that the whole reason darkness is scary is *because* of our fear that something is in it.

Conceptually, the article thus loses an edge on the original. There's no 'unknown', there's no claustrophobia, no imminent threat that serves to make your readers piss their pants. It feels like a very diluted modern interpretation that misses what made the original scary.

This is reflected even in the writing style. The original is so scary because there is a lack of explanation. Two sentences and an image is all you get, and in my 3 years in the backrooms fandom I distinctly remember it being one of the most chilling pieces of writing attached to these sites. This one however is way too verbose. It goes on and on about how the protagonist was trained to do this and trained to do that, and hopelessness and fear set in at like 3 or 4 different times in the whole article. We get a whole part at the end where the protagonist shows the cool props he's acquired from the backrooms and how they fail in a power showcase against the room, and then a philosophical account that tries to take itself way more seriously than it should. The philosophical musings I find the most egregious because they are the very most basic of environmental commentary — "ah, we're so evil, we disrupt ecosystems, this is our reminder to stay humble". Not that the message doesn't make any sense, but it's not as deep as you'd like it to sound. The implied entity that got bored of us doesn't feel as powerful and imposing as the one in the original. It just feels vindictive and honestly? Kind of lame.

Two sentences was all it took to elicit such a visceral response from me in the original, but here it just goes on and on and loses any effect it *could* have had on me even with a different interpretation, especially so with the author post actively working to justify why it was changed at all when it really should not have been changed. All in all, it's a conceptual loss that's further hindered by the especially long writing and the ham-fisted philosophical discourse. Innovation is nice, seeing how far we've come is nice, but sometimes the original just works because of its simplicity.
